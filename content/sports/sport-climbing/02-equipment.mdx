# Equipment evolution

Climbing has almost no competitive equipment and one enormous one: the route
itself.

Shoes, chalk, a harness and a rope, none of them regulated for performance in
any meaningful way. There is no equipment arms race here, and no version of the
javelin, the swimsuit or the bicycle. A climber's kit is broadly what it was
twenty years ago.

What decides a competition is the **route-setting**, and setters are not
officials. They design the problems for each event, and the choices they make —
whether a boulder rewards power or balance, whether a lead route suits tall
climbers or short ones — shape the result before anyone climbs. A sport whose
field of play is invented afresh for every competition by people exercising
taste is unlike anything else on this site.

The exception is speed, and it is the most interesting equipment fact in this
collection. Since 2007 the speed route has been **identical everywhere**: the
same wall angle, the same fifteen metres, the same holds bolted in the same
positions, worldwide.

That standardisation gives speed climbing a property rowing, sailing and golf
all concluded they could never have. Because every climber attempts the same
problem, speed times are genuinely comparable across venues, countries and
decades — a real world record, in a sport whose other two disciplines cannot
produce one at all.

One sport therefore holds the best and the worst comparability on this board,
and the difference is entirely whether the route was standardised.
