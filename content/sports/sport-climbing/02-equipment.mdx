# The route is the equipment

A climber brings shoes, chalk and a harness, and uses a rope in lead. None of
it is regulated for performance in any way that matters, and the kit is much
what it was twenty years ago. The difficulty comes from the route.

## The setters

<Term id="route-setter">Route-setters</Term> design the climbs for each
competition and bolt the <Term id="hold">holds</Term> to the wall. They are
not officials. Their choices still shape the result before anyone climbs. A
boulder <Term id="problem">problem</Term> can reward power or balance. A lead
route can suit tall climbers or short ones.

<Photos ids="sport-climbing-setter-drill sport-climbing-setter-munich" />

In boulder and lead the playing area is therefore invented new for every
event, by people using their own taste.

## One route for the whole world

Speed is the exception. Since 2007 the speed route has been identical
everywhere: the same wall angle, the same fifteen metres, the same holds
bolted in the same positions.

Before that, speed races on different routes measured different things. There
was no record to chase, and no way to compare two climbers who had never met.

Now every speed climber attempts the same climb, so a time set in one country
can be compared with a time set in another, years apart. Speed climbing has a
true world record. [Rowing](/sports/rowing/), [sailing](/sports/sailing/) and
[golf](/sports/golf/) all decided their courses differ too much for that.
Boulder and lead cannot have a record at all.
