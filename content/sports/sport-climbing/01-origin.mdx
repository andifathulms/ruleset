# From the rock to the wall

Climbing was a way of getting up mountains long before it was a sport.
Mountaineering had no rules, no officials and no way to compare two climbers
except whether each of them reached the top.

Sport climbing appeared in the 1980s, when climbers began fixing permanent
bolts into the rock. With a rope clipped to a bolt, a fall was safe. A climber
could try moves that would otherwise be too dangerous, and the question
changed from how to manage the risk to how hard a climb could be.

<Photos ids="sport-climbing-giggleswick sport-climbing-shipley-bolts" />

Competition followed almost at once. Sportroccia, one of the first organised
climbing competitions, was held in 1985 on a real rock face, the Parete dei
Militi in the Valle Stretta.

Then the competitions moved indoors, almost as quickly. On an artificial wall
the organisers control the <Term id="route">route</Term>. They can build a new
one for each event and hold the event anywhere. By the 1990s the competitive
sport had largely separated from the outdoor one.

<Photos ids="sport-climbing-sportroccia-wall sport-climbing-arco-stadium" />

The International Federation of Sport Climbing was founded in 2007, when it
split from the international mountaineering federation.

<Photo id="sport-climbing-arco-2007" />

The three disciplines grew up separately. <Term id="lead">Lead</Term> is the
closest relative of outdoor sport climbing.
<Term id="boulder">Boulder</Term> came from a tradition of practising low
down without a rope. <Term id="speed">Speed</Term> was a racing format of the
Soviet era. In 2007 [it fixed one route for the whole
world](#ifsc-2007-standard-speed-route), and it now has little in common with
the other two.

<Photo id="sport-climbing-innsbruck-walls" />

Most of the arguments in sport climbing come from that distance between the
three.
