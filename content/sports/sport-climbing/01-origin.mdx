# Origin and invention

Sport climbing is the newest sport on this site and the only one that had to
separate itself from a parent activity that was not a sport at all.

Climbing existed as mountaineering: an outdoor pursuit with no rules, no
officials and no comparison beyond whether you got up. Sport climbing emerged
in the 1980s when climbers began bolting protection into rock permanently,
which changed the activity from a risk-management problem into a difficulty
problem — you could fall safely, so you could attempt things you would
otherwise not.

Competition followed almost immediately, and moved indoors almost as fast.
Artificial walls let organisers control the route, set it fresh for each event
and hold competitions anywhere. By the 1990s the competitive sport had
substantially detached from the outdoor one.

The IFSC was founded in 2007, splitting from the international mountaineering
federation — a governing body created specifically because a sport had grown
out of something that was not one.

The three disciplines developed separately and for different reasons. Lead is
the closest descendant of outdoor sport climbing. Boulder came from a
low-level, no-rope practice tradition. Speed was a Soviet-era racing format
that standardised its route entirely in 2007 and now has almost nothing in
common with the other two.

That divergence is the source of everything contested on this page.
