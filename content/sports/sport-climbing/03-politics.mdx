# Governing body politics

Sport climbing's politics is a quota problem, and the quota produced the worst
scoring system on this site.

For 2020 the IOC granted climbing one medal event per sex. The federation had
three disciplines, all with established international competition and separate
constituencies, and no way to include them within one medal except by combining
them. It chose to multiply the ranks together, because multiplication separates
results more sharply than addition.

The result was a format that asked sprinters to climb endurance routes and
endurance climbers to sprint, produced standings nobody could compute during
competition, and was widely held within the sport to have crowned the wrong
champion. It lasted one Games.

For 2024 the federation obtained a second medal event and split the sport along
the line its athletes actually specialise on: speed separately, boulder and
lead together. That is the correct division and everyone in the sport knew it
in 2016 — the federation simply did not have the medals to do it.

This is worth recording as a governance pattern rather than an error. The IOC
sets a quota, a federation with more disciplines than medals must compress, and
the compression is done with arithmetic that then determines who wins. Modern
pentathlon's points tables, sailing's class reselection and climbing's combined
format are all versions of the same pressure.

The other tension is with the outdoor sport, which has no rules, no rankings
and no interest in acquiring any, and from which a large part of the climbing
public still takes its idea of what climbing is.
