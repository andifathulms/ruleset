# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Tokyo, 2021 — the multiplication.** Climbing's Olympic debut awarded one
medal across speed, boulder and lead, and calculated the result by multiplying
each climber's rank in the three disciplines. A climber placing first, fourth
and second scored eight, and the lowest product won.

Two things were wrong with it and both were predictable. Because the score is a
product of positions, the value of a place depends on everyone else's results —
a climber could improve in one discipline and finish lower overall. And speed
is a five-second sprint while lead is a six-minute endurance route, so the
format required specialists to contest disciplines they did not train.

Commentators could not compute the standings live. Athletes said openly that
the format did not measure climbing. It was abandoned after a single Games,
which makes it one of the shortest-lived scoring systems in this collection —
and it was replaced by exactly the split the sport had wanted from the start.

**Route-setting.** The recurring dispute with no rule attached. A set that
favours one body type or style shapes a result, setters are not neutral
instruments, and the sport has no mechanism for reviewing a set the way it
would review a judging decision.

**Speed's separateness.** Whether speed belongs in the same sport at all is
argued at every format review. It shares a wall with the other two disciplines
and nothing else — different training, different athletes, different physics —
and its standardised route makes it the only part of climbing with a real world
record.
