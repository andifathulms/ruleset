# One medal for three disciplines

## Three into one

Sport climbing was given one medal event for men and one for women at its
first Olympic Games. The federation had three disciplines. Each had its own
international competitions and its own followers. The only way to include all
three was to combine them.

<Photo id="sport-climbing-combined-2018" />

[The federation chose to multiply](#ifsc-2020-combined-multiplication). Each
climber's finishing places in speed, boulder and lead were multiplied
together, and the lowest result won. A climber who came first, fourth and
second scored eight. Multiplication was chosen because it separates results
more sharply than addition.

## Tokyo, 2021

The format was used once, at the Tokyo Games.

Two things went wrong, and both had been predicted. First, a score made by
multiplying places depends on everyone else's places. A climber could do
better in one discipline and finish lower overall. Neither the climbers nor
the commentators could work out the standings while the competition was
going on.

<Photo id="sport-climbing-lead-final-route" />

Second, speed is a five-second sprint and lead is an endurance route with a
six-minute clock. Almost no top climber trains for both. Sprinters had to
climb endurance routes, and endurance climbers had to sprint. Athletes said
openly that the format did not measure climbing, and it was widely described
within the sport as producing the wrong champion.

## The split

[The combined format was dropped after that one
Games](#ifsc-2024-format-split). For 2024 the federation obtained a second
medal event and divided the sport where its athletes divide themselves. Speed
became its own event. Boulder and lead, which share their training, stayed
together.

<Photos ids="sport-climbing-garnbret-bern-2023 sport-climbing-speed-final-2018" />

Adding boulder to lead needed a common scale, and none existed. Boulder had
been counted in <Term id="top">tops</Term> and
<Term id="zone">zones</Term>, and lead by the highest hold reached. [The
federation wrote a hundred-point scale for
both](#ifsc-2024-boulder-lead-scoring), with points for reaching defined
holds and deductions for extra attempts.

The same pressure shows up in other sports. A federation with more
disciplines than medals has to squeeze them together, and the arithmetic it
picks then helps decide who wins. The points tables of
[modern pentathlon](/sports/modern-pentathlon/) answer the same problem.
