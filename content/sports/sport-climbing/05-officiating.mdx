# Officiating and technology

Climbing officiates two different kinds of thing and only one of them is
mechanised.

**Speed** is fully instrumented. Timing pads at the top, false-start detection
at the bottom, times to a thousandth. Because the route is identical
everywhere, there is nothing to interpret and nothing to compare imperfectly.
It is the cleanest measurement in this sport and one of the cleanest on the
site.

**Boulder and lead** are judged. A judge decides whether a climber controlled a
hold, which hold they reached, and whether an attempt counts. Those are
judgements about the state of a body at a moment, and while video review exists
for appeals, the underlying question is a matter of assessment rather than
measurement.

The 2024 hundred-point scale added a second layer of officiating that is not
done by an official at all. Points are attached to specific holds before the
competition, by the federation and the setters, and deductions are set for
attempts. Where the points sit determines what the route rewards — so the
scoring decision is taken in advance, in a document, and then simply applied.

That is now a familiar shape on this site. Gymnastics prices its elements.
Taekwondo prices head kicks and spins. Modern pentathlon converts disciplines
onto a common scale. Climbing prices holds. In each case the most consequential
judgement in the sport is made before anyone competes, by people who are not
officials, and is invisible during the event.
