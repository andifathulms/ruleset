# Where it is played

Climbing's competitive map follows commercial climbing walls, which follow
urban wealth — and it is changing faster than any other map on this site
because the walls are still being built.

Japan is the outstanding nation, particularly in boulder, with a dense network
of commercial gyms and a competition culture that predates the sport's Olympic
inclusion. Slovenia, France, Austria, Great Britain, the United States, South
Korea and increasingly China are the others.

Slovenia is the case worth recording: a country of two million producing
world-leading lead climbers, on the back of accessible limestone, a club
structure and a national programme. It is the climbing equivalent of Ireland in
boxing or Romania in rowing.

Speed climbing has a genuinely different map — Indonesia, China, Poland and
Ukraine are dominant — and the reason is the standardised route. Because the
problem is identical everywhere, a country can build one wall and train against
the world's best times without ever leaving home. Standardisation turned out to
be a development tool as well as a comparability one.

The outdoor sport's geography is entirely different again and follows rock,
which is why the competitive and recreational versions of climbing have
increasingly little to do with each other.

The barrier everywhere is the wall. It is a building, it costs money, and a
town either has one or it does not — the same constraint gymnastics and
swimming have, at smaller scale.
