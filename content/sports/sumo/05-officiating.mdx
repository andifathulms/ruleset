# Officiating

Sumo's officiating is unusual in that the referee can be overruled every time,
and everybody expects it.

The **gyōji** stands in the ring, starts nothing — the wrestlers begin by
mutual consent — and must declare a winner immediately, always, and without
hesitation. He is required to point his fan at one side even when the finish
was too close to see, which is a genuinely strange obligation: the ring
official is compelled to make a call he may not be able to make.

Five **judges** sit around the ring and any of them may raise a hand to
dispute the decision. They then climb into the ring and confer in the open,
sometimes for minutes, in front of the crowd. They may confirm the referee,
reverse him, or order the bout rewrestled. Video review has been used to
support this conference since the late 1960s, which makes sumo an early adopter
of replay by the standards of the sports on this site.

The calls themselves are physical facts, which is why the sport needs no
scoring apparatus. Did a hand touch the clay? Did a heel cross the bales?
Almost every dispute is a near-simultaneous finish, where one wrestler is
falling as the other steps out — and the sport has an entire doctrine about
whether a wrestler who was already irretrievably falling can still win by his
opponent touching down first.

Fouls — a closed fist, hair-pulling, an illegal grip — lose the bout outright.
There is no lesser penalty anywhere in the sport, which is consistent with a
rulebook that has no scale to deduct from.
