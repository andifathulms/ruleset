# A referee who can always be overruled

The <Term id="gyoji">gyōji</Term> stands in the ring, but he does not start
the bout. The wrestlers start it together, in the
<Term id="tachi-ai">tachi-ai</Term>, when both are ready. The gyōji must
declare a winner at once, every time, by pointing his fan at one side. He has
to point even when the finish was too close to see.

<Photo id="sumo-gyoji" />

Five <Term id="judges">judges</Term> sit around the ring. Any of them can
raise a hand to dispute the gyōji's call. The judges then climb into the ring
and confer in front of the crowd, sometimes for minutes. This is the
<Term id="mono-ii">mono-ii</Term>. They may confirm the call, reverse it, or
order a <Term id="rematch">rematch</Term>. Since the late 1960s video replay
has helped them decide.

<Photos ids="sumo-mono-ii sumo-shimpan-1919" />

The questions are about physical facts. Did a hand touch the clay? Did a heel
touch the sand outside the bales? Most disputes are finishes that happen
almost together: one wrestler is falling as the other steps out. Sumo has a
long tradition of argument about whether a wrestler who was already falling
beyond recovery can still win because his opponent touched down first.

A <Term id="foul">foul</Term>, such as a closed fist, pulling the hair or an
illegal grip, loses the bout outright. There is no smaller penalty. With no
score, there is nothing to take points from.
