# What is still argued about

**The two sumos.** The world championships do not include the best wrestlers
in the world, and nothing in the rules could make them. Should the
international federation seek ties with the professional association, or
accept that it runs a separate sport? Most of the other arguments sit under
this one.

**Weight classes.** Professional sumo has [no weight
divisions](#sumo-no-weight-classes). In a small ring, mass is a direct
advantage, so wrestlers eat and train to become enormous. The health costs
are documented: diabetes, failing joints, shorter lives. The amateur
federation adopted <Term id="weight-class">weight classes</Term> at once. The
professional body treats the open form as part of what sumo is.

**Women and the dohyō.** Is the ban a religious rule, discrimination, or
both? It is argued in public in Japan whenever an incident brings it back into
view, and it has not moved.

**Foreign wrestlers.** Wrestlers from Mongolia, Eastern Europe, Hawaii and
elsewhere have held the highest ranks for long stretches. Akebono, of Hawaii,
was the first foreign-born yokozuna. The professional association limits how
many foreign-born wrestlers a stable may hold, a nationality rule inside a
domestic competition.

<Photo id="sumo-akebono-2001" />

**The Olympics.** The international federation has IOC recognition and a
World Games place. An Olympic sumo event would still not be the top of the
sport, which makes it a weak case to put to the IOC.
