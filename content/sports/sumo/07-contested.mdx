# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The two sumos.** The world championship does not contain the best wrestlers
in the world and there is no mechanism by which it could. Whether the
international federation should pursue a relationship with the professional
association, or accept that it governs a parallel sport, is the question
underlying everything else.

**Weight classes in the professional sport.** The absence produces the sport's
characteristic body and its documented health outcomes. The amateur federation
adopted classes immediately in 1992; the professional body has never
seriously proposed them, and treats the open form as constitutive of the sport.

**Women and the dohyō.** A religious prohibition, a discrimination complaint,
or both. It is argued publicly in Japan, it flares whenever an incident forces
it into view, and it has not moved.

**Foreign recruitment limits.** A cap on how many foreign-born wrestlers a
stable may hold is a nationality restriction that no other sport covered here
applies to its own domestic competition.

**Olympic ambition.** The international federation holds recognition and a
World Games place. Inclusion would give sumo an Olympic event that is not the
sport's pinnacle, which is a weak proposition for the IOC and an odd one for
the sport.

**Stable welfare.** The residential apprenticeship system, its discipline, and
the unpaid lower divisions have been examined by Japanese authorities after
deaths and abuse cases. It is governed as a Japanese institutional question
rather than as a sporting one, which is itself part of what is contested.
