# A belt, a ring of clay, and no padding

A sumo wrestler wears one thing, the <Term id="mawashi">mawashi</Term>. It is
a long strip of heavy silk or cotton, wound around the waist and between the
legs. It is also the sport's main piece of equipment. Many of the recognised
winning techniques start with a grip on it, so the opponent's belt is the
handle a wrestler uses to move him. [Judo](/sports/judo/) works the same way
with its jacket.

<Photos ids="sumo-mawashi sumo-mawashi-grip" />

The dohyō is a raised platform of clay. On it is a circle 4.55 metres across,
marked by <Term id="straw-bales">straw bales</Term> half buried in the clay.
The surface is clay and sand, not a mat. Because the ring is raised, a
wrestler who leaves it falls off it.

There is no padding anywhere. [Judo](/sports/judo/) has mats,
[boxing](/sports/boxing/) has gloves, [taekwondo](/sports/taekwondo/) has
body protectors and [fencing](/sports/fencing/) has masks. Sumo has added
none. Its wrestlers land on clay, on sand, or on the people sitting at
ringside.

The building around the ring has changed more than the ring. The roof over
the dohyō once stood on four pillars. They blocked the view, and in the 1950s
they were removed so that television cameras could see. A canopy hung from
above took their place. The sport accepted that change to its arena, and has
accepted almost none to what happens inside the ring.
