# Where sumo travels

Professional sumo exists only in Japan. Amateur sumo is wrestled much more
widely, and it goes where a heavy wrestling culture already exists.

Mongolia is the clearest case. Mongolian wrestlers have held the top of the
Japanese professional ranks for long periods. They come from their own
wrestling tradition, bökh, which carries over well. A path from Mongolia into
the Japanese stables has been in place for decades.

Hakuhō, of Mongolia, went on to win more top-division titles than anyone.

Amateur sumo is strong in Brazil, through its large Japanese-Brazilian
community. It is strong in Eastern Europe, especially Russia, Ukraine,
Georgia, Bulgaria and Estonia, which have their own belt-wrestling and
grappling traditions. In the United States it is strongest in Hawaii, which
has produced several professional champions. It is also wrestled in Egypt,
China and across the Pacific.

Women's sumo exists only in the amateur sport. It is strongest in Japan's
universities, in Eastern Europe and in Brazil. So Japanese women compete at
world championships in a sport whose professional form will not let them
stand in the ring.
