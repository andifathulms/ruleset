# Trouble outside the bout

## Women in the ring

Women may not enter the professional dohyō at all, not even to help. In one
incident, an official collapsed in the ring and women climbed in to give him
medical aid. They were asked over the public address system to leave.

The Japan Sumo Association says the dohyō is a sacred space. Critics call the
ban discrimination kept up under a religious description. [Amateur sumo has
held women's competition since 1997](#sumo-womens-competition), with its own
weight classes and world championships.

## Life in the stables

Professional wrestlers live in their stables, and discipline there has been
violent. Deaths and serious injuries from beatings inside stables forced
changes to the sport's internal discipline. Because of them, the
association's governance has been examined by Japanese authorities, not by a
sporting body.

## Arranged bouts

Investigations into fixed bouts led to a tournament being cancelled and
wrestlers being expelled. A bout has no score, so a thrown match is hard to
spot in the record. All that is written down is who won and how.
