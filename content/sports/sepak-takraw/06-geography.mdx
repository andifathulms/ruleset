# Where it is played

Sepak takraw is a mainstream sport across Southeast Asia and a rare one
everywhere else.

## Thailand first

Thailand has been the strongest nation by a wide margin since the sport was
codified. It has the deepest competition and the closest thing to a
professional level, and it has won the great majority of Asian Games titles
and world championships. Malaysia, the other founding country, is its main
rival. A sport founded as a compromise between two countries has mostly been
won by one of them.

<Photo id="sepak-takraw-kings-cup" />

Myanmar, Indonesia, Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, the Philippines and Singapore all
play it as a mainstream sport, and it is a fixture of the Southeast Asian
Games. South Korea and Japan compete seriously at the Asian Games. India and
Iran have programmes.

## Outside Asia

Europe, North America and Africa have small federations, often started by
people who moved there from Southeast Asia. Germany, the United States and
Canada have real but shallow footholds.

<Photos ids="sepak-takraw-germany sepak-takraw-chermside" />

Money is not what keeps the sport small there. The ball is cheap, the court is
a badminton court, and there is no protective equipment to buy. The barrier is
skill. A rally requires a player to strike a ball above head height with the
foot, and that is very hard to learn from nothing as an adult.

<Photo id="sepak-takraw-acrobatic-smash" />

Where the sport is strong, children learn that skill in the
<Term id="circle-game">circle game</Term>, long before they play over a net.
The international federation works to introduce the sport on other continents,
and this is the wall it meets.
