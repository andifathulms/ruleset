# Where it is played

Southeast Asia, comprehensively, and a thin scattering everywhere else.

**Thailand** is the dominant power by a wide margin and has been since the
sport was codified, with the deepest competitive structure and the closest
thing to a professional layer. **Malaysia** is the historical co-founder and
main rival. **Myanmar**, **Indonesia**, **Vietnam**, **Laos**, **Cambodia**,
the **Philippines** and **Singapore** all play it as a mainstream sport, and it
is a fixture of the Southeast Asian Games.

Beyond the region, **South Korea** and **Japan** compete seriously at Asian
Games level, and **India** and **Iran** have programmes. There are small
federations in Europe, North America and Africa, often started by Southeast
Asian diaspora communities, and the sport has genuine but shallow footholds in
Germany, the United States and Canada.

The concentration has an unusual cause. Sepak takraw does not require money —
the ball is cheap, the court is a badminton court, and no protective equipment
exists. What it requires is having played the circle form as a child, because
the technical demand of controlling a ball with a foot above head height is
almost impossible to acquire from scratch as an adult.

So the barrier is exposure rather than cost, which is the reverse of most
sports on this site. Equestrian and sailing are confined by expense; sepak
takraw is confined by childhood.
