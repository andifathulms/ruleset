# Controversies that forced rule changes

Sepak takraw's legislative history is short, and most of it happened at once in
1965.

**Whose game it was.** The founding controversy, resolved by treaty rather than
by rules: two national traditions with different names and different laws
became one sport, and the name was constructed so that neither won. Everything
about the modern game descends from that settlement.

**Rattan to synthetic.** The ball changed material and the sport had to decide
what a legal ball was. Unusually for this site, the substitution was not an
arms race — a synthetic ball reproduced rattan's behaviour rather than beating
it, and the regulation is about consistency rather than about capping a
performance gain. Compare the javelin, the swimsuit and the composite bat,
where the equipment got better and the governing body had to intervene.

**Scoring.** The sport moved from service-based scoring to 21-point rally
scoring, as most net sports did in the same era. This site does not enter a
date for the change, because none that can be cited has been checked, and an
approximate year would be a confident wrong one — which is also why the sport's
scoring series is left empty.

**The service circle.** How much of the tekong's grounded foot must stay in the
circle, and when the serve is deemed struck, are the sport's recurring
technical disputes, adjusted more than once.

What sepak takraw has not had is a safety crisis or a doping scandal of any
scale. For a sport contested at continental level for thirty-five years, that
is a notably clean record.
