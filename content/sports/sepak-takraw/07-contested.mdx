# What is still argued about

**Can it leave its region?** Sepak takraw is cheap to play, spectacular to
watch and well organised, and almost nobody plays it seriously outside
Southeast Asia. The federation's development programmes meet a barrier that
money does not remove: the game is very hard to learn as an adult.

**No place at a global games.** The sport is on the programme of the Asian
Games and the Southeast Asian Games. It has never been on the Olympic
programme, and it is not on the World Games programme. Closing that gap is the
federation's main goal.

**One winner.** Thailand has won most of what there is to win for thirty-five
years. [Floorball](/sports/floorball/) has the same problem with Sweden and
Finland, and [kabaddi](/sports/kabaddi/) with India.

**Which form comes first?** <Term id="regu">Regu</Term>, team regu, doubles
and quadrant, a four-a-side form, have all been played at championships. It is
not settled which is the sport's main event. That matters for any bid to join
a multi-sport games, because a programme counts medal events.

**The old forms.** Hoop takraw and the circle games are where the sport came
from, and people still play them. They are not contests in the modern sense.
Burmese chinlone is judged on grace and has no winner. How much a competitive
federation should spend on games that are not competitions is an open
question.
