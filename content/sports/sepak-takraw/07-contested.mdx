# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether it can leave its region.** Sepak takraw is cheap, spectacular and
organisationally mature, and it is played seriously almost nowhere outside
Southeast Asia. The federation's development programmes run into a technical
barrier that money does not solve: the sport is very hard to learn as an adult.

**No global multi-sport place.** It is on the Asian Games and Southeast Asian
Games programmes and on neither the Olympic nor the World Games one. For a
sport of its age and structure that is a conspicuous gap, and closing it is the
federation's principal institutional goal.

**Thai dominance.** One of the two founding countries has won most of what
there is to win for thirty-five years. It is the same problem floorball has
with Sweden and Finland, and kabaddi with India, and none of the three has
solved it.

**Which formats count.** Regu, team regu, doubles and quadrant have all been
contested at various championships, and which of them is the sport's flagship
is not settled — a question that matters directly for any multi-sport bid,
because programmes count medal events.

**The traditional forms.** Hoop takraw and the cooperative circle games are the
sport's living root and are not contests in the modern sense; the Burmese
chinlone is explicitly judged on grace rather than won. How much a competitive
federation should invest in forms that decline to be competitions is a real
question, and one almost nothing else on this site has to ask.
