# Equipment evolution

One ball, and the reason it has holes in it.

The traditional ball was woven from strips of rattan into an open lattice — a
hollow sphere of interlaced cane about 42 centimetres around and very light.
That construction is not decorative. An open woven ball is light enough to be
struck hard with a foot without injuring the foot, deforms slightly on impact
so it can be controlled by a surface with no grip, and is slowed by air enough
to be playable in a court only 13 metres long.

Modern competition uses synthetic balls, moulded or woven from plastic, which
keep the open lattice and standardise what rattan could not: weight,
consistency and durability in humidity. It is one of the cleaner
material-substitution stories on this site, because the synthetic ball
reproduced the traditional one's behaviour rather than improving on it — there
was no performance to be gained, only consistency.

Nothing else is required. No protective equipment, no specialised footwear
beyond a light court shoe, and no implement. Players wear ordinary kit.

The court is borrowed equipment: 13.4 by 6.1 metres is a badminton doubles
court, and sepak takraw is very often played on marked badminton courts in
sports halls across its region. That overlap is a substantial part of why the
sport is cheap to run where it is popular, and it is a rare case of two sports
on this site sharing a playing surface exactly.
