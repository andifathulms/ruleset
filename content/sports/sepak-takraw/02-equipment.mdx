# A ball full of holes

Sepak takraw needs one ball, and the ball is woven with gaps in it for a
reason.

The traditional ball was made from strips of rattan, woven into a hollow, open
sphere about 42 centimetres around. It is very light, so a player can kick it
hard without hurting the foot. It gives a little when it is struck, so a foot
can control it. The air slows it enough for a game on a court only 13 metres
long.

<Photos ids="sepak-takraw-rattan-balls sepak-takraw-ball-maker" />

Competition now uses synthetic balls, moulded or woven from plastic in the
same open pattern. Plastic gave the sport what rattan could not: the same
weight every time, and a ball that lasts in humid air. The synthetic ball
copies the way the rattan ball behaves. It was not made to fly faster or
further, so the rule about the ball only has to keep it consistent.

<Photo id="sepak-takraw-synthetic-ball" />

Nothing else is needed. Players wear ordinary kit and light court shoes, and
no protective equipment.

Even the court is borrowed. At 13.4 by 6.1 metres it is a
[badminton](/sports/badminton/) doubles court, and the game is very often
played on badminton courts in sports halls across the region. That is a large
part of why the sport is cheap to run where it is popular.
