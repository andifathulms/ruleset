# Feet, lines and the net

The ball moves fast on a small court, and the referee judges everything by
eye. Most competition has no video review and no line technology.

**The hand.** The basic fault is touching the ball with a hand or an arm. On a
13-metre court, at the speed the ball travels, telling a touch on the chest
from a touch on the upper arm is a real judgement.

**The service circle.** The <Term id="tekong">tekong</Term> must keep one foot
on the ground inside the <Term id="service-circle">service circle</Term> until
the ball is struck. The kick lifts the whole body, so the standing foot tends
to move. This is the fault called most often. How much of the foot must stay
in the circle, and the exact moment the serve counts as struck, have been
argued over and adjusted more than once.

<Photo id="sepak-takraw-tekong-serve" />

**The quarter circles.** The tekong's two team-mates must stay inside their
<Term id="quarter-circle">quarter circles</Term> until the serve is struck.
The referee has to judge this at the same instant as the tekong's foot.

**The net.** Touching the net is a fault. A blocker may follow through over it
and may not touch the ball on the opponent's side. A
<Term id="block">block</Term> is often made by a player who is in the air and
upside down, so it is hard to see where the touch happened.

[Badminton](/sports/badminton/), whose court the sport borrows, has had
ball-tracking review for years. Sepak takraw has not.
