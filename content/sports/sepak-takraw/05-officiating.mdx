# Officiating

Fast, small court, no hands — and the calls are mostly about feet and lines.

**The hand.** The fundamental infringement is contact with a hand or arm, and
at the speed the ball travels in a 13-metre court, distinguishing a chest
contact from an upper-arm one is a genuine judgement. It is the sport's
equivalent of fistball's fist-or-open-hand call.

**The service circle.** The tekong's grounded foot must stay in contact with
the circle until the ball is struck. Because the serve involves a team-mate's
toss and a kick that lifts the body, the grounded foot is under pressure to
move, and this is the most frequently called fault in the sport.

**Quarter circles.** The two non-serving players must stay inside their marked
areas until the serve is struck, which is a positional call made at the same
instant as the service-circle one.

**The net.** Touching it is a fault. A blocker may follow through over the net
in the act of blocking but may not make contact on the opponent's side — and
since blocks here are frequently made by an airborne, inverted player, judging
where the contact happened is genuinely difficult.

Officiating is by eye throughout. There is no video review in most competition,
no line technology and no instrumentation of any kind — which puts sepak
takraw alongside most of the regionally concentrated sports on this site, and
in contrast with badminton, whose court it borrows and which has had
ball-tracking review for years.
