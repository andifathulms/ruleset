# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether automation improved the sport.** The sensors ended the judging
crisis, and almost nobody wants to go back. But they also produced a decade of
rule changes chasing the optimisation they created — re-pricing the point
table, shrinking the court, and finally abandoning cumulative scoring in 2022.
Whether a sport that must keep redesigning itself around its own instrument is
better governed or worse is genuinely argued.

**The force threshold.** A number set by the federation determines how hard a
kick must be to count, and it is set differently by weight category. It is a
consequential rule that receives almost none of the scrutiny a playing rule
would, and changing it changes who wins.

**Best of three.** As in archery, a competitor can score more across a contest
and lose it. Supporters say it keeps every round live; opponents say a combat
sport should be won by the person who did more.

**The gap between the sport and the martial art.** Millions of people practise
taekwondo as a graded syllabus of forms and technique. Olympic sparring has
diverged far enough from that to be a different activity, and there is a real
argument within the sport that the Olympic version has stopped representing
what the discipline is.

**Head kicks and safety.** Raising the value of head contact was a scoring
decision, and its consequence is more head contact. The sport has sensor-
equipped head protectors and has not published a settled position on the
long-term risk.
