# Beijing, and everything the sensors started

## Beijing, 2008

At the 2008 Games, a point was scored when enough of four judges pressed a
button within a fraction of a second of each other. Competitors could not
verify the results and spectators could not follow them. There was no way to
check a decision afterwards. The judging was widely disputed, and Olympic
programme reviews named taekwondo as a sport that might be removed.

The federation answered within a year. [It installed
sensors](#wt-2009-electronic-scoring), so that its results could be confirmed
by something other than its own officials. Arguments about whether a kick had
touched the body almost stopped.

[Gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) had a similar crisis in 2004. It kept its
judges, split the panel in two and began to monitor the judges statistically.
Taekwondo took the people out of the part that could be measured.

## The cheap kick

The sensors brought a new problem at once. If a point exists when a sensor
feels enough force, the sensible plan is the cheapest contact that reaches
that force. That turned out to be a low, repeated kick to the trunk. Within a
season the sport was dull to watch.

[The federation raised the price of the difficult
techniques](#wt-2010-head-sensors-and-values). A kick to the head became
worth much more than a kick to the body, and a

## Running from a lead

A competitor who is ahead has no reason to fight. On the square mat, leaders
used the corners and the space to stay away from the opponent.
[The octagon](#wt-2011-octagonal-court) took away the corners and made the
area smaller.

That reduced the problem and did not end it. Under a running total, a lead
built early could still be defended to the end. [Since 2022 a contest is the
best of three rounds](#wt-2022-best-of-three), and each
<Term id="round">round</Term> starts again from nothing. A competitor can now
score more points than the opponent across the whole contest and still lose.
[Archery](/sports/archery/) made the same change in 2011 and hears the same
objection.
