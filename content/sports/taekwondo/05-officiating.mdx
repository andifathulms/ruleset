# Who makes the calls

Most of the points in a taekwondo contest are awarded by sensors. The
officials keep the decisions a machine cannot make.

Until 2009 a point existed because judges agreed that a kick had landed.
Since then it exists because a sensor felt more than a set force.
[Tennis](/sports/tennis/) also handed a decision to a machine, but a simpler
one: where a ball landed. Taekwondo handed over the question of how hard a
person was hit.

## The referee and the judges

The referee still runs the contest on the mat and awards
<Term id="gam-jeom">gam-jeom</Term>, the penalty that gives a point to the
opponent. Judges remain for the techniques the system does not register,
mainly spinning techniques and disputed contacts.

<Photo id="taekwondo-rio-referee" />

Penalties can decide a contest, so the arguments moved to them. [A coach's
video review was made formal in 2017](#wt-2017-instant-video-replay). A coach
has a limited number of unsuccessful requests.
<Term id="video-review">Video review</Term> covers what the sensors do not:
the referee's penalty calls, and techniques the system missed.

## The dial

The sensors score a kick above a set force. The federation chooses that force,
and it chooses a different one for each
<Term id="weight-category">weight category</Term>.

<Photo id="taekwondo-sorn-espinoza-2012" />

So there is a dial that decides how hard a kick must be to count, and turning
it changes results. No official makes that decision during a contest. It is
made in advance, in a written specification.
