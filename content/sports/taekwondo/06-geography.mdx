# Where it is played

Taekwondo has the widest national spread of any combat sport on this site and
one of the most even elite distributions of any sport here at all. Medals at
recent Games have gone to competitors from more than twenty countries,
including several with almost no other Olympic success.

The reason is the syllabus. Taekwondo was designed to be taught, exported
deliberately from South Korea from the 1960s onwards, and organised as a graded
curriculum that works in a rented hall with an instructor and no equipment. It
is one of the most widely practised martial arts in the world, and its club
structure exists almost everywhere.

South Korea remains the strongest nation but by a much smaller margin than in
comparable cases: table tennis has China, archery has South Korea, and both
margins are far larger. Iran, Turkey, China, Spain, Great Britain, Croatia,
Serbia, Ivory Coast, Niger, Jordan, Thailand and Uzbekistan have all produced
Olympic champions.

Two of those are worth stating plainly. Ivory Coast's first Olympic gold medal
in any sport came in taekwondo, as did Jordan's and Niger's first medals. A
sport with a low facility requirement and a portable curriculum produces
first-time medallists in a way that pool sports and equestrian sports cannot.

The electronic scoring system had a geographic effect too, and a positive one:
a sport whose results depended on judges' agreement was open to the suspicion
that the judges favoured established nations. A sensor is not.
