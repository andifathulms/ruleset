# Where it is played

Taekwondo medals are spread across more countries than those of most Olympic
sports. At recent Games they have gone to competitors from more than twenty
countries, and several of those countries have had almost no other Olympic
success.

<Photo id="taekwondo-rio-68kg-flags" />

## A sport built to travel

The reason is the syllabus. Taekwondo was designed to be taught, and South
Korea exported it on purpose from the 1960s onwards. A class needs a rented
hall and an instructor and no equipment. It is one of the most widely
practised martial arts in the world, and there are clubs almost everywhere.

<Photos ids="taekwondo-park-2024 taekwondo-iran-fans-2016" />

South Korea is still the strongest nation, but its lead is much smaller than
China's in [table tennis](/sports/table-tennis/) or South Korea's own in
[archery](/sports/archery/). Iran, Turkey, China, Spain, Great Britain,
Croatia, Serbia, Ivory Coast, Jordan, Thailand and Uzbekistan have all
produced Olympic champions.

## First medals

Rio de Janeiro, 2016. Ahmad Abughaush won the men's 68 kg, and Jordan had its
first Olympic medal in any sport. Ivory Coast's first Olympic gold medal also
came in taekwondo. In the men's +80 kg at the same Games, Abdoul Razak
Issoufou took silver for Niger.

<Photos ids="taekwondo-abughaush-2016 taekwondo-rio-heavyweight-podium" />

A sport that needs little space and has a course that can be carried anywhere
produces first-time medallists. Sports that need a pool or a horse do so far
less often.

<Photo id="taekwondo-rashitov-2024" />

Electronic scoring helped here too. While results depended on judges
agreeing, there was always a suspicion that judges favoured the established
nations. A sensor gives no ground for that suspicion.

## Between the two Koreas

Taekwondo has been used more than once as a tool of diplomacy between North
and South Korea. Joint demonstration teams have performed at major events.
[Table tennis](/sports/table-tennis/) is the other sport with a history of
this kind.
