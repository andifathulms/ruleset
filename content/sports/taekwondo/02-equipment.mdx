# The padding that keeps the score

A taekwondo competitor wears a <Term id="trunk-protector">trunk
protector</Term>, a <Term id="head-protector">head protector</Term> and a pair
of socks, and all three are part of the scoring system.

It was not always so. The trunk protector used to be padding and nothing
more. Four judges held buttons, and a point existed when enough of them
pressed within a fraction of a second of each other.

<Photo id="taekwondo-protectors" />

## Sensors

[In 2009 sensors went into the trunk protector](#wt-2009-electronic-scoring).
Magnetic sensors went into the socks, so that the system can tell a foot from
a knee or a glove. Sensors in the head protector came later. A kick that
lands on the right target, above a set force, now scores automatically.

<Photos ids="taekwondo-sensor-socks taekwondo-head-protector" />

This made the equipment specification into a scoring rule. Change the
<Term id="force-threshold">force threshold</Term> and a different kick wins.

<Photo id="taekwondo-london-2012-court" />

## What the fighters did next

Competitors soon found the cheapest reliable way to trigger a sensor. It was a
low kick to the trunk, repeated, and the sport became monotonous to watch.

The federation left the hardware alone and changed the prices. [Head kicks
and spinning techniques were made worth more](#wt-2010-head-sensors-and-values).
[Gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) uses its Code of Points in the same way, to
pay for the movements it wants to see.

## The mat

The contest area changed for a similar reason. A competitor with a lead could
back into a corner of the square mat and stay out of reach.
[The square was replaced by a smaller octagon](#wt-2011-octagonal-court),
which has no corners and less room to retreat.
