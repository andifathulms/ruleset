# Equipment evolution

Taekwondo has the shortest and most consequential equipment history on this
site, because in 2009 its protective equipment stopped being protective
equipment and became a measuring instrument.

Before that, the trunk protector was padding. Scoring was done by four judges
with handheld buttons, and a point existed when enough of them agreed within a
fraction of a second. The equipment had nothing to do with it.

The **Protector and Scoring System** put sensors in the trunk protector,
magnetic sensors in the athlete's socks so the system can distinguish a foot
from a knee or a glove, and later sensors in the head protector. A valid
contact above a force threshold registers automatically.

This is a larger change than it sounds, and its consequences fill the rest of
this page. It moved the sport from judged to measured — the only such crossing
on this site. It made the equipment specification into a scoring rule, so
changing a threshold changes what wins. And it immediately produced an
optimisation problem: competitors found the cheapest reliable way to trigger a
sensor, which was a low kick to the trunk, and the sport became repetitive.

The federation's response was not to change the hardware but to change the
prices — head kicks and spinning techniques worth more — which is the same
instrument gymnastics uses.

The octagonal court in 2011 is the other equipment change, and it was aimed at
the same problem from a different direction: remove the corners, shrink the
area, make disengagement harder.
