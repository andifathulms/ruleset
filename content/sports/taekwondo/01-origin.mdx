# Origin and invention

Taekwondo is the newest sport on this site by a wide margin and one of the few
whose creation was an act of state.

It was assembled in South Korea in the 1950s from Korean martial traditions and
from Japanese karate, which had been taught in Korea during the occupation. The
name was settled in 1955, and the unification of competing schools into a
single system was pursued deliberately with government support — a national
martial art was an instrument of cultural identity after occupation and war.

The World Taekwondo Federation was founded in 1973 with headquarters in Seoul,
and the Kukkiwon serves as the technical and grading authority. Taekwondo
became a demonstration sport at the 1988 Games in Seoul and a full medal sport
in 2000.

That origin explains a structural feature the rest of this page depends on.
Taekwondo was designed as a *syllabus* — a graded curriculum with forms,
techniques and belts, intended to be taught to large numbers of people —
before it was designed as a spectator sport. The competitive sparring
discipline is one component of it, and the elite version of that component has
since diverged very far from what most practitioners do.

No other sport here has that shape. Judo has a syllabus too, but its
competition remains recognisably the same activity. Taekwondo's does not.
