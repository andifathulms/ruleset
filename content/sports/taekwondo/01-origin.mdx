# Where it came from

Taekwondo was put together in South Korea in the 1950s, and the state helped
to build it. Its sources were Korean martial traditions and Japanese karate,
which had been taught in Korea during the Japanese occupation. The name was
settled in 1955.

Several schools taught competing versions. They were unified into one system
on purpose, with government support. After occupation and war, a national
martial art was a way to show a national identity.

## A syllabus first

Taekwondo was designed to be taught to large numbers of people. It is a graded
course of forms, techniques and belts, and a student moves up it one grading
at a time. Much of what a student learns is not fighting at all:
<Term id="poomsae">poomsae</Term>, self-defence and board breaking fill most
of the syllabus.

<Photos ids="taekwondo-poomsae-2018 taekwondo-board-breaking" />

<Term id="kyorugi">Kyorugi</Term>, the sparring discipline, is one part of
that course. It is the part that became a spectator sport, and at the top
level it has moved a long way from what most students practise.
[Judo](/sports/judo/) has a syllabus too, but a judo contest is still
recognisably the same activity as a judo class.

## Two institutions

The World Taekwondo Federation was founded in 1973, with its headquarters in
Seoul. It is now called World Taekwondo, and it governs the sport. The
Kukkiwon, also in Seoul, is a separate body. It is the technical and grading
authority for the martial art worldwide. So the rules of the competition and
the definition of the practice are kept in two different places.

Taekwondo was a demonstration sport at the 1988 Games in Seoul. It became a
full medal sport in 2000.
