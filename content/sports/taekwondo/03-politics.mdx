# Governing body politics

Taekwondo's governance is dominated by two facts: its Olympic place was under
genuine threat, and it has an unusually close relationship with one country.

The threat was real and recent. Judging at the 2008 Games was widely disputed,
and the sport was named in Olympic programme reviews as a candidate for
removal. The electronic scoring system introduced the following year is best
understood as an answer to that: a federation demonstrating that its results
could be verified by something other than its own officials.

It is a strikingly effective example of a governing body solving a credibility
problem with hardware rather than with process. Gymnastics faced a comparable
crisis in 2004 and answered it by splitting its judging panel and monitoring
judges statistically — keeping the humans and auditing them. Taekwondo removed
the humans from the part that could be measured.

The other permanent feature is Korea. The Kukkiwon in Seoul is the technical
and grading authority for the martial art worldwide, separate from World
Taekwondo, which governs the sport. That is a split with no exact parallel
here — judo's Kodokan is the closest — and it means the definition of the
practice and the rules of the competition sit in different institutions.

A political footnote worth recording: taekwondo has been used repeatedly as a
diplomatic instrument between North and South Korea, with joint demonstration
teams appearing at major events, which puts it alongside table tennis as a
sport that has done foreign policy.
