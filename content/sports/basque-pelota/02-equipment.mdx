# Equipment evolution

Every implement in this sport exists to do something to the ball that the hand
could not, and the hand game outlived all of them in popularity.

**The hand** came first and is still the most followed specialty. There is no
glove and no padding — only tape, and adhesive pads that players build up on
their own striking surfaces over years. The ball is hard: a wound or rubber
core under a stitched leather cover, and unforgiving. Hands swell, bruise to
the bone, lose sensation and deform permanently, and hand condition rather than
fitness is what determines whether a professional can play.

**Bats** followed. The **pala** is a heavy solid wooden bat and the **paleta** a
lighter one, each paired with a ball chosen to suit it — leather or rubber —
which is why the bat specialties are separate events rather than the same game
with different sticks.

**The share** is a stringed racket used only in the enclosed trinquete.

**The cesta** is the extreme. A long curved wicker basket strapped to the
forearm, it catches the ball and slings it in one continuous motion, and it is
the reason cesta punta produces speeds around three hundred kilometres an hour
— the fastest ball recorded in any sport. Baskets are made by hand from woven
chestnut and reed, fitted to the individual player, and there are very few
people left who make them.

Helmets are compulsory in the basket specialties and were not always. In the
hand game there is nothing to make compulsory, which is the point.
