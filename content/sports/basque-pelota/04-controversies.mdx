# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The hand.** Pelota's central and unresolved problem. Professional hand
players sustain permanent damage to the hands they compete with, and the sport
has no equipment answer available — putting a glove on it would make it a
different specialty, which the federation already contests separately. What
exists instead is management: taping, pads, restricted playing schedules and
careers that end when the hands do.

**Speed and safety in the basket specialties.** A ball at three hundred
kilometres an hour in an enclosed court is a serious hazard to players and,
historically, to spectators. Helmets became compulsory, protective netting
separates the crowd, and the court's geometry is specified precisely.

**Betting.** Live in-play wagering called aloud during matches is integral to
the professional hand game and creates the conditions for manipulation that
every sport in that position faces. It is regulated as gambling rather than as
sport.

**Losing America.** The 1988 strike and the expansion of legal gambling in
Florida ended jai alai as a substantial industry. It changed no rule of pelota
and removed a professional market that had employed hundreds of players.

**Whether the specialties should be consolidated.** A federation contesting a
dozen combinations of court and implement spreads a small sport very thin, and
the case for choosing a flagship recurs. Nobody has been willing to tell a
specialty it is finished.

**Frontball.** A format invented for export, which purists regard as not the
sport and which is the only version with a plausible route into countries that
have never built a fronton.
