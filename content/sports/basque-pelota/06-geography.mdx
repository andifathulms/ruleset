# Where it is played

Pelota is played in the western Pyrenees and in the places where Basque and
Spanish emigrants settled.

The heartland is the Basque Country, on both sides of the border between Spain
and France, together with Navarre. Almost every village has a
<Term id="fronton">frontón</Term>. It belongs to the public, costs nothing to
use, and is in use all the time.

The rest of Spain and France plays too. The south-west of France has its own
strong tradition in the <Term id="trinquete">trinquete</Term> and in the
long-wall games.

## Across the Atlantic

Latin America is the second home. Mexico, Argentina, Cuba, Uruguay, Chile and
Venezuela all have organised pelota. Mexico has a deep tradition of
<Term id="cesta-punta">cesta punta</Term>, and many of the professionals who
filled the American frontons were Mexican.

## Where betting carried it

In the United States, jai alai was a large industry in Florida from the 1930s,
kept alive by betting. It has almost disappeared. The Philippines had a
jai alai scene through the twentieth century on the same betting model, and it
ended in a similar way.

<Photo id="pelota-manila-1955" />

Other games have travelled with people in the same way.
[Fistball](/sports/fistball/) followed German emigrants, and
[soft tennis](/sports/soft-tennis/) followed Japanese rule. Pelota followed
the Basques and the Spanish. The American industry is the exception: it
existed because of a gambling law.
