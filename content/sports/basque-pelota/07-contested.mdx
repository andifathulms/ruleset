# What is still argued about

**The hands.** Professional hand players damage their hands for life as a
condition of playing. Any equipment that would protect them would turn
<Term id="mano">mano</Term> into another game. Careful management is the only
answer anyone has, and it does not solve the problem.

**Too many games.** The federation runs about a dozen combinations of court
and implement, each with its own event, in a sport with a small international
base. The case for choosing one main event keeps returning. Nobody wants to be
the one who ends a tradition.

**Frontball.** <Term id="frontball">Frontball</Term> was designed for export.
Purists say it is not really pelota. It is also the only form with a realistic
way into countries that will never build a frontón.

**Who runs the professional game.** Private companies contract the players and
schedule the matches. The federation runs world championships in specialties
with far smaller audiences. Neither side expects that to change.

**Can jai alai come back?** Nearly all the American frontons have closed. A
few carry on in a reduced form, tied to a gambling licence that requires
matches to be staged. A game that is played only because a licence demands it
is in a weak position.
