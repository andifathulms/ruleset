# The judge on the court

A pelota judge stands on the court itself, with line judges to help, and
watches a ball that in the basket game moves at about three hundred kilometres
an hour. Most competition has no video review and no line technology.

Most calls are about where the ball went. Did it reach the front wall? Did it
land inside the lines? Did it bounce twice before it was returned?

Interference is the other regular call. Two or four players share one floor,
with no net and no lanes. A player going to the ball has to pass through space
an opponent wants. The judge decides whether the obstruction could have been
avoided, much as a referee does with a hinder in
[racquetball](/sports/racquetball/).

The basket adds a call found nowhere else. The ball must be caught and slung
in one continuous motion. Holding it in the <Term id="cesta">cesta</Term>,
even briefly, is a fault. The judge must decide how long the ball stayed in a
tool built to keep it there as long as possible.

In <Term id="mano">mano</Term> the matching question is whether the ball was
struck or scooped.
