# Governing body politics

The federation governs the world championships; private companies govern the
sport people watch.

The Federación Internacional de Pelota Vasca, formed in 1929, contests each
specialty separately at world championships, promotes the sport
internationally, and has pushed **frontball** — a short three-wall format — as
a way of exporting the game to countries with no court tradition, which is
almost all of them.

Professional **hand pelota** in the Basque Country is a different structure
entirely. Private **promotion companies** contract the players, arrange the
pairings, schedule the matches and sell them to television. The federation does
not run it. This site has recorded the same shape in sumo, muaythai and
snooker: an internationally recognised body running a real competition that is
not where the sport's audience, money or best competitors are.

The betting is inseparable from that. In a fronton, **corredores** work the
crowd during play, calling odds aloud as the match turns and settling wagers by
throwing slit balls containing tickets into the stands. It is legal, public and
constitutive of the atmosphere, and it produces the integrity exposure that
every sport with live in-play betting has.

The American chapter is the sharpest political story pelota has. Florida
legalised parimutuel wagering on jai alai in 1935 and an industry was built —
frontons, imported professionals, daily cards designed around betting formats.
When the players struck in 1988 and the state expanded other gambling, the
proposition disappeared, and American jai alai has contracted to almost
nothing.

A sport imported as a gambling product died when the gambling moved on.
