# Governing body politics

A federation founded in 1946 for one activity, which acquired a second from
outside and put that one forward when the opportunity came.

The World Water Ski Union governed a stable, well-organised sport with three
disciplines and a strong presence in the United States, Europe and Australia
for four decades. Water skiing was a demonstration sport at Munich in 1972,
which was the closest it came to a programme.

**Wakeboarding** grew up in the 1980s outside all of that, from surf and skate
culture, with its own competitions and its own aesthetic. The federation
absorbed it and eventually put it in its name.

When the 2020 Olympic host process opened, wakeboarding was the discipline the
federation advanced — young, board-based, visually strong — and it was
shortlisted. It was not selected. The sports chosen for that cycle included
skateboarding and surfing, which is to say: board sports were exactly what the
organisers wanted, and they took two others.

This site has now recorded three federations that absorbed a board or street
discipline and tried to convert it into a programme place. The roller sports
body took skateboarding and succeeded. A ballroom federation took breaking and
succeeded, briefly. This one took wakeboarding and did not.

The federation's other structural issue is cost. Boat towing needs a boat, a
driver, fuel and a lake per competitor, and the sport's participation is
constrained accordingly. Cable parks are the answer it has, and they are
growing faster than the boat disciplines.
