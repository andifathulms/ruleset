# A teenager on Lake Pepin

Water skiing began with one idea: if a person can ski on snow, a person can
ski on water. Ralph Samuelson, a teenager in Minnesota, had it, and in 1922 he
tested it on Lake Pepin.

He tried barrel staves first, and then snow skis. Neither worked. He made
his own boards, and he found the trick was to lean back with the tips up. He
is generally credited with the first successful ride.

The sport spread through the 1920s and 1930s as motorboats became common.
Contests were held under local rules.

<Photo id="waterski-wakeboard-cypress-gardens-jump" />

In 1946 the World Water Ski Union was founded, and it [wrote one code for
everyone](#waterski-1946-federation). The code set out three disciplines:
<Term id="slalom">slalom</Term>, <Term id="tricks">tricks</Term> and
<Term id="jump">jump</Term>, with an <Term id="overall">overall</Term> title
across them. A tournament today holds the same three contests that a
tournament held in 1950.

## Without a boat

In 1961 [a new way to be towed](#waterski-1961-cable) was invented in Germany.
An overhead <Term id="cable">cable</Term> pulled riders round a lake in a
circuit. There was no boat, no driver and no fuel, so most of the cost went
too. There was also no <Term id="wake">wake</Term>. Cable riders learned to
use obstacles and rails, and cable riding became a discipline of its own with
its own world championships.

## A board from another culture

Wakeboarding arrived in the 1980s. It came from surf and skate culture, and it
had its own contests and its own look. The federation that had been founded
for water skiing took it in, and later put it in its name.
