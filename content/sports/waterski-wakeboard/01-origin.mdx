# Origin and invention

Water skiing was invented by a teenager who reasoned that if you can ski on
snow you can ski on water, and was right.

Ralph Samuelson is generally credited with the first successful attempt, on
Lake Pepin in Minnesota in 1922, after experimenting with barrel staves and
snow skis before settling on boards he made himself and a technique of leaning
back with the tips up. The sport spread through the 1920s and 1930s as
powerboats became common.

The World Water Ski Union was founded in 1946 and codified the three
disciplines that still define tournament skiing: **slalom**, **tricks** and
**jump**, with a combined overall title across them. That structure has been
remarkably stable — a tournament in 2026 contests what a tournament in 1950
contested.

What changed around it is more interesting than what changed inside it.

**The cable system** arrived in Germany in 1961: an overhead circuit towing
riders around a lake with no boat, no driver and no fuel. It removed the
sport's cost structure and, with it, the wake — so cable riding developed
around obstacles and rails instead, and became its own competitive discipline.

**Wakeboarding** arrived in the 1980s from surf and skate culture, was absorbed
into a federation founded for water skiing, and became the discipline the sport
put forward for Olympic inclusion. Water skiing itself was a demonstration
sport at Munich in 1972; wakeboarding was shortlisted for 2020 and not
selected.

Neither has been on an Olympic programme.
