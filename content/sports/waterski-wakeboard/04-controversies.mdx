# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The driver.** Water skiing's permanent structural problem. A competitor's
score depends on a boat held to a precise line and a precise speed by somebody
else, and a boat outside tolerance voids the pass. Electronic speed control —
which holds the boat to the specified speed regardless of load — was adopted to
reduce the driver's influence, and it did not remove it, because the line is
still steered by a person.

**The boat itself.** Hulls and wakes improved steadily and made passes easier
across decades, with no change to the scoring system. The sport has never
addressed what that does to comparisons over time, which puts it in the same
position as bowling: the scale stood still while the difficulty moved.

**Jump safety.** The highest-risk discipline in the federation, where elite
jumpers travel far enough that the landing is the problem. Ramp specification,
speed and equipment have all been adjusted, and the discipline remains the one
with the serious injury history.

**Absorbing wakeboarding.** A discipline from board culture brought into a
federation founded for tournament skiing, with a different aesthetic, a
different judging model and a different constituency. The absorption was
successful institutionally and the two halves of the federation have never
fully merged culturally.

**Cable versus boat.** Cable riding removed the wake and built a discipline
around obstacles instead. It is cheaper, more accessible, growing faster, and
it is not the sport the federation was founded for.
