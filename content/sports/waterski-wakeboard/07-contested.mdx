# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Cable or boat.** Cable riding is cheaper, more accessible, growing faster and
does not involve the object the federation was founded around. Which of them
represents the sport's future is a genuine strategic question, and the answer
that participation numbers give is not the one the tournament tradition wants.

**The driver's influence.** Electronic speed control reduced it and the line is
still steered by a person, and a pass can be voided by their error. No further
technical fix has been adopted.

**Comparing eras.** Boats and wakes improved steadily for eighty years, making
passes easier, with no change to the scoring system. The sport has never
reckoned with what that does to its own history — the same silence bowling
keeps about reactive resin.

**Olympic prospects.** A 1972 demonstration, a 2020 shortlist for wakeboarding
that did not convert, and two other board sports selected in the same cycle.
Whether there is a route left, and which discipline it would run through, is
open.

**Jump.** The federation's highest-risk discipline, with a serious injury
history and distances that keep increasing.

**Cost.** Tournament skiing requires a boat, a driver, fuel and a lake per
competitor, and its participation is bounded by that. It is one of very few
sports here where a rule change could not meaningfully lower the barrier —
which is precisely why the cable park mattered so much.
