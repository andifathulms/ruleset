# Skis, a rope and somebody else's boat

Three pieces of equipment decide a result, and the competitor owns only two of
them.

## Skis and boards

Each discipline has its own. A slalom ski is one ski with a deep fin, made to
hold its edge while the skier leans hard against the rope.
<Term id="trick-skis">Trick skis</Term> are short and have no fin, so they
spin freely. Jump skis are long. A <Term id="wakeboard">wakeboard</Term> is
one board with bindings and moulded fins, ridden sideways. None of them is
any use in another discipline.

## The rope

The rope is easy to overlook, and slalom depends on it. It does not stretch,
and it comes in standard lengths. A slalom score names the
<Term id="rope-length">rope length</Term>: "three at 10.75" means three buoys
on a rope 10.75 metres long. The buoys are 11.5 metres from the boat's path.
So at that length [the rope cannot reach the
buoy](#waterski-slalom-shortening), and the skier must swing wide and stretch
to get round it. The rope is the sport's measuring stick as well as its tow
line.

## The boat

The boat belongs to the tournament and is driven by somebody else. The rules
approve the hull and require electronic speed control. Hulls and wakes have
improved steadily for eighty years, and every improvement makes a slalom pass
a little easier. A slalom score from long ago and one from today were skied
behind different boats.

<Photo id="waterski-wakeboard-howley-2019" />

For a wakeboarder the boat matters even more. The
<Term id="wake">wake</Term> is the ramp. Ballast in the boat shapes the wake,
so every jump a rider makes starts from something a boat builder designed and
a driver set up.
