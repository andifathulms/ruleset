# Where it is played

Warm water, still water, and the money to put a boat on it.

**The United States** is where the sport was invented and remains its deepest
competitive nation, with Florida in particular functioning as a global training
centre — year-round warm water, purpose-built private lakes, and a
concentration of coaches and manufacturers that no other country matches.
**Canada**, **Australia** and **New Zealand** follow the same pattern.

In Europe, **France**, **Great Britain**, **Italy**, **Belgium**,
**Switzerland**, **Germany** and **Russia** all compete strongly, and Europe is
where **cable parks** are densest — Germany in particular has a large cable
culture, which follows directly from the system having been invented there in
1961.

**Latin America** has a growing presence, and cable parks have opened the sport
in the Middle East and Southeast Asia, where the boat model would have been
prohibitive.

The distinguishing feature of this sport's geography is the private lake. Elite
tournament skiing is substantially conducted on purpose-built water — narrow,
sheltered, engineered to minimise wash — often privately owned. That is a
facility profile shared with almost nothing else here, and it concentrates the
sport wherever land, water and disposable income coincide.

Cable parks are the correction. A cable circuit serves many riders at once at
session prices, and the map of cable wakeboarding looks far more like the map
of skateparks than like the map of tournament skiing.
