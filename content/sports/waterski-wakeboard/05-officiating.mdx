# Officiating

An official corps that judges a person who is not competing.

**The boat judge** is unique on this site. Their job is to verify that the
driver held the surveyed line and the specified speed within tolerance — timed
between gates — and to void a pass where the boat was outside it. So an
official is assessing the performance of a non-competitor whose error would
otherwise fall on an athlete.

Electronic speed control has reduced this to the line rather than the speed,
and the line still matters: a boat a little wide changes the geometry the skier
is skiing to.

**Slalom** is otherwise almost self-evident. Did the skier pass outside the
buoy and back through the wake? Buoy judges watch each one, and the run ends at
the first miss.

**Tricks** is scored against a published table of trick values, with judges
identifying each manoeuvre and confirming it was completed cleanly and had not
already been performed in that pass. It is closer to gymnastics' element
recognition than to a subjective mark.

**Jump** is measured, by survey instruments or electronic systems, to the point
of landing.

**Wakeboard** is the federation's only genuinely judged discipline, marked on
execution, difficulty, intensity and composition — the same structure as
skateboarding and snowboarding, and inherited from the same culture.

Equipment inspection covers skis, fins, rope length and boat approval, and the
rope is checked as a measuring instrument rather than as a tool.
