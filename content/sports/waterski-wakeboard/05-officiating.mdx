# Judging the driver

One official at a water ski tournament judges a person who is not competing.
The <Term id="boat-judge">boat judge</Term> checks that the
<Term id="driver">driver</Term> kept the boat on its surveyed line and at the
set speed. The boat is timed between gates, and small limits are allowed. If
the boat was outside them, the pass does not count and the skier gets a
<Term id="rerun">rerun</Term>. The rule protects the skier from a mistake made
by somebody else.

Electronic speed control was brought in to make the driver matter less. It
holds the boat at the set speed whatever the load on the rope. It does not
steer. A person still holds the line, and a boat that runs a little wide
changes the angle the skier has to ski.

## Four ways to get a score

**Slalom** needs little judgement. Did the skier go round the outside of the
<Term id="buoy">buoy</Term> and back across the wake? Judges watch each buoy,
and the run ends at the first miss.

**Tricks** is scored from a published table of values. The judges name each
trick, check that it was done cleanly, and check that it has not been done
already in that pass. Their work is close to the way
[gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) judges recognise elements.

**Jump** is measured to the place where the skier lands, with survey
instruments or electronic systems.

**Wakeboard** is the one discipline that is truly judged. A panel marks each
run on execution, difficulty, intensity and composition.
[Skateboarding](/sports/skateboarding/) is judged in the same manner.

<Photos ids="waterski-wakeboard-wakeboard-turn waterski-wakeboard-cable-podium-2014" />

Officials also inspect the equipment: skis, fins, the boat's approval and the
length of the rope.
