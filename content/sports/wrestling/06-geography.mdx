# Where it is wrestled

Wrestling needs a mat and a partner, and almost any country can afford that.
It is taught in schools across much of Asia, eastern Europe and North
America.

The United States, Russia, Iran, Japan, Turkey, Georgia, Azerbaijan, Cuba,
Bulgaria, Mongolia, India, Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan all produce wrestlers of
world level, and several of them treat wrestling as a national sport. Mijaín
López of Cuba won his fifth Olympic Greco-Roman title in a row at Paris in
2024.

In some countries the attachment is older than any sporting programme. Iran
is devoted to <Term id="freestyle">freestyle</Term>. Mongolia has its own
wrestling, bökh, the centre of the Naadam festival.

In India, kushti wrestlers train and compete in the mud pit of an akhara.

This spread explains the reaction when wrestling was voted off the Olympic
programme in 2013. Countries with few other routes to the Games lost one of
their best. The nations that lobbied to bring it back agree on very little
else: the United States, Russia and Iran issued a joint statement. The
committee had named universality as one of its reasons, and on the evidence
universality was wrestling's strongest point.

Women's wrestling has a narrower map. It is concentrated in Japan, the United
States and China, and more and more in India. Kaori Icho of Japan won four
Olympic titles in a row from 2004 to 2016. The women's sport grew fastest in
the years when the federation was expanding it under pressure from the IOC.
