# Where it is played

Wrestling has one of the widest and most genuinely global competitive maps on
this site, and it is the strongest single argument against the 2013 decision to
remove it.

The United States, Russia, Iran, Japan, Turkey, Georgia, Azerbaijan, Cuba,
Bulgaria, Mongolia, India, Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan all produce world-level
wrestlers, and several of them regard the sport as a national one. Iran's
attachment to freestyle wrestling and Mongolia's to its own wrestling tradition
are cultural facts rather than sporting programmes.

The reason is cost. Wrestling needs a mat and a partner, which is within reach
of almost any country, and it is embedded in school systems across much of
Asia, eastern Europe and North America.

That distribution is why the removal produced the reaction it did. Countries
with few other Olympic pathways lost one of their best, and the coalition that
lobbied for reinstatement included nations that agree on very little else —
the United States, Russia and Iran issued a joint statement, which is not a
sentence that gets written often.

Women's wrestling has a different and narrower map, concentrated in Japan, the
United States, China and increasingly India, and it grew fastest in exactly the
period the federation was expanding it under IOC pressure.

The universality criterion the IOC cited in 2013 was, on the evidence,
wrestling's strongest suit rather than its weakest.
