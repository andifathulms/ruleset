# Equipment evolution

Wrestling has no equipment history, and this page exists to say so and then to
say what the sport regulates instead.

A singlet and a pair of shoes. Headgear in some competitions. No implement, no
protective apparatus of consequence, no material that could outrun a
specification. Wrestling has produced no equipment-driven comparability break
and could not, because there is nothing to break.

What it regulates instead is **the body**, through weight categories and the
weigh-in — and that is where its equivalent of an equipment problem lives.

Extreme weight cutting is wrestling's serious health issue. Wrestlers dehydrate
severely to make a category and then rehydrate before competing, and the
practice has been linked to deaths in collegiate wrestling in the United States
in the 1990s. The federation's response has been procedural: weigh-in timing,
in some competitions daily weigh-ins, and rules on the permitted margin.

That is a genuine parallel to equipment regulation elsewhere on this site. In
athletics the object being measured is a javelin and the specification
constrains it. In wrestling the object being measured is a person, and the
specification is a weight category with a scale attached.

The mat itself is the only other regulated item, and its evolution — from
canvas over sawdust to modern foam — is a safety story that happened without a
rule anybody had to argue about.
