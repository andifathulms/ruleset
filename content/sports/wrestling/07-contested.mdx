# What is still argued about

**Cutting weight.** Drying out before a <Term id="weigh-in">weigh-in</Term>
is standard practice, and it is the sport's most serious unsolved health
problem. International rules control the timing and the margins. They do not
remove the reason to do it. [Judo](/sports/judo/),
[boxing](/sports/boxing/) and [weightlifting](/sports/weightlifting/) have
the same problem, and none of them has solved it.

**Passivity.** The <Term id="shot-clock">shot clock</Term> made the penalty
mechanical and left the first judgement where it was. Is a wrestler refusing
to attack, or simply being held? It is the hardest call in the sport and the
one most often disputed.

**Two styles.** The ban on leg attacks in
<Term id="greco-roman">Greco-Roman</Term> is a nineteenth-century choice of
style. Each time the Olympic programme is reviewed, someone argues that the
sport does not need two styles at the Games. Women compete internationally in
only one of them.

**Whether 2013 went far enough.** The rules were rewritten in seven months to
meet someone else's deadline. They fixed what could not be defended. Some in
the sport argue that a rewrite made under that pressure aimed at being easy
to follow, and stopped short of the best version of wrestling.

**Whether it could happen again.** Wrestling was voted out once and knows
that it can be. Every rule change since has been made with that in mind.
