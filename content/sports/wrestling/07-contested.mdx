# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Weight cutting.** The sport's most serious unresolved health problem.
Extreme dehydration before weigh-in is standard practice, it has been linked to
deaths at collegiate level, and international regulation remains procedural —
weigh-in timing and margins rather than anything that removes the incentive.
Judo, boxing and weightlifting have the same problem and none of them has
solved it either.

**Passivity.** The shot clock made the consequence mechanical and left the
initial judgement where it was. Whether a wrestler is genuinely failing to
engage or is simply being held is the hardest call in the sport and the most
disputed.

**Two styles.** Greco-Roman's prohibition on leg attacks is a nineteenth-century
stylistic choice with a classical name attached, and the argument that the sport
does not need two Olympic styles resurfaces whenever the programme is reviewed
— usually alongside the observation that women contest only one of them
internationally.

**Whether the 2013 rules went far enough.** The overhaul was made in seven
months under an external deadline. It fixed what was indefensible, and there is
a real argument inside the sport that a rewrite done under that kind of
pressure settled for intelligibility rather than for the best version of
wrestling.

**Whether it could happen again.** The sport was voted out once and knows it
can be. Every subsequent rule change has been made in that awareness, which is
a governing condition no other federation here operates under.
