# The oldest contest, in two styles

Wrestling did not need to be invented. Two people trying to put each other on
the ground need no rules to begin, and some form of it appears in almost
every culture that left a written record. Egyptian tomb paintings about four
thousand years old show pairs of wrestlers in holds.

<Photos ids="wrestling-beni-hasan wrestling-kerameikos-relief" />

It was contested at the ancient Olympic Games, and it was at the first modern
Games in 1896.

## Two styles

The split into two styles is the part that was invented, in the nineteenth
century. <Term id="greco-roman">Greco-Roman</Term> was codified in France.
Its rule is that nothing may happen below the waist: no attacks on the legs
and no use of the legs. The classical name has almost nothing to do with how
the ancients wrestled. The rule was a choice of style that was presented as
history.

<Term id="freestyle">Freestyle</Term> grew out of the British and American
catch-as-catch-can traditions, and it allows the legs.

<Photos ids="wrestling-single-leg wrestling-greco-roman-throw" />

The difference began as an arbitrary one and its effect is complete. The two
styles suit different bodies and reward different skills, and different
athletes compete in each. They are two sports under one federation.

Many national styles live beside the two Olympic ones. Iceland's glíma
wrestlers appeared at the London Games of 1908.

## Women

Women have wrestled internationally since the late 1980s. Women's freestyle
joined the Olympic programme in 2004. [From 2016 it has six weight
categories](#uww-2013-womens-categories), the same number as men's freestyle.
