# Origin and invention

Wrestling is the oldest competitive activity on this site and among the least
invented. Two people trying to throw each other to the ground requires no rules
to exist, and versions of it appear in essentially every human culture with a
written record.

It was contested at the ancient Olympic Games, and the modern Games has
included it since 1896 — one of very few sports of which that is true.

What was invented is the split into two styles, and it happened in the
nineteenth century for reasons that were partly national. **Greco-Roman** was
codified in France and given a classical name that has almost nothing to do
with how the ancients wrestled; its defining prohibition — no attacks below the
waist, no use of the legs — was a stylistic choice presented as a historical
one. **Freestyle** developed from British and American catch-as-catch-can
traditions and allows the legs.

That distinction is arbitrary in origin and absolute in effect. The two styles
select for different bodies, reward different skills, and are contested by
different athletes. They are separate sports sharing a federation, in the way
sailing's classes are and taekwondo's forms are not.

Women's wrestling was contested internationally from the late 1980s, entered
the Olympic programme in 2004, and reached parity with men's freestyle in 2016
— under pressure, seven months after the sport nearly lost its Olympic place.
