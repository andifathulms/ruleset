# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The ball.** Between 2005 and 2013, a scoreless period in freestyle could be
resolved by drawing a coloured ball from a bag to decide which wrestler
started with an advantage. A world title could turn on it, and did.

Alongside it, bouts were best of three periods won separately, so a wrestler
could score more points across a match and lose it. Both features were
introduced to make bouts more decisive and both made the sport harder to
believe in.

This is the rule the 2013 removal is most often traced to, and it is the
clearest case on this site of a governing body damaging its own sport by trying
to make it more watchable. Sailing's medal race and archery's set system are
milder versions of the same instinct; wrestling took it to a bag of balls.

**12 February 2013.** The IOC voted wrestling off the core programme. Nobody in
the sport had expected it, and the reaction — national federations, wrestlers,
and several countries' governments — was immediate and international.

**The seven months.** The president resigned. The rules were rewritten: two
periods of three minutes, cumulative scoring, the ball gone, passivity
tightened. Women's freestyle was expanded to six Olympic categories, equal to
men's. Governance was restructured and the federation renamed. In September the
IOC voted the sport back in.

**Weight cutting.** The sport's most serious ongoing problem and the one the
2013 overhaul did not address. Deaths in American collegiate wrestling in the
1990s produced weigh-in reforms there; internationally the practice remains
regulated by procedure rather than removed.
