# The year wrestling was voted out

## A bag of coloured balls

[From 2005 a bout was the best of three periods of two
minutes](#fila-2005-clinch-and-ball), and each period was won on its own. The
federation wanted shorter periods and a certain score in each one.

The result was a bout that a wrestler could lose while scoring more points
than the opponent. A wrestler could lose a period 1–0, win the next 5–0,
lose the third 1–0, and lose the bout with five points against two.

A period with no score needed a way to end. In Greco-Roman it was a clinch.
In <Term id="freestyle">freestyle</Term> a coloured ball was drawn from a bag
to decide which wrestler started with an advantage. A world title could turn
on the ball. People said again and again that a combat sport settling periods
by lot had forgotten what its contest was for.

## 12 February 2013

On that day the Executive Board of the International Olympic Committee voted
to remove wrestling from the core Olympic programme. It gave governance,
universality, gender balance and audience as its reasons. It was widely
reported that the federation had grown complacent. It had not modernised its
rules or developed the women's programme, and it had assumed that a sport of
the ancient Games could not be dropped.

Nobody in wrestling had expected the vote. National federations, wrestlers
and the governments of several countries reacted at once.

## Seven months

The sport had until September to make its case. Within weeks the federation's
president resigned. [The rules were rewritten](#uww-2013-scoring-rewrite): two
periods of three minutes, points added up across the whole bout, the ball
gone, and stricter rules on <Term id="passivity">passivity</Term>. Throws were
given more points. Women's freestyle was expanded to six Olympic categories,
equal to the men's. The federation reorganised how it was governed.

In September 2013 the IOC voted wrestling back onto the programme. In 2014
the federation took a new name, United World Wrestling.

The new rules are better than the old ones. A total score is easier to follow
than three separate periods, and the ball draw could not be defended. The
changes were still made for an outside body that had already voted the sport
out, on a deadline the sport did not choose.
