# Governing body politics

On 12 February 2013 the IOC Executive Board voted to remove wrestling from the
core Olympic programme.

The stated reasons were governance, universality, gender balance and audience.
The unstated one, widely reported, was that the federation had been complacent:
it had not modernised its rules, had not developed the women's programme, and
had assumed that a sport contested at the ancient Games could not be dropped.

The response was the fastest and most complete institutional overhaul in this
collection. Within weeks the federation's president resigned. Within months it
had rewritten the competition rules, expanded the women's programme to parity
with men's freestyle, restructured its governance and renamed itself. In
September 2013 the IOC voted wrestling back onto the programme.

That is the entire political history of this sport in the modern era, and every
rule on this page is either part of it or context for it.

The rules produced in that window are, on their merits, better than what they
replaced. Cumulative scoring across a bout is more intelligible than
best-of-three periods. Abolishing the ball draw removed something no combat
sport should ever have had. The passivity shot clock and the Greco-Roman par
terre rule both make the sport more decisive.

What this site records is the cause. Every other rule change here was made by a
federation responding to an incident, a technology, an audience or its own
athletes. Wrestling's were made to satisfy an external body that had already
voted the sport out, on a deadline it did not set.
