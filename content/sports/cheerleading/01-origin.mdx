# Origin and invention

Cheerleading is the only activity on this site invented to be an audience.

Organised crowd-leading began at American universities in the 1890s, and is
conventionally dated to 1898, when a student at the University of Minnesota
stood in front of a football crowd and directed it in unison. The purpose was
not to compete. It was to make a crowd louder and more coordinated in support
of a team playing a different sport.

The participants were men, and remained so for four decades. Cheerleading was a
male university pursuit with a status attached to it — several American
presidents led cheers as students — and it involved megaphones and organised
chanting rather than anything gymnastic.

Two things changed it. The **Second World War** removed the men, women took the
squads over, and when the war ended the activity did not revert. Cheerleading's
participation flipped from almost entirely male to overwhelmingly female inside
a decade, which very few activities anywhere have done in either direction.

And **the skills escalated**. Through the second half of the twentieth century,
squads added tumbling, then partner stunts, then pyramids and tosses, and a
competitive circuit grew up around them in the United States — largely
commercial, run by event companies rather than by federations.

The International Cheer Union was formed in 2004 to govern it internationally,
received provisional IOC recognition in 2016 and full recognition in 2021, and
cheerleading was contested at the World Games from 2022.

What is contested there is not what was invented in 1898. It replaced it.
