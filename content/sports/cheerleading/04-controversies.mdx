# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Catastrophic injuries.** The controversy that produced most of the rulebook.
Stunting — throwing a person several metres up and catching them — has
generated spinal and head injury rates that studies have repeatedly placed
among the highest of any school or collegiate activity in the United States.
The response has been restriction rather than equipment: limits on pyramid
height, on released stunts, on basket toss forms, on the number of supporters
required, and mandatory matted surfaces, all tightened as age falls.

**Whether it is a sport.** Not an abstract question. In the United States it
has been litigated, because whether cheerleading counts as a sport determines
whether it can be used to satisfy gender-equity requirements in educational
funding. Courts have not answered consistently, and the ICU's entire
institutional posture — the scoresheet, the federation, the Olympic recognition
— is partly an answer to it.

**Commercial concentration.** Much of the American competitive circuit, and the
apparel and camp systems attached to it, is concentrated in a single company.
Antitrust litigation has been brought. It is a governance problem of a kind no
other sport covered here has.

**Safeguarding.** A sport with a young, majority-female participation base and
a coach-dependent training structure has had abuse cases and has had to build
safeguarding systems in response. This is recorded because the rule changes are
real, not because the sport is unusual in having the problem.

**The two divisions.** Cheer and Performance Cheer share a federation and
almost nothing else, which complicates any argument about what the sport is.
