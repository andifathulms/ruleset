# Equipment evolution

The interesting thing about cheerleading's equipment is what is absent.

People are thrown several metres into the air and caught by other people, and
there is **no protective equipment at all**. No helmet, no harness, no padding,
no mat beneath the flyer other than the competition floor. In a sport with one
of the highest catastrophic injury rates recorded anywhere, the entire safety
system is procedural: rules about what may be attempted, and human spotters
standing in defined positions.

That is a genuinely unusual answer. Every other sport on this site that
produces head and spinal injuries has reached for equipment — helmets in
cycling and ice hockey, headguards in amateur boxing and kickboxing, face masks
in softball. Cheerleading reached for restriction instead: pyramid height
limits, permitted toss forms, released-stunt classes graded by age, and a
required number of spotters.

**The surface** is the one piece of hardware the rules govern closely.
Competitive routines are performed on matted, sprung floors, and the surface
requirement is a safety rule rather than a performance one — a substantial part
of the sport's injury history involves what people landed on.

**The megaphone**, the original equipment of the activity, survives as a
sideline prop and has no role in the competitive sport.

**Poms** are equipment in one Performance Cheer category and nowhere else.

Uniforms are regulated for modesty and safety — nothing that can catch, no
jewellery — which is ordinary, and worth noting only because uniform
regulation in a sport with this participation profile has a longer and more
contested history than the rulebook conveys.
