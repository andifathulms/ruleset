# A federation above a business

The International Cheer Union governs a sport whose main competitions it does
not run. Most competitive cheerleading happens in the United States, on a
commercial circuit of all-star club events. Much of that circuit, along with
the supply of uniforms and the training camps, belongs to a single company.

Gyms and families have brought antitrust lawsuits. They allege that this
concentration let prices be set across events, uniforms and training.

The federation itself has done well by the usual measures. It was formed in
2004, won provisional Olympic recognition in 2016 and full recognition in
2021, and has a place at the World Games. But an Olympic assessment normally
asks about national federations, routes for athletes and independent
governance. In cheerleading's largest country, the answer involves a company.

## Is it a sport? The courts were asked

In the United States this question has been taken to court. Schools and
colleges must meet gender-equity requirements in how they fund education, and
whether cheerleading counts as a sport decides whether it can be used to meet
them. The courts have not given one consistent answer.

The scoresheet, the federation and the Olympic recognition are all partly a
reply to that question.
