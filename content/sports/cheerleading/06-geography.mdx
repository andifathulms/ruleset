# Where it is played

One country, overwhelmingly, and a real but much thinner presence everywhere
else.

**The United States** is cheerleading. It has by far the largest participation
base — through school and collegiate squads and a very large all-star club
circuit — the deepest coaching structure, and the commercial event system that
the rest of the world's competitive calendar is modelled on. American teams
have dominated ICU world championships.

Beyond it, the sport has genuine competitive presence in **Japan**, which is
probably the strongest nation outside the US, along with **Canada**,
**Mexico**, **Chinese Taipei**, **Thailand**, **Finland**, **Norway**,
**Sweden**, **England**, **Germany** and **Australia**.

The pattern of spread is worth naming because it is unlike most sports on this
site. Cheerleading did not travel through a diaspora, a colonial administration
or a federation's development programme. It travelled through American cultural
export — film, television and the visibility of American college sport — and
took root as an aspirational activity, which is why it is strong in countries
with high exposure to American media and weak in countries with sporting
traditions of their own in similar space.

Its participation profile is also distinct: overwhelmingly female, young, and
concentrated in school and club structures rather than in the community clubs
that carry most sports covered here.
