# Where it is done

Most of the world's cheerleading is in one country. The United States has by
far the most participants, in school squads, college squads and a very large
circuit of all-star clubs. It has the deepest coaching. Its commercial events
are the model for competition calendars elsewhere, and American teams have
dominated the world championships.

<Photo id="cheer-cornell-sideline" />

Japan is probably the strongest nation after the United States. Canada,
Mexico, Chinese Taipei, Thailand, Finland, Norway, Sweden, England, Germany
and Australia also have real competitive teams.

<Photo id="cheer-finland-2025" />

Cheerleading spread through American film, television and the visibility of
American college sport. It did not travel with emigrants, with a colonial
administration or with a federation's development programme. People in other
countries saw it and wanted to do it. It is strong where American media are
widely watched.

Its participants are overwhelmingly female and young, and they belong to
school and club teams.
