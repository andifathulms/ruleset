# Officiating

Two panels doing two different jobs, and the second one exists because the
sport is dangerous.

**The scoring panel** marks difficulty and execution against a detailed
scoresheet: stunt classes attempted, pyramid construction, tosses, tumbling
passes, synchronisation, and the crowd-leading elements in the Cheer division.
The difficulties are the ones every judged sport on this site has — separating
two clean routines of unequal difficulty, and doing it in one viewing with no
replay in most competition.

**The safety officials** are the distinctive part. Separately from execution
marks, officials watch for skills performed above the level permitted for that
age and division, insufficient spotters, illegal pyramid heights and prohibited
release forms. These carry their own deductions.

That separation produces something rare: a team can perform a skill perfectly
and lose marks for it, because the skill was not theirs to attempt. The
scoresheet's ceiling is set by a safety code rather than by what athletes can
physically do.

Compare gymnastics, which abolished its scoring ceiling in 2006 precisely so
that difficulty could run open-ended. Cheerleading keeps a ceiling on purpose,
and moves it when injury evidence warrants — which makes its governing document
partly a medical one.

There is no instrumentation. Everything is judged by eye, once, on a routine
that is delivered in two and a half minutes and cannot be repeated.
