# Two sets of officials

A cheerleading routine is watched by two groups with different jobs.

The scoring panel marks difficulty and execution against a detailed
scoresheet. It covers the <Term id="stunt">stunts</Term> attempted, how the
pyramids are built, the tosses, the tumbling passes, how well the team moves
together, and the crowd-leading in the <Term id="cheer">Cheer</Term> division.
The judges see the routine once. In most competitions there is no replay and
no instrument.

The safety officials do something else. They look for skills above the
<Term id="level">level</Term> allowed for the team, for too few
<Term id="spotter">spotters</Term>, for pyramids that are too high and for
banned forms of release. Each
<Term id="safety-violation">safety violation</Term> has its own deduction.

<Photo id="cheer-memphis-coed" />

So a team can perform a skill perfectly and still lose marks for it, because
the team was not allowed to try it. The top of the scoresheet is set by the
safety code, and the federation moves it when the evidence on injuries
changes. [Gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) went the other way in 2006, when
it removed the upper limit on its difficulty score.
