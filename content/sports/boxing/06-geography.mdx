# Where it is played

Boxing has the least wealth-correlated geography of any sport on this site.
Its facilities are cheap, its equipment is minimal, and it has been
concentrated for a century in exactly the communities that other Olympic sports
do not reach — which is the reason it has survived repeated pressure to remove
it from the programme.

Cuba is the outstanding case, with a state amateur system that has produced
more Olympic champions per head than any programme in any sport, sustained
across decades in which its boxers could not turn professional. Kazakhstan,
Uzbekistan, Great Britain, the United States, Russia, Ukraine, Ireland, Mexico
and Thailand all produce international boxers from very different structures.

Ireland's is the small-country version worth recording: a club network in a
population of five million producing sustained Olympic success, mostly from
urban clubs with almost no funding.

The governance collapse fell across this map unevenly. When the IOC ran the
2020 and 2024 tournaments itself, it also wrote the qualification system, and
countries whose pathway depended on federation-run continental events had to
adapt to a structure designed elsewhere at short notice.

The sport's Olympic future has been in doubt for a decade, and the argument
made most often in its defence is a geographic one: boxing reaches people no
other sport on the programme reaches, and removing it would remove the only
Olympic pathway available in a large number of places.
