# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Seoul, 1988.** Roy Jones Jr dominated the light-middleweight final by any
visible measure and was awarded the loss 3–2. It was the most conspicuous of
several disputed results at those Games, and it produced the most drastic
response to a judging problem in this collection: from 1992, a point existed
only when at least three of five judges pressed a button for the same blow
within about a second.

The federation replaced judgement with arithmetic. It is exactly what
taekwondo would do twenty years later, except that taekwondo used sensors on
the athlete and boxing used the judges' fingers — so the arithmetic still
depended on the people it was meant to constrain.

**What the arithmetic did to boxing.** Because only cleanly separated,
individually registered blows counted, combinations scored poorly and body work
scored barely at all. Boxers adapted by throwing single visible shots and
holding. The sport spent twenty-four years being fought in a style its own
scoring system had created.

**2016, and the reversal.** The point-counting system was abandoned for the
professional ten-point must system. That restored precisely the discretion the
1992 system existed to remove — and judging controversies followed at the 2016
Games immediately, then the mid-tournament withdrawal of officials, then the
suspension of the federation, then an independent finding that bouts had in
fact been manipulated.

**The headguards, 2013.** Removed from elite men's competition on the argument
that they increased concussion risk. It remains the most counter-intuitive
safety rule on this site and the least satisfactorily explained, because it
applied to men and not to women or juniors.
