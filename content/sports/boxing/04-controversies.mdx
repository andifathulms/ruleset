# Who decides who won

## Seoul, 1988

The light-middleweight final of the 1988 Games. Roy Jones Jr dominated it by
any measure a spectator could see. The judges gave the <Term
id="bout">bout</Term> to his opponent, three votes to two. It was the most
visible of several disputed results at those Games, and an investigation
followed.

## Boxing by button

The federation answered by taking the opinion out of scoring. [From 1992 each
of the five judges had a button](#aiba-1992-computerised-scoring). A point was
recorded only when at least three of them pressed for the same blow within
about a second. The boxer with more points won.

The new system changed how boxers fought. Only blows that were cleanly
separated could be registered one by one. A
<Term id="combination">combination</Term> scored poorly, and a
<Term id="body-shot">body shot</Term> scored hardly at all. Boxers learned to
throw single, visible punches and then hold. For twenty-four years the sport
was fought in a style its own scoring had made.

The buttons did not end the suspicion either, because people still pressed
them. [Taekwondo](/sports/taekwondo/) met a similar judging problem about
twenty years later and put sensors on the athlete, so that a kick registers
without an official.

## Rio, 2016

In 2016 the federation dropped the buttons and adopted the
<Term id="ten-point-must">ten-point must system</Term> of professional boxing:
[judges score each round again](#aiba-2016-ten-point-must). The style the
buttons had produced was widely seen as unwatchable, and professionals were
being admitted that year.

The judges' discretion came back, and so did the disputes. There were judging
controversies at the 2016 Games at once. Officials were withdrawn in the
middle of the tournament. The federation was later suspended, and an
independent investigation found that bouts in Rio had been manipulated.
