# Governing body politics

Boxing is the only sport on this site whose governing body was expelled from
the Olympic movement, and the only one whose Olympic tournament has been run by
the IOC itself.

The sequence is worth setting out plainly. AIBA governed amateur boxing for
decades. Judging controversies recurred across multiple Games. In 2016 the
federation withdrew a large number of officials mid-tournament after disputed
decisions. In 2019 the IOC suspended AIBA's recognition and established a task
force to run the boxing competition at the 2020 Games, writing the rules and
the qualification system directly. It did the same again in 2024.

In 2023 the federation, by then renamed IBA, was expelled outright. An
independent investigation commissioned by the IOC found that bouts at the 2016
Games had been manipulated — a finding, not an allegation. World Boxing, a new
federation, was recognised in 2025 for the 2028 Games.

For two Olympic cycles, therefore, boxing's rules of competition were written
by the Olympic movement because the sport did not have a body it could be
trusted to. Nothing comparable has happened to any other sport here.

That context matters for reading the rest of this page. The 2013 headguard
removal, the 2016 return to professional-style scoring, and the 2016 admission
of professionals were all decisions of a federation that was found, within a
few years, to have manipulated its own competition. This site does not treat
that as evidence about any particular rule's merits, and it does record that
the body giving the stated reasons subsequently lost the presumption of good
faith.
