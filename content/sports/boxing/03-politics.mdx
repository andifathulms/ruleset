# The federation that was expelled

For two Olympic Games, boxing had no federation. The International Olympic
Committee wrote the rules, ran the qualifying events and staged the
tournament itself.

AIBA had governed amateur boxing for decades, and disputes about judging came
back at one Games after another. At the 2016 Games the federation withdrew a
large number of officials in the middle of the tournament, after decisions
that were disputed.

In 2019 [the IOC suspended AIBA's
recognition](#ioc-2019-federation-suspended). It set up a task force to run
the boxing at the 2020 Games, and it did the same again in 2024.

<Photo id="boxing-tokyo-2020-quarterfinal" />

In 2023 the federation, renamed IBA by then, was expelled outright. An
independent investigation had found that bouts at the 2016 Games were
manipulated. World Boxing, a new federation, was recognised in 2025 for the
2028 Games.

This matters for the rules as well. The same federation
[removed the headguard](#aiba-2013-headguards-removed) in 2013, [brought back
professional-style scoring](#aiba-2016-ten-point-must) in 2016 and [admitted
professionals](#aiba-2016-professionals-admitted) the same year. None of that
shows that any one of those rules is wrong. It does mean that the reasons the
federation gave for them can no longer be taken on trust.
