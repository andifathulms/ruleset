# Gloves and headguards

Boxing has two pieces of protective equipment that matter, and the same
argument surrounds both of them: does padding reduce harm, or move it?

## The glove

The Queensberry Rules made gloves compulsory in 1867, on humane grounds. A
glove protects the hand. A protected hand can punch the head harder and more
often than a bare fist can. Whether padded gloves reduced the harm of boxing
or moved it from the hands to the brain has been argued for more than a
century.

## The headguard

The <Term id="headguard">headguard</Term> has gone on and come off again.
[Amateur boxing made it compulsory in 1984](#aiba-1984-headguards), on safety
grounds. For years it was the plainest difference between an amateur and a
professional <Term id="bout">bout</Term>.

[In 2013 the federation removed it from elite men's
competition](#aiba-2013-headguards-removed), and gave safety as the reason
again. It said that a headguard makes the head a bigger target, blocks the
boxer's view to the sides, and gives a boxer the confidence to take punches
he would otherwise avoid. It said its own figures showed more concussions with
headguards than without.

<Photos ids="boxing-headguard boxing-tokyo-2020-final" />

Two objections have never been answered. Independent analysts have questioned
the federation's figures. And the change applied to elite men only. Women and
juniors kept their headguards, and no medical reason was given for treating
them differently. Critics also point to the timing: without headguards,
amateur boxing looked more like the professional sport, just as the
federation was moving to [admit
professionals](#aiba-2016-professionals-admitted).

The rest of the kit is simple. Gloves are specified by weight and hand wraps
by length. A mouthguard is compulsory.
