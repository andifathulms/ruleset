# Equipment evolution

Boxing's equipment history is one item, and the sport has changed its mind
about it in a way no other sport here has.

Gloves themselves are the founding equipment change and the founding paradox.
The Queensberry Rules made them compulsory in 1867 on humanitarian grounds, and
the long-run consequence is contested: gloves protect the hands, which allows
harder and more frequent punches to the head than bare knuckles permitted.
Whether padded gloves reduced harm or redistributed it from the hands to the
brain is a genuine argument, and it is more than a century old.

The **headguard** is the modern version of exactly the same question.

Made compulsory in amateur boxing in 1984, on straightforward safety grounds.
Removed from elite men's competition in 2013, also on safety grounds — the
federation's argument being that a headguard enlarges the target, restricts
peripheral vision, and gives a boxer the confidence to take shots they would
otherwise slip, and that its own data showed more concussions with them than
without.

This is the only rule on this site where protective equipment was removed to
protect people. The entry records it as `disputed`, for two reasons the
federation has never adequately answered: independent analysts have questioned
the data, and the change applied to elite men only while women and juniors kept
their headguards. A medical finding that applies to one group and not to others
in the same sport requires an explanation, and none was given.

Gloves are specified by weight, wraps by length, and mouthguards are
compulsory. Nothing else in the sport is regulated equipment.
