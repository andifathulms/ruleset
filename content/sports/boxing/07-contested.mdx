# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The headguards.** Still the most disputed safety rule on this site. The
federation's data has been questioned, the change applied to elite men and not
to women or juniors without a stated medical reason, and the body that made the
decision has since been expelled from the Olympic movement. Whether headguards
increase or reduce harm is a genuinely open scientific question, and boxing is
currently running both policies simultaneously in different categories.

**Whether the sport should be on the programme.** Medical bodies have argued
for decades that a sport whose objective includes concussing an opponent has no
place at the Games. The counter-argument is geographic and social: boxing
reaches communities nothing else on the programme reaches. Neither side has
moved.

**Scoring.** The ten-point must system restored the discretion that the 1992
arithmetic was built to remove, and the manipulation finding followed. Sensor
scoring of the taekwondo kind has been trialled and not adopted, on the ground
that boxing scores quality rather than contact. Whether that is true or
convenient is the open question.

**Governance.** World Boxing was recognised in 2025 and has to rebuild a
qualification structure, a judging corps and a set of competition rules that
the sport can trust. It is starting from a position no other federation here
has ever been in.

**Long-term neurological harm.** Documented, mechanistic rather than
accidental, and not addressed by any rule currently in force.
