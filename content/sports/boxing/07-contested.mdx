# What is still argued about

**Headguards.** Whether a <Term id="headguard">headguard</Term> reduces harm
or adds to it is an open scientific question. The figures behind the 2013
decision have been questioned, and the body that made the decision has since
been expelled from the Olympic movement. Boxing now runs both policies at
once: elite men fight bare-headed, and women and juniors wear headguards.

**A place at the Games.** Medical bodies have argued for decades that a sport
whose aim includes concussing the opponent does not belong on the programme.
The reply is about who boxes. The sport reaches places and people that
nothing else at the Games reaches, and for many of them it is the only
Olympic route there is. Neither side has moved.

**Scoring by sensor.** Impact sensors in gloves and body protectors have been
trialled and not adopted. The objection is that boxing scores quality, and a
sensor cannot tell a <Term id="jab">jab</Term> that lands from a jab that
lands well. Every sport says something like this before it automates a
judgement. Whether it is true of boxing has not been tested.

**Trust.** World Boxing was recognised in 2025. It has to build a
qualification system, a corps of judges and a set of competition rules that
boxers can believe in.

**Harm over a career.** Repeated blows to the head are how boxing works. The
long-term damage to the brain is documented, and no rule now in force deals
with it.
