# Equipment evolution

Karate has almost no equipment and the little it has exists for a reason that
is the reverse of boxing's.

A karategi, a red or blue belt to distinguish competitors, mitts, a gum shield,
and shin and instep protection. Younger competitors add body protectors and
face shields.

Boxing's gloves exist so that repeated contact is possible — they protect the
hands and permit the sport to happen. Karate's protection exists so that an
error is survivable. Because scoring requires control, a strike that lands hard
is a penalty, and the equipment is insurance against the technique that goes
wrong rather than permission for the technique that goes right.

That is an unusual position on this site and it is worth stating because it
determines what karate could never do.

**The sport cannot be instrumented.** Taekwondo installed sensors in 2009 and
crossed from judged to measured, because its scoring rewards force above a
threshold and a sensor can read force. Karate's scoring rewards a strike
delivered with the form to injure and deliberately withheld — restraint, not
impact. No sensor detects restraint.

So while taekwondo's equipment became a measuring instrument and boxing's
became the subject of its most contested safety rule, karate's stayed what it
was: cloth and padding, with nothing to measure and nothing to argue about.
