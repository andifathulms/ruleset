# Where it is played

Karate is practised by tens of millions of people, in ordinary halls in most
countries of the world. It is taught as a graded syllabus, mostly to children.
It spread through Japanese instructors, through American servicemen coming
home from occupied Japan, and through films.

All it needs is a hall and a suit, so cost does not decide where karate is
found. Teachers do. Where a line of instructors exists, the sport exists.

## The competitive map

The strongest nations in competition are a different list from the countries
with the most students. Japan, France, Spain, Turkey, Iran, Egypt and Italy
have been the strongest at world level. France has a very large licensed
membership, larger than Japan's. It is built on clubs that treat karate first
as an activity for children.

[Judo](/sports/judo/) has almost the same map for the same reason. Both are
Japanese martial arts that were exported as a syllabus and taken up by
European club systems, which then grew larger than the system at home.

## One Games

A practice with tens of millions of students was contested at one Olympic
Games, in the country that codified it, and then removed. Karate's world-wide
spread is its strongest argument for a place, and this is the outcome its
federation has objected to most.
