# Where it is played

Karate has one of the widest participation bases of any sport on this site and
a competitive map that does not follow it.

Practice is genuinely global. Karate is taught in ordinary halls in most
countries, as a graded syllabus aimed largely at children, and its worldwide
participation is in the tens of millions. It exported through Japanese
instructors, American servicemen returning from occupied Japan, and film.

The competitive map is European and Middle Eastern more than Japanese. Japan,
France, Spain, Turkey, Iran, Egypt and Italy have been the strongest nations at
world level, and France in particular has an enormous licensed membership —
larger than Japan's — built on a club structure that treats karate as a
children's activity first.

That mirrors judo's map almost exactly, and for the same reason: both are
Japanese martial arts that were exported as syllabuses and embedded in
European club systems that then out-scaled the country of origin.

The facility requirement is minimal — a hall and a suit — so karate's
distribution is limited by teaching tradition rather than by cost. Where an
instructor lineage exists, the sport exists.

The Olympic consequence is the one this sport feels most. A practice with tens
of millions of participants was contested at one Games, in the country that
codified it, and removed. For a sport whose global spread is its strongest
argument, being admitted once by host-city discretion and dropped by the next
host is the outcome the federation has objected to most.
