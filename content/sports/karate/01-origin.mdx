# From Okinawa to Japan

Karate comes from Okinawa. It grew up in the Ryukyu Kingdom from local
fighting methods and Chinese martial traditions, at a time when weapons were
restricted. It was passed on privately, from a teacher to a student, and was
not taught in public.

## The name

The characters first written for *karate* meant "Chinese hand". In the 1930s
they were changed to characters meaning "empty hand". Karate was then being
introduced to mainland Japan, and Japanese nationalism had made the older name
unwelcome.

## Into the schools

Gichin Funakoshi gave demonstrations in Tokyo from the 1920s. They took karate
into Japan's universities and schools. There it gained a graded syllabus,
coloured belts, standard <Term id="kata">kata</Term> and, in time,
competition.

<Photos ids="karate-funakoshi-kanku karate-funakoshi-makiwara" />

Competition had a problem the art did not have. Karate's techniques are
designed to injure, and nobody can hold a tournament in which every strike
lands at full force. The answer was to score a technique that is kept under
<Term id="control">control</Term> and to penalise one that lands hard. [That
rule is the base of the sport](#wkf-controlled-contact).

## Many styles

Karate did not arrive as one thing. Shotokan, Goju-ryu, Wado-ryu, Shito-ryu
and many other <Term id="style">styles</Term> have their own organisations,
their own syllabuses and their own competitions.

<Photos ids="karate-mabuni karate-jka-2012" />

The World Karate Federation has existed in its present form since 1990. It
governs one competition format, and it does not govern the art. A large part
of the karate world never competes under its rules.
