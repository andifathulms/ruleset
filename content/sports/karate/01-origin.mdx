# Origin and invention

Karate is Okinawan, and the name it now carries is a compromise made to get it
accepted in Japan.

It developed in the Ryukyu Kingdom from indigenous fighting methods and Chinese
martial traditions, in a period when weapons were restricted, and it was
transmitted privately rather than taught openly. The characters originally
written for *karate* meant "Chinese hand"; they were changed in the 1930s to
characters meaning "empty hand", as the art was introduced to mainland Japan
and Japanese nationalism made the earlier name unwelcome.

Gichin Funakoshi's demonstrations in Tokyo from the 1920s brought karate into
the Japanese university and school system, where it acquired the apparatus this
site recognises: a graded syllabus, coloured belts, standardised kata, and
eventually competition.

That competition had to solve a problem the art did not have. Karate's
techniques are designed to injure, and a full-contact version of them is not
contestable at volume. The answer — score a technique that is controlled,
penalise one that lands hard — is the sport's founding rule and the reason it
cannot be scored by machine.

The WKF in its present form dates from 1990. Karate campaigned for Olympic
inclusion for decades, was proposed by Tokyo as a host-nation sport, contested
one Games in 2021, and was not on the next programme.
