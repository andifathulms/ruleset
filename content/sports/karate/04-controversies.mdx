# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The contact problem.** Karate's founding rules controversy is structural: its
techniques are designed to injure, and a sport cannot be built on athletes
injuring each other repeatedly.

The answer — score a technique delivered with the form to work and deliberately
controlled, penalise one that lands hard — is elegant and creates a permanent
judging problem. A panel must decide whether a strike had good form, correct
distance, awareness and control, at speed, and that judgement cannot be
mechanised because the thing being judged is restraint.

Taekwondo escaped its equivalent crisis with sensors in 2009. Karate has no
such exit.

**Senshu.** For several cycles a drawn bout was won by whoever scored first,
which produced competitors taking an early point and then avoiding engagement
for the rest of the bout. It was removed in 2019 — the same problem judo
addressed in 2018 by removing penalties from the score, and wrestling addressed
with a passivity clock.

**Kata's flags.** Kata was decided by flags — two competitors, five judges, one
comparative verdict, no number. That produced a winner and no information. The
2019 move to a points score made kata explicable to an audience and comparable
within a competition, and severed its results from everything before.

It is worth noting where that sits. Karate adopted an absolute scale in 2019.
Skateboarding refused one in the same period, and breaking refused one in 2019
too. Karate could do it because kata sequences are catalogued and agreed, which
is exactly what the newer sports lacked.
