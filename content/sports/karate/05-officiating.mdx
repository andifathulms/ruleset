# Judging a strike that stops

A kumite bout is judged by a referee and four judges with flags. They have to
agree that a technique had good form, correct distance, correct timing,
awareness and control, and they have to do it at full speed.

<Photo id="karate-controlled-contact" />

## Why there are no sensors

[Taekwondo](/sports/taekwondo/) put sensors in its body protectors in 2009. A
kick there scores when a sensor reads enough force, and force is something a
machine can measure.

Karate rewards the opposite. The technique must be delivered with the form,
speed and distance to injure, and then stopped.
<Term id="excessive-contact">Excessive contact</Term> is a penalty. A machine
that read force would penalise exactly the strikes karate rewards, and no
sensor can detect restraint. So the sport is still judged by people.

Video review exists and helps with facts. It can show whether a technique
landed and whether a competitor was inside the area. It cannot answer the main
question, and every major competition produces arguments about whether a
technique had control.

## Kata: from flags to points

Kata used to be decided by flags. Two competitors performed, five judges each
raised a flag, and one of the two went through. That gave a winner and no
other information.

[Since 2019 each kata receives a points score](#wkf-kata-scoring-2019).
Technical performance counts for seventy per cent of it and athletic
performance for thirty. A score can be explained to an audience and compared
with the other scores in the same competition. It cannot be compared with any
result from the years of flags.

<Photo id="karate-kata-final" />

Karate could write a points scale because its kata were catalogued and agreed
long ago. [Skateboarding](/sports/skateboarding/) and
[breaking](/sports/breaking/) had no agreed catalogue of that kind, and in the
same years both chose not to adopt a fixed scale.
