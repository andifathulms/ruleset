# Officiating and technology

Karate is the striking sport on this site that cannot be instrumented, and the
reason is worth setting out precisely because it makes the comparison with
taekwondo exact.

**Taekwondo's** scoring rewards impact. A kick registers when a sensor in a
body protector reads force above a threshold. Force is a physical quantity, a
sensor measures it, and in 2009 the sport crossed from judged to measured.

**Karate's** scoring rewards a technique delivered with the form, speed and
distance to injure — and stopped. Excessive contact is a penalty, not a score.
The thing being assessed is control, which is the deliberate absence of the
quantity taekwondo measures.

There is no sensor for restraint. A machine that read force would penalise
exactly the strikes karate rewards.

So the sport remains judged: a referee and four judges with flags, agreeing
that a technique had good form, correct distance, correct timing, awareness and
control. Video review exists and helps with facts — was the technique landed,
was the competitor in the area — and cannot help with the central question.

**Kata** went the other way in 2019, from flag decisions to a points score with
technical and athletic components weighted seventy to thirty. That was possible
because kata sequences are catalogued and their content agreed, so a scale
could be written against material that already existed.

Breaking and skateboarding had no such catalogue and declined to invent one.
Karate had one and used it, which is the clearest illustration on this site of
what a judged sport needs before it can write a scale.
