# Governing body politics

World Lacrosse has done two things in the last decade that no other federation
on this site has done, and both were about admission.

**It invented a format.** Sixes was published in 2021: six a side, smaller
field, shot clock, no specialist positions, about forty minutes. It was
designed to fit an Olympic athlete quota and a broadcast window, and the sport
was added to the 2028 programme two years later.

Rugby did something similar in adopting sevens, and cricket's Twenty20 was
built for a commercial purpose that later served an Olympic one. Lacrosse's is
the most explicit: a format created from nothing, for a stated external
criterion, and it worked.

**It fought for the Haudenosaunee Nationals.** The confederacy whose people
invented lacrosse holds full membership of the federation and competes as a
nation. That has repeatedly collided with the rest of the world: the team has
been unable to travel to competitions because host countries would not accept
Haudenosaunee passports, and Olympic eligibility is a separate question again,
because the IOC recognises National Olympic Committees rather than nations.

The federation pressed the case, and in 2025 the IOC agreed arrangements
permitting participation at the 2028 Games.

This site records rule changes and their causes, and it is worth being precise
about the cause here. It is not a sporting rule. It is a governing body
deciding that a sport whose originators cannot compete in it has a legitimacy
problem, and spending institutional capital on that rather than on anything
else.
