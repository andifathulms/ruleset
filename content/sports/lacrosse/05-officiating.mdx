# Judging the check, and ending the stall

The hardest call in lacrosse is the <Term id="check">check</Term>. In the
men's game a defender may check an opponent's stick and body, within limits.
The official must decide in a moment what the check hit, how hard it was, and
whether it came from behind.

<Photo id="lacrosse-the-check" />

The women's game asks for a different judgement. Body checking is not allowed
at all, and the rules protect a space around each player's head. The official
is judging how close a stick came, with or without contact.

A player who fouls sits in the <Term id="penalty-box">penalty box</Term> and
the team plays one short. The cost of a foul is plain to everyone at once, and
the officials have running penalties to manage as well as restarts.

## The stall

For most of its history field lacrosse had no rule that made a team shoot. A
team in front could simply keep the ball, and teams did.
[Basketball](/sports/basketball/) met the same problem and answered it with a
shot clock in 1954. International field lacrosse [adopted one in
2019](#lacrosse-2019-shot-clock). The official no longer has to judge whether
a team is really attacking. The clock counts it.

There is little video review at international level. The federation is small,
and the sport has only recently had the broadcast cameras that review needs.
