# A game older than its rules

Lacrosse was played for centuries before anyone wrote a rule for it. The game
belongs to the Haudenosaunee, and also to the Anishinaabe, the Cherokee and
other First Nations. In its original form a match could involve hundreds of
players, cover kilometres of country and last for days. There was no marked
field.

It was more than a pastime. For the Haudenosaunee the game is medicine,
played for healing and for the Creator. It was also a way for nations to
settle a dispute without going to war.

## Montreal, 1867

William George Beers was a dentist in Montreal. In 1867 [he wrote the game
down](#lacrosse-1867-codification): a fixed number of players, a marked field,
a goal and a time limit. He wanted lacrosse to be Canada's national game, and
he wanted settler clubs to be able to play it on a field in an afternoon.

<Photos ids="lacrosse-beers lacrosse-montreal-1867" />

The same code cut the game off from its purpose. And in the same period the
Canadian associations barred Indigenous players from competition. The reason
they gave was amateurism: these players had been paid to play, so they were
professionals. The people who invented lacrosse were shut out of the written
version of their own game for decades.

## The Nationals

That history is why the Haudenosaunee Nationals matter. The confederacy whose
people invented the game [fields a team as a
nation](#lacrosse-2018-haudenosaunee-recognition), under its own flag, and
that team is regularly among the best in the world.
