# Origin and invention

Lacrosse is the only sport on this site with a living Indigenous origin, and
the only one whose codification is also an act of appropriation.

The game is Haudenosaunee — and Anishinaabe, and Cherokee, and others — and it
is far older than any code. In its original form it could involve hundreds of
players across kilometres of country over several days. It was not principally
a recreation: for the Haudenosaunee it is medicine, played for healing and for
the Creator, and it functioned as a means of settling disputes between nations
without war.

**George Beers** codified it in Montreal in 1867: a fixed number of players, a
marked field, a goal, a time limit. That made it a sport in the sense this
whole site means, and it is why lacrosse has rules to record at all.

It also detached the game from its purpose, and the detachment was not
accidental. Canadian associations of the period barred Indigenous players from
competition on the grounds that they were professionals — they had been paid to
play — which excluded the game's originators from the codified version of their
own sport for decades.

That history is why the Haudenosaunee Nationals matter more than a curiosity.
They compete as a nation, they are consistently among the best teams in the
world, and their participation in the 2028 Games required an accommodation the
Olympic movement does not normally make.
