# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Codification itself, 1867.** Not a controversy at the time and unavoidable
now. Beers's rules made lacrosse a modern sport, and Canadian associations of
the same era barred Indigenous players as professionals. The game's originators
were excluded from the codified version of their own sport for decades, by rules
written in the same period as the rules of play.

**The passport.** Repeatedly, the Haudenosaunee Nationals have been unable to
travel to international competition because host countries declined to accept
Haudenosaunee travel documents. It is the only case on this site where a team's
participation turns on a question of sovereignty rather than of sport, and it
produced sustained pressure that the federation acted on.

**The stall.** Field lacrosse had no shot clock until 2019 internationally, so a
team in front could hold the ball indefinitely, and did. It is the same problem
basketball solved in 1954, and lacrosse took sixty-five more years.

**The quota.** Field lacrosse is ten a side with specialist positions and
expensive equipment, which is a large athlete quota and a high barrier for new
nations. Sixes was the answer: six a side, no specialists, no long sticks,
forty minutes.

The federation did not adapt an existing format. It wrote a new one and
published it, and the sport was admitted to the Games two years later. That is
the clearest example on this site of a rulebook being written to satisfy an
admissions criterion.

**Headgear.** Permitted, not mandated, in the women's game from 2017, on the
argument that protection invites contact — the same argument boxing used to
remove headguards, resolved the other way.
