# Equipment evolution

Lacrosse's central equipment is the stick, and the sport's rules about it are
about how securely it holds the ball.

Traditional crosses were wood with leather and gut stringing. Modern ones are
composite heads with synthetic mesh, and the specification governs head width,
sidewall height and **pocket depth** — how far the ball sits into the net.

That last one is the consequential rule. A deeper pocket holds the ball more
securely and makes it much harder for a defender to dislodge with a legal
check, which would shift the whole balance of the sport towards possession.
Pocket depth is measured at inspection, and sticks fail it.

That is a rule of exactly the kind this site keeps finding — a performance
advantage regulated by measurement — and it is the lacrosse equivalent of
squash's tin height or equestrian's noseband gauge.

**Protection** is where the men's and women's games diverge completely. Men's
lacrosse permits body checking and requires helmets, gloves and shoulder pads.
Women's lacrosse does not permit body checking, and historically required only
eyewear, on the reasoning that adding protection encourages the contact it
protects against.

Optional headgear to a standard was permitted from 2017. That the sport chose
to permit rather than mandate is the same debate boxing had about headguards in
2013 — and lacrosse added optional protection where boxing removed compulsory
protection, both citing safety.

Sixes removed long defensive sticks entirely, which lowers cost and removes a
specialism.
