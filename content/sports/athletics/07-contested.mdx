# What is still argued about

**Shoes.** The 2020 rules allow a road sole of 40 millimetres and one rigid
plate, and they left the earlier records in place. Both halves are argued.
Some read the limits as drawn to permit shoes that were already on sale. And
the decision to keep the records sits against the same federation's decision
to restart the javelin list in 1986.

**Eligibility.** The rules on athletes with differences of sex development,
and on transgender athletes, are the most disputed in the sport. They decide
who may compete and in which category. They have been taken to international
courts, with conflicting outcomes, and the question is open.

**Where a record may be set.** Vienna, October 2019. Eliud Kipchoge ran the
marathon distance in under two hours, the first person to do so. It was not a
<Term id="record">record</Term>. He had pacers who rotated in and out, a car
that projected the pace onto the road, and no open competition.

Point-to-point courses, pacing technology, lighting systems used during a race
and heavily engineered surfaces raise the same question that
<Term id="wind-assistance">wind assistance</Term> raised long ago. Which
conditions must be standard before two numbers can be compared?

**Race walking.** The one event judged by eye faces regular proposals to add
sensors, or to replace the event. Either would settle the judging problem by
changing what the event is.
