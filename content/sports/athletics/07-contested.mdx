# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Shoes.** The 2020 rules set a maximum sole thickness and a limit of one rigid
plate, and left every record set in the preceding shoes standing. Both halves
remain argued. The thresholds are read by some as having been drawn where they
were in order to permit shoes already on sale rather than to withdraw them, and
the decision not to touch the record book sits directly against this
federation's own precedent with the javelin in 1986. The sport has not
reconciled those two decisions and shows no sign of trying.

**Eligibility.** Regulations on athletes with differences of sex development,
and on transgender athletes, are the most contested rules in the sport and among
the most contested in any sport. They are eligibility rules in this site's
vocabulary — they decide who may compete, in what category — and they have been
litigated internationally with conflicting outcomes. This site records that the
question is open and does not summarise the arguments, because a one-paragraph
summary of a dispute this consequential would be worse than silence.

**Where a record may be set.** Point-to-point courses, pacing technology,
in-race lighting systems and heavily engineered surfaces all raise the same
question the wind gauge raised a century ago: which conditions must be standard
for a number to be comparable. Every answer the sport gives narrows or widens
what counts as the same performance done twice.

**Race walking's judging.** The one event judged by eye rather than measured
periodically faces proposals to add sensors or to replace the event. Both would
resolve a judging problem by changing what the event is.
