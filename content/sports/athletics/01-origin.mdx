# Origin and invention

Athletics is the only sport in this collection that did not have to be invented,
because running, jumping and throwing did not need a rule to exist. What needed
inventing was the measurement.

The events themselves are inherited — from the ancient Olympic programme, from
Highland and rural games across northern Europe, from military training. What
the nineteenth century added was the apparatus of comparability: a standardised
distance, a standardised implement, a standardised surface, and eventually a
standardised clock. The International Amateur Athletic Federation was founded in
1912 and its first substantial work was ratifying world records, which is to say
deciding what counted as the same performance done twice.

That is why athletics produces more comparability breaks than any other family
in this collection. A sport that keeps a number is a sport that must keep
deciding whether today's number is the same kind of thing as the number from
forty years ago. Every time the answer is no, the record book has to do
something about it — and athletics has answered that question in both directions
within thirty-five years.
