# Where it is done

Running needs shoes and nothing else, so athletics is practised almost
everywhere. Winning is a different matter. Each event has its own map.

Distance running is concentrated in the Rift Valley to a degree
that has no parallel in sport. Sprinting is strongest in Jamaica and the
United States. The throws are strongest in central and eastern Europe. Race
walking is led by a handful of nations that keep up coaching and judging for
it. The pole vault needs a runway, a box, a mat and a coach, and its map
looks like a map of facilities.

At St Patrick's High School in Iten, Kenya, boards on the wall list former
pupils who became Olympic and world champions, with their events. The boards
show that the concentration is real. They do not explain it. Altitude, running
to school, a coaching tradition, the money a running career can bring, and the
sheer number of people training have all been proposed. No single account is
settled.

The rules decide how this geography shows. Athletes enter a Games by reaching
a qualifying standard, as well as through a national quota. A strong region
therefore fills the final. In [badminton](/sports/badminton/) the limit on
places per nation is what keeps strong players at home.
