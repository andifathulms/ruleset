# Governing body politics

Athletics spent its first eighty years as an explicitly amateur federation and
the word sat in its name until 2001. Amateurism was not a philosophy so much as
an eligibility rule, and like most eligibility rules it decided who could
compete rather than how. Its slow collapse through the 1970s and 1980s — the
trust-fund arrangements, then open payment — is the largest rule change in the
sport's history and it left no mark on any record book at all, which is why it
does not appear on the timeline as a comparability break.

Two renames record two repositionings: to the International Association of
Athletics Federations in 2001, dropping amateur, and to World Athletics in 2019,
dropping the federation-of-federations framing entirely.

The federation's authority over records is the thing that makes this sport
legible here. Because ratification is centralised and the criteria are
published, a decision to reset a record list is a dated, attributable act by a
named body. Football has no such office, which is why its section of this site
looks so different.
