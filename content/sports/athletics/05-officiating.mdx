# Who measures, and with what

Officials in athletics mostly measure. Starters work on the
<Term id="track">track</Term>, judges and measurers work at the jumps and
throws, and umpires watch the <Term id="lane">lanes</Term>. A measurement has
one correct answer, so the sport has handed much of the work to machines.

At the finish line a camera replaced the judge. A
<Term id="photo-finish">photo-finish</Term> picture is a record of the line
over time, which is why the runners in it look stretched.

<Photos ids="athletics-photo-finish-1977 athletics-bannister-1954" />

<Term id="automatic-timing">Fully automatic timing</Term> replaced the
stopwatch. A hand-held watch was read to a tenth of a second, and the person
holding it needed time to react, so hand times and automatic times were never
the same measurement. Roger Bannister's 3:59.4 for the mile, on 6 May 1954,
was taken on stopwatches. [From 1977 a hand-timed mark could no longer be a
sprint record](#iaaf-1977-automatic-timing). On 1 July that year, in Dresden,
Marlies Oelsner ran 100 metres in 10.88 seconds, the first time a woman broke
eleven seconds on automatic timing.

At the start, sensors in the
<Term id="starting-blocks">starting blocks</Term> record each runner's
<Term id="reaction-time">reaction time</Term>. A push less than 0.100 seconds
after the gun is ruled a false start, on the assumption that nobody can react
faster than that. A speaker behind each block carries the sound of the gun,
so every runner hears it at the same instant.

<Photo id="athletics-pressure-blocks" />

In the field events, wind gauges and electronic distance measurement do the
same job.

## What a machine cannot do

Two calls are still made by eye.

Race walking is the first. A judge at the roadside decides whether a walker's
feet have both left the ground. That makes it a judged event inside a measured
sport, closer in this one respect to [gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) than to
the sprint.

<Photo id="athletics-race-walking" />

The javelin landing is the second. The point must strike first, and a flat
landing cannot be judged. Athletics solved that one by [changing the
javelin](#iaaf-1986-javelin-redesign) so that the call became possible again.
