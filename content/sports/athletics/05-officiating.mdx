# Officiating and technology

Athletics has automated more of its officiating than any sport here, and it did
so because its officials were being asked to measure rather than to judge. A
measurement has a correct answer, so a machine can be trusted with it — and once
a machine is doing it, the measurement becomes comparable across stadiums and
decades, which is the whole purpose of the rulebook.

**The photo finish** replaced the judge at the line. **Fully automatic timing**
replaced the stopwatch, and from 1977 a hand-timed mark could no longer be
ratified as a record at sprint distances, which reconstituted the record lists
around a different instrument. **Pressure sensors in the blocks** detect a start
under 0.100 seconds after the gun, on the assumption that nobody can genuinely
react faster than that — note that this is not a measurement of a false start
but a threshold below which the sport declines to believe you.

**Wind gauges** and **electronic distance measurement** do the same job in the
field events: they remove the human from the number.

Two things resist all of this. The first is **race walking**, which is judged by
eye — a judge decides whether contact with the ground was lost — and is
therefore structurally a judged sport sitting inside a measured one, with all of
gymnastics' problems and none of its machinery. The second is the **javelin
landing**, where the tip must strike first. A flat landing cannot be judged, and
the 1986 redesign existed partly to make that call possible again. Athletics
solved a judging problem by changing the implement, which is a move no other
federation on this site has made.
