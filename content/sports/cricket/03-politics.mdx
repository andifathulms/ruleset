# Governing body politics

Two structural facts govern cricket, and both are unusual.

**The Laws are owned by a private club.** MCC has held them since 1788 and
still issues the Codes. The ICC runs international cricket and writes playing
conditions on top — which is where the formats, the review system and the
tournament rules actually live. So cricket, like football, separates the
ownership of the rules from the running of the sport, and a change can be a Law
or a playing condition depending on which body made it. This site records which.

**The money is concentrated in one member.** India generates the large majority
of world cricket's revenue, and the sport's governance has restructured around
that fact repeatedly. Financial and administrative arrangements between the
major boards have been rewritten more than once in the last fifteen years, and
each version has been contested by the smaller members.

The format question runs through all of it. Twenty20 created a professional
franchise market that now competes directly with international cricket for
players' time, and the governing bodies have limited leverage over leagues they
do not run. A sport that added a format for commercial reasons in 2003 now has
a commercial structure that its governing bodies did not design and cannot
fully control.

Cricket's return to the Olympic programme in 2028 is itself a governance
decision of this kind: the format chosen is Twenty20, because it is the only
one that fits, which means the Olympic version of cricket will be the newest
and shortest of the three.
