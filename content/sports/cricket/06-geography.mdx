# Where it is played

Cricket spread through the British Empire, and its map has hardly moved in a
century.

Twelve countries play <Term id="test">Test</Term> cricket: India, Australia,
England, Pakistan, South Africa, New Zealand, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, the West
Indies, Zimbabwe, Ireland and Afghanistan. Outside that group the game is
played seriously in very few places. Several of the smaller cricket nations
depend largely on people who have moved there from cricket countries.

## From England to India

Inside the map, the balance has turned over. Cricket's institutions were
English. Its money is now overwhelmingly Indian, and India produces the large
majority of the game's income. The arrangements between the major national
boards have been rewritten more than once in the last fifteen years, and the
smaller members have contested each version. The most-watched cricket in the
world is a domestic Indian league of franchise teams that did not exist
before 2008.

## Afghanistan

Afghanistan is the one real addition to the map in decades. The game arrived
there through refugee camps in Pakistan. No federation planned that route,
and no development programme produced it.

## The format that travels

<Term id="twenty20">Twenty20</Term> is short and needs less equipment and
preparation than the longer formats. Almost all competition among the smaller
nations uses it, and so will the Olympic tournament in 2028. Test cricket
needs five days and a carefully prepared pitch, and it does not travel. If
cricket's map changes, the shortest format will be the reason.
