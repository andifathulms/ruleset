# Where it is played

Cricket has the most concentrated geography of any sport on this site and the
clearest explanation for it: the map is the British Empire, and it has barely
moved in a century.

Twelve nations play Test cricket. India, Australia, England, Pakistan, South
Africa, New Zealand, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, the West Indies, Zimbabwe, Ireland
and Afghanistan. Outside that group the sport is played seriously almost
nowhere, and several of the associate nations that do play it are largely
sustained by expatriate populations.

Within that map the balance has inverted. Cricket's institutions were English
and its money is now overwhelmingly Indian, and the sport's governance has
restructured repeatedly to reflect that. The most-watched cricket in the world
is a domestic Indian franchise competition that did not exist before 2008.

Afghanistan is the one genuine addition in decades, and it arrived through
refugee camps in Pakistan rather than through any development programme — a
route no federation designed.

The rule consequence is the format question. Twenty20 travels: it is short,
needs less infrastructure, and is the format used for Olympic cricket in 2028
and for almost all associate-nation competition. Test cricket does not travel
at all, requires five days and a prepared square, and is played by twelve
countries. If cricket's map ever changes, the shortest format will be why —
which makes the 2003 rule change a piece of geography as well as commerce.
