# The bat that grew

For two hundred years the Laws measured a cricket bat in two directions only,
and the bat grew in the third.

## As wide as the wicket

The early bat was curved, like a hockey stick. The limit on a bat's width
goes back to 1771, when a player arrived at a match with a bat as wide as the
<Term id="wicket">wicket</Term>. The Laws limited the length and the width
from the eighteenth century. They said nothing about how thick a bat could
be. For most of that time it did not matter. A thicker bat was a heavier bat,
and nobody wanted one.

## Thicker, at the same weight

Then bat makers learned to press lighter wood. By the 2010s the edge of a bat
was about twice as thick as a generation earlier, and the bat weighed the
same. Shots that were not hit cleanly began to carry over the
<Term id="boundary">boundary</Term> for six. The balance between bat and ball
had moved, and no rule had changed.

[The 2017 Code set the first limits on the edge and the depth of the
bat](#mcc-2017-bat-size-limits): 40 mm for the edge and 67 mm for the depth.

## Ball and helmet

The ball has hardly changed. Its weight and its circumference have been fixed
for a century and a half.

Protective equipment is where safety has changed the game. Helmets came in
the late 1970s. After the death of Phillip Hughes in 2014, neck guards were
added and the standards for helmets were made stricter.
