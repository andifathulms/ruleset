# Officiating and technology

Cricket adopted decision technology earlier and more thoroughly than most
sports on this site, and it made one choice nobody else made.

The **Decision Review System**, from 2009, lets teams refer an umpire's
decision to a third official with ball tracking, edge detection and replay. The
reviewable questions are dismissals — was it out — which are mostly questions
of fact.

The distinctive part is **umpire's call**. Where ball tracking shows a
marginal outcome, the on-field umpire's original decision stands. That means
the identical delivery can be given out or not out depending on what was said
first, and the sport is explicit that this is deliberate: the tracking system
has a margin of error, and inside that margin the sport prefers the human.

No other sport here does this. Tennis removed its line judges entirely in 2025
because the machine was better. Football's VAR overrules the referee on facts.
Cricket built the instrument's uncertainty into the rule and gave the benefit
of it to the official.

Whether that is sophisticated or incoherent is genuinely argued. It produces
outcomes that look arbitrary to a viewer, and it is the honest response to a
prediction — ball tracking does not observe where the ball went, it computes
where it would have gone.

Edge detection has the same character. Snickometer and hot spot are evidence
rather than verdicts, and the third umpire weighs them. Cricket has more
technology than almost any sport here and has been careful not to let it make
the decision.
