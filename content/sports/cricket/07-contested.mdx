# What is still argued about

**The future of Test cricket.** A Test takes five days and only twelve
countries play it. The professional market increasingly pays more for three
hours of work. Outside three or four nations the oldest format is in decline.
Every plan to help it has been opposed by whoever would lose from it: two
divisions, a shorter Test, a world championship.

**Leagues against countries.** Twenty20 created a market of professional
franchise leagues, and the governing bodies do not run those leagues. More
and more players choose a league over their national team, and the national
boards cannot make them play. No authority co-ordinates the calendar.

**Bat and ball.** The 2017 limits were a first attempt to restore the balance
between bat and ball. Some say they did not go far enough. Others answer that
the shorter formats are meant to favour the batter.

**The Olympic format.** Cricket returns to the Games in 2028 as Twenty20,
because it is the only format that fits the schedule. So the largest audience
in the world will see the newest and shortest form of the game. Whether that
matters depends on what a person thinks cricket is.
