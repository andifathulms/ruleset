# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether Test cricket survives.** Five days, twelve countries, and a
professional market that increasingly pays better for three hours' work. The
sport's oldest format is in genuine structural decline outside three or four
nations, and every proposal to address it — two divisions, a shorter Test, a
world championship — has been contested by whoever it disadvantages.

**Umpire's call.** Still the most argued piece of officiating on this site.
Defenders say it correctly respects the tracking system's margin of error;
critics say a sport should not produce opposite verdicts on identical evidence
depending on who spoke first.

**Franchise leagues against international cricket.** Players increasingly
choose club over country, boards cannot compel them, and the calendar has no
authority co-ordinating it. This is a governance dispute created directly by a
rule change: Twenty20 built a market the governing bodies do not run.

**Bat and ball.** The 2017 bat limits were a first attempt at rebalancing, and
the argument that they did not go far enough is live, as is the counter-argument
that shorter formats are supposed to favour batters.

**The Olympic format.** Cricket returns in 2028 as Twenty20, which means the
version of the sport the largest global audience will see is the one that is
twenty-two years old rather than the one that is a hundred and fifty. Whether
that is a problem depends entirely on what a person thinks cricket is.
