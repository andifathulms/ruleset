# Where it came from

Cricket is one of the oldest organised games. It is mentioned in writing in
the sixteenth century, and the first known written laws date from 1744. An
engraving made a year before those laws shows two stumps, a curved bat like a
hockey stick, and a boy sitting on the grass to keep the score.

## A club that owns the laws

The Marylebone Cricket Club was founded in London in 1787. It took charge of
the Laws of Cricket in 1788 and has held them ever since. It is a private
members' club, and it owns the laws of the game for the whole world.

<Photo id="cricket-lords-pavilion" />

The International Cricket Council, founded in 1909, runs the international
game. It writes playing conditions on top of the Laws. The three formats, the
review system and the tournament rules all live in those playing conditions.
So a change to cricket can be a Law or a playing condition, depending on
which body made it. [Football](/sports/football/) is arranged in the same
way, with one body that owns the laws and another that runs the sport.

## One Olympic match

Cricket has been played at the Olympic Games once. In Paris in 1900 there was
a single match between Great Britain and France. The British side was a
touring club, the Devon and Somerset Wanderers. The French side was made up
largely of people from abroad who lived in France. Cricket returns to the
Games in 2028, after 128 years.
