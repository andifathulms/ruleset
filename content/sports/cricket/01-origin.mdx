# Origin and invention

Cricket is older than almost everything on this site and was codified earlier
than all of it. References to the game exist from the sixteenth century, the
first known written laws date from 1744, and the Marylebone Cricket Club took
custody of them in 1788 and has held them ever since.

That custody arrangement is the structurally interesting thing. MCC is a
private members' club in London that owns the Laws of Cricket for the world.
The International Cricket Council runs the international game and issues
playing conditions on top of the Laws, but it does not own them.

This is football's arrangement exactly — IFAB owns the Laws, FIFA runs the
sport — and cricket arrived at it a century earlier and by a different route.
In both cases the effect is the same: a rule change requires two bodies to
agree, and the body that owns the rules is not the body with the money.

The sport spread through the British Empire, which is the whole of its
geography and much of its politics. It is played seriously in about a dozen
countries and barely at all outside them, and the map has not changed much in
a century.

The one Olympic appearance before 2028 was in 1900: a single match, between
Great Britain and France, the French side largely composed of expatriates. It
was not repeated for 128 years.
