# Governing body politics

The politics of flag football are the politics of American football's
international ambitions.

IFAF, the international federation, governs both the tackle code and the flag
code. Its tackle code cannot be exported: it requires a large field, forty
players, protective equipment for all of them, and a medical infrastructure
that most national federations do not have. Its flag code needs a belt.

So the flag game became the vehicle. IFAF pursued Olympic recognition on the
back of a format explicitly designed for countries with no American football
tradition, and the National Football League backed the campaign — a
professional league in one country funding the Olympic candidacy of a variant
its own competition does not play.

The federation has had its own difficulties. IFAF spent much of the 2010s in a
leadership dispute serious enough to produce two competing bodies claiming the
same name and holding rival congresses, resolved only in the following years.
An Olympic campaign conducted from that position was not obviously going to
work.

It worked in 2023, as a host-city proposal from Los Angeles. That mechanism —
the host organising committee proposing additional sports — is the same one
that brought karate in for Tokyo and dropped it afterwards, and it carries no
commitment beyond the single Games.
