# Equipment evolution

The equipment story here runs backwards. Every other sport on this site added
protective equipment as it discovered what it was doing to its players. Flag
football removed the injury and then removed the equipment.

American football's helmet and pads exist to make collisions survivable, and
they have the well-documented second-order effect of making collisions more
frequent, because a padded player will hit in ways an unpadded one will not.
Flag football has neither, and needs neither, because contact is illegal rather
than cushioned.

What is left is a belt. It carries two flags, one on each hip, attached so that
they detach cleanly under a pull. This is a more consequential piece of
equipment than it sounds: a flag that is tucked into shorts, held down, worn
too tight or attached at the wrong angle changes whether a defender can end a
play at all, and tampering with it is an offence.

The ball is smaller than the eleven-a-side ball, which matters because
everything is a pass and hands vary. Uniforms must not have anything a flag can
catch on, and shirts must be tucked, for the same reason.

The absence of a kicking game means the sport has no kicking tee, no goalposts,
and no equipment associated with the plays that injure most people in the
eleven-a-side game.
