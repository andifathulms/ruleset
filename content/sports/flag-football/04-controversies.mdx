# Rules that keep the contact out

Taking out the tackle did not take out every collision. Three more rules were
needed to finish the job.

## The pile at the goal line

A game without tackling still rewarded a team for shoving its ball carrier
forward one yard behind a wall of bodies. That was a contact play, and it was
the one place where a heavy team still had the advantage. The
[no-run zone](#flag-no-run-zones) ended it. In the five yards before the
halfway line and before the <Term id="end-zone">end zone</Term>, the offence
must pass.

## The kicking game

In tackle football, kick-offs and punt returns injure players at the highest
rate. They are the only plays in which players run into each other at full
speed from a distance. Flag football did not try to make those plays safer.
[It removed kicking altogether](#flag-no-punt-no-kick). There is no kick-off,
no punt and no field goal, and a team that fails on its four
<Term id="down">downs</Term> gives up the ball.

## Guarding the flag

The ball carrier can cause contact too. Shielding a flag with a hand, an arm
or the ball is <Term id="flag-guarding">flag guarding</Term>, and runners do
it by instinct. It is penalised and called constantly. It is also the most
argued call in the game, because a runner's arm has to be somewhere.
