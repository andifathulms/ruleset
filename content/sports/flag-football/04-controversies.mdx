# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The pile at the goal line.** A game with no tackling still rewarded shoving
a ball carrier forward one yard behind a wall of bodies, which was both a
contact play and the exact tactic the sport was meant to eliminate. The
no-run zone — five yards before every first-down line and before the end zone,
inside which the offence must throw — removed it. It is the most artificial
rule in the sport and the clearest example of a rule written to protect the
sport's own premise.

**Kick-offs and punt returns.** These plays produce the highest injury rates in
tackle football, because they are the only ones where players collide at full
sprint from a distance. Flag football did not regulate them; it deleted the
kicking game entirely, and replaced the punt with an ordinary turnover on
downs.

**Flag guarding.** The ball carrier's own contribution to contact is shielding
the flag with a hand, an arm or the ball, and it is instinctive. It is
penalised, called constantly, and remains the most argued decision in the game,
because a runner's arm has to be somewhere.

**Whose sport got in.** Inclusion for 2028 was proposed by the host city and
supported by an American professional league. IFAF's account is that a global
amateur sport with a world championship since 2002 was recognised. Both are
recorded; neither is settled.
