# Where it came from

Flag football is American football with the tackle taken out, and the United
States military took it out.

American football was played for recreation on military bases through the
1930s and 1940s, and players were hurt, as they are wherever the game is
played. During the Second World War an injured serviceman was a man removed
from duty. So the bases played a version in which a ball carrier was down when
a <Term id="flag">flag</Term> was pulled from their belt. [The flag replaced
the tackle](#flag-1940s-flag-pull). The game is generally credited to Fort
Meade, Maryland, in the early 1940s, and it spread through the services.

## Fifty years of local rules

The game came home with the servicemen. For the rest of the century it lived
in the United States as a sport of schools, colleges, church leagues and
parks. There were a dozen local codes that did not match. Teams had four,
seven or eight players. Some codes allowed
<Term id="blocking">blocking</Term> and some did not. The field was whatever
size was free.

## One code for export

The international game is recent. IFAF, the international federation, held
the first world championship in 2002. Its
[five-a-side format](#flag-five-a-side) was written so that a country with no
American football could take it up. IFAF also governs the tackle game, which
needs a large field, a squad of forty and protective equipment for every
player. The flag game needs a belt.

## The road to Los Angeles

IFAF sought a place at the Olympic Games with the flag game, and the National
Football League backed the campaign. The league's own competition does not
play flag football. The federation was in a weak position to run a campaign.
For much of the 2010s a leadership dispute left two bodies claiming the name
IFAF and holding rival congresses.

The campaign succeeded in October 2023. [Flag football was added to the Los
Angeles 2028 Games](#flag-2023-olympic-inclusion), with tournaments for men
and for women. It had never been at a Games before. The proposal came from
the Los Angeles organisers, under the arrangement that lets a host city
propose extra sports. [Karate](/sports/karate/) came into the Tokyo Games the
same way and was dropped afterwards.
