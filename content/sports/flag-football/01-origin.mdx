# Origin and invention

Flag football is American football with one thing taken out, and it was taken
out by the United States military.

American football was played as recreation on military bases through the 1930s
and 1940s, and it produced the injuries it produces everywhere. Injured
servicemen were a cost the war effort would not carry, so bases played a
version in which a ball carrier was down when a flag was pulled from a belt
rather than when they were knocked over. The game is generally credited to Fort
Meade, Maryland, in the early 1940s, and it spread through the services.

It came home with the servicemen. Through the second half of the twentieth
century flag football existed in the United States as intramural, school,
church league and park sport, in a dozen incompatible local codes: four a side,
seven a side, eight a side, with blocking and without, on fields of whatever
size was available.

International codification is recent. IFAF ran the first world championship in
2002, and the five-a-side format on a fifty-by-twenty-five-yard field was
written to be exportable — a game a country with no American football
infrastructure could adopt.

The 2023 decision to include it at Los Angeles 2028 makes it the newest sport
on the programme by a wide margin, and the only one whose founding rule is a
subtraction.
