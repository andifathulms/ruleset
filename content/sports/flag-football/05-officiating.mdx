# Officiating

Flag football's officiating problem is the inverse of most sports on this site.
Elsewhere officials judge whether contact was excessive. Here they judge
whether contact happened at all, and the threshold is very low.

The defining calls:

**Flag guarding.** Did the ball carrier use a hand, an arm, or the ball to
prevent a defender reaching the flag? A runner's arms move. Deciding when
natural motion becomes a shield is a judgement made in a fraction of a second
and it decides possessions.

**The pull.** Was the flag pulled cleanly, or did the defender make contact
with the body first, or hold the runner, or pull an already-detached flag? A
flag that falls off on its own leaves the runner down where it fell, which
means an equipment failure is adjudicated as a play.

**The line of scrimmage and the no-run zone.** Where the ball was when it was
thrown, and whether a run began inside a no-run zone, are positional judgements
on a small field with a running clock.

There is no scrum of bodies to see through, which makes the game easier to
officiate than tackle football. There is also no protective equipment absorbing
marginal contact, which makes the standard stricter. Elite competition uses
video review sparingly; the sport is too young to have settled what it should
be used for.
