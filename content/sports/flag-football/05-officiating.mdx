# Who makes the calls

In most contact sports an official judges whether contact went too far. In
flag football the official judges whether there was contact at all, and very
little is allowed.

**Flag guarding.** Did the ball carrier use a hand, an arm or the ball to keep
a defender from the flag? A runner's arms move. The official has a fraction of
a second to decide when a natural movement became a shield, and the call can
decide who has the ball.

**The pull.** Was the <Term id="flag-pull">flag pull</Term> clean? Or did the
defender touch the runner's body first, hold the runner, or pull a flag that
had already come off? A flag that falls off by itself leaves the runner down
where it fell, so a failure of equipment is ruled on like a play.

**Position.** Where was the ball when it was thrown? Did a run begin inside a
<Term id="no-run-zone">no-run zone</Term>? These are calls about position on a
small field, with the clock running.

There is no pile of bodies to see through, which makes the game easier to
officiate than tackle football. There is also no padding to soften small
contacts, so the standard is stricter. Top competition uses video review
sparingly.
