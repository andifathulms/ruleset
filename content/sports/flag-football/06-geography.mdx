# Where it is played

Flag football's map is wider than American football's and still centred on the
same place.

The United States is the largest playing base by a distance, through school,
collegiate, park and league play, and the National Football League's youth
programmes push the format directly. Mexico is the strongest challenger and has
been for years — Mexican teams have won world championships and the sport has a
domestic depth there that predates any Olympic interest.

Beyond those two, Panama, Austria, Italy, Israel, Japan and Canada have been
competitive at world level, which is a more scattered list than most invasion
sports produce. That scatter is the product of the format: a country needs a
patch of grass and twenty belts, so the sport spreads where somebody
enthusiastic starts it rather than where infrastructure exists.

The women's game is unusually close to the men's in institutional terms,
because both were codified at the same time and neither carries a century of
exclusion. Los Angeles 2028 contests both tournaments, on the same format, with
the same number of teams.

What flag football does not yet have anywhere is a professional layer. The
pathway from a national team place to a living is short and mostly runs through
another sport.
