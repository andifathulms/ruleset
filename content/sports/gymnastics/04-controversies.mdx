# Two scores that changed the sport

## Athens, 2004: a tenth of a point

The men's <Term id="all-around">all-around</Term> final in Athens in 2004.
Yang Tae-young performed on the parallel bars, and the judges gave his <Term id="routine">routine</Term> a start value one tenth of a
point too low. He finished third, 0.049 behind the winner, Paul Hamm.

The federation admitted the error and suspended the three judges responsible.
It did not change the result.

Two years later [the Code of Points was rewritten](#fig-2006-open-ended-code).
Difficulty became a sum of the <Term id="element">elements</Term> performed,
worked out by [a panel that does nothing else](#fig-2006-judging-panels), so
that it can be checked.

Whether Athens caused the rewrite is disputed. The federation's stated reason
was that the old scale had run out of room. As gymnasts learned harder skills,
more and more routines started from 10.0. Only execution could separate them,
and a harder routine could no longer be rewarded. That argument is years older
than Athens. The sources do not agree on which reason decided it.

## Montreal, 1976: the score the board could not show

Nadia Comăneci scored 10.0 on the uneven bars at the 1976 Olympic
Games. The scoreboard had been built on the assumption that nobody ever would.
It showed 1.00.

For thirty years after that, the perfect 10 was one of the few numbers in
sport that needed no explanation. The 2006 Code removed the maximum. A 10.0
from before it has no equivalent on the scale used now, and [no conversion
between the two exists](#gymnastics-scores).
