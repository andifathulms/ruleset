# Origin and invention

Gymnastics is the oldest governed sport in this collection — the federation now
called the FIG was founded in 1881, before the modern Olympic movement existed —
and for most of its early life it was not a competitive sport at all. It was a
system of physical education, in two rival traditions: the German *Turnen*
movement of Friedrich Jahn, built around apparatus, and the Swedish system of
Per Henrik Ling, built around free exercise and posture.

The apparatus tradition won, which is why the sport is organised by apparatus.
But the education tradition left its mark in a way that matters enormously here:
the point of the exercise was to perform a prescribed movement correctly. That
is a fundamentally different object from a race or a throw, and it produced a
fundamentally different kind of rule.

In a measured sport the rules constrain the conditions and the number takes care
of itself. In gymnastics the rules *are* the number. The Code of Points does not
describe how to compete; it defines what a score is. Which means that in this
family, and only in this family, changing the rules and changing the units are
the same act.
