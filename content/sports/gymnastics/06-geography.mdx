# Where gymnasts come from

Top gymnasts come from few countries, and the reason is equipment. The sport
needs a special hall, <Term id="apparatus">apparatus</Term>, deep mats, a foam
pit and a qualified coach. Countries that have many of these produce
gymnasts. Countries without them almost never do.

The historical map is a map of state sports systems: the Soviet Union,
Romania, East Germany, Hungary, Czechoslovakia, and later China. A centrally
funded programme could pick out children at six or seven and place them in
residential training, and gymnastics rewards an early start.

The United States and Japan reached the same level by other routes. The
United States has a dense system of private clubs. Japan had long investment
from its federation.

The strongest programmes recruited very young, and others copied them.
Through the 1970s and 1980s gymnasts were reaching the top level before
puberty, and several federations were reported to have misstated ages. The
minimum age for senior competition rose from fourteen to fifteen in 1981, and
[to sixteen in 1997](#fig-1997-minimum-age).
