# Who gives the score

No instrument measures a gymnastics routine. There is no touchpad and no goal
line. The score is a set of opinions, and the sport depends on people
believing that those opinions are fair.

<Photo id="gymnastics-judges-2010" />

## Two panels

Since 2006 the judges at each apparatus sit in two groups that work
independently. The <Term id="d-panel">D panel</Term> decides the
<Term id="d-score">difficulty score</Term>. The
<Term id="e-panel">E panel</Term> decides the
<Term id="e-score">execution score</Term>. Before that, one panel did both
jobs and produced one number.

[The split](#fig-2006-judging-panels) makes a score easier to check. A single
combined number gives a coach nothing to point at. With two numbers, a
disputed result can be traced to one panel. If it is the D panel, the question
is whether a sum was added correctly.

## What a coach can question

A coach who thinks the difficulty score is wrong can make an
<Term id="inquiry">inquiry</Term>. It must be made within a short time, and
the D panel then looks at the routine again. Difficulty is a sum of listed
elements, so it has a correct answer. Execution is a judgement, and it cannot
be questioned this way.

## Judges and their countries

The oldest complaint about judging is the bloc: judges from allied nations
are accused of scoring each other's gymnasts kindly. The complaint is older
than television. One reason it lasts is that qualified international judges
come from the same small set of countries as the gymnasts.

The federation has added one answer after another. It dropped the highest and
the lowest score on a panel. It made panels anonymous. It split difficulty
from execution. It now compares each judge's scores with the middle score of
the panel.

<Photo id="gymnastics-judges-sofia" />

The federation presented the 2006 Code as a reform of the competition,
because the sport had run out of room above 10.0. It is also the largest of
these measures against biased or mistaken judging. The record does not show
which purpose drove the decision.
