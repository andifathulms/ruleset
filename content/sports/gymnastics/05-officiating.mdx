# Officiating and technology

Gymnastics has the hardest officiating problem on this site, because it must
certify a judgement rather than record a fact. There is no equivalent of a
touchpad or a goal line. The score is a set of opinions, and the sport's entire
credibility rests on those opinions being seen to be impartial.

The countermeasures have escalated for a century. Discard the highest and lowest
scores. Anonymise the panel. Evaluate each judge statistically against the panel
median and remove the outliers. And, from 2006, the largest structural change:
split the panel in two, so that a **D panel** determines difficulty and an
**E panel** scores execution, working independently.

Splitting them is not a technology fix, but it does the thing technology usually
does — it makes a decision auditable. A single composite score cannot be
challenged, because there is nowhere to point. Two scores from two panels can
be, because a disputed result can now be attributed to one panel and, within it,
to the arithmetic of a difficulty sum that is either right or wrong.

The video review that exists here is an **inquiry**: a coach may formally query
the difficulty score, within a short window, and the D panel reconsiders. Note
what is reviewable. Difficulty is a sum of catalogued elements, so it has a
correct answer. Execution is a judgement, and it is not open to inquiry at all.

Athens 2004 is why this shape exists. An error in one start value changed the
medal order in the men's all-around, the judges were suspended, and the result
stood. The sport's response was to make difficulty into arithmetic that could be
checked, and to leave the part that cannot be checked to people whose work is
now statistically monitored instead.
