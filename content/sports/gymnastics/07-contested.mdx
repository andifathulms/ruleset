# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the open-ended Code was worth it.** Two decades on, the argument that
began in 2006 has not closed. The Code rewards difficulty without limit, and the
predictable result is that gymnasts are incentivised towards skills at the edge
of what a body can land safely. Periodic revisions have adjusted the difficulty
values of the most dangerous elements — down, to remove the incentive — which is
the federation using its pricing system as a safety instrument. Whether that is
sufficient, or whether an open-ended difficulty score is inherently a safety
problem, is genuinely disputed within the sport.

**The lost audience.** The counter-argument is not about safety at all. The
perfect 10 was legible to people who knew nothing about gymnastics, and the
current score is not. Whether the sport traded its general audience for its
accountability is an argument that this site can record but not settle — and it
is the reason the series section of this page has an essay where a chart would
be.

**The minimum age.** Raising it protects children and also removes competitors
at exactly the point where the sport's demands and a small, light body most
favourably coincide. Proposals to raise it again, and counter-arguments that
sixteen already excludes athletes at their physical best, both circulate.

**Safeguarding.** The most serious open question in the sport is not a rule of
play. Inquiries in several countries have found abusive coaching cultures in
programmes that were producing results, and the governance response — what a
federation owes athletes it does not directly employ — is unresolved.
