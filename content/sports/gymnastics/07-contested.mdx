# What is still argued about

**Difficulty without a limit.** The argument that began with [the 2006
Code](#fig-2006-open-ended-code) has not ended. The Code rewards difficulty
with no upper limit, so gymnasts have a reason to try skills at the edge of
what a body can land safely. The federation has lowered the values of the
most dangerous <Term id="element">elements</Term> in later revisions, to take
that reason away. Some in the sport think this is enough. Others think an
open-ended <Term id="d-score">difficulty score</Term> is a safety problem in
itself.

**The audience.** People who knew nothing about gymnastics understood a
perfect 10. The score used now is harder to read. The 2006 Code made judging
easier to check and let harder routines earn more. Whether it cost the sport
its general audience is still argued.

**The minimum age.** A higher age limit protects children. It also keeps out
competitors at the age when a small, light body suits the sport best. There
are proposals to raise the limit again, and there are replies that sixteen
already shuts out athletes at their physical best.

**Safeguarding.** The most serious open question is not a rule of
competition. Inquiries in several countries have found abusive coaching in
programmes that were producing results. A federation does not directly employ
the athletes in those programmes, and what it owes them is unresolved.
