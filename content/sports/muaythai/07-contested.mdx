# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Two versions of the sport.** The internationally recognised code is padded
and restricts elbows; the Thai professional sport is neither, and is where the
best fighters are. Whether the amateur version can ever be the sport's summit,
or whether it is a permanent parallel structure, is unresolved.

**Child fighting.** Children compete professionally in Thailand within a
gambling economy, and injuries and deaths have been documented. Legislative
attempts to raise minimum ages and restrict techniques for young fighters have
met resistance from within the industry and from families who depend on the
income. It is the most serious open question in the sport.

**Gambling.** Integral to professional stadium muaythai and structurally
incompatible with the integrity frameworks an internationally recognised
federation must maintain.

**Headguards.** Boxing removed them for senior men in 2016 arguing the evidence
did not support them; muaythai's amateur code kept them. The same evidence,
two federations, two answers — and in a sport with legal elbows the stakes are
higher.

**Naming the regional codes.** Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar contest closely
related forms and the question of what the shared tradition is called has
produced real diplomatic friction, including at regional multi-sport games.

**Olympic prospects.** Recognition since 2021, no place, and the sport's most
distinctive techniques are the ones its amateur code exists to restrain.
