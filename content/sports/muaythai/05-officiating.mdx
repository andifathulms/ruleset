# When to leave a clinch alone

The call that most defines muaythai is about the
<Term id="clinch">clinch</Term>. A [boxing](/sports/boxing/) referee
separates fighters who close on each other. A muaythai referee leaves them to
work, with knees, elbows, <Term id="sweep">sweeps</Term> and control of the
neck, and steps in only when neither is making progress. Judging that moment
goes on through the whole bout, and it is a matter of feel.

<Photo id="muaythai-referee-clinch" />

The referee also has to tell a sweep from a throw. Taking an opponent off
balance from the clinch is legal. A hip throw of the kind
[judo](/sports/judo/) allows is a foul. The difference is between unbalancing
and lifting, and it is judged live.

## Two ways to score

Amateur judges use the <Term id="ten-point-must">ten-point must
system</Term>, round by round. Professional judges in Thailand look at the
bout as a whole and give the later rounds the most weight. They reward
dominance, balance and visible effect, and they do not count strikes.

So a fighter in Thailand can give away the first two rounds on purpose, find
the range, and win the fight in the fourth and fifth. Adding up rounds makes
that impossible. The same performance could produce one winner in Bangkok and
another at a world championship, and a fighter has to know which system is in
use.

Nothing in muaythai is scored by an instrument.
