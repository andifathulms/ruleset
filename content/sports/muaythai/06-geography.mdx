# Where it is played

Thailand, absolutely — and then a very large diffuse world that mostly trains
rather than fights.

**Thailand** is the sport in every sense that matters competitively. The
stadium system, the gyms, the fight economy and the overwhelming majority of
elite fighters are Thai, and a foreign fighter who wants to reach the top has
historically had to go there and fight under Thai conditions.

**Internationally**, muaythai has one of the widest footprints of any combat
sport. Russia, Ukraine, Belarus and Kazakhstan are strong in the amateur code;
so are Britain, France, Italy, Sweden, Turkey and Iran; Brazil, the United
States, Australia and Japan all have substantial scenes. IFMA's membership is
genuinely global.

The distinctive thing about muaythai's map is the ratio. The number of people
who train muaythai worldwide — in commercial gyms, for fitness, with no
intention of ever competing — is enormous relative to the number who fight, and
larger than for almost any combat sport covered here. The sport spread as a
training culture first and a competition second, which is the reverse of how
judo, taekwondo or wrestling travelled.

**Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar** have closely related codes of their own —
kun khmer, muay lao and lethwei, the last of which additionally permits
headbutts — and the question of what the shared ancestor should be called is a
live regional dispute rather than an academic one.
