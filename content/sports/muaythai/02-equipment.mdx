# Equipment evolution

Muaythai's equipment list tells you immediately which version of the sport you
are looking at.

**The professional Thai sport** uses very little: gloves, hand wraps, a
mouthguard and a groin guard. Shins are conditioned rather than protected,
elbows are bare, and the head is uncovered.

**The international amateur code** adds a headguard, shin guards, elbow pads
and, in some categories, body protection — and restricts or removes elbow
strikes at junior and lower grades entirely. That equipment exists so the sport
can satisfy the medical and eligibility requirements the multi-sport system
applies to a discipline with legal elbow strikes to the head.

The historical progression is the same one boxing made and worth setting beside
it. Hands bound in rope became gloves; unlimited contests became timed rounds;
open weight became categories. Boxing's amateur code then went further with
headguards, removed them for men in 2016 on the argument that they may not
reduce concussion risk, and kept them for women and juniors. Muaythai's amateur
code kept its headguards throughout. Two striking sports, the same evidence,
different conclusions — which is the pattern this site keeps finding whenever
two governing bodies face one question.

Two items are ceremonial and regulated anyway. The **mongkhon**, a headband worn
during the pre-fight ritual and removed before the bell, and the **prajioud**
armbands, worn through the bout. Both are part of the competition rules rather
than decoration.
