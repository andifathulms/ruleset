# Two kits and a headband

What a fighter wears shows which version of muaythai is being fought.

The professional sport in Thailand uses very little: gloves, hand wraps, a
mouthguard and a groin guard. The shins are hardened by training and left
unprotected. The elbows are bare and so is the head.

<Photo id="muaythai-hand-wraps" />

The <Term id="amateur-code">amateur code</Term> adds a headguard, shin
guards, elbow pads and, in some categories, a body protector. At junior and
lower grades it restricts the <Term id="elbow">elbow strike</Term> or removes
it. A sport with legal elbows to the head could not meet the medical and
eligibility requirements of multi-sport games without these changes.

<Photo id="muaythai-amateur-kit" />

The history runs beside [boxing's](/sports/boxing/). Rope became gloves,
open-ended fights became timed rounds, and open weight became categories.
Amateur boxing then adopted headguards, removed them for elite men in 2013 on
the argument that they did not reduce concussion, and kept them for women and
juniors. Amateur muaythai has kept its headguards throughout.

## The ceremonial kit

Two items are ceremonial, and the rules cover them anyway. The
<Term id="mongkhon">mongkhon</Term> is a headband worn for the ritual before
the fight and taken off before the bell. The
<Term id="prajioud">prajioud</Term> are armbands worn through the bout.
