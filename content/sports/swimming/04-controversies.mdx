# Three races that changed the rules

## Eight days in Rome

Rome, July 2009, the World Championships. Forty-three world records fell in
eight days. The sport normally sees a handful in a year. Paul Biedermann
beat Michael Phelps over 200 metres freestyle. Biedermann wore a polyurethane
suit and Phelps a textile one, and Biedermann said afterwards that the suit
was worth about two seconds. Nobody seriously argued that the swimming itself
had improved.

<Photo id="swimming-rome-2009-pool" />

The federation's congress met in Rome at the same championships and took two
decisions. The first was to [ban the suits from 1 January
2010](#fina-2010-textile-only-swimwear). Almost everyone agreed with that.
The second was to let the records stand. The federation's reasoning was that
the swims were legal under the rules of the day, and that cancelling them
would punish swimmers for a failure of the people who govern the sport.

[Athletics](/sports/athletics/) had faced the same choice with the javelin in
1986, and it restarted its record list.

The consequence can still be seen. Biedermann's [200 metres freestyle
record](#swimming-200-free-mens) from Rome, 1:42.00, has not been beaten.

<Photo id="swimming-biedermann-2009" />

The [100 metres freestyle](#swimming-100-free-mens) took years to recover.
César Cielo swam 46.91 in Rome. The record is now 46.40, swum by Pan Zhanle
of China at the Paris Olympics in 2024.

## A backstroke race with nobody in sight

Seoul, 1988, the Olympic 100 metres backstroke. David Berkoff swam roughly
the first third of the race underwater. A <Term id="dolphin-kick">dolphin
kick</Term> below the surface is faster than
<Term id="backstroke">backstroke</Term> on it, and the rules did not say how
far a swimmer could go.

The federation set a limit, first ten metres and later fifteen. [The
fifteen-metre rule dates from 1991](#fina-1991-backstroke-fifteen-metres).
The stated reason was the danger of swimmers holding their breath too long,
the same danger that had ended underwater breaststroke. Many people also
believe the federation acted because a race with no visible swimmers cannot
be watched or televised. The sources do not separate the two reasons.

## The kick nobody could see

Breaststroke allows only its own kick. Swimmers had begun to add a downward
dolphin kick as they left the wall. Underwater video showed that the kick was
routine. The officials stood at the surface and could not see it, so the few
swimmers who were disqualified were simply the ones who happened to be caught.

[In 2015 the federation allowed one such kick after the start and after each
turn](#fina-2015-breaststroke-dolphin-kick). It chose to change a rule it
could not enforce.
