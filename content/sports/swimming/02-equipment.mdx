# The suit

For most of the twentieth century a swimmer's equipment did not matter. The
suit covered the trunk because the rules said so. Goggles arrived in the
1970s. There was nothing that needed a rule.

<Photos ids="swimming-amsterdam-1928-start swimming-tsuruta-1928" />

That changed in 2000. Manufacturers had developed low-drag fabrics that
covered the whole body, and they asked for approval. [The federation allowed
them](#fina-2000-full-body-suits). It did so by removing the old restriction,
and it wrote no rule about how a suit could be made. For the next ten years
the suit changed faster than anything else in the sport.

The last step was polyurethane. Panels of it squeezed the body and trapped
air, so the swimmer floated higher and less of the body was in the water.

<Photos ids="swimming-lzr-nasa swimming-pellegrini-2009" />

Which suit a swimmer wore depended on the supplier their national federation
had signed with. A swimmer tied to a slower manufacturer was at a
disadvantage, with no choice in the matter. The federation said the suits had
turned the sport into a contest between manufacturers.

[The rule in force since 2010](#fina-2010-textile-only-swimwear) is the
specification that was missing in 2000. A suit must be made of textile. It
may not cover the neck or go past the shoulders, and it stops at the knee. A
man's suit may not go above the waist. The rule has held.
