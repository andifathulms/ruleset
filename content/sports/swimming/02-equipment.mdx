# Equipment evolution

For most of the twentieth century swimming had no equipment worth regulating.
Goggles arrived in the 1970s, caps were a convenience, and the suit covered the
trunk because the rules said so and nobody had a reason to want otherwise.

Then in 2000 FINA permitted full-body swimwear, and did it by removing a
restriction rather than by writing a specification. That distinction is the
whole story. Athletics, when it faced the javelin, wrote a tight construction
specification and then had to keep amending it. Swimming wrote nothing, and for
a decade the suit was the fastest-moving variable in the sport.

The end state was polyurethane: panels that compressed the body, trapped air,
and raised the swimmer in the water so that less of them was in it. At the 2009
World Championships in Rome, forty-three world records fell in eight days. Paul
Biedermann beat Michael Phelps over 200 m freestyle by nearly a second and said
afterwards that the suit was worth about two seconds. Nobody in the sport
seriously argued that the swimming had improved.

The 2010 rule is a construction specification arriving nine years late: textile
only, no coverage of the neck, nothing past the shoulders or ankles, men's suits
below the waist. It has held. What it did not do was touch the record book, and
the charts on this page are what that decision looks like fifteen years on.
