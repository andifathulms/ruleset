# Governing body politics

FINA — World Aquatics since 2023 — governs six aquatic disciplines through one
body, which gives it an unusual internal politics: swimming's commercial weight
subsidises the rest, and swimming's rule decisions are taken by a congress in
which the other disciplines vote.

The suit episode is the federation's largest modern governance failure and its
largest modern correction, and it is worth separating the two decisions taken in
Rome in July 2009. The first — ban the suits — was close to unanimous and is not
controversial. The second — let the records stand — was a separate judgement,
and it is the one this site exists to record.

The reasoning given was that the marks were set legally under the rules then in
force, and that retroactively annulling legal performances would punish athletes
for a failure of governance. It is a defensible position and it is the opposite
of the one World Athletics took over the javelin in 1986, where marks set
legally under the rules then in force were annulled precisely because the
governance had failed.

Both federations were right by their own lights. The difference is what each
thought a record was for: a ranking of legal performances, or a comparable
series. Swimming chose the first. Athletics chose the second.
