# Officiating and technology

Swimming is the most instrumented sport here and the least dependent on
officials for its result. **Touchpads** are triggered by the swimmer's own hand,
so the finish is recorded without a human in the loop at all, to one hundredth
of a second.

Where the sport stops trusting its instruments is instructive. Two swimmers on
the same hundredth are declared a dead heat and both awarded the place. Swimming
could resolve them by going to a thousandth, and it declines to — on the stated
grounds that a lane cannot be built accurately enough for a thousandth of a
second to mean anything. That is a governing body publicly setting the limit of
its own precision, which is rare enough to be worth recording.

Officials still decide everything the touchpad cannot. **Stroke judges** walk the
deck watching for illegal technique, **turn judges** stand at each end, and the
starter controls the start. Their subject is the stroke definitions, which are
rules rather than facts, so this is the one part of swimming that is judged
rather than measured.

**Underwater cameras** changed that part of the sport, and in one case changed
the rule instead of the enforcement. Breaststroke swimmers were adding a
downward dolphin kick off the walls; the cameras showed it was near universal;
surface officials could not see it. In 2015 the federation legalised a single
kick rather than continue disqualifying the swimmers it happened to catch.

That is the honest response to a rule that technology reveals to be
unenforceable — and it is the opposite of what most federations do, which is to
add more surveillance and keep the rule.
