# Who decides the result

The finish of a swimming race needs no official. Each swimmer stops the clock
by pressing a <Term id="touchpad">touchpad</Term> on the wall, and the time
is recorded to one hundredth of a second.

<Photo id="swimming-peaty-touchpad" />

The sport has set a limit on its own precision. Two swimmers with the same
time to the hundredth are declared a <Term id="dead-heat">dead heat</Term>,
and both are given the place. The clocks could separate them to a thousandth.
The federation does not use that figure, on the stated ground that a pool
cannot be built accurately enough for a thousandth of a second to mean
anything.

People still judge what a pad cannot. A referee runs the meeting. Stroke
judges walk along the pool and watch each swimmer's technique. Turn judges
stand at each end. Their subject is the definition of each
<Term id="stroke">stroke</Term>, and this is the one part of swimming that is
a matter of judgement.

Underwater cameras, now used at major meetings, changed what the judges
could be expected to see. In breaststroke the cameras showed a kick that the
officials on the deck were missing, and the federation
[changed the rule](#fina-2015-breaststroke-dolphin-kick) to match what it
could enforce.
