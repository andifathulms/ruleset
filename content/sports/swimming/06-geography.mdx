# Where it is swum

Nobody can learn to swim without water and a teacher, so the map of swimming
is a map of pools. Pools follow money and climate.

<Photos ids="swimming-dassel-pool swimming-manly-1936" />

The strongest nations are the United States, Australia, the countries of
western Europe, Japan and China. They share three things: many 50-metre
pools, schools or clubs that teach children to swim as a matter of course,
and enough coaches to turn those children into racers.

<Photos ids="swimming-duna-arena-2017 swimming-ledecky-2016" />

This makes swimming very different from [football](/sports/football/), which
almost anyone can play. Most of the world's people never get the chance to
train in a pool. The group that competes is small, well funded and stable.

The suits of the 2000s added a second inequality on top of the first. A
swimmer's suit depended on a national supplier contract, so swimmers were
unequal at the top of the sport as well as at the entrance to it. That was
part of the federation's stated case for [the 2010
rule](#fina-2010-textile-only-swimwear).
