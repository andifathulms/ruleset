# What is still argued about

**The 2009 records.** Several world records were set in suits that have been
illegal since 2010, and the federation let them stand. Proposals to cancel
them, or to keep two lists, have come up from time to time and gone nowhere.
One side says a record nobody can reach under today's rules has stopped being
a target. The other side says the swims were legal when they were made, and
that punishing the swimmers would be worse than living with the oddity.

**The fifteen metres.** The
<Term id="underwater-limit">fifteen-metre limit</Term> caps the fastest part
of a race at a number chosen by the rule makers. Swimmers who are exceptional
underwater are held back by it. Proposals to lengthen, shorten or remove the
limit keep returning. A longer limit would favour a different kind of body.
No limit would bring back the race the 1991 rule was written to prevent.

**Two pools, two lists.** A <Term id="short-course">short course</Term> pool
gives twice as many turns as a <Term id="long-course">long course</Term> one,
and a turn is faster than swimming. The sport has always kept separate
records for the two, so nobody has to compare a time from one with a time
from the other.
