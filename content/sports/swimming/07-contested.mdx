# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The 2009 records.** The single largest open question in the sport, and it has
been open for over fifteen years. Several world records were set in
polyurethane suits that have been illegal since 2010, and the federation chose
to let them stand. Periodic proposals to annul them, or to keep two lists, have
gone nowhere. The argument for annulment is that a record nobody can reach under
current rules is not a target but a wall; the argument against is that the marks
were legal when set, and that punishing athletes for a governance failure is
worse than living with the anomaly.

This site takes no position and notes only that World Athletics faced the same
choice with the javelin in 1986 and decided the other way. Both federations are
still living with their decision.

**Underwater distance.** Fifteen metres is an arbitrary number that caps the
fastest part of a race. Swimmers who are exceptional underwater are limited by
rule rather than by physiology, and proposals to extend, shorten or remove the
limit recur. Extending it would advantage a different body type; removing it
would return the sport to something closer to what the 1988 backstroke rule was
written to prevent.

**Short course and long course.** The sport maintains two record lists for the
same events because a 25-metre pool gives twice as many turns. It is a
comparability break that the sport handled correctly and in advance, by never
merging the series — which makes it the counter-example to almost everything
else on this site.
