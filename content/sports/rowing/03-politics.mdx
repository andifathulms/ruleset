# A fixed number of seats

An <Term id="eight">eight</Term> takes nine athletes to the Olympic Games for
one set of medals. Rowing is therefore costly in athlete places, and the Games
give it a fixed number of them. A new event can only come in if another goes
out.

## The lightweights

<Term id="lightweight">Lightweight</Term> rowing joined the Olympic programme
in 1996. Rowing favours very tall, heavy athletes, and the category was
brought in partly to open the sport to people of ordinary size.

Then the International Olympic Committee required equal numbers of men and
women, inside the same quota. In 2017 the federation
[dropped the men's lightweight four and added a women's coxless four](#worldrowing-2017-lightweight-cut)
for the 2020 Games. That left one lightweight event, the double sculls. It was
removed after 2024, and the category is now gone from the Games.

<Photo id="rowing-lightweight-four-2012" />

The federation's stated reason was the quota. Lightweight rowers had also been
criticised for years over the ways they cut weight before a race. Which reason
counted for more is still argued.

## A race that needs only a beach

A flat-water regatta needs a purpose-built course 2000 metres long with buoyed
lanes. Almost no host city has one, and it is expensive to build for two weeks
of racing.

<Term id="beach-sprint">Beach sprint</Term> rowing
[joins the Olympic programme in 2028](#worldrowing-2021-coastal-beach-sprint).
Crews race a short knockout course on the open sea, with a run on the beach.
It needs no course at all, only a beach.
