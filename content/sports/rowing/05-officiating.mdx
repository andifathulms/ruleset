# Officiating and technology

Rowing has the least officiating of any sport on this site, and it has designed
itself that way.

Crews race in separate lanes, cannot touch each other, and cannot affect each
other's boat except through the water they leave. So there is nothing for an
official to adjudicate except steering into another lane, equipment
compliance, and the start. Umpires follow the race by launch, and most races
require nothing from them at all.

The finish is fully instrumented — photo finish and electronic timing to a
hundredth — and the questions it answers are questions of fact.

What is worth recording here is what rowing has chosen *not* to measure. It
does not measure wind. It does not measure current or water temperature. It
does not invalidate a result for conditions or adjust for them.

Athletics measures wind and voids a record above 2.0 m/s, which is a way of
saying that conditions matter and can be thresholded. Rowing's position is that
its conditions matter and cannot be thresholded, so rather than pretend to
control them it changed what it claims the numbers mean.

That is a genuinely different relationship between a sport and its instruments.
Almost everything on this site is a story of federations measuring more, more
precisely, and then discovering that the measurements are not comparable.
Rowing measures the finish precisely and declines to make any claim about
comparability at all.
