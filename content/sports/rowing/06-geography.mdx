# Where it is rowed

Rowing is strong where there is flat water, money and a university or school
to pay for a boathouse.

Great Britain, Germany, the Netherlands, Romania, New Zealand, Australia,
Italy, the United States and China have been the leading nations for a long
time. Most of them share a club system, suitable water and backing from
institutions, very often schools and universities. In Britain and the United
States that link is also a link with social class, and it goes back to the
years when working men were barred from amateur racing.

The need for water cannot be bought off. A beginner needs flat, sheltered
water close to home, a club and a club's boat, because a racing shell costs
too much for most people to own. A town without a river or a lake does not
produce rowers. [Swimming](/sports/swimming/) has a similar problem with
pools, but a pool can be built.

<Photo id="rowing-sea-forest" />

Romania shows that a small base can be enough. So do several African and Asian
nations more recently. Their programmes are usually built around one good
stretch of water.

Beach sprint rowing may change the map, because far more countries have a
beach than have a 2000-metre course. The rowing machine has already changed
who rows. It has brought in very large numbers of people. It has not changed
which nations win on the water, because rowing on a machine is a different
activity.
