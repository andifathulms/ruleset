# Where it is played

Rowing's map is a map of water, money and universities, in that order.

Great Britain, Germany, the Netherlands, Romania, New Zealand, Australia,
Italy, the United States and China are the sustained powers. What most of them
share is a club structure, suitable water, and institutional sponsorship —
frequently educational. Rowing is the sport on this site most closely tied to
universities and schools, and in Britain and the United States that tie is also
a class one with a documented history.

The water requirement is absolute. A country without flat, sheltered,
navigable water within reach of its population does not produce rowers, and no
amount of investment changes that. It is the same barrier swimming has with
pools, except that a pool can be built and a river cannot.

Romania and, more recently, several African and Asian nations are the
counter-examples worth recording: sustained national programmes producing
international crews from small bases, usually built around a single suitable
venue.

Beach sprint rowing, arriving in 2028, is a deliberate attempt to change this
map. A beach is far more widely distributed than a 2000-metre buoyed flatwater
course, and the format was designed with that in mind. Whether it broadens the
sport or simply gives existing rowing nations another event is the open
question.

The indoor rowing machine has already changed participation enormously without
changing the competitive map at all, because indoor rowing is a different
activity that happens to use the same muscles.
