# Equipment evolution

Baseball's equipment is regulated tightly at the professional level and loosely
everywhere else, and the sport is unusually relaxed about the consequences.

**The bat.** Professional baseball requires a single piece of solid wood.
College and amateur baseball permit aluminium and composite bats, which are
lighter, more forgiving and produce faster exit velocities — and the sport does
not pretend the statistics travel between the two. A college batting average
and a professional one are not compared, and nobody proposes that they should
be. That is the correct handling of a comparability problem, arrived at without
anyone calling it one.

**The ball.** The recurring controversy that has never become a rule change.
Home run rates have moved sharply between seasons in ways that correlate with
changes in the ball's construction and aerodynamic behaviour, and the sport has
been accused repeatedly of altering it, deliberately or otherwise. The
specification — weight, circumference, construction — has not changed. Whether
what is made to that specification has, is the argument.

That is the exact inverse of every other equipment story on this site. In
athletics and swimming, the specification was outrun and had to be rewritten.
In baseball, the specification has held and the object may have moved inside it.

**The glove** grew steadily through the twentieth century and is now specified
by size. **Protective equipment** followed injury: batting helmets became
mandatory in the 1970s, and helmets with extended jaw protection followed later
incidents.
