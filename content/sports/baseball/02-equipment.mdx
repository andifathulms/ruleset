# Bat, ball and glove

A professional baseball bat is one piece of solid wood. That one rule divides
the sport in two.

**The bat.** College and amateur baseball allow aluminium and composite bats.
They are lighter and more forgiving, and the ball comes off them faster. The
amateur game is easier to hit in, and everybody knows it. A college batting
average is not compared with a professional one.

**The ball.** The rules fix the ball's weight, its circumference and how it is
built, and that specification has not changed. The number of
<Term id="home-run">home runs</Term> has still moved sharply from one season
to the next, and the movement follows changes in how the ball is made and how
it flies. The argument is about what happens inside the specification: how
tightly the ball is wound, and how high the seams stand. The sport has been
accused more than once of changing the ball, on purpose or by accident.

In [athletics](/sports/athletics/) and [swimming](/sports/swimming/) the
equipment got ahead of the rules and the rules had to be rewritten. In
baseball the rule has stayed the same and the ball may have changed inside
it.

**The glove.** The glove of the 1880s was small and thinly padded. Gloves
grew steadily through the twentieth century, until the rules had to set a
size.

<Photo id="baseball-bushong-1888" />

**Protection.** Protective equipment followed injuries. Batting helmets became
compulsory in the 1970s. Helmets with a longer guard for the jaw came after
later incidents.
