# Where it is played

Baseball is intensely popular in a small number of places and almost absent
from the rest of the world, which puts it alongside cricket as one of the two
most geographically concentrated sports here — and the two maps do not overlap
at all.

The United States, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Cuba, the Dominican Republic,
Venezuela, Puerto Rico, Mexico, Panama and Nicaragua account for essentially
all of it. That distribution follows American military and commercial presence
in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries almost exactly, in the same way
cricket's follows the British Empire.

The Caribbean and Central American nations are the most productive relative to
population by a wide margin, and the pathway there runs through academies
funded by American professional clubs — a recruitment structure with no
parallel in any other sport on this site, and one with a documented history of
abuses that the league has regulated only recently.

Japan's is the other case worth recording, because it is the one place where a
professional structure developed independently and produced a genuinely
different version of the sport: different ball, different strike zone in
practice, and a distinct playing culture. Nippon Professional Baseball is not
a feeder league.

The Olympic consequence is awkward. Baseball has been on and off the programme
repeatedly, and the reason it keeps being dropped is that its best players are
under contract to a league that will not release them mid-season. A sport
whose rules are made by a league also has its Olympic participation decided by
one.
