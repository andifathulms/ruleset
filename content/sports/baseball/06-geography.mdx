# Where it is played

Baseball is loved in a small number of countries and hardly played in the
rest.

The United States, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Cuba, the Dominican Republic,
Venezuela, Puerto Rico, Mexico, Panama and Nicaragua account for almost all of
it. The list follows the places where the United States had soldiers and
business in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
[Cricket](/sports/cricket/) follows the British Empire in the same way, and
the two maps do not overlap at all.

## Japan

Baseball reached Japan in the 1870s, and Koshien Stadium opened in 1924. A
professional game grew up there on its own. Japan plays with a different ball
and, in practice, a different strike zone, and it has its own playing culture.
Nippon Professional Baseball is not a feeder league for the United States.

## The Caribbean

The countries of the Caribbean and Central America produce the most players
for their population, by a wide margin. The path runs through academies paid
for by American professional clubs. That system has a documented history of
abuses, and the league has regulated it only recently.

## The Olympic problem

Baseball has gone on and off the Olympic programme several times. The best
players are under contract to a league that will not release them in the
middle of its season. The World Baseball Softball Confederation runs the
Olympic tournament, but the league decides whether its players go. The
professionals do play in the World Baseball Classic, so that tournament is
where the best players meet.
