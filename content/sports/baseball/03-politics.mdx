# Governing body politics

Baseball's rules are made by a league, not a federation, and that is the
structural fact behind everything on this page.

The WBSC governs internationally and runs the Olympic tournament. Major League
Baseball governs itself, and every rule change recorded here — the mound, the
designated hitter, replay, the pitch clock — was an MLB decision. This is
basketball's arrangement taken further: at least FIBA and the NBA converge over
time, whereas baseball's international body largely follows the league.

The internal split is the more remarkable governance fact. From 1973 to 2022,
the American League used a designated hitter and the National League did not.
One sport, one championship, two batting rulebooks, for forty-nine years —
including in the World Series, where the rule in force depended on which park
the game was in.

No other sport on this site has done anything comparable. It persisted because
the two leagues had separate identities and separate administrations long after
they had become one competition, and because each side regarded the other's
rule as a departure from what baseball was.

The other permanent feature is labour. Baseball has the most organised player
body in American sport and the most contested history of stoppages, and rule
changes go through collective bargaining. The 2023 pitch clock was negotiated
rather than imposed, which is why it arrived as a package with the shift
restriction and larger bases.
