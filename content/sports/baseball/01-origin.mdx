# Origin and invention

Baseball's origin story was invented, and the invention is more interesting
than the truth.

The Doubleday myth — that Abner Doubleday devised the game at Cooperstown in
1839 — was manufactured by a commission set up in 1905 specifically to
establish that baseball was American rather than descended from English
bat-and-ball games. It was not true. Baseball descends from rounders and
similar games, and the first substantial codification is the Knickerbocker
rules of 1845 in New York.

What the myth tells you is that this sport has always understood itself as a
national institution first and a game second, which is why its rule changes are
argued about in a register no other sport here uses.

The genuinely inventive parts arrived over the following decades and are all
about geometry. Ninety feet between bases is the number the sport is proudest
of, because it makes a routine ground ball an almost exactly even race between
runner and throw. Sixty feet six inches from mound to plate was settled in 1893
and has not moved since.

The National League was founded in 1876 and the American League in 1901, and
the two ran as rivals before merging into one competition — a structure that
would matter enormously seventy years later, when one of them adopted a batting
rule the other refused for forty-nine years.
