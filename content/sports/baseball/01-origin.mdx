# Where it came from

Baseball has two origin stories, and the famous one is false.

## The story that was made up

The famous story says that Abner Doubleday invented the game at Cooperstown,
New York, in 1839. Doubleday was a Union general. He died in 1893 and never
claimed to have invented anything.

The story came from a commission set up in 1905. Its job was to show that
baseball was an American game and did not come from English bat-and-ball
games. In 1907, fourteen years after Doubleday's death, it named him as the
inventor.

Cooperstown still has a ballpark named after him, and the Hall of Fame stands
in the same small town. They show how much the sport wanted the story to be
true.

## What happened

Baseball comes from rounders and similar games. The first substantial set of
written rules is the Knickerbocker rules, drawn up by a New York club in 1845.

The measurements came over the next decades. The bases are ninety feet apart.
At that distance a routine ground ball is an almost even race between the
<Term id="batter">batter</Term> running to first base and the fielder's throw.
The distance from the <Term id="pitcher">pitcher</Term> to
<Term id="home-plate">home plate</Term> was set at sixty feet six inches in
1893 and has not moved since.

The National League was founded in 1876 and the American League in 1901. They
began as rivals and later joined in one competition. Each kept its own
identity and its own administration for a long time afterwards, and for
forty-nine years they even
[batted under different rules](#mlb-1973-designated-hitter).
