# Governing body politics

Kurash's politics are the politics of a state making a sport, and doing it
successfully.

The International Kurash Association was formed in Tashkent in 1998, around a
rulebook produced by one organiser, with Uzbek institutional backing. The
objective was explicit: take a national wrestling tradition and establish it
internationally, with a federation, a competitive calendar, and eventually
multi-sport programme places.

The campaign delivered. Kurash spread through Central Asia, into Iran, the
Middle East, South and Southeast Asia and parts of Africa and Europe, and was
contested at the Asian Games in 2018 — twenty years from codification to a
continental Games, which is faster than any other sport covered here has
managed.

That speed is the interesting political fact. This site records the Asian Games
admitting kabaddi, sepak takraw, wushu and soft tennis together in 1990 —
sports with deep regional participation and long histories. Kurash's modern
code was younger than most of its athletes when it was admitted.

The comparison with wushu is instructive and imperfect. Both are state-backed
codifications of a national martial tradition, pursued for international
recognition. Wushu's campaign has run since 1990 with enormous Chinese backing
and has produced Asian Games inclusion, a Youth Olympic appearance, a
tournament held inside the 2008 Games and no Olympic place. Kurash, from a far
smaller state, has an Asian Games place and no realistic prospect of more.

What it does not have is a route past that. There is no Olympic path for a
third jacket-wrestling sport when judo already occupies the category.
