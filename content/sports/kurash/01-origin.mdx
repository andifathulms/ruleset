# An old tradition and a rulebook from 1998

Kurash is very old as a custom and very young as a sport.

Standing wrestling in jackets has been part of festivals and celebrations in
Central Asia for a very long time. Uzbek accounts say kurash is thousands of
years old. Related traditions are found across the region and the Caucasus
under many names. Most have the same form: two people in jackets and belts,
standing, each trying to throw the other.

<Photos ids="kurash-bukhara-2011 kurash-crimea-1920s" />

Uzbekistan presents the tradition as ancient and national. A carpet in the
Museum of Olympic Glory in Tashkent shows Pahlavon Mahmud, a wrestler of the
thirteenth century, gripping a belt.

## The rulebook

The modern sport has an exact date. [In 1998 Komil Yusupov produced an
international rulebook](#kurash-1998-ika). It set weight categories, the
length of a bout, a matted contest area, and three calls:
<Term id="halal">halal</Term>, <Term id="chala">chala</Term> and
<Term id="tanbeh">tanbeh</Term>. The International Kurash Association was
formed in Tashkent in the same year.

The rulebook made one choice above all. [Kurash is fought standing, and only
standing](#kurash-standing-only). There is no groundwork, no submission and no
hold below the waist. The festival tradition had been a standing contest, and
the code recorded that. It did not borrow the ground fighting of the
international jacket sports.

<Photo id="kurash-aimag-2017" />

[Judo](/sports/judo/) reached part of the same position much later and by
changing its rules: it restricted leg grabs from 2010 and banned them in 2013.
Kurash never had them.

## A national project

The code was part of a plan. Uzbekistan had been independent for seven years.
It set out to turn a home tradition into an international sport, with a
federation, a calendar and, in time, places at multi-sport games. Eight years
after the code was written, kurash was on the country's postage stamps.

<Photo id="kurash-stamp-2006" />

China had done something similar with [wushu](/sports/wushu/) from the 1950s.
Uzbekistan is a far smaller state, and it moved faster. Kurash spread through
Central Asia and into Iran, the Middle East, South and Southeast Asia, and
parts of Africa and Europe. [In 2018 it was contested at the Asian Games in
Jakarta](#kurash-2018-asian-games), twenty years after the rulebook, and it
has been at the Asian Games since.
