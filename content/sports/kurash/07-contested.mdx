# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether there is room for it.** Judo occupies the Olympic jacket-wrestling
category and has done since 1964. Kurash has an Asian Games place and no
plausible route past it, and the sport's institutional ambition runs directly
into an incumbent that is doing much of what kurash does.

**One country's sport.** Uzbekistan codified it, funds it, hosts its
institutions and wins most of it. The federation's development work is real and
the competitive concentration has not changed much.

**How old the sport is.** The tradition is ancient and the rulebook is from
1998. Kurash's own literature tends to present the two as one thing, and this
site separates them — which is a disagreement about history rather than about
rules, and it matters for how the sport presents itself.

**Whether the codification represents the tradition.** A single organiser wrote
the modern rules with state backing. Practitioners of the regional wrestling
traditions kurash draws on have questioned how much of them survived, which is
the same argument traditional Chinese martial artists have had with wushu for
seventy years.

**The narrowness.** Standing only, no submissions, no leg grabs. It makes the
sport fast, decisive and easy to understand, and it means a competitor who
loses the grip battle has nothing else to try. Whether that is elegance or
thinness is argued from outside the sport more than within it.
