# What is still argued about

**How old the sport is.** The tradition is ancient, and the rulebook dates
from 1998. Kurash's own literature tends to present the two as one thing. The
claim of thousands of years is plausible for the tradition. It cannot be true
of the rulebook.

**Whether the code represents the tradition.** One organiser wrote the modern
rules, with the backing of the state. Practitioners of the regional wrestling
styles that kurash draws on have asked how much of those styles survived.
Traditional Chinese martial artists have said the same about
[wushu](/sports/wushu/) since the 1950s.

**One country's sport.** Uzbekistan wrote the code, pays for the sport, hosts
its institutions and wins most of the medals. The federation's development
work is real. The concentration has not changed much.

**Whether there is room for it.** [Judo](/sports/judo/) has held the Olympic
place for jacket wrestling since 1964. Kurash has a place at the Asian Games
and no clear way beyond it.

**How narrow it is.** Standing only, no submissions, no leg grabs. This makes
kurash fast, decisive and easy to follow. It also means a wrestler who loses
the fight for a <Term id="grip">grip</Term> has nothing else to try. Some call
that elegant and others call it thin, and the argument comes more from outside
the sport than from inside.
