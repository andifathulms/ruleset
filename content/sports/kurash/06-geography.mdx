# Where it is played

Central Asia, and a spread that follows a campaign rather than a diaspora.

**Uzbekistan** is the sport's home, its institutional base and its dominant
competitive nation. Kurash is a national sport there, with school and club
structures, state support and by far the deepest competitive field.

Around it, **Kazakhstan**, **Tajikistan**, **Kyrgyzstan**, **Turkmenistan**,
**Iran**, **Turkey**, **Azerbaijan** and **Mongolia** all compete strongly, and
several of them have their own closely related jacket-wrestling traditions that
transfer almost directly.

Beyond that core, kurash has established federations across **South and
Southeast Asia** — India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Iran, Thailand, Vietnam — and
in parts of **Africa** and **Europe**, largely through the association's
development work and through competitors crossing over from judo and belt
wrestling.

Two things distinguish this map from most on this site. It is not a diaspora
map — fistball follows German emigration, boules follows French administration,
soft tennis follows Japanese rule. Kurash's spread is the product of a
deliberate, funded, twenty-five-year federation campaign, which makes it one of
the more successful development programmes covered here.

And it is dense where related traditions already exist. Like sumo, kurash
travels well into countries with their own heavy grappling culture and hardly
at all into countries without one.
