# Where it is played

Uzbekistan is the home of kurash and its strongest nation by far. It is a
national sport there, with schools, clubs and state support, and Uzbek
wrestlers win most of what there is to win.

## The neighbours

Kazakhstan, Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan, Turkmenistan, Iran, Turkey, Azerbaijan and
Mongolia all compete strongly. Several of them have jacket or belt wrestling
of their own, and those skills carry over almost directly. The Tatar and
Bashkir peoples, for example, wrestle with belts at their Sabantuy festivals.

## Further out

Kurash also has federations in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Thailand and
Vietnam, and in parts of Africa and Europe. It arrived there mostly through
the association's development work, and through athletes crossing over from
judo and belt wrestling.

<Photos ids="kurash-congo-2026 kurash-congo-medals" />

Many sports travelled with people. [Fistball](/sports/fistball/) followed
German emigrants, [boules](/sports/boules/) followed French administration,
and [soft tennis](/sports/soft-tennis/) followed Japanese rule. Kurash
travelled through a planned and funded campaign by its federation, about
twenty-five years long.

Like [sumo](/sports/sumo/), it settles best in countries that already have a
grappling culture, and hardly at all in countries without one.
