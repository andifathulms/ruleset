# One call that ends the bout

A kurash bout has a referee on the mat and side judges. Their job is simpler
than in other jacket sports, because there is no ground fighting to watch.

## The throw

The main call is the throw. Did the opponent land squarely on the back, with
force and control? Then it is <Term id="halal">halal</Term> and the bout is
over. A partial landing, or a throw without full control, is
<Term id="chala">chala</Term>. The referee must judge the position of a body
and the control of a throw in a fraction of a second, and the answer can end
the contest.

## Hands, feet and effort

A <Term id="grip">grip</Term> below the waist is illegal. The line is clear
and easy to see, so the referee watches the hands.

A wrestler who avoids the fight has nowhere to hide, because there is no
ground phase to retreat into. The referee gives a
<Term id="tanbeh">tanbeh</Term> for <Term id="passivity">passivity</Term>, and
that keeps a bout from stalling.

## The restart

The most frequent thing a kurash referee does is stop the bout. Each time the
wrestlers go to the ground, the referee halts them and brings them back to
standing. In [judo](/sports/judo/) the same moment starts the ground fight.

Everything is judged by eye. No technology is used, and the call that matters
most is usually final.
