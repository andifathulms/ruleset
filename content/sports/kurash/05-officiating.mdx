# Officiating

Fast decisions, taken often, in a sport engineered to end abruptly.

A mat referee with side judges, and a much simpler adjudication problem than
the neighbouring jacket sports have — because two-thirds of what those
officials watch for does not exist here.

**The throw.** The central call. Did the opponent land squarely on the back,
with force and control? That is **halal**, and the bout is over instantly. A
partial landing or incomplete control is **chala**. The distinction is a
judgement about a body's orientation and a throw's control, made in a fraction
of a second, and it ends contests — so it carries more weight per call than
almost any judgement in the other grappling sports.

**Grips.** Anything below the waist is illegal, which is a bright line and
easily seen. The trousers are out of bounds, the legs are out of bounds, and
the referee is watching hands.

**Passivity.** With no ground phase to retreat into, a competitor avoiding
engagement has few places to go, and **tanbeh** penalties for passivity are the
mechanism that stops a bout stalling.

**The reset.** Whenever action reaches the ground, the referee stops it and
returns both competitors to standing. This happens constantly and is the most
frequent thing an official does — in judo the equivalent moment starts a new
phase, and here it ends one.

Nothing is instrumented. The sport is judged by eye, quickly, and the
consequential call is usually final.
