# Controversies that forced rule changes

Kurash's legislative history is short by construction — twenty-five years — and
its interesting arguments are about what the codification chose rather than
about what has changed since.

**Excluding the ground.** The founding decision. Kurash could have imported the
ground phase that international jacket wrestling had developed elsewhere and
chose not to, on the basis that the tradition being codified was a standing
contest. That keeps bouts short and decisive, and it means the sport has one
phase and no fallback.

**Excluding the legs.** No grip below the waist, and no leg attacks. Judo
reached the same restriction by legislation, trialling it from 2010 and banning
leg grabs outright in 2013, explicitly to stop judo looking like wrestling.
Kurash never had them to remove, and the comparison is the most useful thing
about the sport for this site.

**Whether one person can write a sport.** The modern code was produced by a
single organiser with state backing in 1998. That is an unusually concentrated
origin, and practitioners of the regional traditions kurash draws on have
questioned how representative the codified version is — the same objection
traditional Chinese martial artists have made about wushu since the 1950s.

**Antiquity claims.** The sport asserts a history of thousands of years, which
is plausible for the tradition and not for the rulebook. This site records the
codification date and describes the tradition separately, because conflating
them is how a sport's own literature becomes its history.

**Whether it can grow past the region.** Not a rule change and the sport's
central question.
