# Hoops barely wider than the ball

The <Term id="hoop">hoop</Term> is where croquet's difficulty lives.

A garden set has thin wire hoops much wider than the ball, and getting through
one is a formality. A championship hoop is cast iron, set firmly in ground
prepared so that it will not move. The gap left over when the ball passes
through is a fraction of a millimetre. Running one from any distance, or from
an angle, is a real shot.

<Photos ids="croquet-garden-set croquet-championship-equipment" />

That one measurement is most of the difference between the party game and the
sport. [Billiards](/sports/billiards/) has the same gap between a pub pool
table and a competition snooker table.

The <Term id="mallet">mallet</Term> has moved from wood to composite shafts
with heads of dense material. Either face may strike the ball. Head length,
weight and balance are a personal choice.

The balls are milled and matched so that all four behave alike. That matters
in a game where a player uses the other three balls as tools.

The <Term id="lawn">lawn</Term> is the largest piece of equipment and the real
barrier. It must be level and mown to an even speed. That takes a
groundskeeper, and money alone will not buy it.
