# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The break, and what it does to the opponent.** Croquet's founding problem.
Once a player can construct a break, the other player may sit out most of the
game, and the sport becomes an exhibition rather than a contest. Every
structural response croquet has made comes from this — the bisque handicap, the
time limit, and eventually a whole second code.

**The bisque.** The handicap answer, and unusually powerful because the
recipient chooses when to spend it. It is the reason croquet clubs can run
competitive tournaments across enormous ability ranges, and it means a
handicap result and a level result are different kinds of statement.

**Losing the lawns.** Not a rule change and the most consequential thing that
ever happened to the sport. The club founded for croquet at Wimbledon took up
lawn tennis in 1877 and was taken over by it, and croquet's competitive
constituency in England went with the grass.

**Golf croquet.** A code created by removing the break, given a world
championship in 1996, and now larger than the code it was derived from. Whether
that is croquet's salvation or its dilution is argued in club bars and settled
by participation numbers.

**Hoop tolerance.** Championship hoops are set with a clearance over the ball
measured in fractions of a millimetre, and exactly how tight they should be is
a recurring dispute — tighter hoops reward precision and lengthen games, looser
ones do the reverse.
