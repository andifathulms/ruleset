# The turn that can last a whole game

In <Term id="association">association croquet</Term> a good player can run the
whole course in one turn. The other player may sit and watch for most of the
game. Each large change the sport has made is an answer to that problem.

## The bisque

[The first answer was a handicap](#croquet-bisque-handicap). The weaker side
receives <Term id="bisque">bisques</Term>, free extra turns worked out from
the gap between the two players' handicaps. The player chooses when to take
one. The best moment is just after a break has gone wrong, when a second
chance is worth most.

That choice makes the bisque stronger than the handicap strokes of
[golf](/sports/golf/) or the goals given in [polo](/sports/polo/). It lets
clubs run real tournaments between players of very different standards. A
beginner can play an honest match against a champion.

A time limit is the second answer. A championship game can run for hours, and
with a limit the higher score wins when time is up.

## A second game

The third answer was to take the break out altogether. In
<Term id="golf-croquet">golf croquet</Term> each turn is one stroke and all
four balls go for the same hoop. There are no roquets, no
<Term id="croquet-stroke">croquet strokes</Term> and no breaks. Both players
are certain to play, and a newcomer understands the game at once.

[The federation gave golf croquet a world championship in
1996](#croquet-1996-golf-croquet-worlds). It has since become the larger game
worldwide by number of players. Egypt is the clearest case. Egyptian players
are among the strongest in the world at golf croquet, the form the English
clubs had thought the lesser one.

The federation runs both games and nobody proposes choosing between them. The
old powers are strongest in the harder game, and the growth is in the simpler
one. Whether golf croquet has saved the sport or thinned it is still argued in
the clubs.
