# Where it is played

Croquet belongs to the old clubs of the English-speaking world, and to Egypt.

England, Australia, New Zealand and the United States are the historic
competitive nations. The MacRobertson Shield is played between those four and
is the most prestigious event in association croquet. Their clubs are old and
well organised, and in most places the members are ageing.

Egypt is the exception. Egyptian players dominate golf croquet at world level.
They come out of a deep domestic game centred on the clubs of Cairo.

Croquet is also played in Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Canada, South Africa,
Japan, Spain, Italy and Latvia, mostly in small clubs.

What limits it is the lawn. Croquet needs level, closely mown grass kept at an
even speed. So the sport exists where a club owns ground and looks after it,
and almost nowhere else.

Gateball is a team game that grew out of croquet in Japan in the 1940s. It has
far more players across East Asia than croquet has in the whole world, and it
is governed quite separately.
