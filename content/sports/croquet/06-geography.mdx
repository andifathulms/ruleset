# Where it is played

The old English-speaking clubs, and Egypt.

**England**, **Australia**, **New Zealand** and **the United States** are
croquet's historic competitive nations, and the MacRobertson Shield — contested
between those four — remains the most prestigious event in association croquet.
Club structures in all four are long-established, well organised and, in most
places, ageing.

**Egypt** is the outlier and the most interesting thing in the sport's modern
geography. Egyptian players dominate **golf croquet** at world level, from a
deep domestic structure centred on Cairo clubs, and they arrived in the code
the traditional powers had regarded as the simpler one. It is the same shape of
story this site records for Colombia in ultimate, finswimming and roller speed
skating: a country concentrating on a sport nobody else was funding, and
winning it.

Beyond those, croquet is played in **Ireland**, **Scotland**, **Wales**,
**Canada**, **South Africa**, **Japan**, **Spain**, **Italy** and **Latvia**,
generally in small club numbers.

The constraint is the lawn. Croquet needs level, closely mown grass maintained
to a consistent speed, which is a groundskeeping commitment rather than a
purchase — so the sport exists where a club owns and maintains ground, and
almost nowhere else.

**Gateball**, the Japanese team derivative from the 1940s, has a participation
base across East Asia far larger than croquet's worldwide, and is governed
entirely separately.
