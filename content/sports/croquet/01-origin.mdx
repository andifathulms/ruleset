# Origin and invention

Croquet's founding club became Wimbledon, and tennis took the lawns.

The game arrived in England in the 1850s, probably from Ireland, and spread
with extraordinary speed — a lawn game that could be played by men and women
together, in ordinary clothes, on a garden lawn, at a moment when very little
else could. Manufacturers sold sets by the hundred thousand.

Competition followed quickly. The first championship was held at
Moreton-in-Marsh in 1867, and the **All England Croquet Club** was founded in
1868 at Wimbledon to run the sport properly.

Nine years later that club added lawn tennis to its name and held the first
Wimbledon championship on its croquet lawns. Tennis was faster, more physical
and more appealing to the club's younger members, and it displaced croquet so
completely that the club dropped croquet from its title within a few years —
later restoring it as a courtesy. The most famous tennis venue in the world is a
croquet club that was taken over by a sport it let in.

Croquet was contested at the 1900 Paris Olympics, almost entirely by French
entrants, with **women among the competitors** — it is one of the first Olympic
sports in which women took part. It has never been contested since, and the
tournament is best remembered for having reportedly drawn a single paying
spectator.

The World Croquet Federation was formed in 1986, and in 1996 established a
world championship for **golf croquet** — a code that removes the break
entirely, and which has since become the larger game.
