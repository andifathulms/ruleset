# Calling a fault against yourself

Ordinary croquet has no referee. Players call their own
<Term id="fault">faults</Term>.

The reason is practical. Most faults can be seen only by the person who
commits them. Did the mallet push the ball or strike it cleanly? Did both
balls move in a croquet stroke? These things happen at the feet of the
<Term id="striker">striker</Term>, and nobody else has the angle.

So the laws put the duty on the striker, and the sport's manners about it are
strict. A player who is winning is expected to call a fault against
themselves, and nobody treats it as generous.

A referee is there for players who ask. Before a difficult stroke, such as a
cramped shot near a hoop, the striker may call a referee over to watch it.
The official is sent for in advance, to watch one stroke. A disputed stroke or
a question of law also goes to a referee. At a tournament the tournament
referee's ruling stands.

[Boules](/sports/boules/) also leaves its main decision to the players. There
the question is which ball is nearer, and a tape answers it.
