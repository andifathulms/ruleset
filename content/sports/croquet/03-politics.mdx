# Governing body politics

A federation formed in 1986 for a sport that had already lost its century, now
governing two codes of which the newer one is winning.

The World Croquet Federation exists to hold world championships and to
coordinate national associations, principally in England, Australia, New
Zealand, the United States and — increasingly and decisively — Egypt. It has
no Olympic ambition worth the name; croquet was contested once, in 1900, and
nothing about the sport's scale suggests a route back.

Its interesting institutional question is internal. **Association croquet** is
the historic championship code and it is difficult: the break means a good
player can finish the game without the opponent playing, which is thrilling to
watch if you understand it and dispiriting to be on the wrong end of. **Golf
croquet** removed the break and guarantees both players a stroke, and it has
grown into the larger game worldwide.

Egypt is the clearest evidence. Egyptian players dominate golf croquet at world
level, from a national structure with real depth, and their strength is in the
code the English clubs regarded as the lesser one.

So the federation is in the position several bodies on this site occupy: the
form it was built around is not the form that is growing. Roller sports had it
with skateboarding, water ski with cable riding, and croquet has it with a code
that succeeded by subtracting the sport's most distinctive feature.

There is no professional structure in either code anywhere in the world.
