# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether anyone can watch.** The sports' central problem, and harder than the
equivalent for orienteering or air sports because there is no moment when the
action comes to the spectator. Underwater cameras and streaming help and do not
solve it, and no rule change reaches it.

**Deep pools.** Underwater rugby needs at least three and a half metres and new
pools are built shallower. The sport's growth is limited by decisions made by
municipal planners for reasons that have nothing to do with it.

**Contact and air.** The line between contesting a ball at depth and preventing
an opponent surfacing is the sport's most consequential judgement, rewritten
repeatedly, and it will keep being rewritten because the underlying tension is
permanent.

**Officiating coverage.** Referees on scuba still cannot see a whole volume.
More officials, better positioning and surface support are the answers so far,
and none is complete.

**Spearfishing under the same federation.** Competitive spearfishing is
restricted or banned in several countries on conservation grounds and is
governed by the same body that runs marine environmental programmes. The
objection comes from outside the sport, and the federation has not resolved it.

**Whether the team sports are a priority.** They sit inside a federation whose
larger business is diving standards and technical work, alongside finswimming,
freediving, orienteering and photography. What share of a small organisation's
attention two invisible team sports should get is a genuine internal question.
