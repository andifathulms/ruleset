# Officiating

The only officials on this site who are equipped better than the athletes.

Referees work **in the water, on scuba**, moving with play. A surface official
cannot see a game played on the floor of a pool, and in underwater rugby cannot
see most of the field at all — so the sport put its officials into the volume,
carrying the breathing apparatus the competitors are forbidden.

Even that does not give full coverage. Underwater rugby is contested through a
three-dimensional space with bodies obscuring each other in every plane, and
multiple in-water referees plus surface officials are used to close the gaps.
It is the hardest sport covered here to see completely while it is happening.

The calls themselves divide into two kinds:

**Ordinary fouls.** Attacking a player who does not have the ball, obstruction,
illegal use of the stick in hockey, holding without contesting.

**Air offences.** Anything that stops an opponent reaching the surface —
holding them down, pulling them back, interfering with mask or snorkel. These
are treated with a severity that has no equivalent in another sport's foul
list, because the consequence is not a disadvantage but a drowning risk.

A distinctive practical problem: an official cannot blow a whistle
intelligibly under water, so play is stopped with signals and with acoustic
devices, and communication between in-water and surface officials is part of
the competition's design rather than an afterthought.
