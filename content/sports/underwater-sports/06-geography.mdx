# Where the deep pools are

Diving clubs carried both games, and pools decide where they can live.

Underwater hockey is strong in Great Britain, where it was invented, and
stronger still in New Zealand, Australia, South Africa, France, Colombia,
Turkey and Canada. New Zealand and Australia have dominated for long periods.
The game from Southsea is now won on the other side of the world.

<Photo id="underwater-sports-stellenbosch-2014" />

Underwater rugby is almost wholly a northern European game. Germany invented
it, and Norway, Sweden, Denmark and Finland are powers. Colombia is the one
outsider. Colombia is also strong in
[flying disc](/sports/flying-disc/), [finswimming](/sports/finswimming/) and
[roller speed skating](/sports/roller-sports/): a small, focused national
programme in a sport that few others fund heavily.

<Photos ids="underwater-sports-rugby-finland underwater-sports-rugby-germany" />

Both games are limited by pools, and rugby most of all. It needs water at
least three and a half metres deep. For decades new public pools have been
built shallower, because a shallow pool is cheaper to build, cheaper to heat
and better for swimming lessons. A country's underwater rugby depends on how
many deep pools it happens to have from earlier times.

Most sports spread where somebody invested, or where emigrants took them.
Underwater rugby spreads where the twentieth century left deep water indoors.
