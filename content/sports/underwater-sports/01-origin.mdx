# Origin and invention

Both of these sports were invented by diving clubs to solve the same problem:
what do divers do in winter?

**Underwater hockey** was devised at the Southsea Sub-Aqua Club in England in
1954, credited to Alan Blake, and originally called octopush. The club wanted
something that would keep members diving and fit through the cold months, in a
swimming pool, and built a game around what a pool offers: a flat bottom, and
divers who can hold their breath.

The consequences were immediate and are still the sport's structure. Six a
side, short sticks, a lead puck heavy enough to stay on the floor — and **no
goalkeeper**, because nobody can remain stationary at depth long enough to be
one. A sport whose defenders must all keep surfacing cannot have a fixed last
line, so it does not have one.

**Underwater rugby** was devised in Germany in 1961, out of the same club
culture and a more radical idea. Rather than adapt a surface game downwards,
it was built around the physical properties of a deep pool. The ball is filled
with saltwater so that it sinks. The goals are heavy baskets on the bottom. The
pool is three and a half to five metres deep, and all of it is in play.

That last decision made it the only team sport contested in three dimensions.
Everything else on this site is played on a surface, whether that surface is
grass, ice, a court, a mat or water. Underwater rugby is played in a volume.

CMAS, the world underwater federation founded in 1959, took both on, and
established world championships in 1980.
