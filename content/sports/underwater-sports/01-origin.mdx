# Two games for divers in winter

Diving clubs invented both games, to give divers something to do in winter.

## Hockey on the floor

Underwater hockey [began at the Southsea Sub-Aqua Club in England in
1954](#uw-1954-hockey). It is credited to Alan Blake, and its first name was
octopush. Open-water diving is unattractive in the cold months, and the club
wanted to keep its members diving and fit. So it built a game from what a
swimming pool offers: a flat floor, and divers who can hold their breath.

<Photo id="underwater-sports-hockey-bangor-2009" />

The first design is still the game. Six players a side push a lead
<Term id="puck">puck</Term> along the floor with short
<Term id="stick">sticks</Term>. Every attack begins with a breath at the
surface.

There is no goalkeeper. Nobody can stay in one place on the bottom long
enough to be one. Every defender has to keep
<Term id="surfacing">surfacing</Term>, so the game has no fixed last line of
defence.

## Rugby in deep water

Underwater rugby [was devised in Germany in 1961](#uw-1961-rugby), by diving
clubs that wanted competitive training under water. Its makers did not move a
surface game downwards. They built a new game around a deep pool.

The <Term id="ball">ball</Term> is filled with saltwater, so it sinks. The
goals are heavy <Term id="basket">baskets</Term> on the bottom. The pool is
three and a half to five metres deep, and all of that water is in play.

<Photo id="underwater-sports-rugby-basket" />

Most team games are played on a surface: grass, ice, a court, a mat, or the
top of the water. Underwater rugby is played in three dimensions. An attack
can come from above, from below or from any side.

## One code

Both games spread through diving clubs, and the rules differed from country
to country. CMAS, the world underwater federation founded in 1959, took on
both. [In 1980 it set up world championships](#uw-1980-world-championships),
and with them a common code for pool sizes, team sizes, substitution and
referees.
