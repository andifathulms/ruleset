# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Eventing deaths.** Through the late 1990s a run of horse and rider fatalities
in eventing made the long format indefensible. Horses arrived at the
cross-country course having already completed roads and tracks and a
steeplechase, and the fatigue that produced was the mechanism nobody could
argue with. The endurance phases were removed for 2005.

That is the same kind of act modern pentathlon performed in 2022 and the same
kind of loss: a component removed for welfare reasons, and a score that no
longer means what it meant. Traditionalists argued then, and still argue, that
the long format was the test and what remains is a different discipline.

**Blood.** A mark on a horse is indefensible in a televised competition
whatever caused it, and the discretionary standard produced inconsistent
decisions and repeated public damage. Elimination for blood, tightened from
2013, removed the discretion.

**Tokyo, 2021 — the other sport.** The incident that transformed equestrian's
politics did not happen in equestrian. It happened in modern pentathlon, and it
ended riding in that sport within nine months. Every FEI decision since has
been taken in the knowledge of how fast that went.

**Warm-up arena practices.** Periodic footage of training methods — rollkur in
dressage, and various practices in jumping warm-ups — has produced successive
tightenings of what stewards may inspect and what equipment is permitted. This
is the sport's recurring controversy and the one most directly responsible for
the 2023 changes.

**Teams of three.** Not a scandal, and included because the sport created a
rule that pushes against its own welfare position. A rider with a struggling
horse now knows that retiring ends their team's competition.
