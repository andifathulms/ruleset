# Four arguments about the horse

## The long day

Eventing once had an endurance day in four parts. The horse covered roads
and tracks, then a steeplechase, then roads and tracks again, and only then
started the <Term id="cross-country">cross-country</Term> course. It arrived
at the fixed fences already tired.

Through the late 1990s a run of horses and riders died in eventing. Attention
turned to the fatigue the long format built up. [The endurance phases were
removed for 2005](#fei-2005-short-format-eventing), and the cross-country was
left on its own. The phases that went were also the most expensive to stage
and the hardest to watch, and the change came while the sport's Olympic place
was under review.

Traditionalists said the long format was the test, and that what remains is a
different discipline. An eventing score from before 2005 cannot be compared
with one from after it, because the total no longer adds up the same parts.

## Blood

A mark on a horse in a televised competition cannot be defended, whatever
caused it. Officials once judged how serious the mark was, and their
decisions were inconsistent. [From 2013 blood on the horse means
elimination](#fei-2013-blood-rule), whatever the rider intended and however
well the round was going.

## An incident in another sport

The event that changed equestrian's politics happened in
[modern pentathlon](/sports/modern-pentathlon/), at the Tokyo Games in 2021.
After a televised incident in the riding phase, that sport removed riding
within nine months. Equestrian's federation saw how quickly a horse
discipline could leave the Olympic programme.

In 2023 the FEI's own Equine Ethics and Wellbeing Commission reported. Its
surveys found that a large share of the public was not confident that horses
in sport are treated well, and that a significant minority opposed the sport
outright. The commission said the sport depends on a "social licence": it
continues because the public allows it. The federation accepted the finding.
[It tightened the rules on training, on warm-up practices and on
equipment](#fei-2023-social-licence), and gave stewards more to inspect.

Footage from warm-up arenas had been causing trouble for years. Rollkur in
dressage, and some practices in jumping warm-ups, had each led to tighter
rules before 2023.

## Three riders, no spare

Olympic teams once had four riders, and the worst score was dropped. The IOC
wanted fewer athletes and more nations. [Since 2021 a team has three riders
and every score counts](#fei-2020-teams-of-three).

The welfare rules encourage a rider to <Term id="retire">retire</Term> when a
horse is struggling. Without a <Term id="drop-score">drop score</Term>,
retiring ends the whole team's competition. Riders said this puts pressure on
them to carry on. The federation answered with a substitution rule and did
not bring back the fourth rider.
