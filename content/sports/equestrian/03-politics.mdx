# Governing body politics

The FEI's central political problem has no equivalent anywhere else on this
site: it must justify the sport's existence to people who are not interested in
it.

In 2023 its own Equine Ethics and Wellbeing Commission reported. The framing it
introduced — a **social licence to operate** — is now the sport's governing
concept, and it means something specific: equestrian sport continues because
the public tolerates it, that tolerance is measurable, and the measurements are
not reassuring. A substantial share of the surveyed public was not confident
horses in sport are well treated, and a significant minority opposed the sport
outright.

A federation commissioning that work and accepting the finding is unusual. Most
governing bodies on this site respond to external pressure by contesting the
evidence. The FEI's response was to tighten training and equipment rules and to
tell its own membership that the question is real.

The reason it moved is a year old and one sport over. Modern pentathlon lost
its riding discipline in 2022, nine months after a televised incident, and the
demonstration effect was immediate: an equestrian discipline can be removed
from the Olympic programme very quickly if the footage is bad enough.

The other live governance tension is internal and specific. The IOC's quota
reduction produced teams of three with no drop score from 2021, which means a
rider whose horse is struggling must weigh retiring — which the welfare rules
encourage — against ending their team's competition. The federation created
that conflict itself, for quota reasons, in the same period it was tightening
welfare rules everywhere else.
