# Officiating and technology

Equestrian officiates two different kinds of thing at once, and it is the only
sport here with a third category of official who can remove a competitor for
reasons unrelated to the contest.

**Jumping** is measured. A fence is down or it is not; the clock is a clock.
There is almost nothing to dispute, and video review handles the little there
is.

**Dressage** is judged, and it has the full apparatus of a judged sport: a
panel positioned at different points around the arena, published guidelines for
what each movement should look like, and statistical review of scoring
patterns to identify judges whose marks diverge from the panel. It is
gymnastics' problem in a different arena, and the sport has arrived at
gymnastics' answers.

**Veterinary officials** are the category nothing else here has. A veterinary
delegate can withdraw a horse from competition over the rider's objection, at
an inspection before the event or during it. That is an official empowered to
end a competitor's participation for the competitor's own protection, and it
has no equivalent in any other sport on this site — the closest is boxing's
referee stopping a bout, which is a judgement about the same person who chose
to be there.

**Stewards** police the warm-up arena, and the sport treats that as
competition officiating rather than crowd control, because the warm-up is where
the practices that damage the sport occur.

The measurement worth noting is the noseband gauge. A rule about how tightly a
strap may be fastened, enforced with an instrument, is welfare converted into a
number — and converting judgements into numbers is what this whole site keeps
watching federations do.
