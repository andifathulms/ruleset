# Judges, clocks and vets

Jumping is measured. A fence is down or it is still up, and the clock gives
the time. There is very little to dispute, and video review handles what
there is.

Dressage is judged. A panel sits at different points round the arena, and
each judge marks every movement of the <Term id="test">test</Term>. There are
published guidelines for what each movement should look like. The scores are
reviewed statistically to find judges whose marks differ from the rest of the
panel. [Gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) has the same problem and has reached
similar answers.

Equestrian sport also has officials whose job is the horse. A veterinary
delegate can withdraw a horse at the
<Term id="horse-inspection">horse inspection</Term> before the event, or
during it, even when the rider objects. A referee in
[boxing](/sports/boxing/) can stop a bout to protect a boxer, but the boxer
chose to be there.

<Term id="steward">Stewards</Term> watch the warm-up arena. The sport treats
this as part of officiating the competition, because the practices that harm
horses mostly happen there. One of their tools is the noseband gauge, which
turns a welfare rule into a number.
