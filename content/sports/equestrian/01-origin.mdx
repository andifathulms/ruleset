# Origin and invention

Equestrian sport descends directly from cavalry training, and the three Olympic
disciplines are three cavalry skills with the army removed.

**Dressage** is the schooling that made a horse controllable in formation and
under pressure. **Jumping** is the ability to cross obstructed country.
**Eventing** was explicitly a test of a military charger: obedience on the
first day, endurance and courage across country on the second, and soundness
enough to jump on the third.

That origin was not incidental. Until 1948 the Olympic equestrian events were
restricted to commissioned military officers, which is why the sport has no
women in its early results — not by a rule about women, but by a rule about
rank. Civilians and women were admitted to dressage for 1952, jumping in 1956
and eventing in 1964.

The consequence is the fact this sport is best known for. Because the
restriction was lifted without anyone deciding to create separate categories,
equestrian became the only sport on the Olympic programme in which men and
women compete directly against each other in every event, and it has been so
for sixty years. It happened by omission rather than by design, which makes it
no less real.

The FEI was founded in 1921. The cavalry it was built around had effectively
ceased to exist within thirty years, and the sport has spent the century since
working out what it is a test of.
