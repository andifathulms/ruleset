# From the cavalry to the arena

The three Olympic disciplines are three cavalry skills.
<Term id="dressage">Dressage</Term> is the schooling that made a horse
controllable in formation and under pressure. <Term id="jumping">Jumping</Term>
is the ability to cross country with obstacles in the way.
<Term id="eventing">Eventing</Term> was a test of a military charger:
obedience on the first day, endurance and courage across country on the
second, and enough soundness left to jump on the third. The competition was
then called the "military".

<Photos ids="equestrian-roycewicz-1936 equestrian-endrody-1936" />

The international federation, the FEI, was founded in 1921. Until 1948 the
Olympic events were open only to commissioned military officers. That is why
the early results have no women in them. The rule was about rank, and it shut
out every civilian.

## The rule about rank is lifted

[Civilians and women were admitted to dressage for
1952](#fei-1952-women-admitted). At Helsinki that year Lis Hartel of Denmark
rode Jubilee to the individual silver medal, against men. Jumping opened in
1956 and eventing in 1964.

<Photo id="equestrian-hartel-1952" />

Nobody created separate events for men and women when the restriction went.
So equestrian became the only sport on the Olympic programme in which men and
women compete against each other in every event. It happened by omission, and
it has stayed that way.

The cavalry the sport was built around had almost disappeared within thirty
years of the federation's founding. The sport has spent the time since
working out what it is a test of.
