# Where it is played

Equestrian has the narrowest social base of any sport on this site and a
geography that follows wealth almost exactly.

Germany, Great Britain, the Netherlands, France, Sweden, the United States,
Australia and New Zealand account for most international results, with Japan
and several Gulf states investing heavily more recently. What those have in
common is not a riding culture in any romantic sense but the ability to
sustain horses: land, stabling, veterinary infrastructure and enough disposable
income for an expensive animal to be a hobby.

The purchase question makes it different from every other sport here. In
athletics or judo, money buys coaching and facilities and thereby improves an
athlete. In equestrian, money can buy a horse that is already capable, and the
sport is candid that this happens. The rider still has to ride it — but the
ceiling of what a rider can achieve is bounded by what they can acquire.

This is also why the welfare argument lands so hard. A sport that is expensive,
socially narrow and involves animals is unusually exposed when public attitudes
to animals in sport shift, because it has a small constituency and a visible
subject.

The Gulf states are the recent variable, with substantial investment in
endurance and in jumping. Endurance is also the discipline with the sport's
worst welfare record, and the FEI has had to regulate it separately and
repeatedly — which is a geography problem and a governance problem arriving
together.
