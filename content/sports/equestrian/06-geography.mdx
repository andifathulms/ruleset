# Where the horses are

Equestrian sport is strong where people can afford to keep horses.

Germany, Great Britain, the Netherlands, France, Sweden, the United States,
Australia and New Zealand account for most international results. Japan and
several Gulf states have invested heavily more recently. What these countries
share is land, stabling, veterinary care and enough spare income to keep an
expensive animal.

Money works differently here than in most sports. Elsewhere it buys coaching
and facilities. Here it can also buy a horse that is already capable. The
rider still has to ride it, but what a rider can achieve is limited by the
horse they can get. Riders stay at the top into their fifties and beyond,
because the sport does not depend on a young body.

The Gulf states have put money into jumping and into endurance, the
long-distance discipline held away from the Games. Endurance has the worst
welfare record in the sport, and the FEI has had to regulate it separately
and more than once.
