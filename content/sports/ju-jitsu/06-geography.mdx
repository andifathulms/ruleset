# Where it is played

Competitive ju-jitsu under this federation is a European sport with an Emirati
patron and an Asian foothold.

**Europe** is the competitive core, and specifically the countries where the
sport was assembled: Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, France, Italy,
Sweden, Russia and Ukraine. The JJIF grew out of European federations and its
world championships still reflect that.

**The United Arab Emirates** is the outlier and the most consequential
development in the sport's recent history. Abu Dhabi has invested very heavily
in grappling — hosting major events, funding programmes, and putting jiu-jitsu
into the school curriculum — and the UAE is now a genuine power. It is the
clearest case on this site of a small country buying itself into the top of a
combat sport, and it worked.

**Asia** followed the money and the Asian Games appearance in 2018: Kazakhstan,
Mongolia, Vietnam, Uzbekistan and Iran all compete.

What the map does not show is the sport's actual population. Far more people
train grappling under the Brazilian codes, in Brazil, the United States and
across Europe, than compete under the JJIF — and most of them would say they do
jiu-jitsu. The federation's competitive geography and the activity's global
distribution are almost unrelated, which is a stranger situation than any other
sport covered here has.
