# The jacket, and fighting without it

The main piece of equipment in ju-jitsu is a jacket. The
<Term id="gi">gi</Term> gives both athletes something to grip, and a large
share of the throws and controls depend on holding it. Ju-jitsu took the gi
from the Japanese tradition. So did [judo](/sports/judo/), which always
requires it.

## No-gi

Some Ne-waza categories are fought without the jacket. This is
<Term id="no-gi">no-gi</Term>, and it changes the contest a great deal.
Without a jacket there are no grips on the lapel or the sleeve and no
strangles with the collar. The athletes use underhooks and body locks, and
they control the head and the limbs directly.

<Photos ids="ju-jitsu-gi-armbar ju-jitsu-no-gi" />

In other sports a change of equipment made the same event faster or easier.
The polyurethane suit did that in [swimming](/sports/swimming/), and the bench
shirt did it in [powerlifting](/sports/powerlifting/). Taking off the gi does
something else. It makes grappling a different contest, and athletes
specialise in one or the other.

## Gloves for the first phase

Fighting adds gloves, shin protection and a mouthguard, because its first
phase contains strikes. The gloves are light. Every strike must be a
<Term id="controlled-strike">controlled strike</Term>, made to land and not
to damage. In this ju-jitsu stands with [karate](/sports/karate/) and apart
from [boxing](/sports/boxing/).
