# Equipment evolution

A jacket, and an argument about whether to wear it.

The **gi** is the sport's fundamental equipment, and in a grappling art it is
not clothing but apparatus: the jacket provides grips, and a huge proportion of
throwing and controlling technique depends on being able to hold it. Ju-jitsu
inherited the gi from the Japanese tradition, and judo, which shares that
inheritance, made it mandatory permanently.

The interesting development is **no-gi**. In the ne-waza discipline, some
categories are contested without a jacket, and this changes the sport more than
any equipment substitution recorded elsewhere on this site. Without a jacket
there are no lapel grips, no sleeve control, no collar strangles, and the
entire technical vocabulary shifts to underhooks, body locks and control of the
head and limbs. Gi and no-gi grappling are effectively two sports, and athletes
specialise.

Compare that with the equipment questions on this site's measured sports. A
polyurethane swimsuit, a composite bat and a bench shirt all made the same
event easier or faster. Removing the gi does not make grappling easier or
harder — it changes what grappling is.

**Fighting** adds gloves, shin protection and a mouthguard, because the first
phase contains strikes. The gloves are light, because the strikes are required
to be controlled rather than damaging, which puts ju-jitsu in the same category
as karate rather than boxing.

**Duo** needs nothing at all beyond the gi.
