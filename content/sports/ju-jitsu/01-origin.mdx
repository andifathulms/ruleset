# An old art, a young sport

Ju-jitsu began as a way to fight without a weapon. The Japanese systems that
share the name were battlefield methods for a warrior who had lost his weapon
or could not use it. They taught throws, joint locks, strangles and strikes,
and defences against all of them, often against an opponent in armour.

The systems were taught in schools, each called a *ryū*. There were many
schools, and each had its own curriculum.

## The child that became a sport first

In the 1880s Jigoro Kano took part of this material and built
[judo](/sports/judo/) from it. He removed the techniques that could not be
practised at full resistance without injury. That is why judo worked as a
sport. Judo joined the Olympic programme in 1964, and ju-jitsu is not on it.

<Photo id="ju-jitsu-kano-1892" />

## In Europe before it was a sport

Ju-jitsu reached Europe as self-defence. In November 1905 it was demonstrated
at the police headquarters in Paris. In London, Edith Garrud taught it to
suffragettes, and in 1910 the magazine Punch printed a cartoon called "The
Suffragette that Knew Jiu-Jitsu". The name was already in public use.

## Building a competition

Competitive ju-jitsu came much later. European federations put it together
from the 1970s, and each country ran its own format. They had a problem judo
never had. Judo already owned the throwing, and the grappling arts owned the
ground. What was a ju-jitsu competition for?

The first answer was the <Term id="fighting">Fighting system</Term>. [A bout
passes through striking, then throwing, then
groundwork](#jujitsu-three-phase-fighting), always in that order, so no
fighter can avoid a <Term id="phase">phase</Term>. A format that let athletes
stay in one range would have turned ju-jitsu into a sport that already
existed.

The second answer was the <Term id="duo">Duo system</Term>. Much of the art is
self-defence against attacks that would injure a resisting opponent if they
were done at full force. [In Duo, two team-mates perform those techniques
together](#jujitsu-duo-system) and judges mark them. A large part of the
curriculum became something that could be contested.

<Photo id="ju-jitsu-kacchu-ryu" />

[The Ju-Jitsu International Federation took its present form in
1998](#jujitsu-1998-federation), joining the European federations under one
body that could hold championships.
