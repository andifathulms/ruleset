# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Who owns the name.** A classical Japanese art, a Brazilian sport, hundreds of
independent schools and one international federation all use it. The JJIF's
competitive code is a claimant, not a consensus, and no mechanism exists to
resolve it.

**Whether the three phases work.** Requiring a bout to pass through striking,
throwing and groundwork preserves the sport's identity and asks athletes to
compete against specialists in every range. Whether that produces good
competition or a shallow version of three better sports is argued inside the
federation.

**Duo's status.** A cooperative, judged event at the world championship of a
combat sport. It makes a large part of the art contestable; it is also the
hardest thing about the sport to explain to anyone outside it, and its place on
any future programme would be difficult to defend.

**Grappling's centre of gravity.** Adding ne-waza in 2015 acknowledged that the
largest grappling constituency was outside the federation. It has not brought
that constituency in, and the Brazilian bodies continue independently.

**The judo problem.** The Olympic programme already contains a sport made by
removing most of ju-jitsu. Arguing for the parent art alongside its more
successful descendant is a genuinely difficult institutional case, and nobody
in the sport has found a version of it that works.

**Gi and no-gi.** Two technical codes, two sets of specialists, one federation
maintaining both.
