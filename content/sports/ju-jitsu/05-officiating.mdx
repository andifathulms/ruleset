# Three kinds of referee

The three systems give officials three different jobs.

## Fighting

The Fighting referee decides when the bout changes <Term id="phase">phase</Term>.
The referee judges when the conditions are met to move from striking to
gripping, and from gripping to the ground, and then moves the bout. A mistake
costs a fighter. It can take away the range where that fighter is strongest,
or pull them out of it too early.

Inside each phase the referee judges control. A strike must land without
doing damage. [Karate](/sports/karate/) asks its officials for the same
judgement, and it is just as hard there: the referee must tell an effective,
restrained technique from an uncontrolled one, at full speed.

## Ne-waza

The Ne-waza referee awards points for position and recognises a
<Term id="submission">submission</Term>. There is also a safety decision.
When a <Term id="lock">lock</Term> is on and the athlete has not submitted,
the referee must decide how long to wait before stopping the bout.

## Duo

In Duo a panel of judges marks a performance by two partners. The marks are
for realism, control, speed and effectiveness. The pair is rewarded for making
an attack look real and for defending against it without hurting anybody. The
judges mark both things at once.

## By eye

Ju-jitsu uses no scoring technology. [Taekwondo](/sports/taekwondo/) put
sensors in the socks, and [fencing](/sports/fencing/) wired its weapons.
Controlled contact, a better position and the realism of a demonstration are
all judged by eye.
