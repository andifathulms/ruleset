# Officiating

Three systems, three completely different officiating jobs, one federation.

**Fighting.** The referee manages the phase structure, which is an
administrative task no other combat sport has: deciding when the conditions for
moving from striking to gripping, and from gripping to the ground, have been
met, and moving the bout accordingly. Get that wrong and an athlete is either
denied the range they are strongest in or dragged out of it early.

Within the phases, the referee is judging **control**. Strikes in part one must
land with control rather than damage, which is the same judgement karate makes
and the same difficulty: a technique that is clearly effective and clearly
restrained is a fine distinction made at speed.

**Ne-waza.** Positional scoring and submission recognition, plus the safety
judgement every grappling sport needs — when a lock has been applied and the
athlete has not submitted, how long before the referee stops it.

**Duo.** A judging panel marking a cooperative performance on realism, control,
speed and effectiveness. The paradox is explicit here: a pair is rewarded for
making an attack look genuine and for defending it without hurting anybody, and
the judges are marking the gap between those two things.

None of this is instrumented. Taekwondo put sensors in the socks and fencing
wired the weapons; ju-jitsu judges controlled contact, positional advantage and
the realism of a demonstration entirely by eye, which no available technology
would help with.
