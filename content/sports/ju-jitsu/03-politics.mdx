# A name with many owners

The Ju-Jitsu International Federation governs a word that many others also
use. "Ju-jitsu", "jujutsu" and "jiu-jitsu" are the names of classical Japanese
schools that do not compete, of hundreds of independent organisations with
their own gradings, and of a very large Brazilian grappling sport with its own
federations. The federation runs one competitive code among all of these, and
it holds international recognition for it.

Other combat sports have their own disputes. But in [judo](/sports/judo/),
[taekwondo](/sports/taekwondo/), [wrestling](/sports/wrestling/),
[boxing](/sports/boxing/) and [karate](/sports/karate/) the international
federation governs something clearly central to the activity. In ju-jitsu most
of the people who use the name are somewhere else. Whether one federation can
govern such a name is disputed, and the independent schools are among those
who dispute it.

The confusion has practical costs. A person looking for a club finds Japanese
schools, Brazilian academies and independent groups under the same word, and
most of them do not compete under the federation's rules.

## Grappling grows somewhere else

Through the 2000s and 2010s submission grappling became one of the
fastest-growing combat activities in the world. Almost all of that growth
happened under Brazilian and independent bodies. [In 2015 the federation added
its own groundwork contest](#jujitsu-newaza-discipline),
<Term id="ne-waza">Ne-waza</Term>.

The new discipline brought a version of the activity inside the federation. It
did not bring the Brazilian federations, their athletes or their events. Those
continue as before.

## The Olympic problem

For its size the sport has done well. It has a place at the World Games, it
appeared at the Asian Games in 2018, and it has international recognition. It
has no Olympic place. Part of the obstacle is that the Olympic programme has
had judo since 1964, and judo was made by removing most of ju-jitsu. Nobody in
the sport has found a convincing case for adding the parent beside its child.
