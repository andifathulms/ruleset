# The match that would not end

## Three days at Wimbledon

Wimbledon, June 2010, a first-round match. John Isner and Nicolas Mahut
reached two <Term id="set">sets</Term> each and began a fifth. Wimbledon
played no <Term id="tiebreak">tiebreak</Term> in the deciding set, so the set
could only end when one player led by two <Term id="game">games</Term>.
Neither could get there.

<Photos ids="tennis-isner-mahut-court-16 tennis-isner-mahut-court-18" />

The scoreboards around the grounds showed the fifth set going past fifty
games. The match took eleven hours and five minutes, spread over three days.
The last set finished 70 games to 68. Afterwards the two players and the
umpire, Mohamed Lahyani, stood beside the scoreboard on Court 18 for a
photograph.

## The cure was forty years old

The tiebreak already existed. James Van Alen had argued for years that the
old scoring made matches impossible to manage, because a set could run to any
length. [His tiebreak was introduced at the US Open in
1970](#itf-1970-tiebreak), first as a nine-point sudden-death game. The
version to seven points with a lead of two became the standard. Many in the
sport saw it as an artificial device added to a system that had worked for a
century.

The US Open used a tiebreak in the deciding set from 1970. The other three
majors did not.

## Four tournaments, four rules

Tennis has no single owner. The International Tennis Federation owns the
Rules of Tennis. The four Grand Slam tournaments are independent and set some
of their own conditions on top of those rules. The ATP and the WTA run the
men's and women's professional tours. None of these bodies can give orders to
the others.

<Photos ids="tennis-philippe-chatrier tennis-rod-laver-arena" />

So the answer to the 2010 match came slowly and in pieces. [In 2019
Wimbledon brought in a tiebreak at twelve games all, and the Australian Open
a ten-point tiebreak at six all](#itf-2019-final-set-tiebreaks). The US Open
kept its own. The French Open had none. For one season a player could reach
six games all in a deciding set at the four biggest tournaments and meet four
different rules.

[In 2022 all four agreed on one](#itf-2022-final-set-harmonised): a ten-point
tiebreak at six games all. That was fifty-two years after the tiebreak was
first used.
