# What is still argued about

None of these questions has been settled.

**The calendar.** The professional season runs for close to eleven months.
Ranking points expire if a player does not defend them, so a player has to
keep entering tournaments. Players have asked for a shorter season for about
twenty years. The tours earn their money from events, and no single body owns
the whole calendar, so nobody is in a position to shorten it.

**Best of five.** At the majors men play the best of five
<Term id="set">sets</Term> and women the best of three. Some want the men's
matches shortened, to make them more predictable and easier on the body.
Others want the women's matches lengthened, because the difference rests on
history alone.

**What is left for the umpire.** The line judges have gone from the tours.
Foot <Term id="fault">faults</Term>, touches of the net and double bounces
could in principle be detected by machine as well. Each proposal meets the
same objection: at some point the officials are being replaced, and the sport
has not said where that point is.

**One surface.** Some argue that a champion should not be the champion of one
kind of ground, and that the sport should choose a single surface. The idea
returns regularly and goes nowhere. The surfaces are part of the identity of
the four tournaments that matter most.
