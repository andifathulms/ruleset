# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The calendar.** Tennis runs close to eleven months with no meaningful
off-season, individually rather than in teams, with ranking points that decay if
a player does not defend them. Player bodies have pressed for a shorter season
for two decades against tours whose revenue depends on events. It is a
governance dispute with a health cause and no obvious mechanism, because no
single body can shorten a calendar it does not own.

**Best of five.** Men play best of five at the majors and women best of three,
and the arguments for changing either direction are both live. Shortening the
men's format would make matches predictable and reduce load; lengthening the
women's would remove a difference that is hard to justify on any ground other
than history.

**What is left for an umpire.** Having removed line judges in 2025, tennis has
to decide what else is a solved question. Foot faults, net touches and
double bounces are all in principle detectable, and each proposal to automate
one meets the same objection: at some point the officials stop being
supplemented and start being replaced, and the sport has not said where that
point is.

**Surfaces.** Tennis is the only scored sport here that does not standardise
its playing surface, and the argument for doing so — that a champion should not
be a champion of one ground — resurfaces regularly. It has never got anywhere,
because the surfaces are the identities of the four tournaments that own the
sport.
