# Racket, strings and ball

The tennis racket changed the game completely without a single rule being
written, and then one odd set of strings forced a rule within a year.

## The frame

Rackets were made of wood for about a century. Then came aluminium, and then
graphite composite. Heads grew larger and frames much stiffer. The game moved
from touch and placement to power and spin. The <Term id="serve">serve</Term>
became a weapon, and <Term id="rally">rallies</Term> grew shorter.

<Photos ids="tennis-wood-racket tennis-dunlop-comet" />

No rule changed at any point. The laws limited the length of the racket and
little else, so every new frame was legal.

## The spaghetti racket

In 1977 a racket appeared with two layers of loose strings, threaded through
tubes and not woven together. It put so much spin on the ball that opponents
could not read the bounce. Within weeks, players with no ranking were beating
established professionals.

<Photo id="tennis-spaghetti-patent" />

The federation acted the following year. [Strings must now be
interlaced](#itf-1978-spaghetti-string-ban), which means woven in the usual
pattern. To write that rule, tennis had to say for the first time what a
racket face was.

A slow change that altered the whole sport brought no rule. A sudden change
that upset a few months of results brought one at once.

## The ball and the ground

The ball has been defined by how high it bounces since the 1920s, and that
rule has not needed to move. The court surface is left open on purpose.
Grass, clay and hard courts each give a different bounce and speed.
