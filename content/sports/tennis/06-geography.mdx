# Where it is played

Tennis champions come from all over the world, and almost all of them come
from families or systems that could pay for a long training.

In recent decades the winners have come from Switzerland, Spain, Serbia, the
United States, Russia, Japan, Australia, Germany, Romania, Poland, Italy and
China. The road to the top is much the same in each. A young player needs
court time, private coaching, new strings and travel abroad from an early
age. The countries usually named for widening that road, Spain and more
recently Italy, did it by building public courts.

## Four old addresses

The four majors are held in Australia, France, Britain and the United States.
That map was drawn in the nineteenth century, and the sport has never redrawn
it.

## Three kinds of ground

Each major has its surface. Wimbledon is on grass, the French Open on clay,
and the Australian and US Opens on hard courts. The rest of the world follows
a pattern too. Clay is the usual surface in continental Europe and South
America. Hard courts are usual in North America and Asia. Grass is found
almost nowhere. The surface a player grows up on shapes that player's game
for life.
