# Officiating and technology

Tennis has gone further with officiating technology than any other sport on
this site, and it is the only one to have used technology to abolish a job.

The sequence is instructive. Line judges called the ball in or out; the chair
umpire could overrule; both were making a judgement about a ball travelling at
up to 200 km/h and touching the ground for a few milliseconds. Television had
been showing the audience the correct answer since the 1990s.

**2006** gave players a limited number of challenges, with a ball-tracking
system rendering the landing point on a screen. This is the model most sports
here adopted: keep the official, add a check.

**2025** removed the officials. Electronic line calling now makes every call in
real time at tour level, the challenge is unnecessary and gone, and the line
judge no longer exists. No other sport on this site has done this. Football
kept its referee and added VAR; volleyball kept its officials and added a
coach's challenge; judo added a review layer above the mat referee. Tennis
concluded that a class of decision was fully solved and removed the humans from
it.

That was possible because tennis's central question is unusually clean. Was the
ball inside the line? It is a fact about the position of an object, with a
correct answer, requiring no interpretation of anyone's intent. Football's
handball law will never be automatable for exactly the reason tennis's line
call was.

What remains for the chair umpire is the part that was always judgement:
hindrance, the code of conduct, time violations, and the crowd.
