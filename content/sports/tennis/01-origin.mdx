# Where it came from

Tennis began as a game played with the bare hand. From the twelfth century,
players in French monastery cloisters hit a ball with the palm, and the game
was called *jeu de paume*. It moved into enclosed courts with a sagging net
and took up rackets. That older game is now called real tennis. It is still
played on a handful of courts, and it was briefly an Olympic sport in 1908.

<Photos ids="tennis-jeu-de-paume-paris tennis-hampton-court" />

The scoring comes from the old game. Points are still called 15, 30 and 40,
and nobody can say for certain why. The best explanation anyone has is a
clock face.

## A game sold in a box

Lawn tennis is a Victorian invention. Walter Clopton Wingfield patented a
portable version in 1874: a net, rackets, rubber balls and a book of rules,
sold together in a box for garden parties. He called it Sphairistikè. The
court he drew was an hourglass, wider at the <Term id="baseline">baselines</Term>
than at the net.

<Photos ids="tennis-wingfield-1874 tennis-hourglass-court-1874" />

The All England Croquet Club took up the game and added "and Lawn Tennis" to
its name. In 1877 it held the first Wimbledon championship, at Worple Road.
The court for that championship was a rectangle, and the rectangle stayed.
The nets were much higher than they are now. Spencer Gore won.

<Photos ids="tennis-wimbledon-1877 tennis-wimbledon-1877-draw" />

Two things from those years still shape the sport. One is the scoring, which
tennis has adjusted many times and never replaced. The other is the ground.
Lawn tennis was played on grass, and the sport never required everyone to
play on the same surface. Its biggest tournaments are still played on grass,
clay and hard courts.

## Amateurs and professionals

The draw sheet of 1877 says the championship was "open to all amateurs". For
most of the next century, amateurs and professionals played separate
tournaments. The argument over who could be paid cost tennis its place at the
Olympic Games. It was dropped after Paris 1924, where Helen Wills won the
women's singles.

<Photo id="tennis-wills-1924" />

The two sides came together in 1968, when the Open era began and
professionals and amateurs could enter the same events. Tennis returned to
the Olympics as a full medal sport at Seoul in 1988, sixty-four years after
it had left.

<Photos ids="tennis-laver-1969 tennis-seoul-1988" />

## Equal prize money

In 1973 the US Open paid its men's and women's champions the same, the first
of the four majors to do so. The others followed one tournament at a time,
and the last did so in 2007. No governing body ordered it.
