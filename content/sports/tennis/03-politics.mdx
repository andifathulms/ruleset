# Governing body politics

Tennis has the most fragmented governance of any sport on this site, and it is
the reason a single rule took fifty-two years to apply consistently.

The ITF owns the Rules of Tennis. The four Grand Slam tournaments are
independent and set some of their own conditions on top of them. The ATP and
WTA run the men's and women's professional tours. No one of these can compel
the others, and the Grand Slams — which are the sport's four most important
competitions — answer to nobody on this list.

The deciding-set tiebreak is what that looks like in practice. The US Open used
one from 1970. Wimbledon introduced one at 12–12 in 2019, the Australian Open a
ten-point version at 6–6 the same year, and the French Open had none. For one
season a player could reach 6–6 in a deciding set at four tournaments and face
four different rules about what happened next. It was harmonised in 2022.

The other structural fact is the amateurism split, which tennis handled worse
than football and better than athletics. The sport tore itself in two over
professionalism, was dropped from the Olympic programme after 1924 because of
it, and only reunified in 1968. The Open era is late by the standards of most
sports here.

Tennis is also unusual in having genuinely comparable men's and women's
professional tours with equal prize money at all four majors, achieved
tournament by tournament between 1973 and 2007 rather than by any governing
body's decision.
