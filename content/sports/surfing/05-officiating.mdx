# Officiating and technology

Surfing has the hardest judging problem on this site, and it is harder than
gymnastics' for a specific reason: gymnastics judges compare performances of
the same routine on the same apparatus. Surfing judges compare performances on
different waves.

Five judges score each ride out of ten, the highest and lowest are discarded,
and the remaining three are averaged — the standard defence of a judged sport,
and surfing uses it exactly as gymnastics does.

What the criteria then ask is unusual. Judges assess difficulty, innovation,
variety, speed, power and flow — **relative to what the wave made possible**.
A nine on a small wave and a nine on a large one are the same score for
different achievements, and the rules explicitly instruct judges to score
against the wave's potential rather than against an absolute standard.

That is a judging system with a moving reference point, and no other sport here
has one. Gymnastics fixed its reference by writing difficulty values.
Taekwondo fixed its by installing sensors. Surfing cannot fix its, because the
reference is the sea.

Until 2028. A wave pool produces an identical wave for every competitor, which
would give surfing a fixed reference for the first time and make its judging
comparable within a heat, across heats and across events.

It is the only case on this site where a comparability problem has a
technological solution, and the only one where the solution is contested on the
grounds that it changes what the sport is.
