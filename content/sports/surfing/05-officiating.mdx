# Judging a wave that will not come again

Five judges watch every <Term id="ride">ride</Term> and each gives it a score
out of ten. The highest and the lowest scores are dropped, and the other three
are averaged. [Gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) protects its scores from one
odd judge in the same way.

<Photo id="surfing-jbay-judges-2015" />

The judges look at how difficult the
<Term id="manoeuvre">manoeuvres</Term> are, how new and how varied, and at
the speed, power and flow of the ride. Then comes the hard part. They must
score all of this against what the wave made possible. A nine on a small wave
and a nine on a large wave are the same number for two different feats.

<Photo id="surfing-nazare" />

Gymnastics judges compare the same kind of routine on the same apparatus.
Surfing judges compare rides on different waves, and the sea never gives the
same one twice. There is no fixed standard to hold a score against, so a
surfing score cannot be checked later or compared with a score from another
day.

## Who gets the wave

The rules must also settle who rides each wave. One wave carries one surfer,
so they give one surfer at a time <Term id="priority">priority</Term>: the right
to take any wave they choose. Priority moves on when a wave is taken.

Getting in the way of the surfer with priority is
<Term id="interference">interference</Term>, and the penalty is heavy. The
guilty surfer loses one of their two counting waves. Good waves are scarce,
and a surfer who is blocked may not get another chance.

Priority shares out the right to choose. It cannot share out the waves. If the
good ones come during one surfer's turn and none come during another's, the
sea has helped to decide the heat.
