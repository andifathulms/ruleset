# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled, and the first of these is the most consequential open
question on this site.

**The wave pool.** From 2028 Olympic surfing is contested on a machine-made
wave, identical for every competitor. It would make the sport comparable for
the first time, remove the luck that has decided heats for as long as there
have been heats, and let people surf a thousand miles from an ocean.

A large part of the sport says it is not surfing. The argument is that reading
the sea, choosing a wave and responding to something nobody designed is the
activity, and that a repeatable wave turns it into a routine performed on
water. The counter is that a judged sport in which competitors face different
tasks was never fair, and that fairness is worth the trade.

Both positions are coherent. No other federation here has ever had to choose
between comparability and identity with both genuinely on the table.

**Whether competition suits the activity at all.** The older version of the
same argument, and unresolved for sixty years.

**Venue and environment.** Teahupo'o produced objections about construction on
a reef; wave pools use large quantities of water and energy. A sport with a
strong environmental self-image has an uncomfortable relationship with both of
its Olympic venue options.

**Judging criteria.** Scoring relative to a wave's potential is the only way to
judge unequal conditions and it produces scores nobody can check. In a pool
that problem disappears — which is one more argument for the pool, and one more
thing that would change.
