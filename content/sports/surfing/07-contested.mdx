# What is still argued about

**Wave pools.** A machine can give every surfer in a heat the same wave. Those
in favour say this makes judging fair for the first time, because every surfer
faces the same task, and it lets a contest start on time. Those against say it
is no longer surfing. For them, reading the sea, choosing a wave and answering
something nobody designed is the sport itself, and a wave that repeats turns
surfing into a routine performed on water. The sport has not settled it.

**Whether surfing should be a contest at all.** This is the older form of the
same argument. Many surfers think competition is a small part of what they do.
Some think a ride cannot be marked out of ten, because it was an answer to a
wave that will never come again.

**Venues and the environment.** Surfers tend to see their sport as close to
nature. The Olympic contest at Teahupoʻo brought objections about building on
a reef. Wave pools use a great deal of water and energy. Neither kind of venue
sits easily with that self-image.

**The judging criteria.** Scoring a ride against the
<Term id="wave-potential">wave's potential</Term> is the only way to judge
surfers who get different waves. It also gives scores that nobody can check.
