# The board, the leash and the machine

A surfer may ride almost any board they like. Olympic surfing is on
<Term id="shortboard">shortboards</Term>, and a <Term id="leash">leash</Term>
is required. Past that, the surfer picks a board to suit the wave. A bad
choice can lose a heat, and it breaks no rule.

<Photo id="surfing-leash" />

The boards themselves have changed a great deal. Early boards were solid wood
and could weigh sixty kilograms. Balsa made them lighter, and then foam and
fibreglass made them lighter again. In the late 1960s boards lost several feet
of length, and the turns and jumps of modern surfing became possible. New
arrangements of fins changed the sport once more in the 1980s. Wetsuits opened
cold coasts that nobody had been able to surf.

<Photo id="surfing-hot-curl-1947" />

None of this needed a rule. A surfing score is a judgement of one ride on one
wave, so there is no record for a better board to break.
[Cycling](/sports/cycling/) and [athletics](/sports/athletics/) had to limit
their equipment to protect their numbers. Surfing has no such numbers to
protect.

## A wave that is made

A <Term id="wave-pool">wave pool</Term> is a different kind of equipment. A
machine makes the wave, the machine has settings, and several companies build
machines that work in different ways. In the sea nobody decides what the wave
is like. In a pool somebody must, and for a contest that choice would have to
be written down as a rule.
