# Two Olympic beaches, two arguments

## Gold in small surf

A professional surfing contest waits for the sea. It sets aside a
<Term id="waiting-period">waiting period</Term> of up to two weeks and runs
its heats on the days when the waves are good. The Olympic Games have a fixed
timetable and cannot wait that long. To join them, surfing [accepted a narrow
window](#isa-2016-olympic-inclusion) and the risk that a gold medal would be
decided in poor waves.

The first Olympic contest showed what that meant. It was held in 2021 at
Tsurigasaki beach in Chiba, Japan, and the surf was small and messy. Ítalo
Ferreira of Brazil won the men's gold in it.

<Photos ids="surfing-tsurigasaki surfing-italo-tsurigasaki" />

Some surfers were already asking whether a champion crowned in whatever waves
arrive on one day is truly the best. The Games did not start that argument,
and they have not ended it.

## A tower on the reef

Paris has no surf. For the 2024 Games the organisers [chose the wave over the
city](#isa-2024-teahupoo-venue) and held the contest at Teahupoʻo in Tahiti,
fifteen thousand kilometres away. Teahupoʻo is a
<Term id="reef-break">reef break</Term>: a heavy wave that folds over shallow
coral.

<Photos ids="surfing-teahupoo surfing-teahupoo-2" />

The judges needed a tower, and the tower had to stand on the reef. Local
residents and environmental groups objected to the building work on fragile
coral. The plans were changed, a modified structure was built, and the contest
went ahead.
