# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The scarcity problem.** Surfing's founding rules problem is not a
controversy but a constraint: a wave can only be ridden by one person, arrives
unpredictably, and is not equal to the next one. Every rule in the sport is a
response.

**Priority** allocates the right to choose a wave and rotates it. **Two-wave
scoring** stops volume beating quality. **Interference penalties** are unusually
severe because interfering removes an opponent's opportunity rather than merely
gaining an advantage. None of these solves the underlying problem, which is
that the waves themselves are not distributed evenly, and everyone in the sport
knows it.

**The Olympic window.** Professional surfing runs waiting periods of up to two
weeks and starts when the sea cooperates. An Olympic schedule cannot, so the
sport accepted a fixed window and the possibility of a gold medal decided in
poor surf. It is the clearest concession any sport on this site has made to get
onto the programme.

**Teahupo'o, 2024.** Holding an Olympic event fifteen thousand kilometres from
the host city, at a heavy reef break, produced objections from local residents
and environmental groups over the judging tower construction required on the
reef. The plans were modified and the event proceeded. It is the same class of
objection golf faces over land and water and equestrian faces over animals.

**The wave pool.** Not a controversy that forced a rule — a rule that produced
a controversy, and the largest identity argument in this collection.
