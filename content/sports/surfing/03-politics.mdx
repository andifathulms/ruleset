# Governing body politics

Surfing has two governance problems that no other sport on this site combines:
an unresolved argument about whether it should be a competitive sport at all,
and a venue question with no good answer.

The first is cultural. A substantial part of surfing regards competition as
peripheral to the activity, and regards judging in particular as a category
error — you cannot mark a ride out of ten because the ride was a response to a
wave that will never happen again. The ISA governs a competitive sport inside a
much larger culture that is ambivalent about it.

The second is practical. A surfing competition requires waves, and an Olympic
host does not necessarily have any. In 2024 the answer was to hold the event at
Teahupo'o in Tahiti, fifteen thousand kilometres from Paris — a decision no
other sport on the programme has had to make, and one that drew objection from
residents and environmental groups over construction on a fragile reef.

For 2028 the answer is a wave pool, and it resolves the venue problem
completely while opening the cultural one wide. A machine-generated wave is
identical for every competitor, which makes the judging fair for the first time
in the sport's history. It also means the competition is no longer a response
to the ocean, which is what a large part of the sport thinks surfing is.

The federation has chosen fairness and stageability. It has not persuaded
everybody, and it has not really tried to.
