# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Drug testing, and the schism it caused.** The founding controversy and the
one that shaped everything after it. Testing did not clean up one sport; it
divided it, permanently, into tested and untested federations whose results are
kept in separate books that nobody reconciles.

**The bench shirt.** Supportive equipment moved from garment to device, and
federations answered differently — some allowing multi-ply, some single-ply,
some none. The IPF's eventual answer in 2012 was to stop choosing and run raw
as a separate division with its own records, which is a comparability break
adopted deliberately rather than suffered.

**Squat depth.** The rule is that the hip crease passes below the top of the
knee, judged by eye by three referees from three angles under a maximal load.
It is the most argued call in the sport, standards differ measurably between
federations, and it is a genuine reason their records are not comparable. No
federation has instrumented it.

**Redrawing the bodyweight categories.** The IPF changed its classes in 2011,
which annulled the records attached to the old ones. Weightlifting did the same
thing in 1993 and again in 2018. It is the standard tool of a strength sport
that wants a clean book, and it works by detaching the old numbers rather than
disproving them.

**The points coefficient.** Comparing lifters across bodyweight categories
requires a formula, the formula has been revised more than once, and each
revision changes the historical answer to who the best lifter at a given meet
was. A scoring coefficient is a rule, and this one has been rewritten with
retroactive effect.
