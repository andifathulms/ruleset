# Where it is played

Powerlifting's map is unusually flat, and the reason is that it needs almost
nothing.

The United States, Russia, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Japan, Great Britain, Sweden,
Norway, Finland and Poland all field strong programmes, and the sport has real
depth in places that have very little else on this site — a rack, a bench and a
loaded barbell are available in ordinary commercial gyms almost everywhere, and
a meet needs a platform and three referees.

Two things make its geography different from other strength sports. First,
there is no facility requirement beyond a gym: no pool, no track, no hall, no
snow. Second, the sport's growth since the 2010s has been driven by online
culture rather than by clubs or schools, which spread it into countries with no
federation history at all and produced a generation of competitors whose
introduction was a video rather than a coach.

Because federations are separate rather than territorial, geography and
institution cut across each other. A country may have several competing bodies
running meets in the same city on the same weekend under different rules, which
is a shape no other sport covered here has.

Women's participation has grown faster here than in most sports on this site
over the last decade, from a very low base, and the raw division is where most
of that growth is.
