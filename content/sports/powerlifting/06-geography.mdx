# Where it is played

Powerlifting needs a rack, a bench and a loaded bar, and ordinary commercial
gyms almost everywhere have all three. A meet needs a platform and three
referees. The sport is spread unusually evenly as a result.

<Photos ids="powerlifting-melitopol-2014 powerlifting-sweden-2013-bench-2" />

The United States, Russia, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Japan, Great Britain, Sweden,
Norway, Finland and Poland all have strong programmes. There is no pool, track
or hall to build first.

## A meet in a gym

<Photos ids="powerlifting-adlington-2007 powerlifting-spangdahlem-2025" />

In 2007 seven men in T-shirts lined up in a plain room at the Adlington
Barbell club in England, with a barbell on the floor in front of them. It was
the first Sugden Barbell meet. Entry-level competitions are still like this:
open, held in ordinary gyms, with beginners lifting in the same session as
champions.

Since the 2010s the sport has grown through online culture more than through
clubs or schools. That carried it into countries with no federation history,
and many new lifters first met the sport in a video and had no coach.

## Rival bodies in one city

Federations are separate bodies and do not divide the map between them. One
country may have several, running meets in the same city on the same weekend
under different rules.
