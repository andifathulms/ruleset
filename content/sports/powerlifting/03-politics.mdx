# Governing body politics

No sport covered here is as institutionally fractured as this one.

The IPF was formed in 1972 and introduced drug testing. Lifters and promoters
who objected — to the cost, to the methods, or to the principle — founded
alternatives, and the alternatives kept forming. The result is a sport with
many international federations, some strictly tested, some explicitly untested,
each with its own equipment rules, its own judging standards and its own world
records.

The consequences run all the way down to what a number means. A world record
total from a tested single-ply federation and one from an untested multi-ply
federation are both real results, honestly recorded, and cannot be set against
each other in any way that survives examination. There is no body with the
standing to arbitrate, because standing is exactly what is in dispute.

The IPF is the federation inside the international system: recognised, on the
World Games programme, and compliant with the world anti-doping framework.
That gives it a claim the others do not have, and it does not give it control
of the sport — a large fraction of competitive powerlifting happens outside it,
and the online culture that has driven the sport's growth since the 2010s does
not organise itself by federation at all.

There is one further institutional oddity. Para powerlifting is Paralympic,
contested as a bench press alone, and governed by World Para Powerlifting
rather than the IPF. The sport with the Paralympic place and the sport with the
World Games place are different organisations doing different things under
almost the same name.
