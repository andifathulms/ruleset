# One sport, many federations

Powerlifting has no single world record. It has one record list for each
federation, and the federations do not recognise each other.

## The split over testing

The International Powerlifting Federation was formed in 1972 and brought in
drug testing. Some lifters and promoters objected to the cost, some to the
methods, some to the principle. [They founded other
federations](#pl-federation-schism), and new ones kept forming. Later splits
came over equipment and judging.

Today some federations test strictly and some openly do not test at all. Each
has its own equipment rules, its own judging standards and its own world
records. Testing did not clean up one sport. It divided it into two kinds of
federation whose results nobody reconciles.

So a number means different things in different places. A record
<Term id="total">total</Term> from a tested federation that allows one layer
of equipment and a record from an untested federation that allows several are
both real results, honestly recorded. They cannot be set side by side. No body
has the standing to decide between them, because standing is the thing in
dispute.

## Inside the system and outside it

<Photo id="powerlifting-ipf-worlds-2022" />

The International Powerlifting Federation is the one inside the international
system. It is recognised, it is on the World Games programme, and it follows
the world anti-doping rules. That gives it a claim the others lack. It does
not give it control. A large share of competitive powerlifting happens outside
it, and the online culture that has driven the sport's growth since the 2010s
does not sort itself by federation.

## Records cut loose

Even inside one federation the record book has been restarted. [In 2011 the
federation redrew its bodyweight categories](#pl-2020-weight-classes), and the
records attached to the old <Term id="category">categories</Term> were
annulled. Some had stood since before the current testing rules.
[Weightlifting](/sports/weightlifting/) did the same in 1993 and again in
2018. The method does not disprove an old number. It leaves the number
attached to a category that no longer exists.

## A different sport with almost the same name

<Photos ids="powerlifting-sydney-2000-para powerlifting-atlanta-1996-para" />

Para powerlifting is a Paralympic sport. It is the bench press alone, and it
is governed by World Para Powerlifting, a separate body. Suzanne Twelftree of
Australia lifted in the 48 kg class at the Sydney Paralympics in 2000, lying
strapped to the bench. The sport with the Paralympic place and the sport with
the World Games place are run by different organisations and contest
different things.
