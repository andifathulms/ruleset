# Three referees, three angles

The weight on the bar is a fact. Whether the lift counts is a vote. Three
referees sit around the platform, one in front and one at each side. Each
presses white or red, and the majority decides. No sensor is involved anywhere
in the sport.

## The squat

<Photo id="powerlifting-referee-squat-depth" />

<Term id="depth">Depth</Term> is the main call. The crease of the hip must
pass below the top of the knee. Under a maximum load the lowest point of a
squat lasts a fraction of a second. The lifter is wearing a belt and perhaps a
suit, and three officials are judging a joint from three lines of sight. In
the photograph a side referee crouches to watch for exactly this.

<Photo id="powerlifting-europeans-2022-squat" />

It is the most argued call in powerlifting. Federations differ in how strictly
they judge it, and that is one documented reason their records cannot be
pooled.

## The bench press

<Photo id="powerlifting-sweden-2013-bench" />

The bar must <Term id="pause">pause</Term> without moving on the chest. The
lifter's buttocks must stay on the bench, the bar must not sink during the
press, and there are three <Term id="commands">commands</Term> to obey. More
bench attempts at the top level fail on a command or a pause than on strength.

## The deadlift

<Photos ids="powerlifting-deadlift-start powerlifting-deadlift-lockout" />

The bar must not be <Term id="hitching">hitched</Term> or ramped up the
thighs, and the lifter must finish upright with the shoulders back. There is
no command to start. The lift ends when the referee says "down".

## No machines

Other sports have handed calls like these to equipment.
[Shooting](/sports/shooting/) put sensors in its targets,
[fencing](/sports/fencing/) wired its weapons,
[taekwondo](/sports/taekwondo/) put sensors in the socks, and
[swimming](/sports/swimming/) has touchpads. A camera could plausibly measure
squat depth. No powerlifting federation has tried it.
