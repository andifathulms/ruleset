# Officiating

Powerlifting is a measured sport whose measurement is gated entirely by human
judgement, which is a stranger combination than it sounds.

The weight on the bar is a fact. Whether the lift counts is decided by three
referees — one in front, one at each side — pressing white or red, with the
majority deciding. There is no appeal to a sensor anywhere in the sport.

**Squat depth** is the central call. The hip crease must pass below the top of
the knee. Under a maximal load a squat's lowest position lasts a fraction of a
second, the lifter is wearing a belt and possibly a suit, and three officials
are judging a joint angle from three different lines of sight. Federations
differ in strictness, and this is not a rumour about them — it is a documented
reason their records cannot be pooled.

**The bench press** is judged on a motionless pause on the chest, on the
lifter's buttocks staying in contact, on no downward movement of the bar during
the press, and on obeying three separate commands. More elite bench attempts
fail on commands and pauses than on strength.

**The deadlift** is judged on the bar not being hitched or ramped up the thighs
and on the lifter finishing upright with shoulders back. It has no start
command, only a "down".

What is notable by comparison with the rest of this site is what powerlifting
has never adopted. Shooting instrumented its targets, fencing wired its
weapons, taekwondo put sensors in the socks, and swimming has touchpads. A
sport that could plausibly measure squat depth with a camera has, across every
one of its federations, declined to.
