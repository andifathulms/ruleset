# Origin and invention

Powerlifting is what was left over.

Through the first half of the twentieth century, weightlifting competition
included a shifting menu of lifts, and the international sport gradually
narrowed to the ones that were most athletic and most spectacular — ending, by
1972, with the snatch and the clean and jerk alone. The lifts that were dropped
did not stop being contested. In the United States and Britain especially,
meets continued for the "odd lifts": the squat, the bench press, the deadlift,
the curl, the press.

By the 1960s those meets had consolidated around three. The squat, bench press
and deadlift are the movements that let a person express maximal strength with
the least technical mediation, which is precisely why the Olympic sport had
discarded them and precisely why a rival sport could be built on them.

The first American national championship was held in 1965 and the International
Powerlifting Federation was formed in 1972 — the same year Olympic weightlifting
finished narrowing itself. The IPF fixed the order, three attempts at each, and
the total.

What followed is the part that defines the sport now. Drug testing arrived, and
lifters and promoters who objected founded alternative federations. Supportive
equipment arrived, and federations disagreed about how much to allow. Judging
standards for squat depth diverged. Each split produced another body with
another record book, and none of them ever merged.

So powerlifting was assembled from a larger sport's offcuts and then broke into
pieces itself. It is on the World Games programme and has never been on the
Olympic one.
