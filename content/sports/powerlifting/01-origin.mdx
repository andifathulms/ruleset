# The odd lifts

Powerlifting grew from the lifts that the Olympic sport did not use.

Through the first half of the twentieth century,
[weightlifting](/sports/weightlifting/) competitions used a changing list of
lifts. The international sport slowly narrowed, and by 1972 it kept only two,
both of which put the bar overhead. Other lifts went on being contested
outside it. In the United States and Britain especially, meets were held for
the "odd lifts": the <Term id="squat">squat</Term>, the
<Term id="bench-press">bench press</Term>, the
<Term id="deadlift">deadlift</Term>, the curl, the press. The list changed
from one meet to the next.

By the 1960s those meets had settled on three. The squat, the bench press and
the deadlift let a person show as much strength as possible with little
technique in the way. The first American national championship was held in
1965.

Lifters under different regional rules still had totals that could not be
compared. [The International Powerlifting Federation was formed in
1972](#pl-1972-ipf-founded), the year the Olympic sport finished narrowing. It
fixed the three lifts, their order, three
<Term id="attempt">attempts</Term> at each, and the
<Term id="total">total</Term>.

<Photos ids="powerlifting-us-nationals-1982-squat powerlifting-us-nationals-1982-deadlift" />

Two photographs survive from the United States national championships in
Portland, Oregon, ten years later. In one a lifter in a singlet squats 374
pounds between two stands, with spotters and officials around him.
In the other a lifter pulls a deadlift of 418 pounds in front of the
championship banner.

Then the sport divided. Drug testing arrived, and lifters and promoters who
objected founded their own federations. Supportive equipment arrived, and
federations disagreed about how much to allow. Standards for squat
<Term id="depth">depth</Term> drifted apart. Each split produced another body
with another record book, and none of them ever merged.

Powerlifting is on the programme of the World Games. It has never been an
Olympic sport.
