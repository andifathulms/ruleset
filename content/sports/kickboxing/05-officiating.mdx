# A different job in every code

The referee's work in kickboxing depends on the discipline.

In <Term id="full-contact">full contact</Term> someone has to count kicks.
Each fighter owes a <Term id="minimum-kicks">minimum number</Term> every
round. The count is kept in real time while the bout is being judged, and a
fighter who falls short is warned and then loses points.

<Term id="point-fighting">Point fighting</Term> stops and starts. The referee
halts the bout at every scoring technique, the point is awarded by a vote of
three referees, and the fighters go back to the centre. The question is who
landed first, and with what. Speed and timing decide it, and damage does not
build up. In structure it is a [fencing](/sports/fencing/) bout fought with
hands and feet.

<Photo id="kickboxing-point-fighting" />

The continuous ring codes are refereed as [boxing](/sports/boxing/) is. The
referee deals with fouls, knockdowns and standing counts, and breaks every
<Term id="clinch">clinch</Term>. [Muaythai](/sports/muaythai/) lets its
fighters work inside the clinch, and it allows elbows. Those two rules are
the clearest lines between the two sports.

Legal targets cause the most trouble. A kick below the waist is a foul in
full contact and the whole idea of low kick. At a championship with several
disciplines, an official applies different definitions of a foul on the same
weekend.
