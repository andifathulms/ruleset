# Officiating

The referee's job changes completely depending on which discipline is being
contested, and one call exists nowhere else in sport.

**Counting kicks.** In full contact, a fighter must throw a minimum number of
kicks per round. Somebody has to count them, in real time, while also judging
the bout — and issue a warning and then a points deduction if the quota is not
met. It is the only technique quota enforced by an official on this site.

**Point fighting** is a stop-start discipline. The referee halts the bout at
every scoring technique, judges award the point, and the fighters restart at
the centre. The judgement is about which fighter landed first and with what —
speed and priority rather than damage — and it has the same character as
fencing's before the weapons were wired.

**The continuous codes** are refereed as boxing is: fouls, knockdowns, standing
counts, and breaking a clinch rather than allowing it to be fought in. That
last point separates kickboxing from muaythai as sharply as the elbow rule
does.

**Legal targets** are the recurring difficulty across the codes. In full
contact a kick below the waist is a foul; in low kick it is the point of the
sport. An official working a multi-discipline championship is applying
different definitions of a foul on the same weekend.

Nothing is instrumented. Taekwondo faced comparable scoring disputes and
answered with sensors in the socks and the body protector; kickboxing has kept
everything on the eye of the judge.
