# Origin and invention

Kickboxing is what happened when karate practitioners decided they wanted to
hit each other properly.

Competitive karate scored controlled techniques and stopped the bout to award
them, which is a coherent way to contest a martial art and left a group of
practitioners dissatisfied. Through the 1960s and 1970s, in the United States,
Japan and Europe more or less independently, they built formats in which
strikes were thrown at full force and the bout ran continuously — "full contact
karate", as the American version was called before the name changed.

Each country's version borrowed a different amount from boxing. Rings, gloves,
rounds, weight categories and the ten-point must scoring system all came
across, and what was added on top was kicks.

That inheritance produced the sport's characteristic problem almost
immediately. Kicks are slower than punches, more tiring, and easier to counter,
so a fighter trying to win rather than to demonstrate a style has a standing
incentive to stop throwing them. Early full-contact bouts drifted towards
boxing with heavier gloves, and the answer — still in the rulebook — was to
require a minimum number of kicks per round.

WAKO was formed in 1976 to consolidate the national versions, and it never
consolidated them into one. It has governed a family of codes from the
beginning: full contact, low kick, K-1 style, point fighting, light contact,
kick light, and judged musical forms.

Provisional IOC recognition came in 2018, full recognition in 2021, and a World
Games place from 2022. There has never been an Olympic place.
