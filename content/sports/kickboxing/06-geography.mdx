# Where it is played

Europe competitively, the world recreationally, and Japan commercially.

**Europe** is where WAKO's competitive strength is concentrated. Italy, Russia,
Ukraine, Hungary, Greece, Serbia, Croatia, Germany, England and Ireland all
field deep squads, and the federation's championships are substantially
European events with international participation. WAKO itself was founded in
Europe and has always been headquartered there.

**Japan** produced the commercial version of the sport that most of the world
has actually seen. Japanese kickboxing promotions built the ring formats that
gave the K-1 discipline its name, and for a period they were among the largest
combat sports properties anywhere. That commercial history is why a discipline
in an amateur federation's rulebook is named after a promotion.

**Recreationally**, kickboxing is enormous and almost entirely disconnected from
any of this. Bag and pad classes are a staple of commercial fitness worldwide,
and the overwhelming majority of people who say they do kickboxing have never
entered a competition, do not know which federation would sanction one, and are
not training in any of the six disciplines.

That gap — between an activity with vast global participation and a competitive
structure that is regionally concentrated and institutionally divided — is
wider here than for almost any sport covered on this site. Muaythai has the
same shape and at least has an unambiguous competitive summit. Kickboxing does
not.
