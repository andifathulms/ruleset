# Equipment evolution

Equipment in kickboxing tells you which discipline you are in before anyone
throws anything.

**The shared kit** is gloves, wraps, mouthguard, groin guard and shin
protection. That much is common to every code the federation runs.

**Foot protectors** mark the tatami disciplines. Point fighting, light contact
and kick light are contested with padded feet, because a padded foot makes a
scoring kick safe at speed — and would be an active obstacle in a low-kick bout,
where the shin is the striking surface and the target is a thigh.

**Headguards** follow the same logic: standard in the light codes, variable in
the ring codes by category and level. Boxing removed them for senior men in
2016 on the argument that the evidence did not support them; the kickboxing
codes that kept them are the ones where the contact was already restrained.

**The surface** is equipment too. The full-contact codes are fought in a roped
ring; the light codes on a matted tatami. A federation whose disciplines are
staged on two different surfaces, in two different kits, with two different
scoring systems, is running more than one sport, and the equipment list is the
plainest evidence of it.

What the sport has never needed is an implement, a target sensor or any
instrumentation. Taekwondo put sensors in the socks and body protectors to
resolve its scoring disputes; kickboxing, with the same problem, has not.
