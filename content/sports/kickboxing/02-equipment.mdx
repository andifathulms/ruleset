# What the kit says about the code

A kickboxer's equipment shows which discipline is being fought before anyone
throws a strike.

Every code shares the same base: gloves, hand wraps, a mouthguard, a groin
guard and shin protection.

<Term id="foot-protectors">Foot protectors</Term> mark the mat disciplines.
Point fighting, light contact and kick light are fought with padded feet,
because a padded foot lets a kick score safely at speed. In a
<Term id="low-kick">low kick</Term> bout the same padding would get in the
way. There the shin is the striking surface and the target is a thigh.

<Photos ids="kickboxing-light-contact kickboxing-k1-style" />

Headguards follow the same pattern. They are standard in the light codes. In
the ring codes they depend on the category and on whether the competition is
amateur.

<Photo id="kickboxing-full-contact" />

The floor differs too. The full-force codes are fought in a roped ring, and
the light codes on a matted <Term id="tatami">tatami</Term>, as
[karate](/sports/karate/) is. Two surfaces, two kits and two scoring systems
sit inside one federation.

<Photos ids="kickboxing-kick-light kickboxing-wako-pro-2011" />

Kickboxing has no scoring instruments at all.
[Taekwondo](/sports/taekwondo/) put sensors in the socks and the body
protector to settle its scoring disputes. Kickboxing has the same kind of
dispute and still relies on the judge's eye.
