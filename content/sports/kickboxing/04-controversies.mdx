# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Fighters who stopped kicking.** The founding problem and the sport's most
revealing rule. Early full-contact competition showed that fighters left to
optimise freely would abandon kicks, because they are slower, more tiring and
easier to counter than punches — turning a sport defined by kicks into boxing.
The answer was a minimum kick count per round, enforced by warning and then by
points deduction. Very few sports on this site have had to make their athletes
use the technique the sport is named after.

**Whether the legs are a target.** Full contact bars kicks below the waist
entirely; low kick permits them; K-1 style adds knees. Rather than settle the
question, the federation ran all three as separate disciplines. It is a
legitimate answer and it means the sport has no single set of rules.

**Point fighting's stoppages.** Halting the bout after every scoring technique
makes the discipline safe and legible and makes it a fundamentally different
contest from the ring codes — closer to fencing than to boxing. Whether it
belongs in the same federation is periodically argued.

**Multiple world champions.** Rival sanctioning bodies award competing titles.
IOC recognition went to one federation, which settled the question for the
Olympic movement and for nobody else.

**Judging.** Continuous kickboxing is scored round by round by eye, with the
same difficulties boxing has had — and boxing's response, first electronic
scoring buttons in 1992 and then their abandonment in 2016, is a cautionary
history this sport has watched without acting on.
