# Many world champions

Kickboxing has more than one world champion at every weight, because it has
more than one world body.

WAKO is the federation the Olympic movement recognises. It has a large
membership and a big championship programme, and it holds the place at the
World Games.

Several other international organisations sanction kickboxing and award world
titles of their own. Beside them are commercial promotions with their own
rules, and most of the sport's money and audience is there. A fighter can be
world champion under a body the Olympic movement does not acknowledge.
Nothing reconciles one claim with another.

[Powerlifting](/sports/powerlifting/) has the same structure: rival
federations of similar standing. [Sumo](/sports/sumo/) and
[muaythai](/sports/muaythai/) have a related one, in which a national
professional institution sits outside the international federation.

The split inside WAKO adds to the difficulty. Its six fighting disciplines
disagree on whether the legs may be kicked and whether knees are legal. That
breadth makes kickboxing hard to offer as one Olympic sport. A place on the
programme would mean choosing one code, and that choice would leave out most
of the federation's own athletes.

WAKO has been fully recognised since 2021. No place on the Olympic programme
has followed.
