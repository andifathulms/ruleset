# Equipment evolution

A pack of cards, and then three inventions whose entire purpose is to stop
information leaking.

**Duplicate boards** hold each player's thirteen cards in a numbered slot so
that the deal survives being played and can be passed to the next table intact.
Without them there is no duplicate bridge, and without duplicate bridge there is
no competitive bridge — the cards would decide too much.

**Bidding boxes** replaced spoken bids with printed cards laid on the table.
The stated reason is noise and clarity; the real one is tone. A bid made after
a pause, or briskly, or reluctantly, tells a partner something the rules forbid
them to be told, and a card laid on a table has no tone. It is a piece of
equipment designed to remove a channel of communication from the game.

**Screens** go further. At the highest level a diagonal barrier crosses the
table so that partners cannot see each other at all, with bids passed
underneath on a sliding tray. A player can therefore see one opponent and their
partner is invisible to them. Screens were adopted after cheating scandals in
which partnerships were accused of signalling by gesture, expression and card
placement, and they are the most direct physical anti-cheating apparatus on
this site.

**The convention card** is the opposite kind of object: a written summary of a
partnership's private agreements, kept on the table for the opponents to read.

So bridge's equipment list divides neatly. Some of it exists to keep your
partner from learning things. The rest exists to make sure your opponents do.
