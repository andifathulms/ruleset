# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether it is a sport.** Answered no by a European court in 2017 for tax
purposes, answered yes by the IOC's recognition and by the Asian Games. The
question has funding consequences in several countries and no settled answer.

**Online cheating.** Screens and bidding boxes cannot exist on a website, and
a partnership communicating by any outside channel is very difficult to detect.
The sport has built statistical detection and high-profile cases have divided
it, because the evidence is probabilistic and the accusations are career-ending.

**Unauthorised information.** The rule that penalises the innocent partner is
correct in principle and produces rulings that feel unjust to the person
penalised. Every serious event generates disputes about it, and the appellate
process exists because of it.

**System regulation.** How artificial a partnership's bidding system may be is
capped, and the caps are argued between those who see complex systems as the
sport's intellectual frontier and those who see them as a barrier to entry.

**The demographic.** Bridge's population is old and shrinking across most of
its strongholds. Federations know it, school programmes are the main response,
and nothing has yet reversed it.

**Whether the Asian Games place survives.** Bridge appeared in 2018. Its
presence on any programme depends on hosts willing to stage a sport that
requires quiet rooms and produces no spectacle, and there is no reliable
argument the federation can make about that.
