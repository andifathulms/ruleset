# Where it is played

Bridge is strongest in Europe, in the United States and in China, and in most
of those places its players are growing old.

Italy, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Poland, France, England and
Germany all have deep club systems and strong national teams. Italian teams
have dominated long stretches of the game's history. European teams were on
top early too: Austria won the world title in 1937.

The United States has the largest single national organisation and a busy
tournament circuit. China has invested seriously and is now one of the
leading nations. India, Indonesia, Japan, Chinese Taipei and Singapore all
compete, and Indonesia's hosting brought bridge to the Asian Games in 2018.

## An ageing game

In most of its strongholds, bridge's players are older than in other sports,
and there are fewer of them each year. Clubs that ran full sessions for
decades are closing. New players are hard to find, because the game asks a
lot before it starts: four people, in two partnerships, and a bidding system
that both partners must learn together.

Where bridge is growing, it is mostly because a national body has put it into
schools. China and several European federations have done this. Online play
has added players, but it has not replaced the clubs that teach them.
