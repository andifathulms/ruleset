# Officiating

A tournament director rules on things that already happened, and often on what
would have happened instead.

**Irregularities** are the routine work: a revoke, a lead out of turn, a bid
out of turn, an insufficient bid. The Laws prescribe rectifications for each,
and applying them correctly is technical rather than judgemental.

**Unauthorised information** is where the difficulty is, and it has no
counterpart on this site. When a player hesitates, or asks a question, or
fidgets, their partner has received information they were not entitled to. The
director must then decide whether the partner's subsequent action was one that
a player *without* that information could reasonably have chosen. If it was
not, the score is adjusted.

That involves ruling on a counterfactual — what would probably have happened at
this table had the infraction not occurred — and directors consult expert
opinion on what actions were "logical alternatives" for a player of that
standard. It is the only officiating decision covered here that requires
reconstructing an alternative history of the contest.

**Adjusted scores** follow from that, and they are unusual as a remedy: rather
than penalising, the director substitutes the result that probably would have
occurred.

**Screens** shift the officiating burden into the equipment. With partners
unable to see each other, a whole class of infraction becomes physically
impossible and does not need to be judged at all.

Appeals committees hear disputes on the ruling, which makes bridge one of very
few sports here with a routine appellate process on individual decisions.
