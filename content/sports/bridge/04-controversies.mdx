# Signals and suspicion

## Messages outside the bidding

A pair is allowed to talk through its bids and cards, and through nothing
else. That is why cheating by any other channel is so damaging, and so hard
to prove. Major championships have been disrupted by accusations of coded
coughs, finger positions, the placing of cards, and the angle at which a
board was held.

The answers were physical. The <Term id="bidding-box">bidding box</Term> took
away the voice. The <Term id="screen">screen</Term> took away the sight of a
partner. Both make a kind of cheating impossible, where a rule could only make
it forbidden.

## Bridge goes online

Online play grew sharply, and it brought a new problem. A website has no
screen and no bidding box. Two partners talking over a video call cannot be
seen from across a server. Several high-profile cheating cases followed, and
the sport had to build statistical ways of spotting cheats that it had never
needed at the table.

## How clever a system may be

Highly artificial bidding systems can describe hands very precisely, and they
are hard for opponents to follow even when [fully
disclosed](#bridge-full-disclosure). So the regulations limit which
conventions may be used at which level of play. The rule caps how
sophisticated a pair's private language is allowed to be.
