# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Signalling between partners.** Bridge's defining controversy and the source
of most of its apparatus. Because a partnership legitimately communicates
through bids and cards, illegitimate communication through anything else is
both devastating and hard to prove. Major championships have been disrupted by
accusations of coded coughs, finger positions, card placement and the angle at
which a board was held.

The responses were physical. **Bidding boxes** removed tone of voice.
**Screens** removed sight of a partner entirely. Both are equipment introduced
to make a category of cheating impossible rather than merely illegal.

**Unauthorised information.** The subtler and more common problem, and the one
that produces bridge's strangest rule. A hesitation transmits something even
when nobody intended it — and the law falls on the *innocent* partner, who must
then choose among their available actions the one least suggested by what they
were not supposed to know. A player can be penalised for doing the right thing
for a reason they were not entitled to have.

**Online play.** Screens cannot be built into a website, and a pair on a video
call is undetectable across a server. The move online produced cheating cases
and forced the sport to develop statistical detection it had never needed.

**Whether it is a sport.** Settled against bridge in a 2017 tax ruling, unsettled
everywhere else, and consequential for funding in several countries.

**System complexity.** Highly artificial bidding systems are powerful and
opaque, and regulation limits which conventions may be used at which level — a
rule limiting how sophisticated a partnership's private language may be.
