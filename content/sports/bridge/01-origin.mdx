# From whist to contract

Contract bridge was made by changing one scoring rule, in 1925.

The game comes from whist. Whist had four players in two partnerships, and
they played for <Term id="trick">tricks</Term>. It had no auction at all.
Bridge whist and then auction bridge came after it, and by the early 1920s
auction bridge was the main form.

<Photo id="bridge-whist" />

Auction bridge had an <Term id="auction">auction</Term>, but the auction did
not matter much. Tricks won above the number a side had bid still counted
towards game. So bidding too high cost nothing, and a bid told a partner very
little.

## The revision on the cruise

Harold Vanderbilt changed that. In the well-known account, he worked out the
new scoring on a cruise through the Panama Canal in 1925. Under his rule, only
the tricks a side had promised to take counted towards game. A side that
failed was punished, and more heavily when it was vulnerable. Bid too high and
you lose points. Bid too low and you waste a good hand. This is [the change
that created contract bridge](#bridge-1925-contract).

<Photo id="bridge-vanderbilt" />

After it, the auction was the heart of the game. A bid became a precise
statement about a hand. The pair that described its two hands to each other
most exactly reached the right <Term id="contract">contract</Term>.

Eight years later, contract bridge was a public event. The Culbertson–Beasley
match of 1933 was played in front of an audience, by people who were writing
the game's bidding systems.

<Photo id="bridge-culbertson-beasley-1933" />

## Taking the luck out of the deal

In rubber bridge, the social game, every hand is freshly dealt, and the cards
decide a great deal. Competition needed something else. In
<Term id="duplicate">duplicate</Term> play [the same deals are played at every
table](#bridge-duplicate-play), and each result is compared with the others.
A pair dealt a poor hand loses nothing by it, because every pair in the same
seat held the same poor hand. That is what turned a card game into a contest
of skill.

The World Bridge Federation was formed in 1958.
