# Origin and invention

Contract bridge was devised in 1925 and the change that created it was a
scoring rule.

The game descends from whist, through bridge whist and auction bridge, and by
the early 1920s auction bridge was the dominant form. It had an auction, and
the auction did not matter much: tricks made above the contracted level still
counted towards game, so bidding aggressively cost nothing and the bidding
conveyed little.

Harold Vanderbilt's revision — worked out, in the well-known account, on a
cruise through the Panama Canal in 1925 — was to credit towards game only the
tricks a partnership had actually contracted to make, and to penalise failure
according to vulnerability. Overbid and you are punished; underbid and you have
wasted the hand.

That turned the auction into the game. A bid became a precise statement about a
hand, and the partnership that describes two hands to each other most
accurately reaches the right contract. Everything distinctive about bridge
follows from that one scoring change.

The competitive form followed. **Duplicate** play — the same deals at every
table, scored by comparison — removed the luck of the deal, which is what made
bridge a sport rather than a card game. The World Bridge Federation was formed
in 1958.

Bridge was contested at the Asian Games in 2018. It holds IOC recognition and
has never been on an Olympic programme — and in 2017 a European court ruled
that it is not a sport at all, for the purposes of a tax exemption, on the
ground that it lacks a significant physical element.
