# Where it is played

Squash is played mostly in countries that Britain once ruled or influenced,
and for twenty years it has been won mostly by Egypt.

## Egypt, and Pakistan before it

Egypt leads both the men's and the women's game by a long way. Its strength
comes from a tight group of clubs in Cairo and Alexandria and from a coaching
tradition that builds on itself. It was built without much wealth, on courts
that were already there.

Pakistan dominated in a similar way from the 1950s into the 1990s, through
the players of the Khan family.

England, Australia, New Zealand, Malaysia and France are also strong, and the
United States is growing stronger.

## The disappearing court

Squash needs a closed room built for the purpose. For a business that runs a
sports club, a court is a poor use of floor space. In Britain and Australia
especially, courts are being turned into gyms and studios. A sport that loses
its courts loses its players, and this has been happening in some of the
oldest homes of squash for twenty years.

<Photo id="squash-club-court" />

This is part of why the Olympic place for 2028 matters so much to the sport.
Being seen brings money for buildings, and the federation has said openly
that it wants the Games for that reason.
