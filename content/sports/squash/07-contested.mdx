# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The let.** Still the sport's central dispute and still unresolved. Video
review informs it and cannot decide it, because the question is what would have
happened rather than what did. Proposals to reduce the referee's discretion —
by defining interference more tightly, or by removing the stroke as an outcome
— circulate and have not been adopted, because every tighter definition
produces its own unfairness.

**Whether the tin should come down further.** Lowering it in 2001 shortened
rallies and rewarded attacking play, and there is a live argument that it did
not go far enough and that elite squash remains attritional. The counter is
that squash's rallies are the sport, and shortening them further makes it
something else.

**Courts.** The sport is losing facilities in several of its traditional
strongholds because a squash court is a commercially poor use of floor space.
No rule can fix this, and Olympic inclusion is the federation's stated
strategy for reversing it.

**Whether twenty-four years of redesign was worth it.** Squash changed its
walls, its scoring, its court dimensions and its officiating largely in pursuit
of Olympic admission, and got it in 2023 for 2028. Whether the sport that will
appear at those Games is better than the one that started campaigning, or
merely more televisable, is a question its own players disagree about.
