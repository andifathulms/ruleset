# What is still argued about

**The let.** This is still the main dispute in the sport. Some want
<Term id="interference">interference</Term> defined more tightly, to leave
the referee less to judge. Others want to remove the
<Term id="stroke">stroke</Term> as a possible answer. Neither idea has been
adopted, because every tighter definition brings an unfairness of its own.

**A lower tin still.** Some argue that the change of 2001 did not go far
enough, and that the top level of the game is still a contest of fitness. The
reply is that long rallies are what squash is.

**Courts.** Clubs in several of the sport's old homes keep closing courts. No
rule of the game can stop that. The federation's stated plan is to use the
Olympic Games to turn it around.

**Was it worth it?** Squash changed its walls, its scoring, its
<Term id="tin">tin</Term> and its refereeing, largely to win a place at the
Olympics. Its own players disagree about whether the game is now better or
only easier to televise.
