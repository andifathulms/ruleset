# Where it came from

Squash was invented by schoolboys who were waiting to play something else.

At Harrow School in the nineteenth century the game was rackets, a fast game
with a hard ball played in a walled court. Rackets was itself an Olympic
sport in 1908. Boys waiting for a court knocked a punctured ball about
against the walls outside. A punctured ball squashed when it hit the wall and
did not spring back hard. It was slower, and it died in the corners. The game
that came out of it needed more running and less power. The ball squashes,
and that is the name.

Two things from those beginnings still define the sport. The first is the
closed court, taken from rackets, which puts both players on the same floor.
The hardest rules in squash, the ones about
<Term id="interference">interference</Term>, follow from that. The second is
the soft ball, which plays properly only once it is warm. It produces long
<Term id="rally">rallies</Term>, and long rallies made squash a game for the
very fit.

The international federation was formed in 1967.
