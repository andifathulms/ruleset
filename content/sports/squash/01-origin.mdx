# Origin and invention

Squash was invented by accident, by schoolboys, in a game they were waiting to
play.

At Harrow School in the nineteenth century, boys waiting for a court at rackets
— a fast, hard-balled game played in a walled court, itself an Olympic sport in
1908 — knocked a punctured ball about against the walls outside. The punctured
ball squashed on impact rather than rebounding hard, which made it slower, made
it die in the corners, and produced a game requiring more retrieval and less
raw power.

That is the whole origin, and the name is descriptive: the ball squashes.

Two inherited features still define the sport. The **enclosed court**, taken
from rackets, means both players occupy the same space — every other racket
sport on this site separates them with a net — and everything difficult in the
rulebook follows from that. And the **soft ball**, which only performs properly
once warmed by play, produces the long rallies that made squash a fitness sport
and eventually a broadcast problem.

The international federation formed in 1967. Squash bid for the Olympic
programme for 2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024 and was rejected each time, and was
finally admitted for 2028 — twenty-four years after the scoring change made
largely to secure it.
