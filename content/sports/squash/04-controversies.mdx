# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Nobody could see it.** Squash's founding problem was not a scandal. A
conventional court is a sealed box with a small gallery, and there is nowhere
to put a camera or a crowd. A sport that cannot be watched cannot be sold, and
squash spent decades in that position.

The all-glass court in the 1980s is one of the more elegant solutions on this
site: rather than change the game, change what the walls are made of.

**Matches that would not end.** Under hand-in hand-out scoring, only the server
could score, so a game had no bounded length. A world-class match could run
past two hours with the scoreboard barely moving, and no broadcaster could
schedule it.

Point-a-rally scoring in 2004 fixed that and severed the sport's statistics
doing it — the fourth federation on this site to make exactly that trade in
seven years, after volleyball, table tennis and before badminton.

**Attrition.** By 2000 elite squash had become a fitness contest: rallies of
enormous length, decided by who broke first. Lowering the tin by two inches
enlarged the target and rewarded a player who went for a winner, which is a
physical change to the court made to alter the character of the sport.

**The let.** Squash's permanent controversy and the one nothing has fixed.
Because both players share the court, interference is constant, and the referee
must decide between a let, a stroke and no let — three outcomes turning on what
would have happened. Video review arrived in 2019 and has informed those calls
without making them any less contested.
