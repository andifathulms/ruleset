# Governing body politics

Squash's governance for the last quarter of a century has been a single
project: getting into the Olympic Games. It bid for 2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024,
was rejected each time, and was admitted for 2028.

Read the rule changes on this page in that light and they stop being separate
decisions. The glass court made the sport filmable. Point-a-rally scoring in
2004 made match length predictable. Lowering the tin in 2001 shortened rallies
and rewarded attacking play. Video review in 2019 addressed the sport's most
visible officiating weakness.

Each is defensible on its own terms and the cumulative direction is
unmistakable. Squash is the clearest case on this site of a federation
redesigning its sport to satisfy an external admissions criterion, and — unlike
modern pentathlon, which is doing the same thing to stay in — it worked.

That should be recorded carefully, because the causal claim is unusually
strong. Most sports here changed a rule and then discovered the consequences.
Squash's federation stated the objective, made the changes, and got the result,
over twenty-four years.

The internal politics is the usual professional-tour question. The men's and
women's tours are commercially separate from the federation, prize money parity
was achieved comparatively early, and the professional game adopted
point-a-rally before the amateur game did — the same order badminton's change
followed, and for the same reason: the tour has the broadcast contracts.
