# Fast, and would it have worked?

A lifesaving official judges two things in the same race. Was the competitor
fast? Would the rescue have worked?

The clock is the ordinary half. Pool events are timed as swimming is, with
touchpads and lane judges.

The technique is the other half, and it has one penalty:
<Term id="disqualification">disqualification</Term>. A
<Term id="manikin">manikin</Term> whose
<Term id="airway">airway</Term> goes under the surface is a disqualification.
So is a carry with a hold that is not approved, a dropped manikin, coming up
during an underwater section, or a wrong release at the wall. There is almost
no scale of penalties. The rescue was valid or it was not.

<Photos ids="lifesaving-airway lifesaving-tube-clip" />

None of this is measured by a machine. An official stands at the poolside and
judges the position of the manikin's face by eye.

This strictness has a reason. Any timed event pushes competitors to find
something quicker. In lifesaving that search ends, sooner or later, in a
carry, a <Term id="tow">tow</Term> or a release that is faster and would save
nobody. So [the rules say that technique must follow current rescue
practice](#lifesaving-technique-follows-practice).

## The event with no clock

In the <Term id="serc">simulated emergency response</Term>, a team meets a
staged incident it has not seen before. Officials mark how the team reads the
scene, how it responds and how it resuscitates, against the protocols in
force. The officials hold rescue qualifications, not sporting ones.

When the bodies that write resuscitation guidance change it, the marking
changes too. A team that trained the old sequence has trained the wrong
answer. The people who redefine correct technique here have nothing to do with
sport.

## On the sand

<Term id="beach-flags">Beach flags</Term> is judged on the beach. The officials
check that each start from the face-down position was legal. When two
competitors dive for the same <Term id="baton">baton</Term> and collide, they
decide whether one blocked the other. It is the most argued call in the sport.
