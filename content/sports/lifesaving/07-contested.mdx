# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether competition serves prevention.** The federation's stated position is
that the sport exists to support drowning-prevention work. How much resource
should go to elite competition rather than to education in the countries where
people are actually drowning is a live internal argument, and it is a question
no other federation on this site has to ask itself.

**Pool or ocean.** Two programmes, two traditions, one federation since 1993.
The pool events travel and appear at the World Games; the ocean events are the
older competitive culture and cannot be held without a surf beach. Which is the
sport is not settled.

**Technique versus speed.** The rule that competition technique must reflect
real rescue practice is the sport's foundation and a permanent source of
friction, because every timed event generates pressure towards something
faster. Where the line sits is redrawn whenever guidance changes.

**Beach flags safety.** The event with the highest injury rate in a sport
dedicated to preventing harm. Rule adjustments to the start and to contact have
been made and the tension has not gone away.

**Olympic candidacy.** Recognition and a World Games place, and a sport that
looks like swimming with obstacles to anyone watching it cold. Whether to
campaign at all, given the federation's other priorities, is genuinely open in
a way it is not for the other sports here.

**Standardising the unstandardisable.** Ocean results depend on conditions that
differ between heats. The sport records the times and declines to treat them as
records, which is honest and leaves it without the record-progression story
that most measured sports use to explain themselves.
