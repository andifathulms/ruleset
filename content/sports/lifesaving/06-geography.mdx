# Two maps that do not match

The countries that win at lifesaving are not the countries where most people
drown.

The sport belongs to Australia and New Zealand. Their surf lifesaving clubs
are large and wealthy, and beach patrol is a mass volunteer activity there as
it is almost nowhere else. The clubs start with children, called nippers.

It belongs also to Germany, Italy, France, the Netherlands and Belgium, whose
pool tradition goes back to the federation of 1910. Japan and Canada compete
strongly. These countries win almost everything.

The drowning is somewhere else. South and Southeast Asia and sub-Saharan
Africa have the large majority of the world's drowning deaths. Few people
there have access to swimming lessons, and there is almost no organised
lifesaving sport.

The federation aims its prevention work at the second map and holds its
competitions on the first. The sport needs pools, patrolled beaches and
volunteer clubs, and all of these cost money. So the countries that win the
world championships are not the countries the federation exists to help.

There is a second split inside the sport. Ocean events are strong in countries
with surf beaches and a patrol tradition. Pool events can go anywhere that has
a fifty-metre pool. They make up most of the World Games programme, and a
European nation can be a world power in half of the sport.
