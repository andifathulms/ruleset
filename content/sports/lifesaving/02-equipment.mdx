# Equipment made for rescue

Every main object in lifesaving is a piece of rescue equipment, and that
limits how it can change.

The <Term id="manikin">manikin</Term> is the best known. It is a weighted
torso of a set size that fills with water. A competitor must fetch it from a
set depth and bring it in with its <Term id="airway">airway</Term> clear of
the surface. Its size and weight are fixed so that the
<Term id="carry">carry</Term> in a race is the carry a real rescue would
need.

<Photos ids="lifesaving-manikin-lift lifesaving-manikin-tow" />

The <Term id="rescue-tube">rescue tube</Term> is the float and strap that a
lifeguard on patrol carries, and competitors use it unchanged. The line in the
<Term id="throw-line">line throw</Term> is a real line on a real reel. The
board and the surf ski come from the patrol craft of Australia and New
Zealand, and they have followed what beach patrols use.

<Photos ids="lifesaving-rescue-tube lifesaving-boards-skis" />

In most timed sports, equipment gets faster until the governing body stops
it. [Athletics](/sports/athletics/) had to rein in the javelin,
[swimming](/sports/swimming/) the swimsuit and
[powerlifting](/sports/powerlifting/) the bench shirt. Lifesaving's equipment
cannot go that way. A rescue tube built only for racing would stop being a
rescue tube, and the federation cares more about the tube than the race.

There are two exceptions. Fins are used in several pool events for speed, and
they are not standard rescue equipment everywhere. And
<Term id="beach-flags">beach flags</Term> uses short lengths of hose pushed
into the sand. They are not flags and they are not rescue equipment. They are
there to give the runners something to dive for.
