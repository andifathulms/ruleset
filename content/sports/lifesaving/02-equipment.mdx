# Equipment evolution

Every object in this sport is a piece of rescue equipment, and that constrains
its development in a way nothing else here experiences.

**The manikin** is the sport's signature item: a weighted torso of specified
dimensions that fills with water, must be retrieved from a specified depth, and
must be carried with its airway clear of the surface. It is not a piece of
sports equipment that resembles a person; it is a standardised person, and its
specification is set so that the carry demanded is the carry a rescue would
demand.

**The rescue tube** is the strap-and-float device a patrolling lifeguard
actually carries, used in competition as issued. **The throw line** is a real
line on a real reel. **The board and the surf ski** are descended directly from
Australasian patrol craft, and their evolution has tracked what beach patrols
use rather than what would be fastest.

That is the constraint. In every other measured sport on this site, equipment
develops towards performance until a governing body decides it has gone too far
— the javelin, the swimsuit, the composite bat, the bench shirt. Lifesaving's
equipment cannot develop that way, because a rescue tube optimised for racing
would stop being a rescue tube, and the federation's purpose is the tube rather
than the race.

The one deliberate departure is **fins**, which are used in several pool events
for speed and are not standard rescue equipment everywhere. And **beach flags**
uses lengths of buried hose, which are not flags, are not rescue equipment, and
exist purely because somebody needed something to dive for.
