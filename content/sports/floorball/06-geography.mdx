# Played in many countries, won by two

Sweden and Finland have won almost every world championship, men's and
women's. Both have largely professional leagues, deep club systems and
floorball in the school curriculum. In each country it is among the largest
team sports by number of players.

<Photos ids="floorball-sweden-2022 floorball-finland-2018" />

The Czech Republic and Switzerland are the only steady challengers.
Switzerland was one of the three associations that founded the federation in
1986.

Past those four, the game is well established in Norway, Denmark, Latvia,
Estonia, Germany and Poland. It is growing in Asia. Singapore has invested in
it as a school sport, and Japan, Thailand and Malaysia also compete, as do
Australia and New Zealand.

<Photos ids="floorball-singapore-2022 floorball-thailand-2022" />

## Why the results stay narrow

Many people in many countries play floorball, because it is cheap, safe and
fits in any sports hall. A small rink even fits in a town square. Few of those
countries win anything. Sweden and Finland are the only places where a player
can make floorball a full-time job, so the best players from elsewhere end up
in those two leagues.

<Photo id="floorball-street-floorball" />

The federation has answered with development work: school programmes,
small-sided formats, and world championship divisions with promotion and
relegation between them. Participation has spread. The list of winners has
not changed.

## The hall is already taken

Floorball needs the same halls, the same school hours and the same funding as
[handball](/sports/handball/), [basketball](/sports/basketball/) and
[volleyball](/sports/volleyball/). All three are already Olympic sports. A
game can be cheap, safe and popular in schools and still lose the argument
over a hall to a sport that got there first.
