# Officiating

Two referees, both with equal authority, moving diagonally around a small
boarded rink — and one call that dominates everything.

**Stick height.** The stick may not go above the waist at any point: not on the
backswing, not on the follow-through. It is the sport's safety foundation and
its most frequent penalty, and it is judged by eye against a body part whose
height changes as a player bends. A hard shot is naturally taken with a bigger
swing, so the rule is fighting the technique players instinctively want to use.

**Contact.** Body checking is prohibited outright, so as in flag football and
korfball the official is deciding whether contact happened rather than whether
it was excessive. In a forty-by-twenty rink with rolling substitutions and no
offside, players are in each other's space constantly.

**Playing the stick.** Lifting, holding or striking an opponent's stick is a
foul, and separating a legitimate attempt at the ball from a played stick at
speed is the sport's most argued judgement.

**The crease.** An outfield player may not enter the goalkeeper's area, and the
goalkeeper — kneeling, without a stick — is protected by rules about how far
they may come out and how long they may hold the ball.

Penalties are served in a box as in ice hockey, so a foul costs a team a player
for two or five minutes rather than conceding a set piece. That makes each
individual call more consequential than a free hit would be, and it is why the
sport's officiating is scrutinised more than the low profile of the game
suggests.

There is no video review in most competition, and no ball-tracking anywhere.
