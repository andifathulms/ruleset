# Two referees and one call above all

A floorball match has two referees with equal authority. They work diagonally
around the rink, one near each end of the play.

<Photo id="floorball-referees-2022" />

## Stick height

The call they make most is the <Term id="high-stick">high stick</Term>. The
stick may not go above the waist at any point, on the backswing or on the
follow-through. The referee judges it by eye against a waist that moves as
the player bends. A hard shot wants a big swing, and a natural follow-through
rises above the waist, so players have to train it out. The rule is enforced
strictly, and how strictly it should be called is argued at every level of
the game.

<Photo id="floorball-penalty-signal" />

## Contact, sticks and the crease

Body checking is banned outright. The referee decides whether contact
happened, with no need to judge whether it was too much. On a small rink with
no offside and players changing on the fly, players are in each other's space
all the time.

<Term id="playing-the-stick">Playing the stick</Term> is the most argued
judgement. A player may go for the ball and may not lift, hold or hit an
opponent's stick. At speed the two look alike.

An outfield player may not enter the <Term id="goal-crease">goal
crease</Term>. The goalkeeper is limited too, in how far they may come out and
in how long they may hold the ball.

## The penalty box

A penalised player sits in the <Term id="penalty-box">penalty box</Term> for
two or five minutes, and the team plays one short. A single call therefore
costs more than a free hit would, and the referees are watched closely for a
sport with so low a profile.

<Photo id="floorball-penalty-box" />

Most competitions have no video review, and none uses ball tracking.
