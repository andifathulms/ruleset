# From a gym toy to three games, then one

Floorball began as school equipment. Plastic floor hockey sets, with light
sticks and hollow balls, were made in the United States for physical education
classes. In the late 1960s and early 1970s they were exported to Sweden. They
were toys for a gym lesson.

The sport grew up away from the country that made the sticks. Swedish, Finnish
and Swiss players started clubs and competitions around the equipment, and by
the late 1970s each country had its own written game. Sweden's national
championship began in 1982. The first Swiss national league season opened on
6 November 1983, and UHC Urdorf won the first official Swiss championship in
March 1984. Finland's first championship season began in 1986.

## Three codes become one

The three national games were close relatives, and they did not match. The
size of the <Term id="rink">rink</Term>, the number of players and the way
play restarted were different in each. Teams from the three countries could
not play each other.

In 1986 the Swedish, Finnish and Swiss associations formed the International
Floorball Federation to solve that. [Its first job was to make one rulebook
out of three](#floorball-1986-iff). The result is the game played now: five
outfield players and a goalkeeper, a rink forty metres by twenty with low
<Term id="boards">boards</Term>, three periods of twenty minutes, and a
<Term id="free-hit">free hit</Term> to restart play after most stoppages.

Three choices made then still define the sport. The rulebook has no offside
rule, and none has ever been added. [Field hockey](/sports/hockey/) had one
and abolished it in 1998. [The goalkeeper was given no
stick](#floorball-goalkeeper-no-stick). And [the stick was kept below the
waist, with body checking banned](#floorball-stick-below-waist). None of the
three has been reversed.

The federation received full [recognition from the International Olympic
Committee in 2011](#floorball-2011-ioc-recognition). Floorball has been on the
World Games programme since 2017. It has never been played at an Olympic
Games.
