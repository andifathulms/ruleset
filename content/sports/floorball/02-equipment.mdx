# A ball with holes and a stick held low

The ball is hollow plastic, 72 millimetres across, and weighs 23 grams. It has
26 holes. The holes cut air resistance and steady the ball in flight, and that
is what lets a player control so light a ball at speed in a small hall.

## The stick

The stick is made of composite plastic and weighs around 350 grams, far less
than a field hockey stick. It ends in a moulded plastic
<Term id="blade">blade</Term>.

The rules limit how deep the blade may curve, and the curve is measured. A
deeper curve lets a player lift and shoot the light ball with more control
than the rules intend. Makers keep pressing against the limit, and this is the
one equipment contest the sport has had.

<Photo id="floorball-blade" />

## The goalkeeper carries nothing

The <Term id="goalkeeper">goalkeeper</Term> wears padding, a helmet and
gloves, and [has no stick](#floorball-goalkeeper-no-stick). They play from
their knees, catch the ball and throw it back into play. No outfield player
may touch the ball with a hand, and the goalkeeper plays with the hands the
whole time.

The reason given is the size of the game. The ball is light and fast, the rink
is small and the goal is low. A kneeling, padded goalkeeper who covers the
lower part of the goal with body and hands was judged safer than a standing
one with a stick, and a better fit for the goal.

<Photo id="floorball-goalkeeper-2022" />

## No padding for anyone else

Outfield players wear no protective equipment at all. Two rules make that
possible: no <Term id="body-check">body checking</Term>, and [no stick above
the waist](#floorball-stick-below-waist). The same low swing limits how hard
the ball can be hit.

## The rink

The boards are part of the equipment too. They are fifty centimetres high with
rounded corners, so the ball comes back off them and keeps moving. The steady
flow of the game owes as much to that shape as to any rule about restarts.
