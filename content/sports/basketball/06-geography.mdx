# Where it is played

Basketball has the second-widest participation of any sport on this site and a
competitive geography that has moved further in thirty years than any other
sport's.

The spread is a function of the facility. A hoop and a hard surface is the
entire requirement, it fits in a schoolyard, and unlike football it does not
need a large flat field. That makes basketball the dominant urban sport in much
of the world.

The competitive map used to be simple: the United States, and everyone else.
The 1989 decision to allow professionals at the Games and the 1992 team that
followed marked the peak of that, and paradoxically began its end — the global
audience it created fed a generation of players who now populate the
professional league itself, which is why the international game has closed and
in several tournaments overtaken.

The rule consequence is real. FIBA's 2010 changes to the arc and the key were
explicitly about reducing the difference between the international and
professional games, and they happened because players were routinely moving
between them and having to adapt to a different court. Rule convergence
followed player movement, not the other way round.

Women's basketball has a different and narrower map, concentrated where
national federations funded the game early, and that funding history is
directly legible in the medal table.
