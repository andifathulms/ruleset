# Equipment evolution

Basketball's equipment history is quiet, and the quietness is the finding.

The peach baskets had their bottoms cut out within a few years. The ball
stopped being a laced soccer ball and became a moulded one in the 1940s.
Backboards appeared to stop spectators in the balcony interfering with shots,
then became glass so the crowd behind them could see. Breakaway rims arrived in
the 1970s after dunking began shattering backboards. None of these produced a
comparability break, and none required a fight.

The reason is the same as football's: Law-level specification here is loose.
The ball is given by circumference, weight and rebound, and the rim by diameter
and material. Nothing in the sport's history has outrun those numbers.

What did change the sport was not equipment but geometry — the three-point arc,
the shape of the key, the no-charge semi-circle. Basketball legislates the
floor, not the object, and that is unusual enough among the sports here to be
worth stating plainly. Athletics regulates the javelin; swimming regulates the
suit; badminton regulates the shuttle. Basketball regulates where you may
stand.

The one piece of equipment that has never moved is the one that matters most.
The rim has been 3.05 metres from the floor since 1891, while the average
height of an elite player has risen substantially. The sport has quietly become
harder to reach without changing a single specification.
