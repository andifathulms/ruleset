# Two rulebooks

Basketball has two rulebooks, and both matter. FIBA governs the international
game. The NBA, the professional league in the United States, writes its own
rules and is not a member of FIBA.

The numbers differ. The NBA court is slightly larger. Its
<Term id="three-point-arc">three-point arc</Term> is further from the basket.
A quarter lasts twelve minutes in the NBA and ten under FIBA. A player is out
of the game on the sixth <Term id="foul">foul</Term> in the NBA and on the
fifth under FIBA. Any fact about the rules of basketball has to say which game
it means.

## Who follows whom

The NBA has usually moved first. It adopted [the shot
clock](#nba-1954-shot-clock) in 1954. It adopted [the three-point
line](#nba-1979-three-point-line) in 1979, after a rival league, the American
Basketball Association, had used it through the 1970s. It [allowed zone
defence](#nba-2001-zone-defence) in 2001.

FIBA followed at its own pace and with its own measurements. It added an arc
in 1984. In 2000 it [cut its shot clock from thirty seconds to
twenty-four](#fiba-2000-shot-clock-and-quarters) and replaced two halves with
four quarters. In 2010 it [moved the arc out to 6.75 metres and redrew the
key](#fiba-2010-arc-and-lane).

## The vote of 1989

For decades the other argument was about who could play at the Olympic Games.
FIBA settled it in 1989, when it voted to allow professional players. The
first result was the United States team of 1992 in Barcelona.

<Photo id="basketball-dream-team-1992" />

The longer result was a worldwide audience for the professional league. That
is how the NBA's rulebook came to pull FIBA's toward it. Players began to move
between the two games and had to adapt to a different court each time. FIBA
said its 2010 changes were meant to reduce that difference.

The two bodies now agree on the shot clock and are closer on the arc. They
have never agreed on one set of rules. Neither can make the other change, and
the professional league has no reason to accept an outside authority.

Women's basketball entered the Olympic programme in 1976, forty years after
the men's game.
