# Governing body politics

Basketball is the only sport on this site with two rulebooks that both matter,
and the relationship between them is the whole of its politics.

FIBA governs the international game. The NBA governs itself, is not a member
federation, and has repeatedly innovated first: the shot clock in 1954, the
three-point line in 1979 following the ABA, the legalisation of zone defence in
2001. FIBA has followed each time, at its own pace and at different dimensions
— the arc in 1984 and again in 2010, the twenty-four second clock in 2000.

The result is a sport where a fact about the rules has to name its jurisdiction.
The court is a different size. The arc is a different distance. A quarter is ten
minutes or twelve. A player fouls out on five or six. None of these are small
enough to ignore when comparing anything.

The other long-running dispute was eligibility, and it was settled in 1989 when
FIBA voted to allow professional players at the Olympic Games. The immediate
consequence was the 1992 United States team, and the longer consequence was the
globalisation of the professional league — which is why the NBA's rulebook now
exerts gravitational pull on FIBA's rather than the reverse.

Women's basketball entered the Olympic programme in 1976, forty years after the
men's, and its professional structures remain differently resourced in a way
that is a governance fact rather than a sporting one.
