# Origin and invention

Basketball is the only sport on this site that was invented, on a known date,
by a named person, to solve a stated problem. In December 1891 James Naismith,
an instructor at the YMCA training school in Springfield, Massachusetts, was
asked for an indoor activity that would occupy a class of restless young men
through a New England winter without the injury rate of indoor football. He
wrote thirteen rules and nailed a peach basket to the balcony railing.

Two of his decisions still govern the sport.

**The target is horizontal and above head height.** Naismith reasoned that a
goal you cannot run at in a straight line cannot be defended by brute force, so
the game would reward placement over collision. Every distinctive feature of
basketball follows from that: the arc of a shot, the value of height, the
impossibility of goalkeeping.

**A player holding the ball may not run with it.** This is the anti-collision
rule, and it is why the dribble had to be invented afterwards — Naismith's
original rules did not contain one.

The balcony railing in Springfield was ten feet up. The rim has been 3.05 m
ever since, in every competition in the world, for no reason other than that.
It is the most consequential accident on this site: a sport's entire
relationship with the human body was fixed by the height of a piece of
architecture nobody chose.
