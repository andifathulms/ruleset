# One man, thirteen rules and a peach basket

Basketball was invented by one man in December 1891. James Naismith was an
instructor at the YMCA training school in Springfield, Massachusetts. He was
asked for an indoor game that would keep a class of restless young men busy
through a New England winter, with fewer injuries than indoor football. He
wrote thirteen rules and nailed a peach basket to the balcony railing of the
gymnasium.

Two of his decisions still shape the game.

The first was the target. Naismith put it flat and above head height. He
reasoned that a goal nobody can run at in a straight line cannot be defended
by force, so the game would reward placement over collision. The arc of a
shot and the value of a tall player both follow from that choice. So does the
absence of a goalkeeper.

The second was that a player holding the ball may not run with it. This kept
players from crashing into each other. It also left a gap, because the
thirteen rules gave no way to move with the ball at all. The
<Term id="dribble">dribble</Term> was invented afterwards.

## The height of a railing

The balcony railing in Springfield was ten feet from the floor. The rim of a
<Term id="basket">basket</Term> has been 3.05 metres high ever since, in every
competition in the world. Nobody chose the number. It was the height of a
piece of the building.

Elite players have grown much taller since 1891, and the rim has stayed where
it was.

## What changed around the basket

The equipment changed quietly, and nobody had to fight over any of it. The
peach baskets had their bottoms cut out within a few years. Backboards were
added to stop spectators in the balcony from interfering with shots, and later
they were made of glass so that the crowd behind could see. The first ball was
a laced soccer ball, and a moulded ball replaced it in the 1940s. Rims that
bend and spring back arrived in the 1970s, after dunking began to shatter
backboards.

<Photo id="basketball-naismith" />

The rules describe the ball loosely, by its size and how it bounces, and the
ball has never caused an argument. The rules that changed the game were about
the floor: the <Term id="three-point-arc">three-point arc</Term>, the shape of
the <Term id="key">key</Term>, and the semi-circle under the basket.
[Athletics](/sports/athletics/) regulates the javelin and
[swimming](/sports/swimming/) regulates the suit. Basketball regulates where a
player may stand.
