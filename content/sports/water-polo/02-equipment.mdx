# Equipment evolution

The ball, the caps and the pool, and only the last of them has been a matter
of serious argument.

**The ball** has been specified by size, weight and pressure for decades. The
women's ball is a little smaller and softer than the men's — the sport's one
concession to a difference between the men's and women's games.

**The cap** carries malleable ear protectors in the cap's colour, which the
rules require. White or blue for the two teams, red for the goalkeeper, and a
number on each cap so the officials can identify a player from the deck.

<Photo id="water-polo-ball-on-water" />

**The field of play** is the real equipment story. The men's field was 30
metres long until 2024 and the women's had been shortened to 25 in 2009. In
November 2024 the men's was cut to 25 as well, which makes the same number of
players cover a sixth less water and brings every attack closer to goal.

None of this has produced a comparability problem of the kind athletics or
swimming has, because water polo keeps no records that a pool or a ball could
break. What it has changed is the shape of the game.
