# Governing body politics

Water polo is governed by an aquatics federation whose main business is
swimming, and its rule-making has been through two very different processes
in six years.

The 2019 rules were made the old way. They were proposed at a water polo
congress in Budapest in April 2018, trialled at youth and World Cup events,
and adopted at an extraordinary congress in Hangzhou in December 2018 by 161
votes to 2. The trials had shown more goals and fewer fouls, and the
federation said so.

The 2024 rules were made the new way. The technical committee recommended a
package in October 2024 — a 25-metre field for men, a 25-second clock, a
15-second exclusion, a video challenge — and the federation's Bureau adopted
it by postal vote in November. It had been presented as a set of trials for
the 2025 World Cup and was adopted outright before them. Prominent coaches
objected less to the content than to the process.

Six months later, after the World Cup had been played under the new rules,
the federation partly reversed them: 28 seconds and 18 rather than 25 and 15.
Its letter gave speed, efficiency, safety and fairness as the reasons. That
sequence — a change adopted without a congress, contested, and halved within
a season — is unusual on this site, and it is recorded here as it happened.

The other governance story is women's water polo. It was played at world
championships from 1986 and entered the Games in 2000 with six teams; in 2025
the IOC agreed a twelve-team women's tournament for 2028, the same size as the
men's for the first time.
