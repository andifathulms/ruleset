# Refereeing from above the water

Two referees walk the sides of the pool, one near each end of the play. Goal
judges help them, and a table of timekeepers and secretaries runs the clocks.
Each player wears a numbered <Term id="cap">cap</Term> so that the officials
can name a player from the deck. What they are watching happens mostly below
the surface.

## Ordinary or major

The call that matters most is the line between an
<Term id="ordinary-foul">ordinary foul</Term> and a major foul. An ordinary
foul costs a <Term id="free-throw">free throw</Term>. A major foul costs an
exclusion. Holding an opponent who has the ball is ordinary. Holding, sinking
or pulling back an opponent who does not have it is major. Often the
difference is which arm was where, under broken water, seen from above.

An exclusion usually leads to a goal, so players work hard to win one. A
<Term id="centre-forward">centre forward</Term> who can make a defender's hold
look illegal is worth as much as one who can shoot.

## Video

Video came late. The 2019 rules allowed a review of whether a goal had been
scored, and of violent action the referees had missed. The 2024 package gave
each coach one challenge per match. The committee said it would "reduce the
human factor in certain calls". In 2025 each review was limited to two
minutes.

<Photo id="water-polo-yellow-card" />

A camera above the pool still cannot see under the water. The challenge has
made the disputes fewer. It has not ended them.
