# Officiating

Two referees walk the sides of the pool, one at each end of the play, with
goal judges and a table of timekeepers and secretaries. They are watching a
contest that happens mostly below the surface.

The referees' central judgement is the distinction between an **ordinary
foul**, which costs a free throw, and a **major foul**, which costs an
exclusion. Holding an opponent who has the ball is ordinary; holding,
sinking or pulling back one who does not is major. The difference is often a
matter of which arm was where underwater, seen from above through broken
water.

Because an exclusion usually leads to a goal, players work hard to draw them.
A centre forward who can make a defender's hold look illegal is worth as much
as one who can shoot. The officiating problem and the tactical skill are the
same thing seen from two sides.

**Video** arrived late. The 2019 rules allowed video review of whether a goal
was scored and of violent action the referees missed. The 2024 package added
a Coach's Challenge, one per match, which the federation said would reduce
the human factor in certain calls; in 2025 each review was capped at two
minutes. Nothing a camera above the water can see settles what happened
below it, so the challenge has narrowed the disputes rather than ended them.

Everything else is on the clock. Possession, exclusion and game time are run
from the table, and the numbers on them are the rules this sport changes most.
