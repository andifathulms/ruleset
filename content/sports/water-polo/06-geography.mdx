# Where it is played

Water polo has the most concentrated geography of any team sport on this site,
and the concentration is in central and southern Europe.

**Hungary** is the outstanding case: nine Olympic men's golds, from 1932 to
2008, and a medal at every Games from 1928 to 1980. The sport is part of the
national culture in a way it is almost nowhere else.

**The Balkans** are the other centre. Yugoslavia won three Olympic titles, and
its successors have kept the strength — Serbia won the men's gold in 2016,
2020 and 2024, and Croatia and Montenegro are perennial contenders.

<Photos ids="water-polo-serbia-2016 water-polo-shot" /> **Italy**
and **Spain** complete the European core.

**The United States** is the exception. Its men's only Olympic title is from
1904, but its women have dominated the women's tournament, with gold in 2012,
2016 and 2020. Australia won the first women's title in 2000, the Netherlands
in 2008 and Spain in 2024.

<Photo id="water-polo-paris-2024-women" />

The reason for the concentration is infrastructure and tradition together.
Water polo needs a deep pool given over to it for hours at a time, which
limits it to places with many pools, and it grows through club systems that
take decades to build. Where those clubs exist the sport is professional and
deep; elsewhere it is a minor sport for swimmers.
