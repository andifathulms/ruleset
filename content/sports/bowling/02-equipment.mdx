# A new ball, and then a new lane

Around 1991 the bowling ball changed, and scores rose for good. The sport
answered by changing the oil on the lane.

## The ball

The outer shell of a ball is its <Term id="coverstock">coverstock</Term>. It
was hard rubber first, then polyester, then urethane. [Around 1991 reactive
resin arrived](#bowling-reactive-resin). A reactive ball grips the dry part of
the lane much harder than anything before it. It
<Term id="hook">hooks</Term> more sharply into the pins and knocks down pins
that older balls left standing.

<Photos ids="bowling-reactive bowling-urethane" />

The rules did not stop it. They set the weight, size, hardness and balance of
a ball. They said nothing about how its surface gripped the lane, and the
makers worked inside them. [Softball](/sports/softball/) met the same problem
with the composite bat, and [swimming](/sports/swimming/) with the
polyurethane suit.

Scores went up and stayed up. A
<Term id="perfect-game">perfect game</Term> had been rare enough to be news,
and it became common. The scoring system did not change at all, so averages
from before and after sit in the same record books under the same numbers.
They cannot fairly be compared, and the sport has never separated them.

A modern ball goes further. Inside is a weight block of a designed shape, and
the finger holes are drilled at a chosen angle to it. The surface can be
sanded or polished to change its grip. Competitive bowlers carry several balls
and switch between them during a <Term id="block">block</Term>.

## The lane

A machine lays oil on the lane before play, in an
<Term id="oil-pattern">oil pattern</Term> the bowler cannot see. The ball
slides where there is oil and grips where there is none.

<Photo id="bowling-oil-machine" />

Other sports dealt with a new technology through the equipment.
[Athletics](/sports/athletics/) redesigned the javelin and started its record
again. Swimming banned the suits and let the times stand.
[Finswimming](/sports/finswimming/) split its records by type of fin. Bowling
left the ball alone. [It adopted competition oil
patterns](#bowling-sport-patterns) that are longer and flatter than the
<Term id="house-pattern">house pattern</Term> of everyday bowling, with far
less room for error. On a <Term id="sport-pattern">sport pattern</Term> the
scores separate the bowlers again.

It could do this because the lane surface is laid down fresh before every
session and used up during it. One result is strange: the same bowler, with
the same ball, scores very differently on two lanes that look identical.
