# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Reactive resin.** The defining controversy. Ball coverstocks developed in the
early 1990s hooked far harder than anything before them, and scoring rose
permanently — perfect games went from rare to routine — without a single change
to the scoring system. Averages from before and after are not comparable, and
the sport has never annulled or separated them.

Compare the three answers this site has already recorded. Athletics redesigned
the javelin and reset the record book. Swimming banned the supersuits and let
the marks stand. Finswimming could ban nothing and split the book by fin type.
Bowling did something none of them could: it left the equipment alone and
changed the surface.

**Sport patterns.** Competition oil patterns — longer, flatter, far less
forgiving than recreational house conditions — were adopted so that scores
would again separate competitors. It worked, and it created a sport in which
the same bowler scores dramatically differently on two lanes that look
identical.

**Whether 300 still means anything.** A perfect game was once a career
achievement. It is now common enough that the sport's own recognition of it has
had to change. The number is fixed; what it certifies is not.

**Lane maintenance.** Because the oil breaks down during play, and breaks down
differently depending on where bowlers have been throwing, the fairness of a
block depends on the sequence of who bowled where. Pattern design and
re-oiling schedules are governed and argued.

**Ninepin and tenpin.** One federation, two sports, no overlap in athletes.
