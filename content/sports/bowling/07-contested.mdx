# What is still argued about

**What a score means.** Reactive balls raised scores for good in the early
1990s, and no record was cancelled, separated or restarted. No proposal to
change that has made progress. The sport seems to have decided it does not
need one.

**What 300 is worth.** A perfect game was once the achievement of a career.
It is now common enough that the sport has had to change how it honours one.

**The surface of the ball.** The rules are strict about weight, size, balance
and hardness. They are much looser about how the surface grips the lane, which
is the property that changed the sport. Makers keep developing inside them.

**Difficulty nobody can see.** Making competition hard through the
<Term id="oil-pattern">oil pattern</Term> works. It also means that the
difficulty of the sport is set by a specification most bowlers have never
heard of.

**The easy lane.** Most people bowl on a pattern designed to flatter them.
That brings people in, and it teaches them that bowling is easy. Whether this
is the sport's greatest strength or a false picture of it is an open question.

**Two games, one body.** Ten-pin and nine-pin use different equipment and
different lanes and share no players, and one federation governs both.
