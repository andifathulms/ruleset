# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**What a score means.** Reactive equipment raised scoring permanently in the
early 1990s and nothing was annulled, separated or reset. Averages and perfect
games from before and after sit in the same records under the same number. No
proposal to address it has gained ground, and the sport is unusual in having
apparently decided it does not need to.

**Equipment regulation.** Specifications govern weight, dimensions, balance and
hardness. Coverstock friction — the property that actually changed the sport —
is regulated far more loosely, and manufacturers continue to develop inside
the rules.

**Oil patterns as the fairness mechanism.** Making competition hard by changing
an invisible surface works, and it means the sport's difficulty is set by a
specification most participants have never heard of, on a variable no spectator
can see.

**Olympic prospects.** Enormous participation, a 1988 demonstration appearance,
repeated bids, no place. The obstacles — a commercial rather than club base,
modest professional structures, and a spectacle that hides its own difficulty —
have not changed.

**Ten-pin and nine-pin.** One international body governing two sports with
different equipment, different lanes and no shared athletes.

**Whether the recreational game helps or hurts.** Bowling's vast popular base
is played on conditions engineered to flatter, which recruits people and
teaches them that the sport is easy. Whether that is the federation's greatest
asset or its central misrepresentation is a genuine strategic question.
