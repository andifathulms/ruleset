# Played everywhere, never at the Olympics

Tens of millions of people bowl, and there are bowling centres in most cities
in the world. The sport has still never been on the Olympic programme.

[Bowling was a demonstration sport at the Seoul Games in
1988](#bowling-1988-olympic-demonstration). The International Olympic
Committee ended demonstration sports after 1992. Bowling has bid for a place
again and again since then, and has not been chosen. Other multi-sport games,
such as the Asian Games, do include it.

<Photo id="bowling-incheon-2014" />

Three reasons are usually given. Most bowling is a paid leisure activity in a
commercial centre, with few clubs behind it. Its professional tours are modest
and are found mostly in the United States and East Asia. And it is hard to
show on television, because the thing that makes it difficult cannot be seen.

A viewer watching the best bowlers sees people knock down all the pins, time
after time, and cannot see why it is hard. The difficulty is in the oil, and
a camera does not show the oil.

The decisions that shaped modern bowling most were made outside the
federation. Ball makers made them in the early 1990s, and the sport has been
responding ever since.
