# From nine pins to ten

Knocking down pins with a ball is one of the oldest games there is. Ten-pin
bowling is the form that the United States fixed in the nineteenth century.

Pin games were played across Europe for centuries, in many forms. Nine pins
stood in a diamond, in a square or in a line. A Dutch painting of about 1660
shows a handful of pins, a ball and a stretch of open ground outside an inn.

<Photos ids="bowling-steen bowling-nine-pins-1818" />

German and Dutch settlers took the games to North America. There, through the
nineteenth century, the game became ten pins in a triangle, on a lane of fixed
length, with a ball of fixed size. The businesses that were building the
lanes drove the change. Before machines, boys reset every pin by hand, late
into the night.

## A score that stops at 300

The scoring dates from that time and has not changed. A game is ten
<Term id="frame">frames</Term> of two deliveries. A
<Term id="strike">strike</Term> takes its bonus from the next two deliveries
and a <Term id="spare">spare</Term> from the next one. The highest possible
score is exactly 300, and bowlers do reach it.
[Gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) also had a top score, the perfect 10, and
abolished it in 2006 because gymnasts were reaching it. Bowling kept its
limit.

## One federation, two games

[An international federation was founded in
1952](#bowling-1952-fiq) as the Fédération Internationale des Quilleurs. It
brought ten-pin and the European nine-pin games under one body. It later
became World Bowling and is now the International Bowling Federation. It still
governs nine-pin, which is a different game, played by different people,
almost entirely in Central Europe.
