# Officiating

Bowling barely needs officials during play, and needs them badly before it.

**During a game** almost nothing requires judgement. Pins fell or they did not.
The count is objective and, in modern centres, automated. The one in-play
offence — crossing the foul line — is detected electronically and voids that
delivery's pins.

That makes bowling one of the least-adjudicated sports covered here, alongside
orienteering, whose result is an electronic record, and boules, where the
players measure.

**Before and around a game**, officiating is entirely about conditions and
equipment, and it is where the sport's integrity lives:

**Lane dressing.** Verifying that the oil pattern applied matches the
specification for the competition, and that re-oiling happens on schedule. The
pattern decides how hard scoring is, so an error here changes the event.

**Ball inspection.** Weight, dimensions, balance, hardness and surface
preparation are all specified, and a coverstock can be altered by sanding or
polishing — so what a ball's surface is, and when it was changed, is checked.

**Pair rotation.** Because bowlers' deliveries change the oil for the person
they share lanes with, and because a pattern breaks down unevenly, who bowls
where and in what order is administered rather than left to chance.

The result is a sport whose officials spend their attention on the field of
play rather than on the competitors — the opposite of almost everything else on
this site.
