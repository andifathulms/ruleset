# Wherever someone builds a centre

Bowling lanes are private businesses, and that decides where the sport is
played.

**The United States** is where ten-pin was fixed in its present form and
where the industry that built the lanes grew up. It has by far the most
centres and the most bowlers, and the professional game is based there.

**East and Southeast Asia** is the other stronghold, and in competition it is
often the stronger one. South Korea, Japan, Chinese Taipei, Singapore,
Malaysia and Hong Kong all produce world-class bowlers. Singapore and Malaysia
have won at world level far beyond what their size would suggest. Bowling
arrived in the region as a business and was then taken up as a serious sport.

<Photo id="bowling-queenstown" />

**Europe** plays ten-pin in Sweden, Finland, Denmark, England, Germany and the
Netherlands. The centre of the continent has a separate tradition. Nine-pin,
in several forms, is a large club sport in Germany, Austria, Croatia, Serbia
and Slovenia. It comes under the same international federation and is played
by different people.

## When the building is worth more

A country gets bowling when someone decides there is money in building
centres. It loses bowling when the property becomes worth more as something
else. The same thing hit [racquetball](/sports/racquetball/) in the United
States, where clubs turned their courts into gym floor.

<Photo id="bowling-amherst" />

Bowling has been luckier, because a bowling centre is hard to turn into
anything else. Even so, whether a country has the sport depends on a
commercial decision more than on any federation.
