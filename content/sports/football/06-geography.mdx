# Where it is played

Everywhere, which sounds like a non-answer and is actually the most important
fact about the sport's rules. Football is the only sport on this site with no
meaningful geographic barrier to entry, and that is why its Laws are so short.

A rulebook that has to work in every country, on every surface, at every level
of resourcing cannot specify much. Law 1 gives the pitch as a range rather than
a dimension. Law 2 specifies the ball by weight and pressure and says nothing
about how it is made. There is no requirement for a marked pitch to have grass,
no requirement for goal-line technology, no requirement for a fourth official.
Everything that a wealthy competition adds is an option that competitions may
take up, not a Law.

That looseness is what lets the same seventeen Laws govern a World Cup final and
a game on packed earth, and it is why football produced no equipment-driven
comparability break while athletics produced several.

The competitive geography is more concentrated than the participation geography
and has moved. European and South American nations have won every men's World
Cup; the women's game has a different and shifting map, with the United States,
Germany, Japan, Spain and England having held the title, largely reflecting
which countries funded the women's game and when — England's Football
Association banned women's football from its members' grounds for fifty years,
which is a rule with a measurable effect on where the sport is strong today.
