# Where it is played

Football is played everywhere, and its Laws are short because they have to
work everywhere.

A rule book for every country, every surface and every budget cannot ask for
much. The Laws give the size of the pitch as a range. They describe the ball
by its size, weight and pressure, and leave the material open. They do not
require grass, goal-line technology or a fourth official. What a rich
competition adds is an option. The same seventeen Laws cover a World Cup final
and a game on packed earth.

Winning is more concentrated than playing. European and South American
nations have won every men's World Cup.

The women's game has a different map, and it is still moving. The United
States, Germany, Japan and Spain have all held the world title. The map largely
follows which countries paid for the women's game, and when. In 1920 Dick,
Kerr Ladies played France at Preston in the first women's international. A
year later England's Football Association banned women's football from its
members' grounds. The ban lasted fifty years.
