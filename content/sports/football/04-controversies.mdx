# Rules written against a way of playing

Football's law-makers have rarely acted against a team. Several times they
have acted against a method that was working too well.

## The offside trap

Until 1925 the <Term id="offside">offside</Term> law asked for three
opponents between an attacker and the goal line. Defenders at Newcastle United
learned to use it. They stepped up the pitch together and left the attacker
offside. The <Term id="offside-trap">offside trap</Term> is associated with
their defender Bill McCracken, and it became reliable enough to hold down
scoring across the English league. Attendances were understood to be suffering.

In 1925 the Board [changed three opponents to two](#ifab-1925-offside). The
modern defensive line comes from that change.

## Italy, 1990

At the 1990 World Cup in Italy, teams wasted time by passing the ball back to
their goalkeeper, who picked it up and held it. The tournament had the lowest
goals per game of any World Cup finals.

<Photo id="football-italia-90-ball" />

Two years later [the goalkeeper lost the right to handle a
back-pass](#ifab-1992-back-pass). A ball deliberately kicked to the goalkeeper
by a team-mate now has to be played with the feet, and picking it up is an
indirect <Term id="free-kick">free kick</Term>. Goalkeepers have been chosen
for their feet ever since.

## The golden goal

In 1993 competitions were [allowed to end a match at the first goal scored in
extra time](#ifab-1993-golden-goal). The stated aim was more attacking play in
<Term id="extra-time">extra time</Term> and fewer matches decided by penalty
kicks. It was widely reported to have done the opposite. One goal against a
team ended its match with no chance to reply, so teams became more careful.

In 2004 the Board [removed the golden goal](#ifab-2004-golden-goal-abolished),
along with the silver goal that had briefly replaced it. Extra time went back
to two full periods. It is rare for a governing body to reverse a rule and
accept that it had failed.

## The tackle from behind

A player tackled from behind cannot see the challenge coming and cannot
protect the standing leg. After a long pattern of serious leg injuries, such a
tackle that endangers an opponent [became a sending-off
offence](#ifab-1998-tackle-from-behind) from the 1998 World Cup. Before that
it had been a caution.
