# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled, and football has more open arguments than any other sport
here because IFAB amends the Laws annually and publishes the debate.

**Handball.** The most rewritten clause in the book and still the least
settled. The test has moved between intent, position, whether the arm made the
body unnaturally bigger, and whether the deflection came off the player's own
body, and each revision has traded one kind of unfairness for another. The
underlying problem is that the Law is asking for a judgement about a player's
state of mind and then being enforced from a slow-motion camera that cannot see
one.

**Offside and precision.** The Law dates from 1925 and was drafted for a
linesman's eye. Enforced to the centimetre by tracking systems, it produces
decisions that are correct under the Law and widely felt to be wrong. Proposals
to add a tolerance, or to require daylight between attacker and defender, are
attempts to rewrite the Law to match the precision now available. None has been
adopted.

**Concussion substitutes.** Temporary concussion substitutions have been
trialled, and player unions have pressed for them against a Board that has
preferred permanent additional substitutions. This is a safety argument with an
integrity objection attached — a temporary substitution is exploitable — which
is an unusually clean example of two of this site's causes pulling against each
other.

**Time-wasting and effective playing time.** Proposals to stop the clock, as
most other sports do, resurface regularly. Football's added time is the only
mechanism of its kind here and the only one left entirely to one official's
discretion.
