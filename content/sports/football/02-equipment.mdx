# Equipment evolution

Football has changed its equipment more than the timeline suggests, and almost
none of it appears as a rule change, because Law 2 has always specified the ball
loosely — circumference, weight, pressure — and left the construction alone.

The leather ball absorbed water and roughly doubled in weight over a wet match,
which is a fact about the game before the 1980s that no rule records. Synthetic
coatings, then thermally bonded panels, changed the flight of the ball
substantially over three decades within a specification that never had to move.
Every argument about a World Cup ball since 2006 has been an argument about an
object that is entirely legal.

Goalkeeper gloves went from optional to universal in the 1970s without a rule.
Shin guards became compulsory in 1990, which is one of the few equipment changes
here with a safety cause and a date.

The point of this section for a site about rules is the negative one. Football's
equipment was allowed to evolve inside a loose specification, so it produced no
comparability break — while athletics wrote its specification tightly and
therefore had to keep amending it, and each amendment cut the record book. How
tightly a sport specifies its equipment determines how often it has to break its
own numbers.
