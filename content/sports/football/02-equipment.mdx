# The ball and the kit

The Laws say how big the ball is, how heavy it is and how hard it is pumped.
They say nothing about what it is made of.

That gap let the ball change completely without a single rule change. The old
leather ball was made of stitched panels and soaked up water, so it grew
heavier through a wet match. Synthetic coatings came next, and then panels
bonded together by heat. Each kind of ball flew differently from the last, and
every one of them was legal. Players complained about the flight of the ball
at the 2010 World Cup, and that ball was inside the specification too.

The kit changed the same way. Gloves for the
<Term id="goalkeeper">goalkeeper</Term> went from optional to universal in the
1970s, and no law asked for them. Shin guards are the exception. They became
compulsory in 1990, for safety, and they are one of the few pieces of football
equipment with a rule and a date behind them.

<Photo id="football-shin-guard" />

[Athletics](/sports/athletics/) took the other path. It describes its
implements tightly, so it has had to amend the description again and again,
and its record book has been cut each time. Football described its ball
loosely and never had to.
