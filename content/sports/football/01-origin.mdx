# Origin and invention

Football's origin is a schism, not an invention. Through the middle of the
nineteenth century English schools and clubs each played their own code, and the
codes disagreed on two questions: whether the ball could be carried, and whether
an opponent could be hacked. The meetings that produced the Football
Association's laws in 1863 turned on exactly those points, and the clubs that
wanted carrying and hacking left. What they left with became rugby.

So association football and rugby are not related sports so much as the two
surviving answers to one argument, and the descent is legible in both rule
books to this day.

The second structural fact is that the FA did not keep the laws. The
International Football Association Board was formed in 1886 by the four British
associations to hold them jointly, and it still does — FIFA runs the sport's
competitions and holds half the Board's votes, but a change to the Laws of the
Game is an IFAB act. Football is therefore the only sport in this collection
where the body that owns the rules is not the body that owns the sport.

That separation is why football has no record book. There is no office with the
authority to ratify a football number, so there is no number for a rule change
to break.
