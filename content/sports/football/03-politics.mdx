# Governing body politics

The Laws are held by the International Football Association Board: the four
British associations hold one vote each, FIFA holds four, and a change requires
six. Neither side can amend the Laws alone. This arrangement is a settlement of
a 1900s dispute about who owned the game, and it has survived every subsequent
realignment of power in the sport.

Its effect on this site is concrete. Because IFAB publishes an annual edition of
the Laws plus a circular listing that season's amendments, football rule changes
are among the best-documented in this collection — a dated act by a named body
with a published text. Compare gymnastics, where the scoring system is
republished wholesale each quadrennium and the amendments are not separately
enumerated.

The professionalism schism came early here and was settled early. The FA
accepted professionalism in 1885, decades before athletics, tennis or rugby
union faced the same question. Football therefore has no equivalent of the
amateurism rules that shaped who could compete in the other sports on this
timeline, and no eligibility rule changes to show for it.
