# Where it is played

Esports is played all over the world, and every game has its own map of
strong countries.

South Korea built professional esports first. From the early 2000s it had
televised leagues, salaried players, team houses and a culture of training.
Gaming cafés with rows of networked computers appeared at the same time.

<Photo id="esports-pc-bang-2001" />

China is the largest market and, more and more, the strongest competitor. It
has franchised leagues, huge home audiences and mobile games with more
players than anything in the West.

<Photo id="esports-lpl-2015" />

Europe and North America have major franchised leagues and most of the
Western audience. Denmark, Sweden, France, Germany, Poland, Ukraine and
Russia all have strong scenes, and the Nordic countries and Eastern Europe
have long led in shooter games. Katowice in Poland has hosted one of the
biggest yearly events for more than a decade.

<Photo id="esports-spodek-2019" />

Brazil and Southeast Asia are large and often underrated, above all in mobile
games.

A country strong in one <Term id="title">title</Term> may count for nothing
in another, because the games are separate products with separate scenes. A
title's strong regions can change within a couple of years.

All a player needs is a device and a connection. Many of the biggest games
are free, and the game finds an opponent of the same level in seconds. Where
connections are poor, people are shut out. Mobile games have partly answered
that, and growth is fastest where phones outnumber computers.
