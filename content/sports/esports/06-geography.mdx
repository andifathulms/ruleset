# Where it is played

An unusually genuine global spread, with three centres of gravity and a
different map for every title.

**South Korea** built professional esports first — televised leagues, salaried
players, team houses and a training culture from the early 2000s — and remains
the sport's reference point for professionalisation.

**China** is the largest market and, increasingly, the largest competitive
force, with franchised leagues, enormous domestic audiences and mobile titles
whose player numbers exceed anything in the West. Hangzhou 2022 was a statement
of that.

**Europe and North America** carry major franchised leagues and much of the
Western audience, with strong national scenes in Denmark, Sweden, France,
Germany, Poland, Ukraine and Russia — Nordic and Eastern European dominance in
shooter titles being long-standing and unexplained by anything but culture and
early adoption.

**Brazil** and **Southeast Asia** are major and often underrated, particularly
in mobile titles, where the device barrier is far lower than a gaming computer.

What no other sport on this site has is that the map is **per title**. A country
strong in one game may be irrelevant in another, because the games are
unrelated products with separate scenes, and a title's regional strength can
shift within a couple of years.

The binding constraint is a device and a connection. That is a lower barrier
than any facility on this site and a real exclusion where connectivity is poor
— which mobile titles have partly answered, and which is why the fastest growth
is where phones outnumber computers.
