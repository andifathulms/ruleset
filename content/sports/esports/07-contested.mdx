# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether a sport can be privately owned.** The question underneath everything
else. No federation controls any esport's rules, and no institutional
arrangement has been proposed that would change that without a publisher
volunteering its property. Recognition, governance reform and multi-sport
places do not touch it.

**Two federations.** The IESF and the GEF have overlapping mandates and
neither owns a game. Which speaks for esports matters to them and to almost
nobody else.

**The Olympic content line.** A virtual programme built on sports simulations
excludes the titles esports actually consists of. The industry's view is that
this is not esports; the IOC's is that its values are not for sale. There is
no visible compromise.

**Match fixing.** Structural conditions — betting, young players, low pay in
lower tiers — that other sports have taken decades to manage, arriving all at
once in a sport whose employers are companies.

**Player welfare.** Careers peaking at twenty-two, practice hours that have
caused injury, and contracts that have needed reform. Improving, and driven by
leagues rather than by governing bodies.

**What happens when a game dies.** A discipline can end because its owner stops
supporting it, and its professionals' careers end with it. Every other sport on
this site can at least promise its athletes that the sport will still be there
next year.
