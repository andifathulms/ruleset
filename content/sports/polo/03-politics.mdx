# Governing body politics

Polo's international federation runs a world championship its best players
cannot enter, and the sport's real summit is a club tournament in Buenos Aires.

The Federation of International Polo was formed in 1982 and received IOC
recognition in 1998. Its world championship is contested at a **capped team
handicap**, which means the highest-rated players — the people who are
unambiguously the best in the world — are ineligible. That is a direct and
deliberate consequence of the handicap system, intended to make international
competition broad rather than a two-nation affair, and it has the effect of
detaching the federation's flagship event from the sport's elite.

Where the elite actually is: the **Argentine Open** at Palermo, contested at the
highest team handicaps in existence, is the tournament every top player wants,
and the British and American high-goal seasons are the other professional
markets. All of them are club competitions funded by **patrons** — amateur
players who fund a team and play in it alongside professionals, which is a
financing model with no equivalent anywhere else on this site.

Argentina's dominance is close to total. Almost every ten-goal player is
Argentine, the breeding industry is Argentine, and the professional labour
market runs on Argentine players contracted to foreign patrons.

The Olympic question is effectively closed. A sport requiring each competitor
to transport several horses, played seriously by a small number of nations, and
identified with wealth, has no realistic path, and the federation's energy goes
into the world championship and into growth rather than into a campaign.
