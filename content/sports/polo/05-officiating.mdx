# Umpires on horseback

Polo is umpired from the saddle. Two mounted umpires ride with the play, and
they are judging a line that cannot be seen. At every moment they must know
where the path of the ball runs, which rider is following it most closely, and
whether another rider cut across in front. They do this at a gallop, among
eight horses, on a field three hundred yards long.

The foul they look for is danger. A rider who is penalised for
<Term id="crossing">crossing</Term> may have touched nobody. The umpire is
ruling on a collision that did not happen.

When the two umpires disagree, they ask the
<Term id="third-man">third man</Term>, who sits on the sideline. Few sports
build in a way to settle a disagreement between officials of equal rank.

Penalties are graded by how dangerous the foul was. The lightest are free
hits from set distances. The heaviest is an
<Term id="penalty-goal">automatic goal</Term>, given without the ball being
struck. A great deal therefore rests on one judgement about an invisible
line.

Most competitions have no video review, and nothing measures the line.
