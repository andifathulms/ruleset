# Where it is played

Argentina, comprehensively, and then a set of markets that buy Argentine
players and Argentine horses.

**Argentina** is polo in a way that few countries are any sport on this site.
It has the deepest club structure, the highest-rated tournament — the Argentine
Open at Palermo — the breeding industry that supplies the world, and almost
every ten-goal player alive. Argentine professionals are contracted to teams
across the world and return for their own season.

**Great Britain** and **the United States** run the other two significant
high-goal seasons, funded by patrons, and both import Argentine players and
horses to fill them. **Uruguay**, **Chile**, **Brazil** and **Mexico** have real
domestic structures.

Beyond the Americas and Britain, polo is played in **India** and **Pakistan**,
where its modern codification began; in **Spain**, **France**, **Italy** and
**Germany**; in the **Gulf states**, where investment has been substantial; and
in **South Africa**, **Australia**, **New Zealand**, **Nigeria** and **Kenya**.

The constraint everywhere is horses. A polo club needs land, stabling, grooms
and a supply of trained ponies, and those requirements set both where the sport
can exist and who inside a country can play it. The result is a genuinely
international sport with a very narrow social base almost everywhere it is
found — and a professional workforce, largely Argentine, whose members
frequently began as grooms rather than as patrons.
