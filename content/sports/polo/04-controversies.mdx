# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Collisions.** The founding safety problem, and the source of polo's most
distinctive law. Two half-tonne animals converging on the same ball cannot be
managed by punishing contact after it happens, so the code assigns priority
before it does: the **line of the ball**, and right of way to whoever follows
it most closely. Crossing it is penalised on the danger created, whether or not
anything was hit.

**Left-handed play.** Prohibited in the 1970s, and the reasoning is geometric.
A left-handed player and a right-handed player contesting the same line
approach each other head-on with mallets and horses converging. Requiring
everybody to play right-handed means two players on the same line pass on the
same side. It is one of very few rules on this site that bans a physical
characteristic outright.

**Horse welfare.** The live controversy, and it arrived at polo by the same
route it arrived at equestrian: after riding was removed from modern
pentathlon in 2022, the argument that public consent for using animals in sport
cannot be assumed did not stop there. Polo's specific exposures are the
intensity of the work, the number of animals a professional career consumes,
and the use of drugs — a mass fatality at a high-goal match in Florida in 2009,
caused by an incorrectly compounded supplement, killed twenty-one ponies and
forced changes to veterinary oversight.

**Cloning.** Arrived through a gap in the rules and remains unaddressed by any
governing body. The question underneath it is whether the pony is a competitor
or equipment, and polo has never had to answer that before.

**The handicap.** Rated by committee rather than by result, which makes a
player's rating a judgement with direct financial consequences, and appeals and
disputes about ratings are a permanent feature of the sport.
