# Equipment evolution

The equipment is alive, there are several of them per player, and since 2010
some of them are copies.

**The ponies** are the sport, and "pony" is a courtesy: they are full-sized
horses, mostly Thoroughbred crosses, bred for acceleration, stopping and the
temperament to be ridden into another horse. A player brings a **string** —
several animals, changed between chukkas, because none can gallop for a whole
match. A high-goal player may use six or eight in a game.

This is the distinction from equestrian, which this site also covers. There,
one horse is the registered competitor: passported, inspected, named in the
result, judged. Here no single animal is the competitor. The string is rotated
through the match, and what the sport actually rewards is the depth and quality
of it.

That has consequences the rules never intended. The string is widely reckoned
to account for the majority of a player's performance, and it is bought. Since
2010 the best of it can also be **cloned** — copies of an exceptional mare have
played and won at the top of the Argentine game, several of them in one
player's string in the same match. No rule permitted this and none prohibited
it, because polo's regulations govern handicaps, right of way and mallets, and
had nothing to say about where a horse came from.

**The mallet** is a cane shaft with a hardwood head, struck with the side
rather than the end, and it must be carried in the right hand. **The ball** was
willow root and is now plastic. **Helmets** are compulsory, and face guards and
knee guards are common.

The most consequential equipment change in a century was not to any of these
objects. It was to how the animals are produced.
