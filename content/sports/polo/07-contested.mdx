# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Cloning.** Copies of an exceptional mare have played and won at the top of
the sport, and no governing body has ruled on it. The question underneath is
whether a polo pony is a competitor — in which case cloning is an eligibility
matter — or equipment, in which case it is manufacturing. Polo has never had to
decide, and the practice is expanding while it does not.

**Horse welfare.** The argument that removed riding from modern pentathlon in
2022 has not stopped there. Polo's exposures are the intensity of the work, the
number of animals a career consumes, and veterinary practice — the 2009 Florida
fatalities, where twenty-one ponies died from an incorrectly compounded
supplement, remain the sport's worst incident.

**A world championship without the best players.** The federation caps its
championship's team handicap to keep it broad, which excludes the highest-rated
players by design. Whether an international title that the world's best cannot
contest is worth having is argued inside the sport.

**Ratings.** Handicaps are set by committee, not by results, and they determine
a professional's employability and fee. Disputes about them are constant and
structural.

**Access.** A sport whose equipment eats is not going to become cheap, and
club chukkas and school programmes are the only levers anyone has. Polo's
handicap makes it unusually welcoming once you are inside and does nothing
about getting in.

**Whether the Olympic question is closed.** The federation holds recognition
and does not campaign seriously. Nothing about the logistics has changed since
1936.
