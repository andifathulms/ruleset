# Controversies that forced rule changes

**1976, and the end of an era.** Not a scandal, and the largest thing in this
sport's history. Olympic hockey moved to artificial turf, and a game built over
fifty years for grass ceased to exist. India, six-time consecutive Olympic
champions, and Pakistan, its principal rival, both declined at the top level
within a decade and neither has won since 1984.

There was no incident and no villain. A federation chose a faster, more
watchable surface, and the sport's competitive geography reorganised around who
could afford it.

**Congestion, and the end of offside.** By the 1990s offside on a small pitch
with a fast ball had produced a congested midfield and the sport's most
disputed decision. The FIH removed the rule entirely in 1998.

Coaches predicted it would make defending impossible. It did not — defences
reorganised inside a season — and hockey became the only major invasion sport
without an offside law. Football, rugby, handball and lacrosse all still have
one.

**Dead time.** Free hits used to mean a stoppage, a repositioning and a pass.
The self-pass in 2009 let the player simply carry on. Quarters and a stopped
clock in 2015 removed time-wasting entirely.

**The circle.** Almost everything decisive in hockey happens inside a small
semicircle with a hard ball at speed and bodies between the umpire and the
incident. Video referral, introduced in 2011, is limited to that area for
exactly that reason.
