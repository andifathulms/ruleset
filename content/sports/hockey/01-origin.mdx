# Where it came from

People have hit a ball with a curved stick in most parts of the world for a
very long time. Modern hockey is younger. It was written down in Victorian
England, in the same decades that gave [football](/sports/football/) its
Laws. The Hockey Association's rules date from 1886, and the international
federation from 1924.

<Photo id="hockey-empire-1919" />

Two rules from that first code still shape every match.

The first is about the stick. It has a flat side and a rounded side, and only
the <Term id="flat-face">flat face</Term> may touch the ball. Half the natural
movements of a stick are therefore fouls. Players had to invent the
<Term id="reverse-stick">reverse stick</Term>, turning the stick over, to
reach a ball on the wrong side.

<Photo id="hockey-flat-face" />

The second is about the goal. A goal counts only if an attacker plays the
ball inside the <Term id="shooting-circle">shooting circle</Term>. A shot from
anywhere else is worth nothing. So the whole game pushes towards two small
areas, and from that rule came the <Term id="penalty-corner">penalty
corner</Term>.

## The grass years

Hockey spread through the British Empire, and its early international game
grew through the Commonwealth.

<Photo id="hockey-amsterdam-teams-1928" />

Amsterdam, 1928. India beat Switzerland 6–0 on a grass pitch and went on to
win the Olympic title. It was the first of six in a row, up to 1956. On
grass the ball did not run true, and India's close, careful stickwork suited
it.

<Photos ids="hockey-india-1928 hockey-amsterdam-1928" />

That era ended when the ground under the game changed, in 1976.
