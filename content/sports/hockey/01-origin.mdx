# Origin and invention

Stick-and-ball games are ancient and appear across most of the world, but
modern hockey is a Victorian English codification, from the same decades and
the same impulse that produced football's Laws. The Hockey Association's rules
date from 1886; the international federation from 1924.

Two decisions from the original code still define the sport, and both are
restrictions rather than permissions.

**The ball may only be played with one face of the stick.** The stick has a
flat side and a rounded side, and touching the ball with the rounded side is a
foul. That single rule is why hockey looks unlike any other stick sport: half
the natural movements are illegal, and the reverse-stick technique had to be
invented to work around it.

**A goal counts only from inside the circle.** A shot from anywhere else is
worth nothing, which concentrates the entire sport into two small semicircles
and produces the penalty corner.

Hockey spread through the British Empire, and its early competitive history is
almost entirely Indian and Pakistani: India won six consecutive Olympic titles
from 1928 to 1956 on grass, with a stickwork tradition suited to a surface
where the ball did not run true.

That era ended when the surface changed, which is the subject of the next
section and the largest thing that has ever happened to this sport.
