# Where it is played

Hockey's map was redrawn in one year, 1976, when the game moved from grass to
artificial turf.

Before that, India and Pakistan led the world. India won six Olympic titles in
a row from 1928 to 1956, and eight in all. Pakistan won three. Their game was
built for grass, where the ball did not run true and close stickwork decided
matches.

After the switch, the strongest teams were the Netherlands, Australia,
Germany, Belgium, Great Britain, Argentina and Spain. Neither India nor
Pakistan has won an Olympic title since 1984.

Part of the reason is money. A water-based pitch has to be built and watered,
and the countries that built many of them were wealthy ones. A sport that had
grown through the Commonwealth now had a cost barrier. India came back to the
Olympic medals after forty years away, following long investment in exactly
those pitches.

The women's game has a different map. The Netherlands leads it. Argentina,
Las Leonas, has stayed near the top with far fewer pitches than its rivals.

The federation is trying to lower the barrier it raised. Hockey5s, five a side
on a smaller pitch, is cheaper to stage, and new turf standards allow surfaces
that need less water.
