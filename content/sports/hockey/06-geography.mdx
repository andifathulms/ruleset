# Where it is played

Hockey has the most abruptly redrawn competitive map of any sport on this site,
and the date is 1976.

Before it: India and Pakistan, overwhelmingly. India won six consecutive
Olympic titles from 1928 to 1956 and eight in total; Pakistan won three. Their
game was built on a grass surface where the ball did not run true and close
stickwork decided everything.

After the switch to artificial turf: the Netherlands, Australia, Germany,
Belgium, Great Britain, Argentina and Spain. Neither India nor Pakistan has won
an Olympic title since 1984.

The mechanism is not mysterious and it is not entirely about technique. A
water-based pitch is a facility with a construction cost and an irrigation
requirement, and the countries that built them in quantity were wealthy ones.
A sport that had grown through the Commonwealth acquired a capital barrier
overnight.

India's recent return to Olympic medals, after a forty-year absence from them,
followed sustained investment in exactly that infrastructure.

The women's game has a somewhat different map with the Netherlands dominant,
and Argentina is the outlier worth noting — sustained success from a much
smaller facility base than its rivals.

Hockey5s and reduced-water turf specifications are the federation's attempts to
undo a barrier it created, which makes hockey the only sport here actively
legislating to reverse its own geography.
