# The ground, the stick and the ball

The biggest change in hockey's equipment was the ground itself.

From 1976 Olympic hockey was played on [artificial
turf](#fih-1976-artificial-turf). On grass the ball bounces off bumps, so the
game rewards close control and quick hands. On turf the ball runs true and
fast. That made new skills possible: the <Term id="drag-flick">drag
flick</Term>, long spells of reverse-stick play, and passes at a speed grass
would never carry.

<Photo id="hockey-artificial-pitch" />

Every skill moved, and so did the balance of power. India and Pakistan had
led the sport for half a century with a game built for grass. Neither has won
an Olympic title since 1984.

Turf also made hockey expensive. A water-based pitch costs a lot to build and
needs watering. A game that could be played on any field now needed a
facility. Countries that could not build one fell away. The federation has
since approved surfaces that need less water, partly to undo this.

Sticks went from wood to composite. The new sticks made drag flicks much
faster, and the rules added limits on the shape of the stick and the curve of
its head.

<Photos ids="hockey-wooden-stick hockey-drag-flick" />

Protection followed injuries. Players wear mouthguards and shin pads, and
defenders may wear face masks at a penalty corner, where they run straight at
a flick struck from about fifteen metres.
