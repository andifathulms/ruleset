# Governing body politics

The FIH's defining decision was the surface, and its defining problem for forty
years has been what that decision did to the sport's geography.

Moving to artificial turf in 1976 produced a faster, more televisable game and
removed the dependence on host groundskeeping that had made international
results partly a matter of grass quality. It also priced out most of the
world. A federation that had grown through the Commonwealth found its
strongest nations unable to afford the facility its rules now required.

Everything since has been an attempt to manage that. Hockey5s — five a side on
a smaller pitch — exists to be stageable cheaply. Turf specifications have been
revised to approve surfaces needing less water. The federation's development
programme is unusually prominent for a sport of its size, and the reason is
that its own rule created the problem.

The other governance thread is a willingness to legislate that is rare on this
site. Hockey removed offside entirely in 1998, introduced the self-pass in
2009, and restructured its match into quarters in 2015. Most federations here
amend cautiously; the FIH runs a mandatory experiment programme and adopts
changes quickly.

That has largely worked. The offside abolition is regarded within the sport as
straightforwardly successful, which is a sentence this site has not been able
to write about many rule changes.
