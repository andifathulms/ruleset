# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The surface, still.** Fifty years on, the cost of a competitive hockey pitch
remains the sport's largest structural problem. Reduced-water and dry turf
specifications are being approved, and the argument inside the sport is whether
they play well enough to be used at the top or will simply create a second
tier of surface with a second tier of nations on it.

**The penalty corner.** A large share of international goals come from one set
piece, defended by players running at a drag flick from fifteen metres. It is
both the sport's signature and its main injury risk, and proposals to change
the defending numbers, the injection rules or the sanction itself recur
constantly.

**Whether more should be removed.** Having abolished offside successfully,
hockey has an appetite for simplification that other federations lack.
Obstruction is the usual candidate — it is the sport's least consistent call
and the only one asking an umpire about purpose rather than position — and it
has survived every review so far.

**Hockey5s.** A cheaper five-a-side format with its own rules, and a live
question about whether it is a development tool or a rival. Rugby and lacrosse
both got onto the Olympic programme with reduced formats, which the FIH has
noticed.
