# Who makes the calls

Two umpires run a hockey match, one for each half of the pitch. That is few
for such a large field. It works because a goal can only be scored from the
circle, so the decisions that matter happen in two small places.

<Photo id="hockey-umpire-2012" />

A team can ask for a <Term id="video-referral">video referral</Term> on an
incident in the circle. If the team is right, it keeps its referral. The
video umpire answers questions of fact. Did the ball come off an attacker's
stick? Did it touch a foot? Did it cross the line? Was there a foul before
the goal? [Cricket](/sports/cricket/) uses the same system.

Two calls stay with the umpires on the pitch.

The first is <Term id="obstruction">obstruction</Term>: putting yourself
between an opponent and the ball without playing it. The umpire has to decide
whether a player was playing the ball or shielding it. That is a judgement
about purpose, so no camera can make it.

The second is dangerous play. The ball is solid and fast, so whether a raised
ball was dangerous depends on who was near it and how. The umpire decides in
a fraction of a second.

<Photo id="hockey-cards" />

The [end of offside in 1998](#fih-1998-offside-abolished) was also a decision
about officiating. Hockey's most disputed call disappeared because the rule
behind it was deleted.
