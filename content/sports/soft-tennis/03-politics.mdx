# Governing body politics

Soft tennis is governed competently, played by a small number of countries very
seriously, and has almost no prospect of going further — and its federation
appears to have made peace with that.

The ISTF was formed in 1974, ninety years after the sport was invented, because
until then it had been essentially Japanese. Its membership grew from the
countries where the game had been carried during the period of Japanese rule —
Korea and Taiwan — into a wider Asian and then international membership.

The Asian Games place from 1990 is the sport's institutional foundation, and
the company it entered in is instructive. Kabaddi, sepak takraw, wushu and soft
tennis were all admitted at the same Games: four sports with deep regional
participation, no global federation weight, and no realistic Olympic route. The
Asian Games programme exists to hold exactly that category, and this site has
now covered all four.

The Olympic question barely arises. Tennis is already on the programme, has
been since 1896 with an interruption, and occupies the slot any racket-and-ball
argument would have to be made in. A second tennis, played with a softer ball
and contested seriously by three countries, is not a proposition anyone is
making.

What the federation does instead is spread the game as a school and community
sport, where its accessibility is a genuine asset — a slow, soft, cheap ball is
a good way to introduce racket sport to children, which is what it was invented
for in the first place.
