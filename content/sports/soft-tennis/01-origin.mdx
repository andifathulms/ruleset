# Origin and invention

Soft tennis exists because tennis balls were too expensive.

Tennis was introduced to Japan in the 1870s by Western teachers, and it ran
immediately into a supply problem. Pressurised cloth-covered balls had to be
imported, they cost a great deal, and they did not last — which made it
impossible to spread the game through a school system that had no way to keep
replacing them.

Around 1884 the answer was to substitute a soft, air-filled rubber ball that
could be made domestically. The change was economic, not sporting, and the
intention was to preserve the game rather than to alter it.

It altered it completely. A thirty-gram rubber ball loses speed quickly and
deforms against the strings, so it cannot be hit through an opponent. Power
stopped being a weapon. Rallies lengthened. The serve ceased to dominate. And
the racket had to change too — lighter, softly strung — at which point the two
games could no longer be played with each other's equipment.

By the time tennis balls were readily available in Japan, the country had a
different sport with its own technique, its own scoring and its own school
structure, and no reason to abandon it. Soft tennis spread into Korea and
Taiwan during the period of Japanese rule and took root in both.

The International Soft Tennis Federation was formed in 1974, and the sport
became an Asian Games medal event at Beijing in 1990 — the same edition that
admitted kabaddi, sepak takraw and wushu.
