# A cheaper ball, and a new game

Soft tennis began with a ball. Tennis reached Japan in the 1870s, brought by
Western teachers. Its balls were pressurised and covered in cloth. They had to
be imported, they cost a great deal, and they wore out. Schools could not keep
buying them, so the game could not spread.

Around 1884 Japan found an answer it could make at home: a soft rubber ball
filled with air. The aim was to keep tennis going, with a cheaper ball. [The
new ball](#softtennis-1884-rubber-ball) changed the game instead.

A <Term id="soft-ball">soft ball</Term> of about thirty grams slows down
quickly and squashes against the strings. It cannot be hit hard past an
opponent. Power stopped winning points, and each <Term id="rally">rally</Term>
grew longer. The serve lost its force. The racket changed as well, becoming
lighter and more softly strung. After that, neither game could be played with
the other's equipment.

By the time tennis balls were easy to buy in Japan, the country had a
different sport. It had its own strokes, its own scoring and its own school
competitions, and nobody had a reason to give it up.

## Across the sea

Soft tennis spread to Korea and Taiwan during the period of Japanese rule, and
both kept it. Club photographs from Taipei survive from that time. In Korea the
national festival held soft tennis finals in the early 1920s.

<Photos ids="soft-tennis-taihoku soft-tennis-korea-1921" />

For about ninety years the game had no international body. It had grown up
under separate national bodies in Japan, Korea and Taiwan, and nobody held
competition between the three. In 1974 the [International Soft Tennis
Federation was formed](#softtennis-1974-istf). It had to write one rulebook
for three countries that had played apart for decades. The same rules
[made doubles the main form of the game](#softtennis-doubles-primary), because
that was how schools already played it.

In 1990 soft tennis became [an Asian Games medal
sport](#softtennis-1990-asian-games), at Beijing. Three other sports joined at
the same Games: [kabaddi](/sports/kabaddi/), [sepak
takraw](/sports/sepak-takraw/) and [wushu](/sports/wushu/). All four had many
players in Asia and little standing elsewhere. Soft tennis has been on the
Asian Games programme ever since.
