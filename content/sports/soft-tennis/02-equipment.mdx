# Equipment evolution

One object, substituted for economic reasons, and a hundred and forty years of
consequences.

**The ball** is hollow air-filled rubber, about 30 grams and 6.6 centimetres
across, with no felt covering. A tennis ball is roughly twice the weight, is
pressurised, and is covered in a nap that grips the strings and the court. Every
difference between the two sports descends from that comparison.

A soft ball decelerates fast, so the court plays larger. It deforms on impact,
so it stays on the strings longer and spin behaves differently. It cannot be
driven flat past a competent opponent, so points must be built rather than
struck.

**The racket** followed the ball. Soft tennis rackets are considerably lighter
than tennis rackets and strung at much lower tension — a stiff string bed would
simply fail to control a soft ball, and a heavy frame is unnecessary when there
is no pace to absorb. Modern frames are graphite and composite, as tennis's are,
but the specifications went in a different direction from the same starting
point.

That divergence is what made the split permanent. A soft tennis racket cannot
usefully play tennis and a tennis racket cannot usefully play soft tennis, so
there was never a point at which the two could quietly merge back.

This site records several equipment substitutions — synthetic for rattan in
sepak takraw, steel for wood in boules, resin for urethane in bowling. Every
one of those reproduced or improved what came before. This is the only case
where a substitution made for cost produced a different sport and both survived.
