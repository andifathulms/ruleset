# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the sport has a case beyond Asia.** Soft tennis is cheap, safe, easy
to learn and played on courts that already exist worldwide. It has still not
spread, because in most countries tennis occupies the same space and there is
no supply problem to solve. Whether the federation can find a different
argument — as a school introduction to racket sport, principally — is its
central strategic question.

**Doubles as the flagship.** Sensible for participation, unusual for a racket
sport, and it makes the game read oddly to anyone arriving from tennis.
Whether singles should be promoted for visibility is argued.

**Scoring for television.** Four-point games are short and legible and the
sport has never rewritten them. Five comparable sports on this site did rewrite
theirs between 1999 and 2006 and severed their statistics doing it. Whether
soft tennis was wise or merely overlooked is a genuine question.

**Living beside tennis.** The two share a court and an ancestry, and one of
them has been Olympic since 1896. Any argument for soft tennis has to explain
why a second racket-and-ball sport on the same court is worth a place, and no
version of that argument has worked.

**Depth.** Three countries take it seriously. That is enough for excellent
competition and not enough for any universality claim, and there is no obvious
route to changing it.
