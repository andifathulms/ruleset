# What is still argued about

None of this has been settled.

**A case beyond Asia.** Soft tennis is cheap, safe, easy to learn and played on
courts that exist almost everywhere. It still has not spread. In most
countries tennis fills the same space. The federation's best argument may be
the game as a first racket sport for schoolchildren.

**Doubles first.** Every <Term id="pair">pair</Term> splits the court between
a forward and a back player, and doubles is the main event. That suits
schools, which fit twice as many players on each court. It also makes the game
look strange to anyone who knows tennis, where singles comes first. Some argue
that singles should be pushed for the sake of attention.

**Scoring and television.** A <Term id="game">game</Term> is the first to four
points, with <Term id="deuce">deuce</Term> at three-all. The sport has never
rewritten this. [Badminton](/sports/badminton/),
[volleyball](/sports/volleyball/), [table tennis](/sports/table-tennis/) and
[fistball](/sports/fistball/) all changed their scoring between 1999 and 2006 to suit broadcasters. Whether soft tennis
was wise to stay put, or was simply never asked, is an open question.

**Living beside tennis.** The two games share a court and an ancestor, and one
of them is an Olympic sport. A case for soft tennis has to explain why a
second racket game on the same court deserves a place. No version of that
case has worked yet.

**Depth.** Three countries take the game seriously. That is enough for strong
competition at the top. It is not enough for a claim to be a world sport, and
there is no clear way to change it.
