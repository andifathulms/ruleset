# Who makes the calls

The calls are the ones tennis players know. Was the ball in or out? Did the
<Term id="serve">serve</Term> land in the right box? Was it a <Term
id="foot-fault">foot fault</Term>? Did a player touch the net, or hit the ball
before it crossed? Championship matches have an umpire and line judges. Lower
down, players make their own calls, as in tennis.

The soft ball makes several of these calls easier. A slow ball is easier to
judge at the line than a fast one, so in and out calls cause less argument. A
soft ball stays on the strings longer, so a double hit can be seen. And the
serve is not a weapon, so a wrong call on a serve rarely decides a match.

Soft tennis has not taken up the ball-tracking review that tennis and
[badminton](/sports/badminton/) use. The problem it solves is much smaller
here.

Doubles adds one call that tennis does not have. Where a format fixes the
places of the <Term id="forward-player">forward player</Term> and the <Term
id="back-player">back player</Term>, a player who is <Term
id="out-of-position">out of position</Term> loses the point. The umpire has to
see it as it happens.
