# Officiating

Tennis's officiating, with several of the hard parts removed by the ball.

The calls are the familiar ones: in or out, the serve landing in the correct
box, foot faults, net touches, and whether a ball was volleyed before it
crossed. An umpire and line judges at championship level; self-officiation
below it, as in tennis.

What the soft ball removes is instructive. A slow ball is easier to judge at the
line than a fast one, so out calls are less contentious than tennis's. A ball
that stays on the strings longer makes double hits and carries visible rather
than momentary, which removes a class of dispute. And with no serve worth more
than a rally, there is nothing corresponding to a service ace being called
wrongly at a decisive moment.

The result is that soft tennis has never needed the ball-tracking review
systems that tennis and badminton adopted, and does not use them. It is not
that the sport declined the technology on principle — it is that the problem
the technology solves is much smaller here.

The doubles positions add one call the neighbouring sports do not have: where a
format specifies a forward and a back player, playing out of position is an
offence, and it must be judged live.
