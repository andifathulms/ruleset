# Where it is played

Three countries play it seriously and everybody else plays it a little.

**Japan** is the sport. It was invented there, it is a mainstream school and
university sport with participation numbers in the millions, and the domestic
competitive structure is far deeper than anything international. A very large
proportion of the people who have ever played soft tennis have been Japanese
schoolchildren.

<Photo id="soft-tennis-japan-2024" />

**South Korea** and **Chinese Taipei** are the other two genuine powers, and
both acquired the sport during the period of Japanese rule. All three have
national programmes and contest Asian Games medals seriously between them.

<Photos ids="soft-tennis-dong-a-1923 soft-tennis-korea-1924" />

Beyond that, soft tennis is played in **China**, **the Philippines**,
**Thailand**, **Indonesia**, **India** and across a scattering of European and
American federations, generally on a small scale and often introduced by
Japanese cultural or educational programmes.

<Photo id="soft-tennis-india-2019" />

The geography is therefore an almost perfect map of one country's twentieth
century, and the sport's spread outside East Asia has been shallow for a
reason that has nothing to do with cost — a soft tennis ball is cheap, the
racket is cheap, and the court already exists in most countries because tennis
is played on it.

What has not travelled is the reason to play it. Where tennis is available,
soft tennis is a solution to a problem that country does not have.
