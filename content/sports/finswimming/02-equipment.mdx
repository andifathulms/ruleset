# Equipment evolution

This is the sport where the equipment question has no exit.

**The monofin** is a single large blade worn on both feet. It converts a
whole-body undulation — chest, hips, legs, blade — into propulsion far more
efficiently than kicking with separate feet, and it is the reason finswimming
is quicker than swimming over the same distance. Blades are made from
composites and glass fibre, vary in stiffness and profile, and are matched to
the athlete and the distance.

CMAS regulates it by **dimension**: maximum surface area and dimensions are
capped, materials are broadly open. That choice is the whole story, and it is
worth setting against the two other answers this site records.

- **Swimming**, faced with polyurethane suits in 2009, banned the suits and let
  the records stand. Several are still unbeaten.
- **Athletics**, faced with a javelin flying too far, redesigned the implement
  and reset the record book.
- **Finswimming** could do neither. Ban the fin and there is no sport. Redesign
  it to a single standard and you remove the athlete's ability to match
  equipment to their own undulation, which is a real part of the craft.

So it capped the dimensions, left the materials open, and split the record book
by fin type: monofin records and bi-fins records are kept separately, because
two conventional fins and one large blade are not the same event.

The other equipment follows the discipline. A large-bore centre snorkel for
surface events, so the head never lifts. Nothing at all for apnea. An
open-circuit compressed-air cylinder for immersion, carried through the race —
the only racing event covered here in which the athlete brings their air with
them.
