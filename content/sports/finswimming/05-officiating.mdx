# Swimming's officials, and a ruler

Most of the officiating comes straight from swimming. There are starters,
turn judges and lane judges. A <Term id="touchpad">touchpad</Term> stops the
clock to a hundredth of a second, and a
<Term id="false-start">false start</Term> is caught in the same way. Records
are approved for each discipline and each distance.

<Photo id="finswimming-jarolim-blocks" />

Finswimming adds three checks of its own.

**Surfacing.** In an <Term id="apnea">apnea</Term> race the swimmer must stay
under the water for all 50 metres. Any
<Term id="surfacing">break of the surface</Term> ends the race. The judges
watch a swimmer who is trying to stay out of sight, and the offence is to be
seen.

**The fin.** Fins are measured before competition. The surface area and the
dimensions are checked against the limits. A sport that lets fins differ has
to guard the edge of that freedom, and it does so with a ruler.

**The air.** In immersion the breathing set must be of the permitted
open-circuit type. It is a diving inspection, carried out on a racer.

Nobody judges the kick itself. Swimming has rules on how breaststroke and
butterfly must be swum. Finswimming has no rule of that kind, because there is
only one good way to drive a monofin.
