# Officiating

Mostly swimming's officiating, with two additions that are unlike anything
else on this site.

The familiar half: touchpads, lane judges, starters, false-start detection and
turn judges, all working as they do in swimming. Times to the hundredth,
records ratified per discipline and per distance.

**Surfacing.** In apnea events the swimmer must remain underwater for the whole
50 metres. Any break of the surface ends the race, and judging it is a binary
observation of a swimmer who is deliberately invisible for the duration. It is
the only race on this site where being seen is the infringement.

**Equipment inspection.** Because the monofin is capped by dimension rather
than standardised, fins are measured before competition — surface area and
dimensions checked against the limits. A sport that permits equipment variation
has to police the boundary of that variation physically, and finswimming does
it with a ruler.

The immersion discipline adds a third: the breathing set must be an
open-circuit configuration of the permitted type, which is a technical-diving
inspection performed on a racing athlete.

What none of this instruments is the technique itself. The undulation that
makes the sport fast is not judged at all — there is no equivalent of
swimming's stroke-legality rules for breaststroke or butterfly, because there
is only one way to drive a monofin and no incentive to do anything else.
