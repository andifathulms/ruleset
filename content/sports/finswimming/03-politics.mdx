# A pool sport outside the pool federation

Finswimming is raced in a swimming pool, and swimming's federation has nothing
to do with it.

World Aquatics governs [swimming](/sports/swimming/),
[diving](/sports/diving/), [water polo](/sports/water-polo/),
[artistic swimming](/sports/artistic-swimming/) and open water. CMAS governs
finswimming, as one activity among many under water. The two share a pool, a
lane, a start and a <Term id="touchpad">touchpad</Term>. As organisations
they are not connected.

This closes a door. A sport that wants a place at a multi-sport event competes
with the sports of its own kind. For finswimming that kind is aquatics, and
aquatics already belongs to a federation with five Olympic disciplines and a
century of standing. A pool sport that arrives from the diving federation has
no clear way in. There is no Olympic prospect in view.

CMAS also has other work. It certifies divers, sets technical standards and
runs scientific and environmental programmes. Racing is a smaller part of that
work than finswimmers would like. [Lifesaving](/sports/lifesaving/) knows a
stronger form of the same tension: its federation exists first to prevent
drowning.

The World Games have been the sport's home since the first edition in 1981.
They were made for sports like this one: well organised, played in many
countries, and with no route to the Olympic Games. The sport has largely
accepted that this is as far as it goes.
