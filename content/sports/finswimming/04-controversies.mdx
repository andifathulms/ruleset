# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Capping the fin.** The recurring one. Monofin design advanced quickly once
the sport was competitive, and an unregulated blade would have made equipment
the only variable that mattered. CMAS capped surface area and dimensions and
left materials open — a partial answer that keeps equipment selection in the
sport, deliberately, and is periodically argued about for exactly that reason.

**Splitting bi-fins off.** Two conventional fins and one monofin produce
different speeds and different techniques, and pooling them would have made one
of them pointless. The sport separated them into disciplines with separate
records rather than choosing between them — the same instrument powerlifting
used for raw and equipped, and cycling for the hour record.

**Apnea safety.** Racing 50 metres underwater at maximum output on a single
breath carries a hypoxic blackout risk, and the supervision requirements around
apnea competition exist because of it. It is the sport's real safety question
and it is handled by protocol rather than by changing the event.

**Immersion and the cylinder.** An event in which the athlete carries
compressed air raises equipment questions no other racing sport has: cylinder
type, valve configuration and what counts as an open circuit. The rules are
technical diving rules imported into a race.

**Which federation should govern it.** Not a rule change and arguably the
sport's largest structural issue: a pool racing sport administered by an
underwater-activities body, in a world where the pool federation is one of the
most powerful in the Olympic movement.
