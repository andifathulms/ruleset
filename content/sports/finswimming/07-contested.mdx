# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**How much the fin should be allowed to do.** Dimensions are capped and
materials are open, so blade design is a live technical field and part of elite
preparation. Whether that is a legitimate craft or an unpoliced performance
variable is argued, and it is the same argument powerlifting has about
supportive equipment.

**Whether the record books should stay split.** Monofin and bi-fins, surface
and apnea and immersion — the sport keeps several books, which is honest and
which fragments an already small competitive field.

**Which federation should govern a pool sport.** Finswimming is administered by
an underwater-activities body while the pool is institutionally owned by
another federation entirely. Nothing about this is likely to change, and it
constrains every ambition the sport has.

**Apnea's risk.** Racing underwater at maximal output on one breath carries a
blackout risk managed by supervision protocol rather than by altering the
event. Whether that is sufficient is a question the sport revisits.

**Olympic prospects.** There are none in view. Finswimming is faster than
swimming, contested internationally since 1976, and has no path onto a
programme where the aquatic slots belong to somebody else. The World Games is,
realistically, the ceiling — which the sport has largely accepted, and which
makes it one of the more clear-eyed federations covered here.
