# What is still argued about

**How much the fin may do.** The size of a fin is limited and the materials
are open, so blade design keeps moving and choosing a blade is part of
preparing for a race. Some see a real craft. Others see an advantage that
nobody controls. [Powerlifting](/sports/powerlifting/) has the same argument
about supportive equipment.

**So many record books.** There are separate books for monofin and bi-fins,
and for surface, apnea and immersion. That is honest, because the events are
different. It also divides a small sport into smaller fields.

**The risk in apnea.** A swimmer who races 50 metres at full effort on one
breath can black out from lack of oxygen. The sport answers with supervision
rules around apnea races and has left the event itself unchanged. Whether that
is enough is a question it keeps coming back to.

**Who should govern it.** A pool race is run by an underwater federation,
while the pool belongs, in sporting politics, to another body. Nothing
suggests this will change, and it limits every ambition the sport has.
