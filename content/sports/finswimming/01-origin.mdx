# Divers who began to race

Finswimming was started by divers, and it still shows.

On 17 May 1959 four diving clubs met at the covered municipal pool in Bologna
to race in fins. They came from Bologna, Genoa, Milan and Turin, and Franco
Pavone of the club Sub Bologna organised the meet. It has been described as
the first finswimming competition held in a pool anywhere.

<Photo id="finswimming-bologna-1959" />

In the same year [the world underwater federation, CMAS, was
founded](#finswimming-1959-cmas). Its first president was Jacques Cousteau.
Its business was everything done under water: teaching people to dive,
underwater photography, scientific work. Racing was one item on a long list.

The disciplines carry that history. In
<Term id="surface">surface</Term> races the swimmer breathes through a
<Term id="snorkel">snorkel</Term>. In <Term id="apnea">apnea</Term> the
swimmer holds one breath for the whole race. In
<Term id="immersion">immersion</Term> the swimmer carries a
<Term id="cylinder">cylinder</Term> of compressed air. These are three answers
to one question, how a person breathes under water, and that is a diver's
question.

The <Term id="monofin">monofin</Term> made the sport fast. Through the 1960s
and 1970s it was developed above all in the Soviet Union and in Italy, and a
diving aid became a racing tool. In 1965 Pavone took Italy's first national
team to Russia. In the team photograph, air cylinders stand at their feet.
In 1972 Moscow hosted a
European championship, and the Soviet post office printed a card for it.

<Photos ids="finswimming-italy-1965 finswimming-moscow-1972" />

[The first world championships were held in
1976](#finswimming-1976-disciplines). They fixed the disciplines the sport
still races. Finswimming has never been on an Olympic programme. It was at the
first World Games, in Santa Clara in 1981, and the World Games are still its
largest stage.
