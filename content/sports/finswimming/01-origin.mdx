# Origin and invention

Finswimming exists because a diving federation decided to hold races.

CMAS — the Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques — was founded in
1959 to govern underwater activity broadly: diving instruction, underwater
photography, scientific and technical work. Jacques Cousteau was its first
president. Racing was one item among many, and the sport that resulted carries
the fingerprints of that parentage in a way nothing else on this site does.

Look at the disciplines. **Surface** is a pool race swum face-down with a
snorkel. **Apnea** is a pool race swum entirely underwater on a single breath.
**Immersion** is a pool race swum underwater carrying an open-circuit
compressed-air cylinder. Those are not three formats of one event; they are
three answers to the question of how a person breathes, and only a federation
whose primary business was being underwater would have organised a racing sport
that way.

The monofin is what made it fast. A single blade worn on both feet, driven by a
whole-body undulation, moves a swimmer through water substantially quicker than
any stroke — finswimming is the fastest human-powered movement in water there
is. Development through the 1960s and 1970s, particularly in the Soviet Union
and in Italy, turned a diving aid into a racing implement.

The first world championships were held in 1976, codifying the disciplines the
sport still contests. Finswimming has never been on an Olympic programme, in
any form, and is contested at the World Games.
