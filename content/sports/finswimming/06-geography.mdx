# Where it is played

Finswimming's map looks nothing like swimming's, and the difference is
institutional rather than aquatic.

The historic powers are **Russia** and **Ukraine**, whose Soviet-era
development of the monofin and of the sport's technique still shows in results;
**Italy**, one of the sport's founding centres; **Hungary**, **Greece** and
**Spain**; **China**, which has invested heavily; and **Colombia**, which is
the most striking case — a country with a modest swimming record and a genuinely
world-class finswimming programme.

None of that follows the map of swimming strength. Australia and the United
States, which dominate swimming, are marginal here. The reason is that
finswimming grew inside CMAS-affiliated diving federations, so the sport took
root where those federations were active and funded, not where pools and
swimming clubs were most numerous.

That is an unusually clean example of a phenomenon this site keeps finding: a
sport's geography is often a map of which institution happened to adopt it
rather than of who is best placed to play it. Fistball follows German
emigration. Sepak takraw follows childhood exposure. Finswimming follows
diving federations.

The facility requirement is the ordinary one and it is a real constraint: a
50-metre pool with lane time available, which is contested everywhere. Where
finswimming has to share water with a strong national swimming federation, it
generally loses.
