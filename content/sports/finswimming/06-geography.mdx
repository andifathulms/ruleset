# A map drawn by diving federations

The strong finswimming countries are not the strong swimming countries.

Russia and Ukraine lead the list. The Soviet Union did much to develop the
monofin and the technique, and that work still shows in the results. Italy is
one of the founding centres and hosted the world championships in Bologna in
1980, four years after the first. Hungary, Greece and Spain are strong. China
has invested heavily. Colombia is the most striking case: a country with a
modest record in swimming and a world-class finswimming programme.

Australia and the United States dominate swimming and are marginal here. The
reason is where the sport grew. Finswimming grew inside the diving federations
that belong to CMAS. It took root where those federations were active and had
money, whatever the number of pools and swimming clubs.

Other sports have maps like this. [Fistball](/sports/fistball/) followed
German emigrants, and [sepak takraw](/sports/sepak-takraw/) is strong where
children grow up playing it.

The sport needs a 50-metre pool with free lanes, and pool time is fought over
everywhere. Where finswimming must share water with a strong national swimming
federation, it usually loses.
