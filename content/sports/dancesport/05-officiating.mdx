# Judging six couples at once

In [gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) or [diving](/sports/diving/) one
competitor performs alone and the judges score that performance against a
standard. Dancesport judges watch six or more couples dance the same dance on
the same floor, all moving, and mark every one of them in about ninety
seconds.

Two things follow from that.

Being seen is part of competing. A couple stuck in a corner, or cut off again
and again, is marked on less evidence.
<Term id="floorcraft">Floorcraft</Term> is a real competitive skill, and
spectators mostly do not notice it.

And the judging is a comparison whatever the system says. A judge has six
couples in view and cannot help weighing one against another.

## From an order to a mark

For most of the sport's history judges gave no scores. Under
<Term id="ordinal-placement">ordinal placement</Term>, a method borrowed from
figure skating, each judge put the couples in order and a counting rule
settled the result. The result could say that a couple was third. It could
not say by how much, and it was hard to explain to a spectator.

[In 2013 the federation changed this in its own
competitions](#dancesport-absolute-judging). Under
<Term id="absolute-judging">absolute judging</Term> each judge gives marks for
four <Term id="components">components</Term>: technical quality, movement to
music, partnering skill, and choreography and presentation. A federation that
wanted a place at the Olympic Games needed scoring that looked like the
scoring already there.

The change cut the sport off from its own past results. Before it there was
only an order within one field of couples, and [no scale to compare
with](#dancesport-scores). The older method is still used in other parts of
the sport.

## Bias

The conditions for biased judging are all present. A panel gives the marks.
There are national blocs. The pool of
<Term id="adjudicator">adjudicators</Term> is small, and judges, coaches and
couples have known each other for years. Component marks, published marks and
the rotation of adjudicators are the answers so far, and the problem has not
gone away.

Nothing in dancesport is measured by an instrument or reviewed on video.
