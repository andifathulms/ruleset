# Officiating

DanceSport's judges do something no other panel on this site is asked to do:
watch six performances at once and compare them.

In gymnastics, diving, figure skating and skateboarding, a competitor performs
alone and the panel gives that performance a score against a standard. In
dancesport, six or more couples dance the same dance simultaneously on the same
floor, travelling, and the judges mark all of them in the same ninety seconds.

Two things follow.

**Being seen is part of competing.** A couple blocked in a corner, or
repeatedly cut off, is marked on less evidence. Floorcraft — navigating a
crowded moving floor at speed without collision while dancing full out — is a
genuine competitive skill and is largely invisible to spectators.

**The judgement is comparative by construction.** Under the old ordinal system
this was explicit: a judge ranked the couples rather than scoring them. The
WDSF's component marking makes each mark absolute in form, but a judge is still
physically comparing six couples in real time.

The components — technical quality, movement to music, partnering skill,
choreography and presentation — are the sport's attempt to say what is being
marked, and they carry the same difficulty every judged sport on this site has:
they name the categories without making the judgement mechanical.

There is no instrumentation. Nothing in dancesport is measured, sensed or
reviewed on video in the way taekwondo, fencing and shooting have adopted.
Given that one of the judged components is how a couple moves to music, it is
difficult to see what could be.
