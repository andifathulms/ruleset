# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the Olympic campaign has a future.** Thirty years of recognition, a
rename, a governance overhaul and a new judging system have not produced a
place. The federation's recognition did produce one — for breaking, briefly.
Whether to keep pursuing inclusion for ballroom and Latin, having watched that
happen, is the sport's live strategic question.

**Judging.** The absolute system was introduced to make results legible and
comparable. Whether component marks genuinely constrain a judge, or simply
express the same comparative instinct in numbers, is argued inside the sport,
as the equivalent question is in gymnastics and figure skating.

**The federation split.** Amateur and professional bodies governing the same
dances, with competing events and mutual non-recognition, means no world title
is uncontested. Nobody has a mechanism for resolving it.

**Costume cost.** Presentation is judged and therefore money is a competitive
variable. Category regulations cap it and do not remove it.

**Breaking.** The WDSF governs a form that a substantial part of its community
did not want governed by a ballroom federation, that reached one Games and was
dropped. What the federation's responsibility to breaking is now that the
Olympic incentive has gone is an open and largely unexamined question.

**Whether it is a sport.** The oldest argument, and the one that has shaped the
federation's every institutional decision — including its name. Nothing about
the answer has been settled by three decades of behaving as though it were.
