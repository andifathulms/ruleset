# What is still argued about

**Whether to keep trying for the Olympic Games.** Thirty years of
recognition, a new name, a new structure and a new judging system have not
won ballroom a place. The recognition did win one, briefly, for breaking.
Whether to go on is the sport's main strategic question.

**Whether the marks mean anything new.** <Term id="absolute-judging">Absolute
judging</Term> was meant to make results readable and comparable. Some inside
the sport argue that component marks truly limit a judge. Others argue that
the marks only put the old habit of comparing couples into numbers.

**The two federations.** Amateur and professional bodies govern the same
dances, hold rival events and do not recognise each other. Nobody has a way
to settle it.

**Breaking.** The federation governs a dance that many of its dancers did not
want a ballroom body to govern. It reached one Games and was dropped. What
the federation owes breaking now is an open question.

**Whether it is a sport.** This is the oldest argument, and it shaped the
federation's decisions, its name included. Three decades of acting like a
sport have not settled it.
