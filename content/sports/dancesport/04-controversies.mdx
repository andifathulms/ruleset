# Controversies that forced rule changes

**How to score a comparison.** For most of the sport's history judges did not
score couples; they placed them against each other and a counting rule resolved
the placements. That worked internally and produced results that could not be
explained to an audience or compared between events. The WDSF's absolute
judging system — marks against technical quality, movement to music,
partnering skill, and choreography and presentation — was the answer, and it
severed the sport's own results history in the process.

**Judging integrity.** A sport marked by a panel, with national blocs, a small
adjudicator pool, and long-standing relationships between judges, coaches and
couples, has the structural conditions for bias that this site records for
gymnastics, boxing and figure skating. Component marking, published marks and
adjudicator rotation are responses to it. It has not gone away.

**Costume cost.** Presentation is judged, so appearance is a competitive
variable, so money is. Costume regulation by age and level is a genuine attempt
to cap that in the categories where it does most damage.

**Amateur and professional.** The split between governing bodies has produced
eligibility disputes, competing world championships and couples having to
choose a side. It is a governance controversy that shapes what any world title
means.

**Breaking's governance.** Awarding a street form to a ballroom federation was
disputed by dancers within that form. Recorded here because it is the most
consequential thing to happen to this federation, and it happened to a
discipline that is not ballroom.
