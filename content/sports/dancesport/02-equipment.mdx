# Shoes, a floor and a dress code

Shoes are the only equipment in dancesport that changes what the body can do.
The soles are suede. On a sprung floor suede slides a little: enough to turn
on, and too little to lose the foot. The heel is different in the Standard
and Latin programmes, because the posture and the placing of the weight are
different.

The floor is sprung and has no markings. There are no lanes and no places
reserved for anyone, so during a <Term id="heat">heat</Term> the couples
compete for space on it.

## Why the rules say what to wear

Presentation is one of the things the judges mark. So what a couple wears is
part of the performance, and money becomes a way to gain an advantage. Gowns,
tailoring, stones sewn onto the fabric and grooming can cost more than a
season of travel.

<Photo id="dancesport-costume-2012" />

The federation answers with detailed costume rules for each age and level.
They say what may be worn and at which grade the limits relax. The junior
categories have the strictest rules, to keep the cost of starting low for the
people least able to pay it. The rules limit the spending. They do not remove
it.
