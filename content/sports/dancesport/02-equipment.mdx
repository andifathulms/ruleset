# Equipment evolution

Shoes, and a costume rulebook that exists because appearance is being judged.

**Shoes** are functional. Suede soles give a controlled slide on a sprung
floor — enough to pivot, not enough to lose the foot — and heel height and
construction differ between the Standard and Latin programmes because the
posture and weight placement differ. They are the only piece of equipment in
the sport that affects what the body can do.

**The floor** is the other piece of hardware. Sprung, unmarked, and shared:
there are no lanes and no assigned space, so the floor is a contested resource
during a heat rather than a neutral surface.

**Costume** is where this sport has a problem most of the others do not.
Presentation is an explicit judged component, so what a couple wears is part of
the performance being marked — and once that is true, expenditure becomes a
competitive variable. Gowns, tailoring, stoning and grooming can cost more than
a season's travel.

The federation's answer is detailed costume regulation by age and level:
what may be worn, what may not, and at which grade restrictions relax. Junior
categories are the most tightly constrained, precisely to keep the cost of
entry down for the people least able to bear it.

It is the same structural problem this site records for equestrian and for
gymnastics, and it is sharper here because in dancesport the appearance is not
incidental to the judged performance — it is one of the things being judged.
