# Where it is played

An English codification, a European competitive heartland, and an Asian growth
story that has changed the results.

**Britain** wrote the technique and holds the sport's most historically
significant competitions; the Blackpool Dance Festival remains the event that
matters most to many competitors regardless of which federation sanctions it.
**Italy**, **Germany**, **Russia**, **Ukraine**, **Poland**, **Moldova**,
**Lithuania** and **Slovenia** form a deep European competitive belt, and
Russian and Ukrainian couples have dominated large parts of the sport for
decades.

**Asia** is where the sport has grown most. **China** has invested heavily and
produces couples at the highest level, **Japan** has a long-established
competitive culture, and **Chinese Taipei**, **South Korea** and **Hong Kong**
all compete. Chinese formation and couple results have shifted the sport's
balance in a way few federations have managed anywhere.

The **United States** has a large social and studio-based dancing culture and a
comparatively small international competitive presence, which is close to the
inverse of the picture in most sports on this site.

The participation base is enormous and mostly invisible to the competitive
structure. Social ballroom and Latin classes run in almost every country, and
the overwhelming majority of people who dance these dances will never enter a
competition or know which federation would sanction it — a gap between
participation and sport that only disc golf, elsewhere on this site, matches.
