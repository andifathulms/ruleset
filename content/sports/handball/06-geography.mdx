# Where it is played

Handball is the most regionally concentrated major team sport on this site.
Denmark, France, Germany, Norway, Sweden, Spain, Croatia, Hungary, Slovenia,
Poland and Montenegro dominate, with Egypt, Qatar, Brazil and South Korea the
significant non-European presences.

Essentially the entire professional economy is European, and much of it is in
Germany and France, whose domestic leagues employ most of the world's elite
players.

The reason is historical rather than physical. Handball was invented in
northern Europe, embedded in European school systems, and never travelled
through empire or emigration the way football, cricket and rugby did. It has
grown where it was taught and almost nowhere else.

The facility requirement compounds it. A handball court is larger than a
basketball or volleyball court and needs an indoor hall, which is a building —
so the sport competes for exactly the facilities that basketball and volleyball
already occupy in most of the world, and usually loses.

Beach handball is the federation's development answer, in the same way
hockey5s and rugby sevens are for their sports: cheaper to stage, played
outdoors, and with a scoring system that rewards spectacular goals with double
points. It has not yet changed the map.

The women's game has a similar geography with Norway, France, Denmark and the
Netherlands strongest, and unusually for this site its professional structures
are comparably developed to the men's.
