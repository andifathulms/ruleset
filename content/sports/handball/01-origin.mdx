# Origin and invention

Handball is a Scandinavian and German invention of the 1910s and 1920s, and it
was two sports before it was one.

The German version, *Feldhandball*, was played outdoors on a football pitch by
eleven players a side. The Danish and Swedish version was indoors and
seven-a-side, because northern European winters make an outdoor ball sport
impractical for half the year.

The outdoor game was the Olympic sport in 1936, in Berlin, and was contested at
world championships into the 1960s. The indoor game won, became the
international standard in 1966, and the outdoor version simply stopped.

That is one of the cleanest format replacements on this site. Handball did not
add a shorter version and keep both, as cricket and rugby did. It changed which
sport it was, and its Olympic history has a thirty-six-year gap in the middle
where the transition happened.

The rule that gives handball its shape was there from the start: nobody except
the goalkeeper may stand in the six-metre area in front of the goal. Attackers
may jump into it and must release the ball before landing, which is why the
sport's characteristic image is a player suspended in mid-air above the arc.

The IHF in its present form dates from 1946, and the indoor game returned to
the Olympic programme in 1972.
