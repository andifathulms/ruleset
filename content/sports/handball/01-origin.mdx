# Two games that became one

Handball was two sports before it was one. Both were invented in northern
Europe in the 1910s and 1920s.

The German game, *Feldhandball*, was played outdoors on a football pitch by
eleven players a side. The Danish and Swedish game was played indoors by seven
a side, because a northern winter makes an outdoor ball game impractical for
half the year.

<Photos ids="handball-field-1953 handball-field-kiel-1964" />

At first the outdoor game was the bigger one. It was the Olympic sport in
Berlin in 1936, and world championships were played in it into the 1960s. The
indoor game was faster and had more goals. It did not depend on the weather,
and it could be staged in any sports hall. [In 1966 it became the
international standard](#ihf-1966-indoor-standard), and the outdoor game
stopped.

[Cricket](/sports/cricket/) and [rugby](/sports/rugby/) added a shorter
version and kept both. Handball changed which sport it was. Its Olympic record
shows the change as a gap of thirty-six years: eleven a side on grass in 1936,
then seven a side on a court from 1972.

One rule was there from the start and gives the game its look. Nobody except
the goalkeeper may stand in the <Term id="goal-area">goal area</Term>, six
metres deep in front of the goal. An attacker may jump into it and must let go
of the ball before landing. The picture most people have of handball is the
<Term id="jump-shot">jump shot</Term>: a player hanging in the air above the
line.

<Photo id="handball-jump-shot" />

The International Handball Federation has existed in its present form since
1946.
