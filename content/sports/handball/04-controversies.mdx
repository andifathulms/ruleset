# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The empty net, 2016.** Handball's most significant modern change and the one
nobody intended.

The old rule required a goalkeeper substitute to wear a distinguishing shirt
and function as a goalkeeper, which made the substitution slow and rare. The
new rule removed that, on the stated ground that the procedure was clumsy.

Elite teams read it correctly and immediately: if there is no requirement to
have a goalkeeper, attack with seven outfield players against six defenders and
leave the net empty. The numerical advantage in attack is worth more than the
risk of conceding a shot from distance — usually.

Within two seasons it was standard. It is the highest-variance legal tactic in
any team sport on this site, and it emerged from a rule change presented as
administrative.

**The stall.** Handball's passive play rule asked a referee to judge whether a
team was genuinely trying to score, which is the same judgement wrestling and
judo make about engagement and find equally difficult. After a warning, a team
now has four passes to shoot. A judgement became a count.

**The walk back.** Before 1994 a goal was followed by both teams returning to
their halves before the restart, which in a sport scoring thirty times a side
meant a great deal of walking. Allowing an immediate throw-off created the fast
centre, a scoring chance produced entirely by a restart rule.

**Resin.** Not a controversy that forced a rule, and included because it is one
that has not. A performance-enhancing grip substance is used universally,
damages venues, is banned in many halls, and remains unregulated.
