# Three rules and what they did

## The empty net

In 2016 the federation tidied up a small rule. Until then, a player who came
on in place of the goalkeeper had to wear a shirt of a different colour and
play as the goalkeeper. That made the change slow, and teams rarely did it.
The federation called the procedure clumsy and [removed the
requirement](#ihf-2016-seventh-field-player).

Top teams saw at once what the new wording allowed. If no player has to be the
goalkeeper, a team can send on a seventh
<Term id="court-player">court player</Term> and attack seven against six. Its
own goal stands empty. If the attack breaks down, the other team can throw the
ball the length of the court into the
<Term id="empty-net">empty net</Term>. Usually the extra attacker is worth the
risk.

Within two seasons this was normal play at the top level. Nobody had predicted
it. A change that was presented as tidying up had changed how the game is
played, and the federation has argued ever since about whether that should
have been allowed to happen.

## The stall

A team that is ahead may want to keep the ball and let time run. The rule
against <Term id="passive-play">passive play</Term> asked the referees to
judge whether a team was really trying to score. They gave a warning, and then
had to decide alone when to punish. Warnings were uneven, and teams stalled
after one and argued about it.

[Since 2016 a warned team has four passes](#ihf-2016-passive-play), and then
it must shoot. The referees now count. [Basketball](/sports/basketball/) did
the same thing with its shot clock, and [wrestling](/sports/wrestling/) with
its passivity clock.

## The walk back

Handball has many goals. Before 1994 both teams walked back to their own
halves after each one, and only then did play restart. In a game where each
side scores about thirty times, that was a lot of walking.

[From 1994 the throw-off could be taken at
once](#ihf-1994-fast-throw-off). A team that has just let in a goal can run to
the centre and attack before the scorers are back in position. The quick
<Term id="throw-off">throw-off</Term> became a way to score in itself.
