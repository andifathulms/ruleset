# Two referees

A handball match has two referees with equal authority. Most court sports
use one.

The reason is contact. A defender may block a moving attacker with a bent arm.
Players collide all the time, at speed, in a small space, and there are often
bodies between the referee and the incident. One person cannot see enough of
it. Two referees standing on a diagonal is the smallest number that works.

Two newer rules take some weight off them. The four-pass limit after a warning
for <Term id="passive-play">passive play</Term> gives them something to count,
where before they had to judge.

The [blue card](#ihf-2016-blue-card), brought in in 2016, does something
similar for the worst fouls. A red card may be for a cynical foul or for a
dangerous one. The referees no longer have to settle on court what should
follow. They show the <Term id="blue-card">blue card</Term> with the red, they
write a report, and a disciplinary process deals with it after the match.

At major tournaments video review is used for two questions: did the ball
cross the goal line, and was the shot taken before time ran out. Contact is
still judged by eye. Whether a defender's arm blocked or pushed is a matter of
degree, and handball's whole style of play sits on that line.
