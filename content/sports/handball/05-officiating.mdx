# Officiating and technology

Handball uses two referees where almost every court sport on this site uses
one, and the reason is that the sport permits contact its officials cannot see
from a single position.

A defender may obstruct a moving attacker with a bent arm. Collisions happen
continuously, at speed, in a confined space, with bodies between the official
and the incident. Two referees positioned diagonally is the minimum that works.

**The two instruments worth recording** are both conversions of judgement into
counting.

The **four-pass rule** after a passive-play warning replaced a vague sanction
with an arithmetic one. Before it, a referee warned a team for stalling and
then had to decide, unaided, when to punish them; now the team has four passes.
That is the same move basketball made with the shot clock in 1954 and wrestling
made with its passivity clock in 2018, and handball is the third sport on this
site to arrive at it.

The **blue card**, from 2016, separates the on-court sanction from the
disciplinary consequence. A referee showing red no longer has to decide whether
the offence was tactical or dangerous — the blue card triggers a written report
and a process afterwards. That is an official being given permission not to
adjudicate everything on the spot, which is rare.

Video review exists at major tournaments for goal-line and end-of-time
questions, which are facts. What remains judged is contact, and it always will
be: whether a defender's arm was obstruction or a push is a question about
degree, and handball's whole style of play sits on that boundary.
