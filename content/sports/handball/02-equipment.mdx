# Equipment evolution

Handball has almost no equipment history, and its one recurring equipment
argument is about a substance that is not part of the sport at all.

The ball is small enough to hold in one hand — that is its whole design
requirement, because the sport's actions are throwing, catching and shooting
one-handed. Sizes differ between the men's and women's game and have been
stable for decades.

**Resin** is the argument. Players use adhesive resin on their hands to control
the ball, which improves grip, ball-handling and shooting accuracy
substantially. It is not regulated as performance equipment and is used
universally at elite level.

It also coats the ball, the floor and everything else, damages court surfaces,
and is banned in a significant number of sports halls — including some that
handball would otherwise use. So the sport has an unofficial performance
substance that determines which venues it can play in, and no rule about it
either way.

That is a genuinely unusual position on this site. Table tennis banned speed
glue on health grounds in 2008. Athletics wrote a shoe specification. Handball
has an ubiquitous grip aid that shapes its facility access and has never
legislated on it.

Protective equipment is minimal — knee support and occasional headbands — in a
sport with heavy permitted contact, which is one reason its shoulder and knee
injury rates are high.
