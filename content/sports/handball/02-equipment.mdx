# A small ball and sticky fingers

A handball is small enough to hold in one hand. That is the whole of its
design, because the game is throwing, catching and shooting with one hand. The
men's ball and the women's ball are different sizes, and neither has changed
in decades.

## Resin

The argument in handball is about <Term id="resin">resin</Term>. Players put
the sticky substance on their fingers to grip the ball. It helps a great deal
with handling and with accurate shooting, and at the top level everybody uses
it.

Resin also ends up on the ball, on the floor and on everything else. It
damages court surfaces. Many sports halls ban it, including some that handball
would otherwise use. So a substance decides how well the game is played and
which buildings it may be played in, and the rules of handball say nothing
about it either way.

<Photo id="handball-resin" />

[Table tennis](/sports/table-tennis/) banned speed glue in 2008 on health
grounds. Handball has never made a rule about its own.

Players wear very little protection: a knee support, sometimes a headband.
The game allows heavy contact, and injuries to shoulders and knees are common.
