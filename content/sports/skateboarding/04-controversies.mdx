# A federation the skaters did not ask for

## Under new management

By 2017 skateboarding had run its own contests for decades. It had
professionals, judges and a commercial circuit, and no international
federation. The Olympic movement wanted sports that were young, urban and
cheap to stage, and it could only admit one through a recognised federation.
World Skate, until then the roller sports body, was the one available.
[Skateboarding was placed under it](#worldskate-2017-governance).

<Photo id="skateboarding-ariake-2020" />

Skaters were divided. Few objected to contests, because skaters already took
contests seriously. The objection was to an outside body, and to having the
sport written down. [Breaking](/sports/breaking/) later went through the same
change, and its community's objection went further, to competition itself.
Skateboarding has kept its Olympic place and breaking has not.

The commercial circuit did not go away. It still runs beside the federation's
events.

<Photos ids="skateboarding-sls-russell skateboarding-sls-ribeiro" />

## Changing the criteria

The federation chose to [judge by overall
impression](#worldskate-holistic-judging), as skaters always had. That kind of
judging is hard to keep the same from one panel to the next. Between the 2020
and 2024 Games [the criteria and their weightings were
revised](#worldskate-2024-criteria-revision). The scale has not stayed still
for one full Olympic cycle. [Sport climbing](/sports/sport-climbing/) rewrote
its Olympic format in the same years.

## Vert left out

In the 1980s and 1990s the best-known form of skateboarding was
<Term id="vert">vert</Term>, on the half-pipe. It is not an Olympic
discipline. <Term id="street">Street</Term> and <Term id="park">park</Term>
were chosen because they are what the sport's own circuit runs now.
