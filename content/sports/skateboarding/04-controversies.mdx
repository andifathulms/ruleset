# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Codification itself.** Skateboarding's largest controversy is the same as
breaking's: an activity with functioning contest traditions acquired an
international federation it had not asked for, because the Olympic movement
wanted it.

The difference is that skateboarding's contests were already professional and
already taken seriously by its own culture, so the argument was narrower —
about who governs and how scoring is written down, rather than about whether
competition belonged at all.

**The table nobody wrote.** The federation had to produce a scoring system, and
the obvious model was gymnastics: list the tricks, assign values, sum them.

It declined. Skateboarding's position is that a trick's difficulty is
inseparable from where and how it is performed, and that a table asserting
fixed values would be false to the activity. So judges award a single
impression score out of 100 and do not publish its components.

That is the only refusal of its kind on this site, and it has a real cost:
judging disputes cannot be settled by reference to a document, because there is
no document.

**Criteria drift.** Between the 2020 and 2024 Games the criteria and weightings
were revised. Holistic judging is harder to keep consistent than tabulated
judging, and the federation has been tuning it since the sport's first
appearance — which means the scale has not held still for a full Olympic cycle.

**Vert's absence.** The half-pipe discipline that defined skateboarding's
1980s and 1990s peak is not on the Olympic programme. Street and park were
chosen because they are what the sport's own circuit currently runs.
