# Surfing on the pavement

Skateboarding was invented by surfers who wanted something to do when the sea
was flat. It began on the coast of California in the 1950s and 1960s. The
first boards had roller skate wheels, and riding them was called sidewalk
surfing. Skaters still use words from [surfing](/sports/surfing/): carving,
lines, style.

Two inventions made the modern sport.

The first was the urethane wheel, in the early 1970s. Earlier wheels were clay
or steel. Urethane gripped the ground and held speed, and serious riding
became possible.

The second was the <Term id="ollie">ollie</Term>. Alan Gelfand worked out how
to take the board into the air without holding it. Rodney Mullen adapted the
move to flat ground. Once a skater could lift the board with the feet alone,
they could jump onto a <Term id="ledge">ledge</Term> or down a set of stairs.
Nearly every street <Term id="trick">trick</Term> since then starts from it.

## A sport with no federation

Skateboarding had contests, professionals, magazines and videos. It had a
strong shared sense of what was good, passed from skater to skater. It had no
coaches to speak of and no governing body. Contests were judged by skaters,
who gave a mark for the whole performance.

<Photo id="skateboarding-kdc-2015" />

The Olympic Games require a federation. In 2017 skateboarding [was placed
under World Skate](#worldskate-2017-governance), the body that had governed
[roller sports](/sports/roller-sports/). Skateboarding and surfing joined the
Olympic programme together, for the 2020 Games. Skateboarding arrived with
contests it had run for decades and a way of scoring that nobody had ever
written down.
