# Governing body politics

Skateboarding's governance is the same story breaking's is, four years earlier
and with a different ending.

An activity with strong internal traditions, its own contests and no
international federation was wanted by the IOC for a youth-facing, urban,
inexpensive programme. An existing recognised body — World Skate, previously the
roller sports federation — was the available route, and skateboarding was
placed under it in 2017.

The community's reaction was divided rather than uniformly hostile, and the
difference from breaking's matters. Skateboarding already had a serious
competitive circuit that professionals took seriously, so the objection was to
codification and to an outside governing body, not to competition itself. That
made the transition survivable.

The federation's central decision was to **refuse the difficulty table**. Every
other judged sport on this site prices its elements, and skateboarding's
culture does not accept that a trick has a fixed value independent of context —
the same trick down a twelve-stair and on flat ground are not the same
achievement.

So the sport kept holistic impression scoring, staffed its panels with skaters,
and accepted that its scores cannot be explained component by component.

The cost has been instability. Criteria and weightings were revised between the
2020 and 2024 Games, and holistic judging without a published reference is hard
to keep consistent between panels. Sport climbing rewrote its Olympic format in
the same period and breaking's was used once — the three newest sports here have
all struggled to hold a scale still.
