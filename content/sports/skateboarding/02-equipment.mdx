# Equipment evolution

Skateboarding's equipment transformed the sport twice and is regulated
essentially not at all.

**Urethane wheels**, from about 1972, replaced clay and steel. They gripped,
rolled over surface imperfections and let riders carry speed, and the sport
that existed before them is barely recognisable as the same activity. Almost
everything skateboarding does now depends on them.

**Board geometry** — concave decks, kicked tails and noses — developed through
the 1980s and made the ollie and its descendants controllable. Trucks,
bearings and wheel durometers have all been refined continuously since.

None of it is meaningfully specified in the rules. There is no maximum deck
length, no wheel diameter limit, no material requirement. A competitor rides
what they choose.

That is coherent for a sport with no difficulty table. Equipment
specifications exist elsewhere on this site to keep a scale honest — gymnastics
must standardise its apparatus so a difficulty value means the same twice,
athletics must specify a javelin so distances compare. Skateboarding has no
values to protect, so it has nothing to standardise for.

**Protective equipment** is required for junior competitors and optional at
senior level, which is a position almost nothing else here takes — most sports
either mandate protection or forbid it. Skateboarding leaves it to the athlete,
which is the clearest surviving trace of the culture's attitude to
institutions in its actual rulebook.
