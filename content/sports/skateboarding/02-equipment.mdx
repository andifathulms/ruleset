# A board nobody measures

The rules say almost nothing about the board. There is no longest
<Term id="deck">deck</Term>, no largest wheel and no required material. A
skater rides what they like.

The equipment still changed the sport twice.

Urethane wheels arrived in about 1972 and replaced clay and steel. They
gripped, they rolled over rough ground, and they let a rider keep speed.
Nearly everything skaters do now depends on them.

<Photo id="skateboarding-street-board" />

Then, through the 1980s, the deck changed shape. It became concave, and both
ends were bent upward into a nose and a tail. That shape made the
<Term id="ollie">ollie</Term>, and the tricks that grew from it, possible to
control. <Term id="trucks">Trucks</Term>, bearings and wheels have been
refined ever since.

<Photo id="skateboarding-board-1990s" />

Other sports measure their equipment to protect their numbers.
[Gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) fixes the size of its apparatus so that a
difficulty value means the same thing twice.
[Athletics](/sports/athletics/) specifies the javelin so that distances can be
compared. Skateboarding gives no trick a fixed value, so it has nothing to
protect by measuring a board.

## Helmets

Young competitors must wear protective equipment. Senior skaters may choose.
In the same kind of final one skater rides in a helmet and another in a cap.
