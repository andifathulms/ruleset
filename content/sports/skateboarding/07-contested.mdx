# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether holistic judging can hold.** Skateboarding's refusal to publish a
difficulty table is principled and costly. Consistency between panels is harder
to maintain, criteria have been revised between the sport's only two Games, and
a competitor cannot challenge a score against any document. The alternative —
writing the table — is rejected by the culture, and the argument has no obvious
resolution.

**Institutionalisation.** National programmes, funding pathways and youth
selection now exist in places that had none, and a substantial part of the
community regards that as a loss even while acknowledging the opportunities.
The same argument breaking had, still running here.

**Age.** Elite street skateboarding has produced medallists in their early
teens, and the injury profile of the sport is cumulative. Whether the
competitive structure is appropriate for the ages it is now attracting is a
live safeguarding question the federation has not fully addressed.

**Vert.** The discipline that defined skateboarding's earlier competitive era
is not on the programme, and there is a persistent argument for its return
alongside street and park.

**Who governs.** World Skate's mandate comes from Olympic recognition, and the
commercial contest circuit that predates it by decades continues alongside.
Which of the two is the sport's real competitive structure is not settled and
may never be.
