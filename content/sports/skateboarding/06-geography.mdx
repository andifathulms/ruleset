# A sport of cities

Skateboarders come from cities more than from national programmes. The sport
needs hard ground and something to jump over, and nearly every city has both.

The United States, Brazil, Japan and Australia win the most. Skaters from
across Europe do well, and China is rising. Japan's rise in
<Term id="street">street</Term> has been the sharpest recent change,
especially among very young skaters. It came after skateboarding was given its
Olympic place.

## Skateparks

A public skatepark costs a small part of what a swimming pool costs. Cities
have built them widely, often as something for young people to do more than as
a sports facility. Public space and skateparks are what produce skaters.

<Photos ids="skateboarding-santo-andre skateboarding-marseille-bowl" />

Brazil shows it best. Its results in street are far better than its spending
on the sport would predict. They come from a large, dense skating culture in
its cities.

## After the Olympics

The Olympic place has brought national programmes and funding to countries
that had none, Japan and China among them. Many skaters have mixed feelings
about that, as they do about the federation. Skating is still learned from
friends, from videos and in public places.
