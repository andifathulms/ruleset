# Where it is played

Skateboarding's geography is urban rather than national, which makes it unlike
almost everything else on this site.

The United States, Brazil, Japan and Australia dominate, with strong showings
from across Europe and increasingly from China. Japan's rise in street
skateboarding — particularly among very young competitors — has been the
sharpest recent shift, and it followed the Olympic decision rather than
preceding it.

What produces skaters is not a national programme but **public space and
skateparks**. Municipal skateparks are cheap by the standards of any other
facility on this site — a fraction of a swimming pool or a whitewater course —
and cities have built them widely, often as youth provision rather than as
sports infrastructure.

That gives skateboarding an unusual distribution: it exists wherever there is a
city with hard surfaces, which is nearly everywhere, and its participation
skews young and urban in a way federations do not control.

Brazil is the case worth recording. Its street skateboarding results are
disproportionate to any institutional investment and come from a large,
dense, urban skating culture rather than from a development pathway.

The Olympic project has produced national programmes and funding in places that
had none — including Japan and China — and the community's ambivalence about
that is the same ambivalence it has about the federation. The sport's
transmission still runs through peers, video and public space, and no
federation has changed it.
