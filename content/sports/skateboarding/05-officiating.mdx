# Officiating and technology

Skateboarding's judging is the most deliberately opaque on this site, and the
opacity is a considered position rather than an oversight.

A panel awards a single impression score out of 100 for a run or a trick, high
and low discarded. What goes into it — difficulty, execution, variety, flow,
use of the course, consistency — is stated, but the weightings are not
published as a table and individual tricks have no assigned values.

Compare what every other judged sport here does. Gymnastics publishes a Code of
Points assigning a value to each element. Diving tabulates degrees of
difficulty. Taekwondo prices head kicks and spinning techniques. Climbing
assigns points to holds. In each case a competitor can compute what a routine
is worth before performing it, and a disputed score can be checked against a
document.

Skateboarding cannot do any of that, because it decided a trick's value depends
on where and how it is done. A kickflip down a large stair set and the same
kickflip on flat ground are different achievements, and no table distinguishes
them without becoming impossibly long.

So the system relies on **judges who are skaters** and on shared cultural
knowledge of what is hard — which is exactly how the sport's contests worked
before there was a federation.

It is defensible and it is fragile. Consistency between panels and between
events is harder to maintain, criteria have been revised between Games, and
there is no mechanism for a competitor to challenge a score on its merits.

Video review exists for landings and course boundaries, which are facts.
