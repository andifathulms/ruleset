# Where it is played

Three disciplines, three maps, and almost no overlap between them.

**Rink hockey** belongs to southern Europe and South America. **Portugal**,
**Spain**, **Italy** and **Argentina** are the powers, with professional or
semi-professional leagues, deep club structures and genuine popular followings
— rink hockey is a mainstream sport in Portugal and Catalonia in a way that
surprises anyone encountering it from outside. **France**, **Switzerland**,
**Germany**, **Chile**, **Brazil**, **Angola** and **Mozambique** all play it,
the last two through Portuguese colonial history.

**Speed** is strongest in **Colombia**, which dominates the discipline
comprehensively and has done for years, along with **Italy**, **Chinese
Taipei**, **South Korea**, **Belgium**, **France**, **Spain**, **the United
States** and **New Zealand**. Colombia's dominance in roller speed is one of the
most complete of any nation in any sport covered here.

**Artistic** concentrates in **Italy**, **Germany**, **Spain**, **Argentina**,
**Colombia**, **the United States** and **Chinese Taipei**.

Two things stand out. First, the geography of roller sports has very little
overlap with the geography of the ice sports they mirror — Colombia and
Portugal are not figure skating and speed skating nations, and that is the
point: these disciplines took root exactly where there was no ice.

Second, no country is strong in all three. A World Skate championship is three
separate competitions attended by three separate sets of nations.
