# Officiating

Three disciplines, three officiating traditions, all inherited from ice.

**Artistic** is judged by a panel on technical elements and components, in a
structure descended directly from figure skating's. Judges verify rotation
counts on jumps, assess execution, and mark programme components. The
difficulties are figure skating's difficulties: an under-rotated jump is a
fraction of a turn observed in real time, and a panel's collective judgement is
open to the bloc-voting concerns that have dogged every judged sport on this
site.

**Speed** is timed, with the officiating concentrated on race conduct —
impeding, lane infringements, and cutting across a rival on a bend in mass-start
racing. Photo finishes and transponders do the measuring.

**Rink hockey** has the busiest referees. Sticks above shoulder height,
charging, playing the ball with the hands or too high, and above all the
**team foul count**: individual offences accumulate against the side, and once
the limit is passed each further foul concedes a direct hit from ten metres.
That makes bookkeeping part of officiating in a way it is not in the other two.

What the disciplines share is that none of them is instrumented beyond timing.
Artistic has no equivalent of a sensor to verify rotations; rink hockey has no
video review in most competition. For a federation whose newest discipline is
judged with the aid of replay at the Olympics, the older ones are officiated
much as they were in 1924.
