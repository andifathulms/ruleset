# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**What the merger delivered.** World Skate's recognition, built over ninety
years by the roller disciplines, produced an Olympic place for skateboarding
four years after it joined. Whether the older disciplines gained anything from
the merger is asked inside the federation and has no obvious answer.

**Living beside the ice sports.** Artistic roller skating and roller speed
skating have counterparts on the Winter programme, and any selection committee
can say the Games already contests those sports. No version of the argument for
the wheeled disciplines has worked, and it is not clear one exists.

**Rink hockey's case.** It is the discipline with the best independent claim —
no close ice equivalent, professional leagues, mainstream status in several
countries — and it had its demonstration in 1992, into a category the IOC then
abolished.

**Incompatible equipment.** Speed on inline, artistic and rink hockey on quads.
It is settled in practice and it means the federation's disciplines share a
name and not a skill.

**Judging artistic.** Inherited from figure skating along with the discipline
itself, including the concerns about bloc voting and about separating clean
performances of unequal difficulty.

**Where the athletes go.** There is a professional pathway in rink hockey in
three countries and effectively none in artistic or speed. A world champion in
either has reached the end of the sport.
