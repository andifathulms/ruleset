# Skating without ice

Roller skating was invented so that people could skate where there was no ice.
Skates on wheels were developed through the eighteenth and nineteenth
centuries. The design that made them practical was the
<Term id="quad-skate">quad skate</Term>, with four wheels in a square, patented
in the 1860s. James Plimpton's prototype survives, and quad skates still follow
its layout.

<Photo id="roller-sports-plimpton-skate" />

Rinks followed, and people found things to do in them. Professional skaters
performed on stage. Others raced.

## Three sports from the ice

Each of the three main disciplines copied a sport played on ice.
<Term id="artistic">Artistic skating</Term> took the events, the jumps, the
names and the judging of figure skating.
<Term id="speed">Speed skating</Term> took the distances of ice speed skating.
<Term id="rink-hockey">Rink hockey</Term> took a stick, a ball and a goal, and
grew into a large sport of its own in southern Europe and Latin America.

<Photos ids="roller-sports-vel-dhiv-1910 roller-sports-vaudrey-1910" />

National bodies ran all three under rules that did not match. In 1924 [an
international federation was founded](#roller-1924-firs) to govern them
together, and they have been contested internationally ever since. Roller
sports were part of the first World Games, at Santa Clara in 1981.
