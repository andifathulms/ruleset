# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Quad to inline.** Speed skating changed its equipment completely around 1990,
which severed its own times and left the other disciplines behind on quads. It
is one of the cleanest equipment transitions recorded on this site, because
nobody had to decide what to do about the old marks — the equipment that set
them left competition entirely.

**Team fouls in rink hockey.** Individual offences accumulate against the side,
and a team over the limit concedes a direct hit from ten metres. It converts
discipline into a scoring variable and is the sport's most distinctive
regulation, borrowed in spirit from basketball rather than from either hockey
code.

**Goalkeeper equipment.** A hard ball struck by a stick at close range in a
boarded rink produced the protective equipment arms race every such sport has,
and rink hockey's goalkeeper is now the most heavily equipped player in any
roller discipline.

**Judging artistic.** Roller artistic imported figure skating's judging
structure and, with it, figure skating's problems — national bloc voting, a
small adjudicator pool, and the difficulty of separating clean performances of
unequal difficulty. It has followed the ice sport's reforms rather than leading
them.

**The 2017 merger.** Not a rule change and the most consequential event in the
sport's modern history: a federation of century-old disciplines acquired a new
one and delivered it to the Olympic programme within four years.
