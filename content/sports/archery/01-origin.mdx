# Origin and invention

Archery is by an enormous margin the oldest activity on this site — a weapon
technology in continuous use for tens of thousands of years — and by a
reasonable margin one of the youngest sports. The gap between the two is the
interesting part.

Target shooting as a formal pastime survived the bow's military obsolescence in
England as a gentry recreation, and the societies that kept it going through
the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries produced the first codified rounds:
fixed distances, a fixed number of arrows, a scoring target. The Grand National
Archery Society dates from 1861.

Olympic archery in its early appearances was chaos. It was contested in 1900,
1904, 1908 and 1920 under rules that differed by host country to the point
where events had almost nothing in common, and it was dropped afterwards —
partly for exactly that reason. There was no international body to standardise
it. FITA was founded in 1931 to be that body, and archery returned to the
Games in 1972 after a fifty-two-year absence, the longest of any returning
sport except golf and cricket.

So archery's competitive rules are a twentieth-century invention laid over a
prehistoric skill, and the sport has been unusually willing to rewrite them —
because unlike football or judo, there is no ancient tradition of how the
*competition* should work to defend.
