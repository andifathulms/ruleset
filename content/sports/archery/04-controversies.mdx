# Making a quiet sport watchable

For decades the hardest problem in archery was the audience. Most of the big
changes to its format were made so that people could follow a contest and see
it end.

## A contest with no ending

Through the 1970s and 1980s, Olympic archery was decided by
<Term id="cumulative">cumulative scoring</Term>. Every archer shot a large
number of arrows over several days, the points were added up, and the highest
total won. It was a sound way to find the best archer. It had no opponent to
point a camera at and no moment of victory, and the sport was under real
pressure because of it.

[In 1992 the Games moved to head-to-head matches](#wa-1992-olympic-round). A
<Term id="ranking-round">ranking round</Term> now sorts the field, and then
archers meet two at a time until one is left. The federation said it wanted a
visible winner at a known time.

## Shooting better and losing

The matches were still added up arrow by arrow, and that brought a new
problem. At the top level a lead of two points over three ends was almost
impossible to recover. Matches were decided early, and the last arrows changed
nothing.

[In 2011 recurve matches were rescored in sets](#wa-2011-set-system). Each
<Term id="set">set</Term> of three arrows is its own small contest, worth two
<Term id="set-points">set points</Term> to the winner, and the first archer to
six takes the match. Every set now matters.

It also became possible to shoot more points than your opponent across a match
and lose it. Archers said so loudly. Their objection was that the one clear
virtue of archery was an objective score, and the score no longer decided the
match. The federation has not disputed that description. It has argued that a
sport nobody watches has a worse problem.

The first Games after the change put the range inside Lord's Cricket Ground
in London, in front of a full grandstand. Four years later it stood in the
carnival parade ground in Rio de Janeiro.

## A clock and a single arrow

[From 2014 each arrow in a match must be shot within twenty seconds, with the
archers taking turns](#wa-2014-timing-and-shoot-off). A level match is settled
by a <Term id="shoot-off">shoot-off</Term> of one arrow each. The rhythm is
one a camera can follow, and a match always ends on a shot that can be shown.

## The bow that is left out

The compound bow is more popular than the recurve in several countries, and it
has been proposed for the Olympic programme again and again. Letting it in
would mean replacing recurve events or adding new ones, and the International
Olympic Committee keeps a fixed limit on the size of the Games.
