# Governing body politics

World Archery's defining problem for forty years has been that its sport is
genuinely difficult to broadcast, and almost every governance decision it has
made follows from trying to solve that.

A cumulative-score competition has no moment of victory. Everyone shoots, the
numbers are added, and somebody has the highest total — which is an excellent
way to determine the best archer and an impossible way to make television. The
federation's response has been a thirty-year sequence: elimination brackets from
1992, a short shot clock, alternating shooting, single-arrow shoot-offs, and in
2011 the set system.

Every one of those changes increased the drama and reduced the extent to which
the result measures accuracy. The set system is the point at which that trade
became explicit, because it made it possible to shoot better and lose. The
federation has not disputed that description; it has argued that a sport nobody
watches has a worse problem.

The other long-running question is the compound bow, which is more popular in
several countries than the recurve and has been proposed for the Olympic
programme repeatedly. Admitting it would mean either replacing the recurve
events or expanding the programme, and the IOC's fixed quota makes the second
difficult. The politics here is entirely about the size of the Games.

The federation renamed itself from FITA to World Archery in 2011, in the same
year it changed how matches are scored.
