# Who reads the target

An arrow is in a ring or it is touching a line. That is a question of fact,
and the arrow stays in the target while people walk up and look at it. Archery
has very few arguments about officiating as a result.

Judges give the value of an arrow where it touches a line, run the clock and
inspect the equipment. The hard call is the
<Term id="line-cutter">line cutter</Term>, an arrow whose shaft touches the
line between two rings. It takes the higher value, so the judge has to decide
whether it touches at all.

<Photo id="archery-line-cutters" />

The sport made that call rarer. [The 9.3 millimetre limit on shaft
width](#wa-2014-arrow-diameter) means fewer arrows reach a line, and less is
gained by building an arrow to reach one.

The clock is mechanical. In <Term id="match-play">match play</Term> the archers
shoot in turn, twenty seconds each, with lights to show the time. An arrow
shot late costs the archer the highest-scoring arrow of that
<Term id="end">end</Term>. No one has to interpret anything.

Technology has mostly served the audience. Electronic systems read where an
arrow landed and show a score before the archers have walked to the target.
Cameras behind the target and on the bow make a small, distant target easy to
follow.

Archery has no panel of judges and no review system. In
[gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) and [football](/sports/football/) those
exist to manage judgement, and archery has very little judgement to manage.
