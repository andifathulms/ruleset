# One country at the top

South Korea's women have won the Olympic team event every time it has been
held. The event was introduced in 1988. The title in London in 2012 was their
seventh in seven Games, and the run has continued at every Games since.

Three explanations are usually given. A system of school and company clubs
finds archers young. The national selection is harder than the Olympic
competition. And the squads train on purpose for pressure: they are reported
to practise in crowded stadiums with noise piped in.

That last point connects to the rules. At the top, archers shoot nearly the
same scores, so nerves decide matches. The shot clock, shooting in turn and
the set system all add pressure. A programme that trains for pressure may gain
from each of them. The federation has never spoken about this, and it remains
one reading of the results.

Beyond Korea the sport is widely spread. The United States, Italy, France,
Turkey, India, China and Mexico all produce international recurve archers.
The compound bow has a different map, with more of Europe on it.

Almost everywhere, archery is a club sport. A beginner needs a supervised
range, which is a facility someone has to build and run.
