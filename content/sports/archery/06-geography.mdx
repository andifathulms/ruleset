# Where it is played

Archery has the most concentrated elite dominance of any sport on this site
except table tennis, and it belongs to one country: South Korea.

Korean women in particular have won the team event at every Games since it was
introduced in 1988, a run without parallel in any Olympic event anywhere. The
usual explanations are a school and corporate club system that identifies
archers young, a domestic selection process harder than the Olympic
competition, and deliberate training against pressure — Korean squads are
reported to practise in crowded stadiums with noise piped in.

That last detail matters for what this sport's rules have become. If the elite
technical margin is nearly nil and the difference is psychological, then a
programme that trains psychology systematically will dominate, and every rule
change that raises pressure — the shot clock, alternating shooting, the set
system — arguably widens rather than narrows that advantage. This site records
that as a reading rather than a finding, because the federation has never
addressed it.

Beyond Korea the sport is broadly distributed: the United States, Italy,
France, Turkey, India, China and Mexico all produce international recurve
archers, and the compound discipline has a different and more European map
again.

The entry barrier keeps archery a club sport almost everywhere. It needs a
supervised range, which is a facility, and that puts it closer to gymnastics
than to basketball in how it recruits.
