# Equipment evolution

Archery's equipment question is settled by a decision most sports never have to
make explicitly: the Olympic discipline uses a deliberately restricted bow, and
the unrestricted one exists as a separate sport.

The **recurve** bow may have a sight, long stabilisers and a clicker. It may
not have magnifying optics or a mechanical release aid, and the archer holds
the full draw weight at full draw. The **compound** bow has cams that reduce
the holding weight at full draw by most of its value, a magnifying scope and a
trigger release, and it is substantially more accurate. It is a full World
Archery discipline with its own world championships and it is not on the
Olympic programme.

That split is unusual and worth noticing. Athletics banned the aerodynamic
javelin outright. Cycling banned the Superman position. Swimming banned the
polyurethane suit. Archery did not ban the compound bow; it put it in a
different competition and let both exist. Two of the sports on this site have
handled a technology fork this way, and the other is cycling's brief attempt at
two hour records — which it abandoned.

The one equipment rule that changed the numbers is small and precise. Arrow
shafts are limited to 9.3 mm for outdoor target archery, because a fatter shaft
touches a higher scoring ring more often. Without the limit, an archer could
buy points that they had not shot, and the manufacture of ever-fatter shafts had
become a measurable route to a better score.
