# Two bows, two competitions

Archery has two modern bows, and it keeps them apart.

The <Term id="recurve">recurve bow</Term> is the Olympic bow. It may carry a
<Term id="sight">sight</Term>, long <Term id="stabiliser">stabilisers</Term>
and a clicker. It may not have a magnifying lens or a
<Term id="release-aid">release aid</Term>, and the archer's fingers hold the
full weight of the string at full draw.

The <Term id="compound">compound bow</Term> has cams at the ends of its limbs
that take most of that weight off at full draw. It has a magnifying scope and
a trigger release, and it is a good deal more accurate. It is a full World
Archery discipline with its own world championships. It is not on the Olympic
programme.

<Photos ids="archery-recurve archery-compound" />

Other sports met a new technology by banning it.
[Athletics](/sports/athletics/) banned the aerodynamic javelin,
[cycling](/sports/cycling/) banned the Superman riding position, and
[swimming](/sports/swimming/) banned the polyurethane suit. Archery left the
compound bow legal and gave it a competition of its own.

## The width of an arrow

One small equipment rule protects the score. An arrow that touches the line
between two rings takes the higher value, so a fatter shaft touches a higher
ring more often. Makers built fatter shafts, and archers could gain points
without shooting any better. [Since 2014 a shaft may be no more than 9.3
millimetres across](#wa-2014-arrow-diameter) in outdoor target archery.
