# Judging a gate

Sprint needs almost no judging. Boats race in separate lanes and do not meet.
The finish is timed to a thousandth of a second. An official has two things to
look for, a false start and a boat leaving its lane.
[Rowing](/sports/rowing/) is officiated in the same light way.

Slalom is harder. At each gate a
<Term id="gate-judge">gate judge</Term> has to decide two things. Did the
boat, the paddle or the paddler touch a pole? Did the boat go through the
gate properly and in the right order?

<Photo id="canoeing-doria-gates" />

Both questions now have a yes or no answer. Before the penalties were
simplified, a judge also had to say how serious a touch or a miss had been,
and that was the part judges disagreed on. The sport could not measure a
touch with an instrument, so it removed the grades. At major competitions the
gate judges are backed up by video review.

Kayak cross brought judgement back. Four boats go down together and contact is
allowed, so officials have to decide whether a piece of contact was fair. That
is a racing decision, like the right-of-way decisions made in
[sailing](/sports/sailing/), and it is new to canoeing.
