# Officiating and technology

Canoeing officiates two different problems and has largely solved both.

**Sprint** is a timing question. Boats race in lanes, do not interact, and the
finish is instrumented to a thousandth of a second. There is almost nothing for
an official to judge — leaving your lane and false starting are the whole list —
which puts it alongside rowing as one of the least officiated sports here.

**Slalom** is a gate question, and the sport's answer to it is the interesting
part. A gate judge decides whether a boat or a paddler's body touched a gate,
and whether a gate was negotiated correctly and in order.

Both of those used to involve grading: how severe was the touch, how badly was
the gate missed. Simplifying penalties to two values around 1990 removed the
grading entirely. A judge no longer assesses severity — they answer two binary
questions, touched or not, negotiated or not, and the penalty follows.

That is a judgement replaced by a classification rather than by an instrument,
and it is a pattern this site has seen in badminton's fixed service height and
rugby's sanction framework: where you cannot measure, at least remove the
gradations.

Video review supports gate judges at major competitions, and the questions it
answers are factual.

**Kayak cross** breaks all of this. Contact is permitted, four boats descend
together, and officials must judge whether contact was legitimate — which is a
racing judgement of the kind sailing makes about right of way, newly imported
into a sport that had spent a century avoiding the need for one.
