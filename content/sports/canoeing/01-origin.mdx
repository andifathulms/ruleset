# Two boats, borrowed

Both boats are much older than the sport, and neither was built to race.

The <Term id="canoe">canoe</Term> comes from North America. First Nations
peoples built it for rivers and lakes. It is open, and it is paddled kneeling
with a single blade. The <Term id="kayak">kayak</Term> comes from the Arctic.
Inuit and Yupik peoples built it for hunting at sea. It is enclosed, with a
deck that keeps the water out, and it is paddled sitting down with a double
blade. A kayak hunter who capsized could <Term id="roll">roll</Term> back
upright.

## A barrister and the Rob Roy

The sport began with a Scottish barrister, John MacGregor. In the 1860s he
built a decked boat, called it the Rob Roy, and paddled it across Europe. He
wrote books about his journeys, and they sold well. People copied him for
pleasure. Clubs followed, then racing, and in 1924 an international
federation.

<Photo id="canoeing-macgregor-1866" />

So the boats are Indigenous and the rules are Victorian.
[Lacrosse](/sports/lacrosse/) has the same history, but there the
Haudenosaunee Nationals compete as a nation. The peoples who invented the
canoe and the kayak have no such place in canoeing.

## Into the Games

<Term id="sprint">Sprint</Term> racing joined the Olympic programme in 1936.
Women raced Olympic kayaks from 1948. <Term id="slalom">Slalom</Term> appeared
in 1972, was dropped, and came back in 1992.

<Photo id="canoeing-amyot-1936" />

Francis Amyot of Canada won a canoe event at those 1936 Games. Events like his
stayed closed to women until 2021.
