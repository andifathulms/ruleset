# Origin and invention

Canoeing is two Indigenous technologies turned into one European sport.

The **canoe** is North American — open, paddled kneeling with a single blade,
built by First Nations peoples for rivers and lakes. The **kayak** is Arctic —
enclosed, paddled seated with a double blade, built by Inuit and Yupik peoples
for hunting at sea, with a spray deck and a roll to recover from capsizing.

Neither was designed for racing. Both were transport and work.

The sport came from a Scottish barrister, John MacGregor, who in the 1860s
built a decked boat he called the Rob Roy, paddled it across Europe, and wrote
books about it that started a recreational canoeing craze. Clubs followed, then
racing, then an international federation in 1924.

So this sport's equipment is Indigenous and its rules are Victorian, which is
the same shape as lacrosse — except that lacrosse's originating peoples still
field a team, and canoeing's do not appear in its competitive structures at
all.

Sprint entered the Olympic programme in 1936. Slalom appeared in 1972, was
dropped, and returned in 1992. Women could compete in Olympic kayak events from
1948 and in canoe events only from 2021.
