# Three arguments that changed the sport

## Eighty-five years without women's canoe

Women first raced at the Olympic Games in kayaks in 1948. The canoe events,
paddled kneeling with a single blade, stayed for men only. The federation held
world championship events for women in canoes for years, and still the Olympic
events did not open. The reasons once given for keeping women out did not hold
up when they were examined.

<Photo id="canoeing-fox-2022" />

The change came from outside. The International Olympic Committee required
equal numbers of men and women across the programme, and the number of
athletes in canoeing was fixed. So
[women's canoe events came in for the Tokyo Games](#icf-2020-womens-canoe), in
both sprint and slalom, and men's events went out to make room. That was
eighty-five years after canoeing joined the Games.
[Boxing](/sports/boxing/) had admitted women in 2012.

## The fifty-second gate

Slalom judges once had to grade mistakes. A light <Term id="touch">touch</Term>
on a gate cost less than a heavy one. The grading was inconsistent and hard
to explain. Around 1990 the federation
[cut the penalties down to two](#icf-slalom-penalties): two seconds for a
touch and fifty for a <Term id="missed-gate">missed gate</Term>.

Fifty seconds is more than the gap between the first and last paddler in a
top field. A missed gate therefore ends the run as a contest. The rule is
harsh, and it is clear. Nobody has to judge how bad a mistake was.

## A race nobody could follow

In slalom one boat goes down at a time. A spectator cannot tell who is winning
without a scoreboard. The federation's answer was
<Term id="kayak-cross">kayak cross</Term>,
[added to the Olympic programme in 2024](#icf-2024-kayak-cross). Four kayaks
drop from a ramp together. Contact is allowed. Each paddler must roll the boat
at a set point, and the first to the bottom wins.
