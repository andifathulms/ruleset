# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Women in canoe.** The longest-running and least defensible. Olympic women
could paddle kayaks from 1948 and could not contest canoe events until 2021 —
eighty-five years after the sport entered the programme. The federation ran
women's canoe world championships for years while the Olympic classes stayed
closed.

The correction, when it came, was forced by the IOC's parity requirement and
paid for by deleting men's events. It is the latest such exclusion to be ended
of any sport on this site.

**The fifty-second gate.** Slalom's penalty structure was simplified around
1990 to two values: two seconds for a touch, fifty for a miss. Fifty seconds is
larger than the spread of an entire elite field, so a missed gate is not a
penalty but an elimination.

That is deliberate and defensible — it makes the sanction unambiguous and
removes a judge's assessment of severity — and it means canoe slalom punishes a
single error more harshly than almost anything else on this site.

**The unwatchable time trial.** Slalom sends one boat down at a time against a
clock, so a spectator cannot tell who is winning. Kayak cross, added for 2024,
is the answer: four boats at once, contact permitted, first down wins.

It is a race inside a discipline built on measurement, and whether it belongs
in the same sport is exactly the argument climbing has about speed.

**The narrowing boat.** Composite construction made slalom boats progressively
narrower and less stable through the 1990s. A minimum width froze the design
before it became an arms race.
