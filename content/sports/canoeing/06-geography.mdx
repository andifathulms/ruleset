# Flat water is everywhere, whitewater is not

Sprint and slalom are strong in different countries, and the reason is the
water each one needs.

Sprint needs flat water, which most countries have, and boats that a club can
own. Its leading nations are spread widely: Hungary, Germany, Belarus,
Ukraine, Spain, New Zealand, Australia, Canada and China, with more and more
paddlers from Africa and Latin America. Hungary treats sprint canoeing as a
national sport.

Slalom needs <Term id="whitewater">whitewater</Term> good enough to race on.
More and more that means a built channel, and only a few countries have built
one. The strong slalom nations are a short list: Great Britain, France,
Germany, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Australia and the
United States.

Starting out is easy in one boat and hard in the other. Most people can paddle
a steady club kayak on flat water within one session. A racing kayak is so
narrow that a beginner tips over again and again. In both, a paddler needs a
club, because few people can afford a boat or reach the right water alone.

Women's canoe events are the newest part of the sport at the Games, and the
list of nations that are good at them is still forming.
