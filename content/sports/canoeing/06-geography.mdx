# Where it is played

Canoeing has two maps and the difference between them is a facility.

**Sprint** is broadly distributed: Hungary, Germany, Belarus, Ukraine, Spain,
New Zealand, Australia, Canada, China and increasingly across Africa and
Latin America. It needs flat water, which most countries have, and boats, which
clubs can own. Hungary in particular treats sprint canoeing as a national sport
and has produced more Olympic champions in it than any other country.

**Slalom** is narrow: Great Britain, France, Germany, the Czech Republic,
Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Australia and the United States. It needs whitewater
of competitive standard, and increasingly that means an artificial course —
which is an expensive engineered facility that a small number of countries have
built.

That is the same dynamic hockey experienced with artificial turf, arriving more
slowly and without being mandated. Nobody required slalom to move to
engineered channels; it simply became necessary to be competitive, and the map
contracted to countries that could build one.

The Indigenous origins of both boats have no presence in the sport's
competitive structures, which is a contrast worth drawing with lacrosse, where
the Haudenosaunee Nationals compete as a nation. Canoeing took two Indigenous
technologies and built a European sport on them, and the peoples who invented
the boats are absent from it.

Women's canoe events, absent from the Games until 2021, have a shorter
competitive history and a map still forming.
