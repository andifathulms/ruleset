# Lighter boats and built rivers

Boats were first made of wood and canvas, then fibreglass, then carbon
composite. Each material made them lighter and stiffer. The rules hold that in check with
[a minimum length and weight for each class](#icf-boat-specifications).

Slalom boats had a second problem. A narrow boat is faster through a
<Term id="gate">gate</Term> and less steady in rough water. Composite
construction let builders make them extremely narrow, and the sport was
heading for a contest between designers. A minimum width stopped it.
[Cycling](/sports/cycling/) did the same kind of thing when it fixed the shape
of the bicycle.

One fitting separates the two boats in sprint. A kayak has a
<Term id="rudder">rudder</Term>. A canoe has none, so keeping it straight with
the paddle alone is a skill that takes months to learn.

## The river that can be switched on

The biggest change was to the water. A natural river is unpredictable, hard
for spectators to reach, and impossible to schedule. For the 1972 Games,
Munich built
[the first whitewater channel made for competition](#icf-1972-slalom-added).
Its flow could be controlled. Slalom has been moving from rivers to an
<Term id="artificial-course">artificial course</Term> ever since.

<Photos ids="canoeing-lee-valley canoeing-la-seu-2019" />

No rule made that move compulsory. Paddlers simply needed a built channel to
stay competitive.
[Hockey](/sports/hockey/) went through a similar change with artificial turf.
The cost is the same in both sports: the facility is expensive, there are few
of them, and a country without one struggles to produce slalom paddlers.
