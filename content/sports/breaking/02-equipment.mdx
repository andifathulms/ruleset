# Equipment evolution

There is none, and the absence is worth a section.

Breaking specifies no equipment at all. Dancers wear what they choose. The only
regulated elements of a competition are the floor — flat and smooth — and the
sound system. It is the cheapest sport on this site to stage and to enter, by a
wide margin, and it grew in public spaces precisely because it required nothing.

The comparison with the other judged sports here is the point. Gymnastics
specifies its apparatus in extraordinary detail, because the apparatus
determines what movements are possible and therefore what a difficulty value
means — change the vaulting table and every vault has to be repriced. Diving
specifies the board and the height. Figure skating specifies the ice.

Breaking has no difficulty table, so it has nothing to standardise apparatus
for. Its judging is comparative rather than absolute: judges say which of two
dancers was better, not what either performance was worth. A system that never
assigns an absolute value never needs a controlled environment to make those
values mean the same thing twice.

The one thing that does function as equipment is the music, and it is
deliberately not controlled. A DJ selects tracks the dancers have not heard, so
the raw material of every performance is chosen by somebody else in real time.

That is the opposite of every other judged sport's arrangement, where the
apparatus is fixed so the performance can be planned.
