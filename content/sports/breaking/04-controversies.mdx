# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Codification itself.** Breaking's central controversy is the existence of its
competition rules. A form with forty years of judging tradition — battles
decided by respected practitioners, holistically, without stated criteria —
acquired an international federation, a criteria list and a scoring instrument,
none of which its community requested.

The rules that resulted are careful. Trivium was built with practitioner
involvement and it declines to do the obvious thing, which would have been to
write a difficulty table. It judges comparatively because breaking had no
absolute scale and inventing one would have asserted a standard the form did
not hold.

That is the most restrained response to a codification problem anywhere on this
site, and it did not settle the argument.

**The scoring problem itself.** Every other judged sport here awards an
absolute mark. Gymnastics prices its elements; diving tabulates difficulty;
figure skating defines its elements and their values. Each of those systems was
built by the sport, over decades, on material the sport already agreed about.
Breaking had no such agreement to codify, and the federation had two years.

**October 2023.** The 2028 programme was announced without breaking, ten months
before the sport was contested at Paris. The Los Angeles organisers proposed a
programme of sports with established American commercial followings, and
breaking was not among them.

There is no rule change to record here. What there is, is the clearest example
on this site of an Olympic decision being commercial rather than sporting, and
of a community that gave up something to be admitted and was not kept.
