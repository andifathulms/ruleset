# Origin and invention

Breaking is the only sport on this site that did not want to be one.

It emerged in the Bronx in the early 1970s, out of block parties, sound systems
and the practice of dancing to the percussive break of a record extended by a
DJ. It is one of hip-hop's founding elements alongside DJing, MCing and
graffiti, and its transmission has always been through crews, cyphers and jams
rather than through clubs, coaches or federations.

It has a competitive tradition of its own and always has: the battle, judged by
respected practitioners, decided holistically and without stated criteria. That
is a functioning adjudication system that ran for four decades before anyone
wrote it down.

What it did not have was an international governing body, a scoring scale, or
any interest in acquiring either. Both arrived from outside. The World
DanceSport Federation — a ballroom-dance body — took breaking on for Olympic
purposes in 2017, and the sport appeared at the Youth Olympic Games in 2018 and
at Paris in 2024.

A substantial part of the breaking community objected throughout, on grounds
that are unusually coherent: the form already had judging traditions, the
federation had no standing in it, and codification would change the culture
more than exposure would grow it.

This site records rule changes and their causes. Here the rules were written
for a form that had not asked for them, and that is the cause.
