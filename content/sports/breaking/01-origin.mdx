# Out of the Bronx

Breaking began in the Bronx, in New York, in the early 1970s. It came out of
block parties and sound systems. A <Term id="dj">DJ</Term> would stretch out
the break of a record, the part where only the percussion plays, and people
danced to it. Parties held in 1973 in the recreation room of an apartment
building at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue are generally credited as the beginning of
hip-hop. Breaking is one of hip-hop's founding elements, with DJing, MCing and
graffiti.

<Photo id="breaking-1520-sedgwick" />

Nobody learned it from a club or a coach. It was passed on in
<Term id="crew">crews</Term>, in the <Term id="cypher">cypher</Term> and at
<Term id="jam">jams</Term>.

It was competitive from the start. Dancers met in a
<Term id="battle">battle</Term>, and respected dancers decided who had won.
They judged the whole performance and gave no list of reasons. That way of
judging worked for four decades before anyone wrote rules down.

<Photo id="breaking-bronx-cypher" />

Breaking had no international governing body and no scoring scale. Both came
from outside. [In 2017 the World DanceSport Federation took breaking
on](#wdsf-2017-governance) for Olympic purposes. It is the federation of
ballroom [dancesport](/sports/dancesport/). Breaking was danced at the Youth
Olympic Games in 2018 and [at the Olympic Games in Paris in
2024](#ioc-2020-breaking-added).

<Photo id="breaking-yog-2018" />

A large part of the breaking community objected throughout. Their case had
three parts. Breaking already had its own way of judging. A ballroom
federation had no standing in it. And written rules would change the culture
more than Olympic attention would help it grow.
