# Where it is danced

Breaking needs a floor and a speaker, so it can be danced anywhere. It began
among Black and Puerto Rican communities in the Bronx in the early 1970s, and
film, music and video carried it around the world long before it had a
governing body.

Ten years later it was already being contested across Europe. In 1984 there
was a contest in the Vondelpark in Amsterdam and a regional championship in
Freiburg in West Germany.

By the 1990s there were settled scenes in Japan, France, South Korea, Russia,
and across Europe and Latin America. No federation created them.

Japan and France are the strongest competitive nations, and Japan's B-Girls
have been dominant. Neither country got there through sports funding. Both
got there through decades of local scenes.

The Olympic structure fitted this badly. National federations had to be built
in places where a scene already existed and had never needed one. In some
countries, the body that selected dancers for Paris was only months old.

The scenes are still there, and the 2028 decision does not touch them. The
Olympic Games were never the centre of competitive breaking, so losing its
place costs breaking less than it would cost most sports.
