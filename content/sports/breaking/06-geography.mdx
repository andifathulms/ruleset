# Where it is played

Breaking has the widest and least institutional geography of any sport on this
site, because it needs nothing and travels through culture rather than through
federations.

Its origin is specific — the Bronx, the early 1970s, Black and Puerto Rican
communities in New York — and it spread globally through film, music and video
long before any governing body existed. By the 1990s there were established
scenes in Japan, France, South Korea, Russia and across Europe and Latin
America, none of them created by a federation and most of them unaware of one.

Japan and France are the strongest competitive nations, and Japan's B-Girl
scene in particular has been dominant. Neither is explicable by sports
investment; both are explicable by decades of local scene-building.

That produces a distribution nothing else here matches. There is no facility
requirement, no equipment cost and no licensing, so breaking exists wherever
young people have a floor and a speaker — which is everywhere.

The Olympic apparatus sat awkwardly on top of that. National federations had to
be constructed in places where a scene already existed and had never needed
one, and selection for Paris ran through structures that in some countries were
months old.

The scenes remain, and are unaffected by the 2028 decision. Breaking's
competitive life was never principally Olympic, which is both why the community
was ambivalent about admission and why removal costs it less than it would cost
almost any other sport here.
