# How do you score a battle?

For forty years nobody did. A <Term id="battle">battle</Term> was decided by
dancers the scene respected. They watched, they chose a winner, and they did
not have to say why.

<Photo id="breaking-lilou-judging" />

The Olympic Games needed more than that. A result had to be open, repeatable
and possible to explain to an audience. Other judged sports do this with a
scale. [Gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) gives every element a value, and
[diving](/sports/diving/) has a table of difficulty. Breaking had nothing of
the kind to borrow.

## The sliders

<Term id="trivium">Trivium</Term> asks a smaller question. Which of these two
dancers was better? The judges answer it five times, once for each criterion:
technique, <Term id="vocabulary">vocabulary</Term>, execution,
<Term id="musicality">musicality</Term> and originality. For each one a judge
moves a slider towards one dancer. The sliders are combined into a
<Term id="split">percentage split</Term>.

Nobody has to say what a move is worth. The judges are still dancers, and
they are making the judgement that dancers always made in a battle. Now they
make it in five parts, and it is recorded.

## What a result cannot say

A split of 62 to 38 describes two dancers on one day. A dancer who wins one
battle 62 to 38 and another 55 to 45 has beaten two different opponents by
two different margins. The two numbers cannot be compared. Breaking has no
personal bests and no records, and [no series of scores can be
drawn](#breaking-scores).

The federation could have invented a scale and chose to leave it out. A scale
would have claimed a standard that breaking did not have.
