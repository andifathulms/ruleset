# Officiating and technology

Breaking's judging system is the most interesting recent invention on this
site, and it deserves to be understood on its own terms rather than as a
curiosity.

**The problem.** Every judged sport needs a scale, and a scale asserts what
things are worth. Gymnastics can do this because it has spent a century
agreeing what a Tkatchev is worth relative to a Kovacs. Diving can do it
because degrees of difficulty are tabulated and uncontested. Breaking could
not, because forty years of battles had been decided holistically by
practitioners who did not need to say why.

**The answer.** Trivium asks only which of two dancers was better, on each of
five criteria — technique, vocabulary, execution, musicality, originality. A
judge moves a slider towards one dancer, and the sliders aggregate into a
percentage split.

Nobody has to say what a move is worth. The system only has to be able to say
that this dancer did it better than that one, which is a judgement
practitioners were already making.

**The cost** is that no result is a measurement. A 62–38 split describes a
pairing on a day. There is no personal best, no standard to reach, no series,
and nothing to chase — which is why this sport's series entry is `impossible`
rather than `unpublished`. There is no quantity anybody declined to publish.

That is a genuine trade, made deliberately, and it is the only place on this
site where a federation chose *not* to invent a scale when it could have. Set
against gymnastics pricing its elements, taekwondo pricing head kicks and
climbing pricing holds, breaking's restraint is the outlier.
