# Governing body politics

Breaking's governance is the most contested on this site, because the sport's
own community largely did not consent to it.

The World DanceSport Federation is a ballroom-dance body with existing Olympic
recognition. When the IOC sought youth-facing, urban, inexpensive and
gender-balanced additions to the programme, breaking fitted every criterion,
and the WDSF was the available route to admission because it already held
recognition.

The objection from within breaking was not reflexive hostility to
institutions. It was specific: the form had functioning judging traditions of
its own, developed over forty years by practitioners; a ballroom federation had
no standing in those traditions; and competitive codification would change what
the culture valued more than Olympic exposure would grow it.

The federation's answer was Trivium — a judging system built with practitioner
input, comparative rather than absolute, deliberately declining to invent a
difficulty scale the form did not have. It is a genuinely thoughtful response
to the objection, and it did not resolve it.

The sequence that followed is the part this site finds hardest to write
neutrally. In October 2023 the IOC confirmed the 2028 programme without
breaking. The sport was first contested at Paris in August 2024, ten months
later.

Whatever breaking did at its only Games could not have influenced whether it
returned. A community that was told codification was the price of Olympic
recognition paid it, and the recognition lasted one Games.
