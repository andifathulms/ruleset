# One Games

## A federation it did not ask for

The International Olympic Committee wanted new events that were urban, cheap
to stage, balanced between men and women, and attractive to young people.
Breaking met every one of those conditions. It had no recognised federation.
The World DanceSport Federation, a ballroom body, already had Olympic
recognition, and so [it became breaking's route into the
Games](#wdsf-2017-governance).

Many breakers objected. They did not object to institutions in general. They
said the dance had forty years of its own judging, built by the people who
did it, and that a ballroom federation had no standing there. They also said
that fixing the rules would change what the culture valued.

The federation's reply was <Term id="trivium">Trivium</Term>, [a judging
system adopted in 2019](#wdsf-2019-trivium) and built with breakers taking
part. The federation had two years to make it. Trivium does not invent a
table of difficulty, because breaking never had one. It did not end the
objection.

## Dropped before the first battle

In December 2020 [breaking was added to the programme of the 2024 Olympic
Games](#ioc-2020-breaking-added), with one event for
<Term id="b-boy">B-Boys</Term> and one for B-Girls.

In October 2023 [the programme for 2028 was confirmed without
it](#ioc-2023-breaking-dropped). The organisers in Los Angeles had proposed
sports with an established commercial following in the United States:
[flag football](/sports/flag-football/), [squash](/sports/squash/),
[lacrosse](/sports/lacrosse/), [cricket](/sports/cricket/),
[baseball](/sports/baseball/) and [softball](/sports/softball/).

Breaking's Olympic battles took place in Paris in August 2024, ten months
after that decision. Nothing the dancers did there could have changed it.
