# Nothing to carry

Kabaddi has no ball, no bat and no net. Two teams, a marked space and a line
across the middle are enough. A game that needs nothing can be played
anywhere, by anyone, and that is why kabaddi has spread so widely.

What has changed is the ground. The traditional game is played on earth or
clay, and much of it still is. Top competition is played on a synthetic mat.
The mat was brought in for safety, because players are held and dragged
along the ground all the time. It had a second effect. A surface that is the
same everywhere made the game easier to televise, and made tackling
technique repeatable.

Players wear ordinary sports kit, cut close so there is less for a
<Term id="defender">defender</Term> to grab. There is no protective
equipment. Many play barefoot, and some wear light shoes.

The one machine in modern kabaddi sits off the court. Professional
competition uses video review to check whether a raider's foot crossed the
line, or whether a defender stepped into the <Term id="lobby">lobby</Term>
too early.
