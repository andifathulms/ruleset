# Equipment evolution

There is nothing to evolve, and that is worth a page of its own.

Kabaddi has no object of play. Every other sport covered on this site has
something at its centre — a ball, a shuttle, a disc, a puck, a rope, a barbell,
an arrow, a blade, a target. Kabaddi has two teams, a rectangle and a line.
That makes it the cheapest sport here by a margin that is not close, and it is
the direct cause of its reach: a game that requires nothing can be played
anywhere by anyone.

What has changed is the surface. The traditional game is played on earth or
clay, and much of the sport still is. Competitive kabaddi is played on a
synthetic mat, which was adopted for safety — the sport involves being dragged
and held on the ground constantly — and which had a second effect nobody
legislated for: a consistent surface made the sport televisable and made
tackling technique repeatable.

Clothing is ordinary sports kit, cut close so there is less for a defender to
grab. There is no protective equipment. Some players wear light shoes and many
play barefoot.

The one piece of technology in modern kabaddi is not on the court. It is the
review system used in professional competition, where an official can go to
video to check whether a raider's foot crossed the line or whether a defender
entered the lobby early — the only place the sport has admitted an instrument
at all.
