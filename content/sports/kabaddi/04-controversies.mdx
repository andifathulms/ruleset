# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Raids that did not happen.** The founding problem of the modern game. The
cant limits a raid, but a fit raider can chant for a long time, and a defence
under no pressure can simply refuse to engage. Matches could become sequences
of raiders stepping in and stepping out. The 2014 answer was two devices at
once: a thirty-second raid clock, and the do-or-die raid, in which a team's
third consecutive empty raid must produce a point or lose the raider.

**Where the rules came from.** Those changes were written for a professional
television league and adopted into the international code afterwards. Whether
a domestic commercial competition should be setting a sport's laws is disputed,
and the argument is sharper here than in the broadcast-scoring cases elsewhere
on this site because the route was so direct.

**The lobbies.** Side areas that are out of play until a tackle begins, at
which point they become live. It is the sport's most technical rule, it decides
a great many points, and its interpretation has been adjusted repeatedly
because the moment a tackle "begins" is not a crisp fact.

**Judging the cant over a crowd.** The sport's defining rule is enforced by ear
in a loud arena. There is no instrument for it and no proposal for one, and
disputes about whether a cant had genuinely broken are as old as the codified
game.

**Which federation.** Competing international bodies claiming the right to run
world championships is a governance controversy rather than a rules one, and it
constrains everything the sport might do next.
