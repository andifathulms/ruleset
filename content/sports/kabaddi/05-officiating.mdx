# Judged by ear

A kabaddi match has a referee, two umpires and a scorer, and part of their job
is to listen.

## The cant

The <Term id="raider">raider</Term> must chant without a break. If the
<Term id="cant">cant</Term> stops while the raider is in the other half, the
raid is over. So an umpire has to decide, by ear, whether a chant was
continuous. The arena is loud, and the raider is often being tackled by
several people at that moment. No instrument is used, and disputes about
whether a cant really broke are as old as the written rules.

## The lobbies

The <Term id="lobby">lobbies</Term> along the sides are out of play until a
<Term id="tackle">tackle</Term> begins. After that they are live. The umpire
has to fix the moment the tackle began, and so whether a defender who stepped
into the lobby did so legally. It is the most technical call in the game, it
decides many points, and the way it is read has been changed again and again,
because the start of a tackle is not a clear moment.

## The line and the holds

Whether any part of the raider got back across the
<Term id="midline">midline</Term>, often while three people hold on, decides
whether the points count. Professional competition uses video review for this
and for the lobby question.

Defenders may not hold a raider by the hair or the clothing. The line between
a legal hold and an illegal one is judged live, in a pile of bodies.
