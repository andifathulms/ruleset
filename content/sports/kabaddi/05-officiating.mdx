# Officiating

Kabaddi is the only sport covered here whose central rule is enforced by
listening.

**The cant.** The raider must chant continuously and audibly. If it breaks
while they are in the opposition half, the raid is over and they must be back
across the line. An umpire is therefore making an auditory judgement — was that
chant continuous? — in an arena with a crowd, about a person who is being
tackled by several opponents at the time. No instrument is used and none has
been proposed.

**The lobbies.** The side areas are out of play until a tackle begins and live
thereafter. Deciding the instant a tackle began, and therefore whether a
defender who stepped into the lobby did so legally, is the most technical call
in the sport and one of the most consequential.

**The line.** Whether a raider got any part of themselves back across the
midline, often while being held by three people, decides whether points are
banked or lost. Professional competition uses video review for this and for the
lobby question, which is the only instrumentation kabaddi has accepted.

**Holds.** Defenders may not hold a raider by the hair or the clothing, and the
boundary between a legal ankle hold and an illegal one is judged live in a pile
of bodies.

The comparison worth making is with ultimate, elsewhere on this site, which
removed officials entirely, and with orienteering, which replaced them with an
electronic record. Kabaddi has kept a human standing at the edge of the mat
deciding, by ear, whether an athlete stopped making a noise — and the sport's
biggest commercial success has been built on top of that without changing it.
