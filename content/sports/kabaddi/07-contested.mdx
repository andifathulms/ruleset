# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Which federation governs.** More than one international body claims the
mandate and competing world cups have been held. Until that is resolved,
kabaddi cannot make the institutional argument that any wider recognition would
require.

**Whether a league should write the rules.** The raid clock and the do-or-die
raid came from a domestic professional competition and were adopted
internationally. They improved the spectacle and they were not produced by the
sport's governing process. Whether that is pragmatism or capture is argued.

**One-country dominance.** India has won nearly everything, and its
professional league is where the money, the coaching and the athletes are. Iran
has shown the gap is closable. Nobody else has.

**Judging the cant.** The sport's defining rule is enforced by ear in a loud
stadium, with no instrument and no proposal for one, in a competition that is
otherwise televised with video review.

**Standard versus circle.** Two codes, governed separately, with different
scoring and different constituencies — and the diaspora communities that keep
the sport alive outside South Asia largely play the one with no international
programme.

**Where it goes next.** Kabaddi has an Asian Games place, no World Games place,
and no realistic Olympic route. Whether to pursue the multi-sport system at all,
or to build on the commercial success it already has, is the live strategic
question and the federations disagree about it — partly because they disagree
about which of them gets to decide.
