# What is still argued about

None of this is settled.

**Who governs.** More than one international body claims the right to run the
game, and each has held its own world cup. Until that is settled, kabaddi
cannot make a strong case for wider recognition.

**Who writes the rules.** The raid clock and the do-or-die raid came from a
domestic league, outside the federations' own process. They made the game
livelier. Some call that sensible, and others see a sport handed to a
broadcaster.

**One country on top.** India has won nearly everything, and its league is
where the money, the coaching and the athletes are. Iran has shown the gap
can be closed. No one else has yet.

**Hearing the cant.** The game's central rule is judged by ear in loud
arenas, while the rest of the professional game has video review.

**Two codes.** The standard game and the <Term id="circle-kabaddi">circle
game</Term> are run separately. The communities that keep kabaddi alive
outside South Asia mostly play the one with no international programme.

**What comes next.** Kabaddi has an Asian Games place, no World Games place
and no clear road to the Olympics. The bodies disagree about whether to chase
the multi-sport programmes or build on the league, and about which of them
gets to decide.
