# From village game to league

Kabaddi needs nothing but people and a line, and it has been played across
South Asia for centuries.

Games in which one person runs into a group's ground, tries to touch them and
escapes are recorded all over the region, under many names: kabaddi,
hu-tu-tu, ha-du-du, chedugudu, sadugudu. Holding the breath is part of all of
them. The chant is how the breath is checked. Nobody can see whether a player
is holding their breath, but anyone can hear whether the
<Term id="cant">cant</Term> has stopped.

## Written rules

In India in the 1920s, the game was given written rules so that clubs and
regions could play each other on equal terms. A family of folk games became
one sport, with a court, a team size and a way of scoring. The cant, already
part of the old game, became [the rule that limits a
raid](#kabaddi-the-cant).

Recognition came slowly. Kabaddi was first a demonstration sport at the Asian
Games, and in Beijing in 1990 it became [a full medal
event](#kabaddi-1990-asian-games). India then won the men's title at every
Asian Games for decades. The International Kabaddi Federation was formed in
2004.

## The league

The biggest change came in 2014. A professional league started in India and
became one of the country's largest television properties. It changed the
rules: a thirty-second <Term id="raid-clock">raid clock</Term> and the
<Term id="do-or-die">do-or-die raid</Term>. The international game then
[took both up](#kabaddi-2014-raid-clock). A broadcast format rewrote the laws
of a folk sport, much as it had rewritten the scoring of
[badminton](/sports/badminton/).
