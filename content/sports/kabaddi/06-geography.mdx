# Where it is played

Kabaddi is a mass sport in South Asia and a minority one everywhere else, and
the imbalance is more extreme than for almost anything on this site.

**India** is the sport. It is played in villages, schools and universities
across the country, it has a professional league that draws a national
television audience, and it has won every men's Asian Games title from 1990 for
decades. The women's game, added to the Asian Games programme in 2010, has
followed the same pattern.

**Iran** is the only country to have seriously broken that dominance, with a
national programme that has beaten India at Asian Games level, and it is the
best evidence available that the sport's concentration is a matter of
investment rather than of something intrinsic.

**Bangladesh** has it as a national sport, and **Pakistan**, **Nepal** and
**Sri Lanka** all play it widely. Beyond South Asia, **South Korea**, **Japan**
and **Chinese Taipei** compete at Asian level, and the Punjabi diaspora has
carried circle-style kabaddi to Britain, Canada and the United States, where it
is a substantial community sport largely invisible to the mainstream.

The circle-style game deserves separating out. It is played on a circular
field, scores differently, has its own competitions and its own world cup, and
is not the sport contested at the Asian Games. Two related codes, governed
apart, with the diaspora largely playing the one that has no international
programme.

Kabaddi is not on the World Games programme, which makes it one of the few
sports covered here whose only multi-sport home is a continental one.
