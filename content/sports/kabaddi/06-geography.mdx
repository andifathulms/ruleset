# Where it is played

Kabaddi is a mass sport in South Asia and a small one almost everywhere else.

India is the centre of the game. It is played in villages, schools and
universities across the country, and the professional league draws a
national television audience. India won every men's Asian Games title from
1990 for decades. The women's event joined the Asian Games in 2010, and India
led there too.

Iran is the one country to have seriously broken that run. Its national
programme has beaten India at the Asian Games. That suggests the gap comes
from investment, and can be closed.

In Bangladesh, where it is also called ha-du-du, kabaddi is the national
sport. Pakistan, Nepal and Sri Lanka play it widely. Beyond South Asia, South
Korea, Japan and Chinese Taipei compete at Asian level.

## The circle game

Punjab has its own form, <Term id="circle-kabaddi">circle kabaddi</Term>. It
is played on a round field, 22 metres across for men and 16 for women. It
scores differently, has its own competitions and its own world cup, and it is
not the game played at the Asian Games. Punjabi communities have carried it
to Britain, Canada and the United States, where it is a large community
sport that few outsiders notice.
