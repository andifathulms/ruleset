# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether annulment achieves anything.** The sport has now annulled its entire
record book twice, twenty-five years apart, for the same reason. The argument
for is that a record set under conditions the sport can no longer accept is a
wall rather than a target. The argument against is that the 1993 reset
demonstrably did not fix the problem it was aimed at, and that erasing numbers
is a substitute for governance rather than an instrument of it.

Set against athletics with the javelin and swimming with the supersuits, this
is the third answer to the same question — and the only one applied twice to
the same sport.

**Olympic status.** Weightlifting's place on the programme has been in genuine
doubt within the last decade, and the sanctions imposed — quota cuts, fewer
contested categories, imposed governance reform — are still working through.
No other sport here is in this position.

**Categories that do not match.** The divisions contested at the Games and the
divisions in which world records are recognised are now different sets. A
lifter can hold a world record in a category nobody can win a medal in, which
is a strange state for a measured sport and has no obvious defender.

**The press-out.** Still the sport's least consistent call, still judged by
eye, and still the same problem that caused an entire lift to be abolished in
1973. Automating it has been proposed and never pursued.

**Weight cutting.** As in judo and boxing, the sport is contested at a made
weight and the practice of extreme dehydration before weigh-in is regulated
lightly.
