# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The press, 1972.** The clean and press was supposed to be a strict overhead
push from a stationary position. By the late 1960s lifters had discovered that
leaning back far enough turned it into something closer to a slow jerk, and the
lean got progressively more extreme because there was no clear line to enforce.
The same lift passed at one competition and failed at another.

The federation's response was not to rewrite the definition. It removed the
event. That is the most drastic answer to a judging problem anywhere in this
collection — gymnastics split its panel, badminton fixed a height, athletics
redesigned an implement, and weightlifting simply deleted a third of its sport.

**1993.** Every world record annulled, categories redrawn, a new list begun. The
stated framing was modernisation; the context was a decade of results that the
sport could not defend and could not individually overturn.

**2008 and 2012, retested.** Stored samples from two Games, reanalysed years
later with better methods, produced positives across this sport at a rate
unmatched anywhere else. Medals were reallocated in numbers that made
reallocation almost meaningless.

**2018.** Every world record annulled again. Categories redrawn again. A
quarter of a century after the first attempt at exactly the same thing.

There is no incident in this section in the sense that the other pages have
one — no single throw, no single game. What forced these changes was an
accumulation the federation could not audit its way out of, and its chosen
instrument each time was to destroy the numbers rather than to relitigate the
performances.
