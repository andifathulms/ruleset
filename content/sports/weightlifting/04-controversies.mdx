# A lift removed, and a record book erased twice

## The press

The clean and press was the third lift. The lifter brought the bar to the
shoulders, stood still, and pushed it overhead with the arms alone.

By the late 1960s lifters had found that leaning back far enough, and driving
with the legs, turned the push into something close to a slow jerk. Nobody
could say where the lean became too much, so it grew. The same lift passed at
one competition and failed at another.

<Photo id="weightlifting-alekseyev-1972" />

The Munich Olympics of 1972 were the last Games with a press. The federation
did not rewrite the definition. [It removed the
lift](#iwf-1973-press-abolished), and from 1973 weightlifting was a two-lift
sport. Some read this as the federation admitting it could not judge its own
sport. Others say no workable definition existed. Either way, every
<Term id="total">total</Term> from before 1973 includes a press, and no total
since does.

## 1993: every record annulled

Many of the world records standing at the start of the 1990s came from a
period of widespread doping, much of it documented later. Lifters competing
under working drug tests regarded those marks as out of reach.

The federation [redrew every bodyweight
category](#iwf-1993-categories-redrawn) and annulled every world record in the
sport. The old marks became historical figures. The new list began with
"world standards", weights a lifter had to beat before any record could be
set. No single case had to be judged. Supporters call it a necessary fresh
start. Critics call it a way to erase results the federation could neither
defend nor overturn.

## 2018: the same answer again

Samples stored from the 2008 and 2012 Games were tested again years later. A
very large number came back positive in weightlifting, many of them from
medallists. By 2017 the International Olympic Committee had put the sport's
place at the Games under review.

<Photo id="weightlifting-talakhadze-2016" />

In 2018 the federation [redrew the categories a second
time](#iwf-2018-categories-redrawn-again) and annulled every world record a
second time. Lasha Talakhadze of Georgia had won the heaviest class at Rio in
2016 with a world-record total of 473 kilograms. That record went with the
rest.

Twenty-five years separate the two resets, and the reason was substantially
the same. The first one had not solved the problem.

## What it cost

The sport's number of places at the Games has been cut more than once, and the
number of categories with it. [From 2024 there are five categories for men and
five for women](#iwf-2024-olympic-categories-cut). The federation itself has
been through reforms imposed from outside.
