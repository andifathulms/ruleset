# Officiating and technology

Three referees, a majority verdict, and one of the shortest lists of decidable
questions in any sport here. Was the bar brought overhead in the prescribed
manner? Was it held motionless with the arms and legs locked? Did anything
except the feet touch the platform?

Weightlifting's officiating problem is a single call: the **press-out**, where
a lifter's arms begin to extend and then continue pushing rather than locking
in one motion. It is fast, it is partly hidden by the bar, and it is the same
class of judgement that killed the press in 1973. The sport has never fully
solved it.

Technology has arrived where it usually does: at the questions with correct
answers. Loading is verified by calibrated plates and a published attempt
sequence; the clock is automated; video review exists at major competitions for
jury overturns. The jury can and does reverse the three referees, which puts
weightlifting alongside judo as a sport where the officials on the floor are
not the last word.

What technology has not been asked to do is judge the lift. There is no sensor
on the bar, no automatic detection of arm extension, no equivalent of
taekwondo's electronic scoring. A sport that could plausibly automate its
central judgement has not tried, and the reason appears to be that a press-out
is a judgement about the *manner* of a movement rather than about a position at
a moment — the same distinction that separates football's offside from
football's handball.

The other officiating function here is not on the platform at all. It is the
weigh-in, and the anti-doping programme, and in this sport those have mattered
more to the record book than any referee ever has.
