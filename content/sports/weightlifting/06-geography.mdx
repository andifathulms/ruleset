# Where it is played

The strongest weightlifting nations are China, Iran, Georgia, Armenia,
Bulgaria, Turkey, North Korea, Colombia, Thailand, Indonesia and Uzbekistan.
The traditional Western sporting powers are mostly missing from the top.

The sport is cheap. It needs a bar, plates and a room with a solid floor, so a
country without much sports infrastructure can still take it up. Hidilyn Diaz
won the Philippines' first Olympic weightlifting medal, a silver, at Rio in
2016, and its first gold in 2021.

State support explains much of the rest. Governments across the former Soviet
bloc and in China backed the sport heavily, and several of those systems
remain. Where a national programme finds children at twelve and trains them
centrally, weightlifting produces results.

Several of the federations punished most heavily after the 2008 and 2012
retests came from countries where the sport is a national priority and the
system is centralised. Whether that shows different behaviour or simply more
testing is disputed, and the evidence does not settle it.

The result is clear enough. Cuts in places and national suspensions have
fallen hardest on the countries where the sport is strongest, so the list of
who lifts at a Games has been shaped by governance as well as by performance.

In the women's sport China dominates, with a wider spread of nations beneath
it.
