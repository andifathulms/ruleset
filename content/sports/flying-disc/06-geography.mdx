# Where it is played

Ultimate's map is North America, northern Europe, Japan, Colombia and Australia
— and Colombia is the part worth pausing on.

The United States and Canada have the deepest structures: college ultimate is
the sport's largest talent pipeline anywhere, and North America has the only
substantial professional leagues. Japan has been elite for decades. In Europe,
Great Britain, Germany, Finland, Sweden, Austria and Switzerland field strong
programmes, and the indoor five-a-side game keeps the sport alive through
northern winters.

Colombia is the outlier and the sport's best argument about itself. A country
with no ultimate tradition, no professional league and very little money
became a genuine world power — particularly in the mixed and women's divisions
— on the strength of a school and community programme. In a sport where the
equipment is one disc and the officials are the players, the cost of entry for
a country is unusually close to zero, and Colombia is what that looks like when
someone takes advantage of it.

Disc golf's geography is different again, and larger. It is a mass
participation sport in the United States and in Finland, which has more courses
per head than anywhere, and it grew sharply through the early 2020s. Almost
none of that participation is visible in the multi-sport programmes WFDF sits
inside.

The gap between how many people throw a disc and how much institutional
standing the disc sports have is probably the widest of any sport on this site.
