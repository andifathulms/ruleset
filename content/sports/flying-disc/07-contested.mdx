# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether self-officiation can survive elite competition.** The question the
sport exists on. Observers were the compromise; whether the compromise is
stable, or a stage on the way to referees, is genuinely unresolved and argued
in public by people inside the sport.

**Whether observers are already referees.** WFDF says the foul call remaining
with the players is the line. Critics say an official who can overrule a
disputed call, penalise misconduct and eject a player is exercising the
authority of a referee under a different name.

**The professional split.** North American professional leagues use referees.
Their players also play for national teams under WFDF rules without them. A
sport whose most visible domestic product is played under a different
officiating model from its world championship has a coherence problem it has
not addressed.

**Olympic candidacy.** WFDF has IOC recognition and no place on the programme.
The obstacles it can name — self-officiation, a sport played to a score rather
than a clock, a mixed division as a flagship — are all features it does not
want to give up, and the honest question is whether inclusion is worth what it
would cost.

**Disc golf's standing.** Far more people play disc golf than ultimate. It has
its own professional tour, its own equipment industry, and effectively no
presence in the federation's multi-sport ambitions. What WFDF is for, if its
largest sport is not the one it campaigns with, is an open question.
