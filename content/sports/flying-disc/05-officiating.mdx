# Officiating

There is none, and that is the rule.

Ultimate is played without referees at every level including the world
championship. A foul is called by the player who was fouled, out loud, in play.
The player called against may contest it. If they contest and the two cannot
agree, the disc returns to the thrower and the passage of play is undone — the
game's answer to an unresolved dispute is to pretend it did not happen rather
than to award anything to anybody.

Spirit of the Game is clause one of the rulebook, not a preface. It places the
obligation on the players to know the rules, to apply them honestly against
themselves, and to avoid deliberate infringement — and it states that a player
who breaks the rules on purpose has committed an offence against the sport
rather than incurred a cost.

Elite competition has added **observers**. An observer resolves a dispute the
players cannot, rules on whether a disc was in or out and whether the stall
count was fair, administers time limits, and may issue misconduct penalties or
send a player off. An observer does not call fouls. That reservation is the
entire distinction WFDF is defending, and whether it is a real one is the
sport's live argument.

What ultimate demonstrates, and no other sport on this site can, is how much of
officiating is actually about deciding facts versus about removing the burden
of honesty from the competitors. Ultimate has offloaded the first and kept the
second, deliberately, for nearly sixty years.
