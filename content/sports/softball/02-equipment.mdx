# Ball, bat and mask

The first softball was sixteen inches round and really was soft. Players
threw it and caught it with bare hands.

Competitive fastpitch settled on a twelve-inch ball. It is wound and covered
like a small baseball, and it must meet standards for hardness and bounce
that a gymnasium ball never had. The sixteen-inch game, still played with
bare hands, survives in Chicago, where the sport began.

## The bat that was too good

Bats went from wood to aluminium in the 1970s, and then to composite
materials. A composite bat can give more energy back to the ball than wood
can. In softball that is dangerous. The pitcher finishes the throw about
forty feet from home plate, still moving forward, with almost no time to
react to a ball hit straight back.

<Photos ids="softball-bat softball-windmill" />

So a softball bat is judged by what it does. A machine tests how fast the
ball comes off the bat, and the standard sets a limit. A bat can lose its
approval after it has gone on sale, if it breaks in so that it becomes
livelier. [The standards were tightened in
2018](#softball-2018-fastpitch-standards). Face masks for pitchers and for
the infielders nearest the batter became common in much of the game for the
same reason.

The limits are still disputed. Because a bat is judged by performance, the
standard can change after a bat has been sold, and a bat that is legal in one
place may be banned in another. Players and makers argue about the limits and
about the tests.

The catcher's equipment followed baseball's. The pitcher wears nothing
special except, more and more often, a mask.
