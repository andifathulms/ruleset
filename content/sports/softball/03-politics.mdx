# Governing body politics

Softball's institutional history has one decisive event, and it happened to
another sport.

The International Softball Federation was formed in 1952 and softball entered
the Olympic programme for Atlanta in 1996, as a women's event. It was contested
four times. In 2005 the IOC session voted both baseball and softball off the
programme after 2008.

Softball's federation has argued since that the vote was not about softball.
The two sports were treated as a pair, and the objections raised — a
professional league that would not release players, and a doping record — were
baseball's. Softball had no equivalent league withholding its athletes and no
comparable case against it. It was removed anyway, and the reasoning was never
separated.

The response was to stop being separable. In 2013 the two federations merged
into the World Baseball Softball Confederation and campaigned jointly, on the
argument that one listing covering a men's and a women's tournament was a
better proposition than two sports competing for the same slot. It worked
once: baseball and softball returned in Tokyo in 2021 as host-city additions,
were absent in Paris, and return in Los Angeles in 2028.

That leaves softball in an unusual position for a sport on this site. It is
governed jointly with a different game, appears on the programme as half of a
shared listing, and its presence depends on host cities in countries where
baseball is popular.
