# Where it came from

Softball began indoors, in Chicago, in 1887, with a boxing glove and a pole.

## One evening at the boat club

It was the day of the Harvard–Yale football game, and men had gathered at the
Farragut Boat Club. One of them threw a boxing glove at another, who swung at
it with a pole. George Hancock tied the glove into a ball and chalked a
diamond on the floor. He wrote rules that evening. The game was called indoor
baseball, and its point was that people could play
[baseball](/sports/baseball/) in a gymnasium in winter.

<Photos ids="softball-chicago-1897 softball-indoor-1906" />

The room shaped the game. The ball was large and soft, so it would not travel
far or break anything. The bases were close together because the floor was
small. The pitch was underhand, because an overhand throw from so short a
distance would be too dangerous for the batter.

## Outdoors, and a new name

The game moved outdoors and the ball got harder. It had many names: kitten
ball, mush ball, diamond ball, playground ball. In 1926 it settled on
softball, and by then the ball was no longer soft. The Amateur Softball
Association formed in 1933 around a tournament at the Chicago World's Fair.
The international federation followed in 1952.

## Two games from one

The game split into two forms. <Term id="fastpitch">Fastpitch</Term> kept the
underhand throw and made it a weapon. <Term id="slowpitch">Slowpitch</Term>
required the ball to travel in an arc. That took the pitcher almost out of
the contest and made the game about hitting. Far more people play slowpitch.
Fastpitch is the form played at the Olympic Games.
