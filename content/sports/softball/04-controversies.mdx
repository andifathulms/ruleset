# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The pitcher who could win alone.** Fastpitch's structural oddity is that the
underhand motion does not destroy the arm, so a great pitcher can throw every
game of a tournament. At the 1996 and 2000 Games this produced scorelines in
which the contest was between one pitcher and everybody else. Later rule
adjustments — moving the pitching distance back to forty-three feet in the
international women's game from 2002 — were about restoring the batter's
chance, and are among the clearest dominance-suppression changes on this site.

**Games that would not end.** Because a pitcher does not tire the way a
baseball pitcher does, extra innings could run indefinitely at exactly the
moment a tournament schedule could least afford it. Softball's answer in 1987
was to begin each extra half-inning with a runner on second base. It is an
artificial device and it was adopted openly as one. Baseball copied it in 2020.

**The 2005 vote.** Covered under politics, and it is a controversy rather than
a decision: a sport was removed from the Olympic programme and the case made
against it was substantially about a different sport.

**Bats that outran the pitcher.** Composite bats in the 2000s raised
batted-ball speeds beyond what a pitcher forty feet away could react to.
Performance-based bat certification and infield face masks followed. This is a
safety change caused by equipment, with a rare direct causal chain from
material science to a rule.
