# Three problems the rules had to solve

## The pitcher who could win alone

Throwing underhand does little damage to the arm. A great
<Term id="fastpitch">fastpitch</Term> pitcher can throw a whole game one day
and another the next, and can pitch every game of a tournament. At the 1996
and 2000 Olympic Games some matches became one pitcher against everybody
else.

<Photo id="softball-ueno" />

From 2002 the pitching distance in the international women's game was moved
back to forty-three feet, to give the batter a better chance. People still
argue about it in both directions. Some say the move did not go far enough.
Others say the sport did not need to move the pitcher at all.

## Games that would not end

A softball pitcher does not tire as a baseball pitcher does, so extra
<Term id="inning">innings</Term> could go on and on. Tournaments pack many
games into a few days and cannot afford that. In 1987 softball adopted
[the tie-breaker](#softball-international-tiebreaker): each extra half-inning
starts with a runner already on second base. It is an artificial device, and
the sport adopted it openly as one. [Baseball](/sports/baseball/) took up the
same <Term id="tie-breaker">tie-breaker</Term> in 2020 and made it permanent
in 2023.

## Bats that were faster than the pitcher

In the 2000s composite bats sent the ball back faster than a pitcher forty
feet away could react to. The sport answered with
[limits on bat performance and face masks for
infielders](#softball-2018-fastpitch-standards). It is a safety rule with a
plain cause: a new material made the ball come off the bat too fast.
