# Officiating

The strike zone is a judgement, as in baseball, and softball adds a second
judgement that is harder.

The legal pitch is defined by body positions in motion. Both feet must be in
contact with the plate at the start; the hand must pass below the hip; the
wrist must not be further from the body than the elbow; the pivot foot must not
leave the ground and land again — the crow hop — and the stride foot must not
leave the circle. An umpire is asked to see several of these at once, at speed,
from a fixed position.

The result is that illegal-pitch calls are the sport's characteristic dispute.
They are made rarely relative to how often the boundary is approached, and the
consequence is immediate — a ball, and runners advance — so a marginal call
changes a game. Video evidence exists in elite competition and has not settled
the question, because what is being judged is a body in motion against a
description of legality, not a fact about where a ball landed.

The rest of officiating is baseball's, compressed. Fair and foul, safe and out,
obstruction and interference, with shorter distances making close plays closer.
Leaving a base before the pitch is released is a softball-only offence, and it
is called by an umpire watching a runner and a hand at the same time.
