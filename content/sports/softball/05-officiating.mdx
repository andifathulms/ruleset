# Who makes the calls

A softball umpire judges the <Term id="strike-zone">strike zone</Term> by
eye, as in [baseball](/sports/baseball/). Softball adds a second judgement
that is harder: whether the pitch itself was legal.

<Photo id="softball-home-plate" />

A legal pitch is defined by where the body is while it moves. Both feet must
touch the <Term id="pitching-plate">pitching plate</Term> at the start. The
hand must pass below the hip. The wrist must not be further from the body
than the elbow. The back foot must not leave the ground and land again, which
is called a <Term id="crow-hop">crow hop</Term>. The front foot must not land
outside the <Term id="pitching-circle">pitching circle</Term>. The umpire has
to watch several of these at once, at speed, from one fixed place.

So the <Term id="illegal-pitch">illegal pitch</Term> is the call that causes
the most argument. Pitchers come close to the limit often, and umpires call
it rarely. The penalty is immediate, so a marginal call can change a game.
Top competitions have video, and video has not ended the argument. The
question is whether a moving body matched a description, and a camera
settles that less easily than where a ball landed.

<Photos ids="softball-safe-at-second softball-sweden-2013" />

The rest is baseball's officiating at shorter distances: fair or foul, safe
or out, obstruction and interference. The shorter distances make close plays
closer. One offence belongs to softball alone. A runner may not leave a base
before the pitcher releases the ball, and the umpire has to watch the runner
and the pitcher's hand at the same moment.
