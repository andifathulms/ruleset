# Where it is played

Top-level women's fastpitch is played in few countries, and the list changes
little.

The strongest are the United States, Japan, Australia, Canada and Chinese
Taipei. Puerto Rico, the Netherlands, Italy, Mexico and China have been
competitive at various times. Every Olympic gold medal has gone to the United
States or Japan. College softball in the United States is by far the largest
competitive structure the sport has, and several other countries develop
their national players through it.

## The game most people play

<Term id="slowpitch">Slowpitch</Term> is a much bigger game. It is played for
fun and between workplaces across North America, and in local forms well
beyond it. It has international championships of its own, but none that lead
to the Olympic Games. The people who play softball and the people who compete
in it at the top are mostly different people.

<Photos ids="softball-geco-1940s softball-slowpitch-nl" />

Sixteen-inch softball, played with bare hands, is still a Chicago
institution. It is the original game, played where it was invented, and
hardly anywhere else.

## A wider map with baseball

The narrow map was part of the case against softball when it was
[voted off the Olympic programme](#softball-2008-olympic-removal). The joint
federation with baseball changes the picture. Baseball is strong in East
Asia, the Caribbean and the Americas, and softball is strong in a set of
countries that partly overlaps. Together they cover more of the world than
either does alone.
