# Where it is played

Softball's map is narrow and stable, and this was part of the case against it.

Elite women's fastpitch is concentrated in the United States, Japan, Australia,
Canada, and Chinese Taipei, with Puerto Rico, the Netherlands, Italy, Mexico
and China competitive at various times. Every Olympic gold has gone to the
United States or Japan. American collegiate softball is by a distance the
largest competitive structure the sport has, and it functions as the
development pathway for several other countries' national teams.

Slowpitch is a different picture entirely and a much larger one. As
recreational and workplace sport it is played across North America and, in
various local forms, well beyond it — but it is not contested internationally
in any form that would count towards Olympic status, so the sport's popular
base and its competitive base barely overlap.

Sixteen-inch softball, played barehanded, remains a Chicago institution: the
original game, still played where it was invented, and not played anywhere else
in any numbers.

The joint federation with baseball changes the geography argument. Baseball is
strong in East Asia, the Caribbean and the Americas; softball is strong in a
partly overlapping set. Combined, the listing looks broader than either sport
does alone, which is the point of the merger.
