# Equipment evolution

Golf's equipment history is a century of distance increasing and the governing
bodies deciding, repeatedly and late, how much of it to allow.

**The ball** went from feather-stuffed leather to gutta-percha to wound rubber
to the modern multi-layer solid core, and each transition added distance. The
governing bodies responded with limits on size, weight, initial velocity and
finally overall distance under a standard test.

**The club** went from wood to steel to titanium and composite, heads grew
enormously, and the face became thin enough to flex and rebound. Limits on head
size and on face flexibility followed, each written after the technology
arrived.

**Grooves** are the most instructive one. Square-cut grooves let a player
impart heavy spin from the rough, which meant missing the fairway stopped
costing anything. Driving accuracy ceased to matter at the top level. The 2010
restriction on groove geometry was written to restore a penalty that technology
had engineered away — a rule reinstating a difficulty rather than removing one.

**The 2028 rollback** is the entry this site finds most striking. The
conforming ball will be required to fly shorter under the standard test. Every
other equipment rule in this collection restricts a new advantage before it is
established; this one takes back distance that has been in the game for
decades.

The governing bodies initially proposed applying it only to elite competition
and dropped that, because golf's unusual property is that everyone plays the
same game under the same rules, and bifurcating would have ended it.
