# The ball, the club and the groove

Each new golf ball has flown further than the one before, and each time the
rule limiting it was written afterwards.

## The ball

The first balls were feathers stuffed into stitched leather. Gutta-percha
replaced them, and wound rubber replaced that. The rubber-cored ball was new
in 1901, when Walter Travis won his second U.S. Amateur title with one, and it
soon took over. The modern ball has a solid core in several layers. The
governing bodies answered each step with limits: on size, on weight, on the
speed of the ball off the club, and finally on overall distance under a
standard test.

## The club

Shafts went from hickory wood to steel, and heads from wood to titanium and
composite. Heads grew enormously, and the face became thin enough to flex and
spring back. Limits on head size and on face flex followed, again after the
technology had arrived. A player may carry fourteen clubs at most.

## Grooves

Square-cut <Term id="grooves">grooves</Term> let a player put heavy spin on
the ball even from the <Term id="rough">rough</Term>. A shot from long grass
could then be controlled almost as well as one from the
<Term id="fairway">fairway</Term>, so missing the fairway stopped costing
anything. At the top level, accurate driving ceased to matter.

[In 2010 the shape and sharpness of grooves were
restricted](#golf-2010-groove-rule). The rule was written to bring back a
difficulty that technology had removed.
