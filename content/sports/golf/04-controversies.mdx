# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The telephone.** For years, viewers watching high-definition coverage could
telephone a broadcaster to report a rules breach, and players were penalised —
sometimes disqualified days later — for infractions they could not have seen at
the time. Because only players on camera were exposed, enforcement depended on
the broadcast schedule.

It produced several high-profile disqualifications and was widely regarded as
indefensible. Ended in 2019, along with the rule that a scorecard signed before
a breach was known was automatically wrong.

**Accidental breaches on camera.** The same era produced penalties for balls
that moved fractionally on a green, visible only in slow motion. The 2019
rewrite removed several of these, on the reasoning that a player should not be
penalised for something no human could detect.

**Grooves.** Not an incident but a measurement. By the 2000s the statistical
relationship between driving accuracy and scoring had weakened to the point
where missing the fairway barely mattered, because square grooves controlled
the ball from the rough. The 2010 restriction was an attempt to make accuracy
count again.

**Distance.** The longest-running argument in the sport, and the one that
finally produced the 2028 ball change. Courses have been lengthened for
decades; several historic venues can no longer test the best players; and
lengthening runs into land, cost and water limits.

The governing bodies' initial proposal would have applied only to elite play.
They withdrew it, because golf's claim that everyone plays the same game under
the same rules was judged worth more than the convenience of leaving amateurs
alone.
