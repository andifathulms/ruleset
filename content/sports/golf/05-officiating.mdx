# The player is the referee

A golfer who breaks a rule is expected to call the penalty on themselves, even
when nobody saw it and nobody would ever know.

Referees and rules officials exist, and players ask them how to proceed. They
cannot watch everyone, because a field of players is spread over about two
hundred acres. [Sailing](/sports/sailing/) works in a similar way: a boat that
breaks a right-of-way rule takes its own penalty turn on the water.

## The viewers who phoned in

Television broke this arrangement for a while. High-definition pictures in
slow motion could show things that no player or official could see. Viewers
at home telephoned the broadcaster to report a breach. Players were then
penalised for something they could not have noticed, and some were
disqualified days later, because the scorecard they had signed was now wrong.

Only players who were on camera were exposed to this. A sport that depends on
the honesty of its players had ended up with enforcement decided by the
broadcast schedule.

The same years brought penalties for a ball that moved a tiny distance on the
green, seen only in slow motion.

## The rewrite

[Penalties from viewers' calls were ended](#golf-2019-video-call-in-ended). A
player is no longer penalised over a scorecard signed before a breach was
known, if the breach could not reasonably have been seen.

[The Rules themselves were rewritten and shortened in
2019](#golf-2019-rules-modernisation). Several penalties for accidents were
removed. A <Term id="drop">drop</Term> is now made from knee height, where it
used to be from the shoulder. A search for a lost ball is limited to three
minutes, and the flagstick may stay in the hole while a player putts.

In most sports, technology showed what officials were missing and the sport
took it up. In golf it showed more than the sport wanted enforced, and the
sport turned it down.
