# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The ball rollback.** Decided in 2023, effective from 2028, and still argued
about. Manufacturers and much of the professional game opposed it; the
governing bodies judged that distance had made historic courses unusable and
that lengthening was reaching its limits. Whoever is right, this will be the
first equipment change on this site to take back distance already established
in a sport, and it will sever golf's distance and scoring figures at the elite
end.

**Bifurcation.** The rollback could have applied only to elite competition. The
governing bodies withdrew that version because golf's claim is that everyone
plays the same game under the same rules. That claim will come under pressure
again, and the next equipment question may not be answered the same way.

**Land and water.** Golf's environmental footprint is contested in most places
it has expanded, and course lengthening is the specific pressure point. It is
one of the few sports here whose rule changes are shaped by a resource
constraint rather than by competition.

**Pace of play.** The 2019 rewrite addressed it and it has not been solved. A
four-hour round is a substantial barrier to participation, and every proposal
to shorten it runs into the fact that the sport is eighteen holes because it
has always been eighteen holes.

**Whether the majors should harmonise.** Four championships, four play-off
formats, four sets of conditions, and no body with authority over all of them.
Tennis harmonised its deciding-set rule in 2022 after fifty-two years. Golf has
not tried.
