# The long Olympic campaign

The International Korfball Federation has spent decades trying to get korfball
into the Olympic Games. It is recognised by the International Olympic
Committee, and it has never succeeded.

The obstacle is simple. Korfball is played seriously in the Netherlands and
Belgium, competitively in a scattering of other countries, and hardly at all
in most of the world. A sport cannot claim to be universal when it is not.

So the federation has worked on building the game. It has helped set up
national federations, in Asia above all. It has also held a place at the World
Games since the 1980s. The World Games exist for sports that are not Olympic,
and korfball is a fixture there.

## The argument from 1902

The federation has a second argument. Korfball is the only team sport whose
laws require mixed teams. Since about 2010 the Olympic programme has added
mixed events to one sport after another: mixed relays, mixed doubles, mixed
team [archery](/sports/archery/), mixed team [judo](/sports/judo/). The
federation says korfball answered that question in 1902.

The argument has not worked. One reply is that a sport with only mixed teams
offers half as many medal events, and the programme counts medal events.

The way sports are chosen now does not help either. Host cities propose extra
sports, and they pick ones with a large following at home. The only host for
which that sport could be korfball is the Netherlands.
