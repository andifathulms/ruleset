# Governing body politics

The IKF's whole political history is one long, unsuccessful Olympic campaign,
conducted from a position no amount of governance could fix.

The federation was formed in 1933, was recognised by the IOC, and has pursued
inclusion for decades. The obstacle is straightforward and the IKF has never
been able to move it: korfball is played seriously in the Netherlands and
Belgium, competitively in a scattering of other countries, and hardly at all
across most of the world. A sport cannot argue universality it does not have.

The response has been to build the sport rather than lobby for it — national
federations across Asia in particular, and a World Games place that has been
held since the 1980s. The World Games is the honest home for korfball: an event
explicitly for sports that are not Olympic, where it is a fixture rather than a
supplicant.

There is a second argument the federation makes and it is more interesting.
Korfball is the only team sport whose laws require mixed teams, in a period
when the IOC has spent two decades adding mixed events to sport after sport —
mixed relays, mixed doubles, mixed team archery, mixed team judo. The
federation's case is that korfball answered in 1902 the question the programme
has been asking since 2010.

It has not worked. Whether that is because the sport is too small or because
the answer arrived too early is the argument the IKF is still having.
