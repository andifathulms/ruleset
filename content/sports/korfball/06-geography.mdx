# Where it is played

Korfball belongs to the Netherlands more than to anywhere else. The Dutch have
the only league that is largely professional and the deepest club system.
Their national team has won the great majority of world championships. Belgium
is the only country that has challenged them year after year, and the two have
met in most of the finals the sport has held.

Beyond those two countries the game is real and thin. Chinese Taipei has been
the strongest side outside Europe for years. Hong Kong, China, Portugal,
England, Germany, the Czech Republic, Poland, Australia and Catalonia all
field competitive teams. The federation has well over a hundred member
federations, which says more about its outreach than about how deep the game
goes in each place.

<Photos ids="korfball-taipei-2009 korfball-wales-2006" />

The concentration feeds itself. The Dutch league is the one place where a
korfball player can be a professional. The best players from other countries
either move there or stop improving, and the international results then repeat
the pattern that produced them.

Korfball does have one kind of balance that few sports have. The teams are
mixed by law, so there is no men's game and women's game growing at different
speeds. There is one calendar, and no history of exclusion to repair. Every
country that plays korfball has always played it with women and men on the
court together.
