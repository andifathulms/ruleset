# Where it is played

Korfball is the most geographically concentrated sport covered on this site.

The Netherlands is the sport. It has the only substantially professional
league, the deepest club structure, and a national team that has won the
overwhelming majority of world championships. Belgium is the only country that
has consistently challenged it, and the two have contested most finals the
sport has held.

Beyond the Low Countries, the map is a scatter of real but shallow structures:
Chinese Taipei is the strongest side outside Europe and has been for years,
with Hong Kong, China, Portugal, England, Germany, the Czech Republic, Poland,
Australia and Catalonia all fielding competitive sides. The IKF has member
federations well into three figures, which is a statement about outreach rather
than about depth.

The concentration is self-reinforcing in a way that shows the sport's problem
plainly. The Dutch league is where a korfball player can be a professional, so
the best players from elsewhere either move there or stop improving — and the
sport's international results then confirm the concentration that produced
them.

What korfball does have is unusual institutional symmetry. Because the teams
are mixed by law, there is no men's game and women's game to develop at
different speeds, no separate calendar, and no historical exclusion to undo.
Every country that plays korfball at all has always played it with both sexes
on the court.
