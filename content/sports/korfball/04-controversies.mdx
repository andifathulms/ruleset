# Controversies that forced rule changes

Korfball's rule history is quieter than most sports on this site, which is
itself a finding: a code written to a social requirement in 1902 has had less
reason to be rewritten than one written to a competitive one.

**Making the game watchable.** The pressure korfball has actually responded to
is not safety or fairness but size — a sport this small needs its matches to
be legible to people who have never seen one. The zone switch every two goals,
short formats like Korfball4, and beach korfball are all answers to that, and
they are commercial and participatory changes rather than corrections.

**The shot that stops the game.** The defended-shot rule is the sport's
foundation and also its most persistent complaint: it can turn a possession
into a long search for a free shot, and a well-drilled defence can make a match
static. The tactical answer — the running-in shot, taken in movement past the
post — is the sport's response, and it developed as technique rather than as
legislation.

**Reducing the zones.** Korfball was originally played across more divisions
than the two it now uses, with players confined to a third of the court and
seeing much less of the game. Consolidating to two zones gave every player both
roles in the same match. This site does not enter a date for it, because none
that can be cited has been checked, and an approximate one would be a
confident wrong date.

**Mixed teams as an argument rather than a rule.** The IKF now presents its
founding requirement as a modern virtue. Nothing about the rule changed; what
changed is the world it is being explained to.
