# A game written for one classroom

Korfball was written in 1902 by a schoolteacher who needed a game for his
class. Nico Broekhuysen taught in Amsterdam. That summer he went on a teaching
course at Nääs in Sweden and saw a game called ringboll, in which players
threw a ball at a ring on a pole. His own class had boys and girls in it, and
he wanted a game they could play together. When he came home he wrote one. He
called it *korfbal*, from the Dutch word for basket.

<Photo id="korfball-broekhuysen" />

The requirement came first and the game was built around it. Boys and girls
on the same court both had to matter. Almost every law follows from that.

[Teams must be mixed, and a player may only defend an opponent of the same
sex](#korfball-1902-mixed-sex). Without the second half of that rule, the
tallest and strongest players would simply be put against everyone.
<Term id="contact">Contact</Term> is banned outright. There is no dribbling,
so nobody can go past an opponent on strength alone. And [a player who is
properly covered may not shoot](#korfball-defended-shot), so a defender wins
by standing in the right place and has no need to out-reach anyone.

<Photo id="korfball-mixed-1959" />

All of these laws belong to the code of 1902. None was added later for safety
or to suit modern ideas. Korfball put women and men in one team about a
century before other sports began adding mixed events.

One thing did change. The early game divided the court into more parts than
the two <Term id="zone">zones</Term> used now. Players were kept to a third of
the court and saw much less of the game. With two zones, every player attacks
and defends in the same match.

## Two Olympic afternoons

The game spread quickly through the Netherlands and into Belgium. It was a
demonstration sport at the Antwerp Games of 1920 and the Amsterdam Games of
1928, both held in the corner of Europe where it was played. It has never been
on the Olympic programme itself.

<Photo id="korfball-amsterdam-1928" />

[The International Korfball Federation was formed in
1933](#korfball-1933-federation) to give the Dutch and Belgian games one
rulebook.
