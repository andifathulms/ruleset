# Refereeing by eye

A korfball referee mostly judges positions: where one player is standing in
relation to another, and to the post.

## The defended shot

This is the call that matters most and the one most argued about. A
<Term id="defended-shot">defended shot</Term> is illegal only when four things
are true at once. The defender is within arm's length. The defender is facing
the attacker. The defender is nearer the post than the attacker. And the
defender is trying to block. The referee has to judge all four in the moment
before the ball leaves the shooter's hands, and the answer decides whether a
goal counts.

<Photo id="korfball-defended-shot" />

The rule can turn an attack into a long search for a free shot, and a
well-drilled defence can bring a match almost to a stop. Players found the
answer themselves, and no change to the laws was needed. It is the
<Term id="running-in-shot">running-in shot</Term>, taken on the move as the
attacker passes the post.

<Photo id="korfball-running-in" />

## The other calls

**Who is defending whom.** A player may only defend an opponent of the same
sex. The rule is simple and awkward in practice. If a defensive switch leaves
a man guarding a woman for a moment, that is an offence even when nobody meant
it. The referee watches the pairings as well as the ball.

**Contact.** None is allowed, so the line is very low. The referee is deciding
whether contact happened at all. Solid defending and holding are very close
together.

**Feet and seconds.** A player with the ball may not move the
<Term id="pivot-foot">pivot foot</Term>, so the referee is checking for
travelling all the time. A <Term id="throw-in">throw-in</Term> must be taken
within four seconds, and somebody has to count them.

Korfball has almost none of the technology that larger sports use. It has no
goal-line cameras and no sensors, and video review plays almost no part. The
game is refereed by eye.
