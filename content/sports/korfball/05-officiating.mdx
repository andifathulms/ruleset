# Officiating

A korfball referee adjudicates geometry, and there is more of it than in any
comparable sport.

**The defended shot.** Four conditions must hold at once for a shot to be
illegal: the defender within arm's length, facing the attacker, closer to the
post, and actively attempting to block. All four must be judged in the instant
before the ball is released, and the call decides whether a goal counts. It is
the most consequential judgement in the sport and the most argued.

**Same-sex defending.** Straightforward as a rule and awkward in practice — a
defensive switch that leaves a man briefly guarding a woman is an offence even
if nobody intended it, so a referee is watching pairings as well as play.

**Contact.** With no contact permitted at all, the threshold is very low, and
korfball has the same problem flag football has: an official is deciding
whether contact happened rather than whether it was excessive. Solid defending
and holding are separated by very little.

**The pivot foot and the four seconds.** No running with the ball and no
dribble means the travelling judgement is constant, and restarts carry a
four-second limit that must actually be counted.

What korfball does not have is any of the officiating apparatus the larger
sports have acquired. There is no video review to speak of, no goal-line
technology and no sensor anywhere. The sport is refereed by eye, and its most
important call is a four-part spatial judgement made at speed.
