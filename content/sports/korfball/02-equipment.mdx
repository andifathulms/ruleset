# Equipment evolution

There is very little equipment and one piece of it is doing an enormous amount
of work.

The korf is a bottomless cylindrical basket on a pole 3.5 metres high. It has
no backboard, and the absence is the design. A backboard gives a shooter a
surface to use and a defence a predictable rebound; without one, a shot must be
correct on its own terms and the ball leaves the post in any direction. The
running-in shot, korfball's signature act, exists because there is nothing
behind the post to shoot against.

The pole's position matters as much as its height. It stands a sixth of the
court length in from the end line rather than on it, so there is playable court
behind the basket. A korfball attack can come from any of 360 degrees, which is
why the game does not resolve into the wall-backed half-court shape basketball
takes.

Early korfs were woven baskets, later rattan, now usually synthetic. The ball
is close to a size 5 football and was one for much of the sport's history.

What is absent is as telling: no backboard, no net to slow the ball, no
protective equipment, no footwear specialisation beyond an indoor court shoe,
and no goalkeeper. A sport that permits no contact and no dribbling has very
little for equipment to regulate.
