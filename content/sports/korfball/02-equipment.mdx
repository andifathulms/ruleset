# The korf and where it stands

Korfball needs a ball and two baskets, and one of those two things does most
of the work.

The <Term id="korf">korf</Term> is a basket with no bottom on a pole 3.5
metres high. It has no backboard, and that is deliberate. A backboard gives a
shooter a surface to play the ball off. It also sends a missed shot back in a
direction a defence can predict. Without one, a shot goes in or it does not,
and a miss can leave the post in any direction.

<Photo id="korfball-the-korf" />

Where the <Term id="post">post</Term> stands matters as much as its height. It
is set one sixth of the court's length in from the end line, so there is court
behind the basket as well as in front. An attack can come from any side. In
[basketball](/sports/basketball/) the basket hangs at the end of the court,
and every attack faces the same way.

The first korfs were woven baskets. Later they were made of rattan, and now
they are usually synthetic. The ball is close to a size 5 football, and for
much of the sport's history it was one.

<Photo id="korfball-swift-1948" />

There is little else. The korf has no net to slow the ball. Players wear no
protection and need only an indoor court shoe. There is no goalkeeper. A game
with no contact and no dribbling leaves equipment very little to do.
