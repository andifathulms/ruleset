# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the founding rule is an asset or a ceiling.** The IKF argues that a
sport requiring mixed teams is what the Olympic movement has spent twenty years
adding mixed events to approximate. The counter-argument is that mandatory
mixing halves the number of medal events a sport can offer, which makes
korfball a poor value proposition for a programme that counts them.

**Whether the defended-shot rule makes a good spectacle.** It is the law that
holds the whole design together and the one most often blamed for matches
becoming static. Nobody inside the sport proposes removing it; the argument is
about how strictly to interpret it.

**Short formats.** Beach korfball and Korfball4 exist to make the game
portable and broadcastable. Whether a federation this small should be
maintaining several codes, or concentrating on one, is a live question — the
same one squash and rugby have argued in public.

**Concentration.** The Netherlands and Belgium have won essentially everything.
A sport making a universality argument to the IOC has a results record that
undermines it, and no obvious mechanism for changing that.

**Whether to keep campaigning.** Korfball has pursued the Olympic programme
since long before the current selection mechanism existed, and the mechanism
now in force — host cities proposing additional sports — favours sports with a
large domestic following in the host country. There is no plausible host for
which korfball is that sport, other than the Netherlands.
