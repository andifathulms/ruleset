# Where it is played

China, and then a genuinely wide but much shallower field.

**China** sets the standard in both disciplines and produces most of the
world's elite taolu athletes through a specialist sports school system that
takes children young and trains them full-time. The depth of the domestic field
is such that Chinese national trials are harder than most world championships,
which is a claim that can be made about very few sports anywhere.

Beyond it, **Hong Kong**, **Macau**, **Chinese Taipei**, **Vietnam**,
**Malaysia**, **Singapore**, **Indonesia**, **Iran** and **Russia** are all
competitive, and Iran and Russia in particular are strong in sanda, which
travels better than taolu because it is enterable by adults from other combat
backgrounds.

Wushu also has a wide recreational footprint carried by the Chinese diaspora —
community schools across Southeast Asia, North America, Europe and Australia —
and a further layer of visibility from film, which introduced far more people
to the sport's imagery than to its competition rules.

The split between the disciplines shapes the map. Taolu's requirement for a
childhood gymnastic base means it concentrates wherever a state or community
structure starts children early, which is overwhelmingly China. Sanda spreads
into countries with existing kickboxing and wrestling cultures, because an
adult can cross into it.

That is a familiar pattern on this site — the discipline that can be entered
late is the one that travels — and wushu contains both halves of it inside one
federation.
