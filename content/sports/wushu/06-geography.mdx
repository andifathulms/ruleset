# Where it is played

China sets the standard in both disciplines. Most of the world's best taolu
athletes come from its specialist sports schools, which take children young
and train them full time. The field at home is so deep that the Chinese
national trials are harder than most world championships.

## The rest of the field

Hong Kong, Macau, Chinese Taipei, Vietnam, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia,
Iran and Russia are all competitive. Iran and Russia are strongest in sanda.

<Photos ids="wushu-iran-taolu-2011 wushu-sanshou-club" />

The two disciplines spread in different ways. Taolu needs a gymnastic base
built in childhood, so it gathers wherever a state or a community starts
children early, and that is mostly China. Sanda can be taken up by an adult
who already fights. It has spread into countries with
[kickboxing](/sports/kickboxing/) and [wrestling](/sports/wrestling/)
traditions.

## Outside competition

Far more people practise wushu than compete in it. Chinese communities abroad
carried it to schools across Southeast Asia, North America, Europe and
Australia. Films showed its movements to many more people than have ever seen
its rules.
