# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether it is one sport.** Taolu is a judged gymnastic-martial performance;
sanda is a full-contact fight on a platform. They share a name and a federation.
For any programme that counts medal events and wants a coherent entry, this is
the central problem, and nobody proposes separating them.

**Whether standardised wushu represents Chinese martial arts.** The argument
that has run since the 1950s. Traditional lineages hold that the state-designed
routines selected for appearance; the federation holds that standardisation
made the sport possible at all.

**Declared difficulty.** Since 2003 taolu has rewarded prescribed aerial
elements with declared values, which made scoring separable and pushed the
discipline towards high-rotation jumps. Whether that produced a martial sport
or a gymnastic one with weapons is genuinely contested.

**One country's standard.** China's specialist school system sets the elite
level in taolu, and no other national programme is comparable. A universality
argument is difficult to make from that position.

**Olympic ambition after 2008.** The sport was staged inside a Games without
being in it, which the federation presented as progress. Eighteen years on
there is no place. What the campaign should look like now, or whether it should
continue, is an open question the federation has not answered publicly.

**The edge of the platform.** Sanda's defining rule is also its safety
question: a throw at the boundary of an 80-centimetre drop is a different
proposition from the same throw in the centre.
