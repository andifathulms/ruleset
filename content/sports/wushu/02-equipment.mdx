# Swords that bend, and a platform with no ropes

## The weapons

In taolu a weapon is apparatus, as a beam or a bar is in
[gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/). The
<Term id="straightsword">straightsword</Term>, the
<Term id="broadsword">broadsword</Term>, the <Term id="staff">staff</Term> and
the <Term id="spear">spear</Term> each have their own events and their own
required techniques. None is sharpened. The rules set each weapon's size, its
weight and how much it bends.

<Photos ids="wushu-staff wushu-broadsword" />

Competition swords are made thin and springy. A blade that whips and rings
looks fast to a judging panel. So a competition straightsword is a poor weapon
and a good piece of apparatus, and nobody pretends otherwise. Traditional
practitioners point to it as proof of their old complaint, that the sport
chose looks over fighting. The federation regulates the bend so that all
athletes compete with the same thing.

## The carpet

Taolu is performed on a marked, sprung <Term id="carpet">carpet</Term>. Since
routines began to include declared jumps and landings, the way the surface
behaves affects what an athlete can attempt, as a gymnastics floor does.

## Sanda

A sanda fighter wears ordinary amateur fighting kit: gloves, a headguard, a
mouthguard, a chest protector and a groin guard.

<Photo id="wushu-mansourian-2018" />

The unusual equipment is under the fighters' feet. The lei tai is a platform
about 80 centimetres high and 8 metres square, with no ropes and no cage. Its
boundary is a drop, and the drop is part of the scoring.
