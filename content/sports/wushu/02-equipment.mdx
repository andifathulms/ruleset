# Equipment evolution

Two disciplines, two entirely separate equipment stories.

**Taolu** uses weapons as apparatus. Straightsword, broadsword, staff and spear
each have their own events with their own required techniques, and each weapon
is a competition implement with specified dimensions, weight and — crucially —
flex. Modern competition swords are made thin and springy, because a blade that
whips and rings reads as fast and dynamic to a judging panel.

That specification is the sport's clearest equipment controversy in miniature.
A competition straightsword is a poor weapon and an excellent piece of
gymnastic apparatus, and traditional practitioners cite it as evidence for
exactly the objection they have been making since the 1950s. The federation
regulates flex to keep the events comparable; nobody pretends the implement is
a functional sword.

The carpet matters too. Taolu is performed on a marked, sprung surface, and
since the difficulty component was introduced — with declared jumps, rotation
counts and landings — the surface's behaviour affects what can be attempted, in
the way a gymnastics floor does.

**Sanda** uses the standard amateur combat kit: gloves, headguard, mouthguard,
chest protector and groin guard. Its distinctive piece of equipment is not worn
at all but stood on. The *lei tai* is a raised platform, around 80 centimetres
high and 8 metres square, with no ropes and no cage — so unlike every other
ring sport covered here, the boundary is a drop, and it is part of the scoring.
