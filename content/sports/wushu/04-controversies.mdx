# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Whether standardisation kept the martial art.** The founding argument, alive
since the 1950s. Traditional practitioners hold that state-designed routines
selected for visual impact and produced a performance discipline; the
federation holds that standardisation is what made competition possible.
Separate traditional categories at some championships are a partial
accommodation and have not settled it.

**Scoring a routine.** Holistic marking compressed the field and could not
separate two clean performances of unequal difficulty. The 2003 introduction of
a declared difficulty component fixed that and did what the same fix did in
gymnastics: severed the sport's scoring history and pushed competitive emphasis
towards high-rotation jumps. Whether taolu is now a gymnastic event with
martial styling is argued inside the sport as well as outside it.

**Giving the sport a contest.** Standardised routines gave wushu a judged
discipline and no fight. Sanda was built to supply one, and the decision to
fight it on a raised platform with a live edge — rather than in a roped ring —
is the clearest deliberate design choice in any combat sport covered here.

**Falling off.** Making the platform edge score means fighters can win by
pushing rather than striking, and the rules have had to define what counts as a
legitimate ejection, what counts as deliberately leaving to avoid a fight, and
what a throw at the edge may not do to an opponent's head.

**Two sports, one entry.** Not a rule change, and the sport's central structural
problem for any programme that counts medal events.
