# At the Games, and not in them

Wushu has never been on an Olympic programme. In 2008 it was held at an
Olympic Games anyway.

## Beijing, 2008

China wanted its national sport at its own Games, and wushu had not been
accepted onto the programme. An arrangement was found. [A wushu tournament was
staged in Beijing while the Games were going
on](#wushu-2008-concurrent-tournament), with the approval of the
International Olympic Committee. It was outside the programme, and its medals
were not Olympic medals. It was not a demonstration sport either, because the
committee had ended that category after 1992.

The federation presented the tournament as a step towards a full place. No
place has followed. Wushu appeared at the 2014 Youth Olympic Games in Nanjing,
and it remains at the Asian Games, the World Games and other continental
events.

## A long campaign

The federation has its headquarters in China, and Chinese support for it has
been steady and large. The campaign for an Olympic place has had state backing
for three decades.

Three things stand in its way. The sport is two disciplines with almost
nothing in common, which makes it hard to present as one entry. Its top
standard is set overwhelmingly by one country. And taolu adopted scoring by
declared difficulty in 2003, just as gymnastics was showing how much argument
that kind of scoring brings.
