# Judging a routine, refereeing an edge

Everything in wushu is decided by eye. No machine measures a routine or a
fight.

## The taolu panel

Taolu is judged much as [gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) is. The panel is
divided by job. One group marks the quality of the movements and takes off a
<Term id="deduction">deduction</Term> for each error on a list. Another checks
the declared difficulty. Another marks the performance as a whole. The athlete
performs once, inside a set time, and in half the events with a weapon in
hand.

## What changed in 2003

A routine used to receive one mark for its quality and completeness. The marks
of the best athletes bunched together. The judges could not separate two
routines that were equally clean and not equally hard.

[In 2003 the scoring was rebuilt](#wushu-nandu-scoring) around
<Term id="nandu">nandu</Term>, a difficulty component. Set jumps, rotations and
landings carry declared values, and the athlete is marked on delivering what
was declared. Gymnastics adopted the same structure in 2006, and the results
were the same in both sports. Scores from before and after cannot be compared.
Athletes turned towards jumps with more and more rotation. People inside the
sport, and outside it, argue about whether taolu is now a gymnastic event in
martial dress.

<Photos ids="wushu-kiani-2018 wushu-rajabi-2018" />

Checking the difficulty is the hardest call on the panel. A jump declared with
a certain rotation either reached it or fell short, and the difference is a
fraction of a turn, seen once at full speed.

## The edge of the platform

Sanda has the calls of any striking sport: clean strikes, legal throws,
strikes to forbidden targets. It has one more that belongs to it alone. The
edge of the platform scores, so the officials must keep deciding whether a
fighter was put off, fell off, or stepped off to avoid the fight. Each has a
different result. Two <Term id="platform-exit">exits</Term> in a round lose
the round, so this call decides bouts.

The edge also changes what a <Term id="throw">throw</Term> can do. A throw
that is legal in the centre may send an opponent head first down an
80-centimetre drop. The rules forbid that, and the officials have to judge it
as it happens. [Sumo](/sports/sumo/) wins a bout by putting a wrestler out of
the ring. Sanda only scores it, so the fight goes on with the edge still
there.
