# Officiating

Two disciplines, two officiating problems, and they belong to different
families of sport entirely.

**Taolu** is judged like gymnastics and has gymnastics' difficulties. The panel
is divided by function: one group marking execution quality against a deduction
list, another verifying the declared difficulty elements — did the rotation
reach its count, did the landing hold — and another marking overall
performance. An athlete performs once, with no second attempt, inside a
prescribed time window, and half the events have a weapon in hand.

The hardest calls are the difficulty verifications, because a jump declared at
a given rotation either achieved it or did not, and the difference is a
fraction of a turn observed in real time.

**Sanda** has the ordinary striking-sport calls — clean strikes, legal throws,
strikes to prohibited targets — plus one that is unique to it. The platform
edge scores, so the referee and judges must continually determine whether an
athlete was **put** off the platform, **fell** off, or **stepped** off to avoid
the fight, and each has a different consequence. Two exits in a round lose it,
so this judgement decides bouts.

There is also the throw-at-the-edge problem: a throw that would be legal in the
centre may drive an opponent off an 80-centimetre drop head first, and
officials police that boundary live.

Neither discipline is instrumented. A judged routine and a contested ejection
are both marked by eye.
