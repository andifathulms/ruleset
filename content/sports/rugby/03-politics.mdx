# Governing body politics

Two things dominate World Rugby's modern governance: the sport's Olympic
readmission, and its legal exposure.

**Olympic readmission** required a different sport. Fifteen-a-side needs large
squads, days of recovery between matches and a tournament of weeks, and no
Games can hold it. Sevens fits three days. The sport was readmitted in 2009 for
the 2016 Games, in a format its own World Cup is not played in.

That is now a pattern on this site. Cricket goes to the Games as Twenty20;
lacrosse invented Sixes for the purpose; rugby sends sevens. Three sports whose
Olympic showcase is not their principal competition.

**Legal exposure** is the newer and heavier pressure. Former professional
players have brought litigation alleging the sport failed to protect them from
the consequences of repeated head impact, and the evidence base linking career
sub-concussive contact to long-term neurological harm has grown substantially.

The federation's response has been to legislate the tackle: the high tackle
framework in 2019, then lowering the legal tackle height from 2024, starting in
community rugby and trialling upwards.

That is a governing body reshaping its defining action under legal and
evidential pressure, and it is genuinely contested inside the sport — some
argue that a lower tackle simply moves injuries to hips and knees, and that the
changes are driven by liability rather than by evidence.

The amateurism history sits underneath all of it. Union enforced amateurism
until 1995 and created rugby league by expelling clubs that wanted to pay
players in 1895.
