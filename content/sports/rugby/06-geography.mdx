# Where it is played

Rugby union's map is the British Empire with two significant additions and one
enormous asymmetry between its formats.

Fifteens is dominated by New Zealand, South Africa, France, England, Ireland,
Australia, Wales, Scotland, Italy and Argentina, with Japan and Georgia the
most substantial recent additions. It is a small, stable and wealthy group.

**Sevens is different, and this is the argument for it.** Fiji is a leading
nation and an Olympic champion — a country of under a million people whose
first Olympic gold medal of any kind came in rugby sevens in 2016. Kenya,
Samoa, Tonga and Spain are competitive in a way they are not in fifteens.

The reason is structural. Sevens needs seven players, not a squad of thirty
with specialist props and locks; it needs three days, not a month; and its
skills are closer to the running and handling that smaller unions can develop
without a professional club system.

That makes rugby's Olympic format a genuine widening rather than merely a
commercial convenience — which is more than can be said for every reduced
format on this site.

The women's game has grown fastest of anything in rugby over the last decade
and has a somewhat different map, with New Zealand, England, France, Canada and
Australia strongest.

Rugby league's geography is narrower again: northern England, eastern
Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and France, which is close to a map of
where the 1895 split reached.
