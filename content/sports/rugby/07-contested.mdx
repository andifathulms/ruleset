# What is still argued about

**Whether a lower tackle works.** Players and coaches argue that tackling
lower brings more contact with hips and knees, so the injuries may move
without becoming fewer. Some also say the change is driven by the threat of
legal action more than by evidence. The evidence is incomplete, and the sport
is changing its central action while that is still true.

**Whether the collisions can ever be made safe.** Repeated impact is part of
rugby itself. No rule short of removing contact takes it away.

**The calendar.** Unions, clubs and player bodies disagree about the length of
the season, the number of matches a player may play and the minimum rest
between them. The physical cost of rugby adds up over time.

**Sevens and fifteens.** Rugby's Olympic game is one that most of its players
do not play. Sevens has widened the map of the sport. It has also divided
money and players between two formats, and smaller unions feel that most.

**Union and league.** The two codes are still separate sports with separate
laws, a hundred and thirty years after a quarrel about paying working men.
Both are professional now, and nobody proposes joining them again.
