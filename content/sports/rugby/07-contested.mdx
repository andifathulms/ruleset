# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The tackle.** The largest open question in the sport. Lowering the legal
tackle height is intended to reduce head-to-head contact, and players and
coaches argue it produces more contact with hips and knees and may relocate
injuries rather than prevent them. The evidence is genuinely incomplete, the
legal pressure is real, and the sport is changing its defining action under
both.

**Whether the collisions can be made acceptable at all.** The harder version of
the same question. Repeated sub-concussive impact is intrinsic to rugby rather
than accidental to it, and no rule short of removing contact addresses that.
Boxing has the same problem and has done nothing; rugby is doing a great deal
and does not know whether it is enough.

**Player welfare and the calendar.** Season length, match load and minimum rest
periods are contested between unions, clubs and player bodies, in a sport whose
physical cost accumulates.

**Sevens and fifteens.** Rugby's Olympic showcase is a format most of its
players do not play. Sevens has genuinely widened the sport's geography, which
is the best defence of any reduced format on this site — and it has also
created a division of resources that smaller unions in particular have to
manage.

**The 1895 split.** Union and league remain separate sports with separate laws,
a hundred and thirty years after a disagreement about paying working men. Both
are now professional, and nobody proposes reunification.
