# Origin and invention

Rugby is one half of an argument, and football is the other.

Through the mid-nineteenth century English schools and clubs played their own
codes, disagreeing on two questions: whether the ball could be carried, and
whether an opponent could be hacked. The meetings that produced the Football
Association's laws in 1863 turned on exactly those, and the clubs that wanted
carrying left. The Rugby Football Union formed in 1871.

So rugby and association football are not related sports so much as the two
surviving answers to one dispute, and both rulebooks still show it.

Rugby then split again. In 1895 northern English clubs wanted to compensate
working players for wages lost to Saturday matches, the union refused, and they
left to form what became rugby league — a separate sport with different laws
and its own federation, created by a disagreement about money.

Union stayed amateur for a hundred more years. It did not accept professional
players until **1995**, which is the latest abolition of amateurism of any sport
on this site: twenty-seven years after tennis, and a hundred and twenty years
after football.

The Olympic history is split too. Rugby was contested as fifteens from 1900 to
1924 — the United States are still the reigning Olympic champions in that
format — and returned in 2016 as sevens, a different game.
