# Where it came from

Rugby is the game of the clubs that wanted to carry the ball. In the middle of
the nineteenth century English schools and clubs each played their own code,
and Rugby School's was one of them. The codes disagreed on two questions:
could the ball be carried, and could an opponent be hacked.

<Photos ids="rugby-rugby-school-1845 rugby-edinburgh-1871" />

In 1863 a series of meetings produced the laws of the Football
Association. The meetings turned on those two questions, and the clubs that
wanted carrying left. The Rugby Football Union was formed in 1871. In the same
year Scotland played England in Edinburgh, the first international rugby
match. Rugby and [football](/sports/football/) are two answers to one
argument, and both rule books still show it.

## The split over wages

Rugby then split again. In 1895 clubs in the north of England wanted to pay
working men for the wages they lost by playing on Saturdays. The union
refused, and the clubs left. On 21 September 1895 Leeds played their first
home game under Northern Union rules. The game those clubs played became rugby
league, a separate sport with its own laws and its own federation.

<Photo id="rugby-leeds-1895" />

Rugby union stayed amateur for another hundred years. Players could not be
paid, and a player who went to the professional code was banned for life. By
the 1990s unofficial payment was common, a rival professional circuit was
being organised, and the 1995 World Cup had shown how much money the game
could earn. [Union opened itself to professionals in
1995](#rugby-1995-professionalism). That was twenty-seven years after
[tennis](/sports/tennis/).

## Two Olympic games

Rugby was an Olympic sport from 1900 to 1924, as
<Term id="fifteens">fifteens</Term>. The last final was played at the Stade de
Colombes in Paris, where the United States beat France. Fifteens has not been
played at the Games since, so the United States are still the Olympic
champions in that format.

<Photos ids="rugby-paris-1924 rugby-usa-1924" />

A fifteens tournament needs large squads, days of rest between matches and
several weeks. No Olympic schedule can hold it. A whole
<Term id="sevens">sevens</Term> tournament fits into three days. In 2009
[rugby was readmitted as sevens](#rugby-2009-sevens-olympic), and it returned
at the 2016 Games after a gap of ninety-two years. The sport's own World Cup
is still played in fifteens.

[Lacrosse](/sports/lacrosse/) and [cricket](/sports/cricket/) took the same
road. Lacrosse created Sixes, and cricket goes to the Games as Twenty20.
