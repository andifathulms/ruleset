# Officiating and technology

Rugby has the hardest officiating job of any sport on this site, and the
federation's response has been unusual: rather than automate a judgement, it
published a procedure for making one.

**The breakdown** is why the job is hard. After every tackle, players arrive to
contest possession under a body of law governing who may do what, from where,
and in what order — and it happens in a pile of bodies, several times a minute,
at speed. No camera resolves it, because the questions are about entry angles,
support and release rather than about position at an instant.

**The television match official** handles what can be resolved: was the ball
grounded, did a foot touch the line, was there foul play in the build-up to a
try. Facts with correct answers.

**The high tackle sanction framework**, from 2019, is the genuinely interesting
instrument. It does not tell a referee what to decide. It gives them a sequence
— was there head contact, was there foul play, what was the degree of danger,
were there mitigating factors — and the answers produce penalty, yellow or red.

That is a published decision procedure, and it is rare. Almost everything else
on this site either leaves a judgement to an official or replaces it with an
instrument. Rugby structured the judgement instead, on the reasoning that
consistent sanctions change player behaviour and inconsistent ones do not.

Gymnastics' judging guidelines are the nearest relative here. Both are
attempts to make a human judgement reproducible without pretending it is a
measurement.
