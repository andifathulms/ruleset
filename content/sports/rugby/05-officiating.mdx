# Who makes the calls

A rugby match has a referee and two assistants. At elite level there is also a
<Term id="tmo">television match official</Term>.

## The breakdown

The hardest thing to referee is the
<Term id="breakdown">breakdown</Term>. After every tackle the carrier releases
the ball and players arrive to fight for it. The Laws say who may do what,
from where and in what order. It happens in a pile of bodies, several times a
minute, at speed. A camera does not settle it, because the questions are about
the angle a player came in from, who was supporting whom, and whether the ball
was released.

## What the video official decides

The television match official deals with questions that have a correct answer.
Was the ball grounded? Did a foot touch the line? Was there foul play before
the <Term id="try">try</Term>?

## A procedure for a judgement

The high tackle framework of 2019 works differently from a camera and from a
plain rule. It gives the referee a set of questions in order. Was there
contact with the head? Was there foul play? How dangerous was it? Was there
anything to lessen the blame? The answers lead to a penalty, a yellow card or
a red card.

<Photo id="rugby-referee-signal" />

Few sports publish a procedure like this. The reasoning is that consistent
sanctions change how players behave and inconsistent ones do not. The judging
guidelines in [gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/) are the nearest thing to it.
Both try to make a human judgement repeatable without calling it a
measurement.
