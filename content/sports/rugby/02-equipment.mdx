# The ball, and the padding rugby refuses

A rugby player wears almost nothing for protection, and the Laws mean it to be
that way.

The ball began as a pig's bladder in a laced leather case. It is now a
synthetic oval with a surface made for grip, which made passing and kicking
more reliable. No rule had to change for that. Boots and studs are specified
for safety. That is nearly the whole list.

<Photo id="rugby-invincibles-ball" />

## Why so little padding

Padding is allowed only within strict limits on its thickness. The reasoning
is stated openly: hard protection encourages harder collisions.

Headgear needs explaining. It is permitted and it is worn. It guards
against cuts and cauliflower ear, and it does not prevent concussion. World
Rugby says so plainly.

<Photo id="rugby-scrum-caps" />

This leaves the sport one tool for its main safety problem, which is the Laws.
Rugby cannot pad the head against impact, so it is lowering the legal height
of the <Term id="tackle">tackle</Term>. It changes what players may do, since
it will not change what they wear. [Boxing](/sports/boxing/) faced a similar
choice and removed its headguards in 2013, on safety grounds that are
contested.
