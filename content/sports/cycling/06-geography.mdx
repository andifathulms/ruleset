# Where it is raced

Almost everyone can ride a bicycle, and a small part of Europe wins most of
the road races.

In much of the world the bicycle is transport. Competitive road cycling is
concentrated in a belt from Belgium and the Netherlands through France, Italy
and Spain. Britain, Denmark, Colombia, Australia and Slovenia have joined in
the last two decades.

What these countries share is a race calendar. A young rider needs many
amateur races within reach, a club to enter them through, and roads that can
be closed. Colombia's rise rests on altitude and a racing culture at home.
Egan Bernal became the first Colombian winner of the Tour de France in 2019.

Track cycling follows the buildings. A <Term id="velodrome">velodrome</Term>
is expensive, there are few of them, and the countries that built them win.
Britain's success rests on a track programme paid for with lottery money from
the late 1990s. Its base is the Manchester Velodrome, which opened in 1994.

The <Term id="hour-record">hour record</Term> belongs to no country. It needs
one rider, one track and one hour, and it has been set in Mexico City,
Manchester and Grenchen.
