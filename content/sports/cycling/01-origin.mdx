# Origin and invention

Cycling as a sport is exactly as old as the bicycle that made it possible, and both arrived in a rush. The safety bicycle of the 1880s — two equal wheels, a chain drive, a diamond frame — was the first machine that ordinary people could ride fast, and racing on it began almost immediately. The Union Cycliste Internationale was founded in 1900.

What is unusual about cycling's origin is that its greatest events were invented by newspapers as circulation devices. The Tour de France was created in 1903 by a sports paper trying to outsell a rival; Paris–Roubaix and the Giro d'Italia have similar parentage. The sport's calendar was shaped by media before broadcast existed, which puts cycling ahead of every other sport on this site in being commercially designed from the start.

The hour record predates all of it. Riding as far as possible in an hour needs no opponent, no course and no organisation — just a track and a clock — and it has been contested since the 1870s. That simplicity is why it became the sport's purest measure and why it has been so difficult to govern: there is nothing in it to regulate except the machine.
