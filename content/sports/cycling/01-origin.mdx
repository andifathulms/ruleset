# Where it came from

Bicycle racing is as old as the bicycle that made it possible. The safety
bicycle of the 1880s had two wheels of equal size, a chain to the rear wheel
and a diamond-shaped frame. It was the first machine ordinary people could
ride fast, and racing on it began almost at once. The Union Cycliste
Internationale, the sport's governing body, was founded in 1900.

## Races invented by newspapers

The great road races were created to sell newspapers. The Tour de France was
started in 1903 by a sports paper that wanted to outsell a rival. On 1 July
1903 the paper, L'Auto, printed the route map on its own front page. The
riders set off from Villeneuve-Saint-Georges on a first
<Term id="stage-race">stage</Term> of several hundred kilometres.

<Photos ids="cycling-lauto-1903 cycling-tour-start-1903" />

Paris–Roubaix and the Giro d'Italia began in a similar way. Maurice Garin won
Paris–Roubaix in 1897 and 1898, and then won that first Tour. The calendar of
the sport was laid out by the press before broadcasting existed, and it is
still built around those races.

## One rider, one hour

The <Term id="hour-record">hour record</Term> is older than all of them. A
rider goes as far as possible in one hour. It needs no opponent and no
course, only a track and a clock, and riders have attempted it since the
1870s. It became the sport's plainest measure of a rider. It also became the
hardest thing in the sport to govern, because the only thing in it that a
rule can control is the bicycle.
