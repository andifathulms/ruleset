# Officiating and technology

Cycling's officiating divides cleanly into two jobs that have almost nothing in common.

**Timing and placing** are fully instrumented and barely contested. Track events are timed electronically to a thousandth of a second, photo finishes settle a sprint, and transponders track riders on the road. These are measurements with correct answers.

**Equipment compliance** is the other half, and it is where the sport puts its effort. Commissaires measure bicycles before events — frame geometry, saddle position, handlebar reach, weight — against the Lugano Charter's numbers. This is closer to a customs inspection than to refereeing, and no other sport on this site does anything like it at this scale.

Since 2016 that inspection includes scanning for concealed motors, using magnetic resonance devices and thermal cameras at races. The sport now routinely x-rays its own equipment for the presence of a machine that would be undetectable by eye.

The interesting officiating gap is on the road, where much of what riders do cannot be observed. Drafting behind a team car, taking a supply outside the permitted zone, holding onto a vehicle on a climb — these happen inside a moving convoy, spread over 200 kilometres, watched by a small number of officials on motorcycles. Cycling has the same enforceability problem swimming had with the breaststroke dolphin kick, and it has not resolved it the way swimming did.
