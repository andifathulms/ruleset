# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the 1997 split should ever have happened.** The federation reversed it in 2014, which is close to an admission, but the underlying question is open in every sport here: when equipment makes old marks unreachable, is the right answer to reset, to retain, or to split? Cycling is the only sport that has tried all three, and it has not said which it thinks was right.

**Where the equipment line sits.** The Lugano Charter defined a bicycle as a shape, and the argument has moved to the edges of that shape — tube profiles, wheel depth, skinsuit fabrics, chain lubricants, tyre construction. Each is legal until it is not, and the pattern of the last thirty years suggests the federation will keep legislating case by case whatever the Charter says.

**The minimum weight.** The 6.8 kg floor dates from 2000 and manufacturers have been able to build safely below it for years. Lowering it would advantage teams that can afford the newest frames; keeping it means the rules require bicycles to carry ballast, which is difficult to defend as a sporting principle.

**Detection.** Technological fraud has been an offence since 2016 and confirmed cases remain very rare, which is either evidence that the problem is small or evidence that detection is poor. Nobody in the sport agrees which, and the honest position is that the data cannot currently distinguish the two.
