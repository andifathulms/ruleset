# What is still argued about

**Whether the record should have been split.** The federation divided the
hour record in 1997 and joined it again in 2014. It has never said which
decision it thinks was right. When new equipment puts old marks out of reach,
a sport can restart its records, keep them, or run two lists. Cycling has
tried more than one of these and has not chosen.

**Where the equipment line sits.** The Lugano Charter defined the bicycle as
a shape, and the argument moved to the edges of the shape: the profile of the
tubes, the depth of the wheels, the fabric of the suits, chain lubricants,
tyres. Each is legal until a rule says it is not.

**The minimum weight.** The floor of 6.8 kg dates from 2000. Manufacturers
have been able to build safe bicycles below it for years, so some bicycles
carry added weight to be legal. Lowering the limit would help the teams that
can afford the newest frames.

**Detection.** Hidden motors have been an offence since 2016, and confirmed
cases are very rare. Either the problem is small, or the checks are poor. The
sport does not agree which, and the evidence so far cannot tell the two
apart.
