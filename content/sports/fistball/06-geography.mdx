# Where it is played

Two clusters, separated by an ocean and connected by emigration.

The European core is Germany, Austria and Switzerland, with Germany the
dominant power in both the men's and women's game by a wide margin. The club
structure there is genuine and old — fistball sits inside the German
gymnastics-club system, which gives it facilities, coaching and continuity that
sports of its size usually lack. Italy and the Czech Republic play it too.

The South American cluster is Brazil, Argentina and Chile, and it exists
because German-speaking emigrants took the sport with them in the nineteenth
and early twentieth centuries. It is not a development-programme import; it is
a century-old tradition, and Brazil and Argentina are consistently among the
best teams in the world.

Namibia is the outlier and the same story again. Fistball arrived with German
settlers under colonial rule and remained after that rule ended, and the
country fields international teams — a sport preserved by a community long
after the circumstances that brought it there had gone.

The pattern says something the sport cannot easily change. Fistball's map is
almost exactly the map of German emigration, and every country where it is
strong got it the same way. There is no example on this site of a sport with a
more legible diaspora footprint, and it means the federation's growth problem
is not distribution but origination: it has never yet taken root anywhere that
did not receive it from a German-speaking community.
