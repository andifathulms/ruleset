# What is still argued about

**Growing beyond its roots.** Every country where fistball is strong got it
from German-speaking emigrants. The federation has not yet made the sport take
root anywhere without that link. Whether it can decides much of its future.

<Photo id="fistball-australia-2014" />

**Rally scoring.** In 2000 fistball moved to [rally
scoring](#fistball-rally-scoring): a point on every rally, and
<Term id="set">sets</Term> to eleven. The
reason given was the clock. Under the old scoring a match had no reliable
length, so it was hard to schedule for television or for a tournament
timetable. [Volleyball](/sports/volleyball/), [table
tennis](/sports/table-tennis/) and [badminton](/sports/badminton/) made the
same change within a few years. Fistball is smaller, so it had less to gain
from a tidy schedule. It also lost the link between its old and new match
statistics. People still ask whether the trade was worth it.

**Indoor or outdoor.** The game has two forms, with separate world
championships, played on different fields in different seasons. Which one is
the real sport is not settled. The World Games uses the outdoor game.

**The tape for women.** Women play under a lower <Term id="tape">tape</Term>
than men, as in most net sports, and it is argued about for the same reasons
as everywhere else.

<Photo id="fistball-germany-women-2004" />

**The Olympic question.** Fistball has a century of play, real clubs and world
championships on two continents, but no likely route onto an Olympic
programme that picks sports for their global reach. Some ask whether the
campaign is worth the federation's small budget, or whether the World Games is
simply the right home.
