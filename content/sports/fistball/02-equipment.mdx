# Equipment evolution

Almost nothing, and one deliberate absence.

The ball is slightly smaller and lighter than a football and inflated hard.
Its behaviour matters more here than in most net sports, because it has to
bounce predictably off grass and off a hall floor and produce the same game on
both — a ball that died on turf or sat up off a wooden floor would change the
sport between seasons.

The striking surface is the human fist, and there is no equipment permitted to
modify it. That is worth noticing against the sport's own ancestor: *pallone
col bracciale* was played with a heavy spiked wooden cylinder strapped to the
forearm, an implement that turned the arm into a bat. Fistball descends from
that family and went the other way, removing the implement entirely.

The barrier between the sides is a tape, not a net — a band strung between two
posts at two metres, with open air above it. This is not a cost-saving
simplification; it changes the game. A volleyball net is a visual and physical
wall, which is why blocking exists and why the sport is played in the narrow
band immediately above it. A tape leaves the sightline open, so play happens
in the depth of the field rather than at the barrier, and there is nothing to
block against.

Otherwise: boots for grass, court shoes indoors, and no protective equipment
at all, because there is no diving defence to protect against and no contact.
