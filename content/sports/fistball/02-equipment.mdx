# A ball, a tape and a fist

Fistball needs almost nothing, and what it leaves out shapes the game.

The ball is a little smaller and lighter than a football, and pumped hard. It
has to bounce the same way off grass in summer and off a hall floor in winter,
so that both seasons give the same game.

The only thing that hits the ball is the player's arm. No equipment may cover
the fist. The sport's ancestor went the other way: in *pallone col bracciale*
a heavy spiked wooden cylinder on the forearm turned the arm into a bat.
Fistball kept the ball and dropped the implement.

<Photos ids="fistball-bracciale-object fistball-bracciale-treia" />

Between the two sides there is a <Term id="tape">tape</Term>, not a net: a
band between two posts, two metres up, with open air above it. A
[volleyball](/sports/volleyball/) net is a wall, so volleyball has blocking
and much of its play happens just above the net. A tape leaves the view open.
Fistball is played in the depth of the field, and there is nothing to block
against.

<Photo id="fistball-tape-serve" />

Players wear boots on grass and court shoes indoors. There is no protective
kit, because there is no contact and no diving defence.
