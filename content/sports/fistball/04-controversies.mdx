# Controversies that forced rule changes

Fistball has had a quiet legislative history, and its two significant changes
both came from outside the sport.

**Rally scoring, in 2000.** The broadcast wave that rewrote volleyball in
1999, table tennis in 2001 and badminton in 2006 caught fistball too. The
stated reason was the same everywhere: a match under service-based scoring has
no predictable length. The consequence was the same everywhere too — the
sport's own match statistics stopped being comparable across the change, and
in fistball's case nobody had compiled them on either side.

**Reconciling two continents.** The 1960 federation had to settle differences
between European and South American codes that had developed apart for
decades. This was a negotiation rather than a controversy, but it is the moment
the sport chose which of its variants was the sport.

**The tape height and the women's game.** Fistball sets a lower tape for
women's competition, which is standard across net sports and periodically
argued in all of them. The argument here is the usual one and has not produced
a change.

What fistball has not had is a safety controversy, an equipment arms race or a
doping scandal of any scale. A sport with no contact, no implement and no
professional money has had comparatively little to legislate about — which
makes it a useful control case against the sports on this site that have had
all three.
