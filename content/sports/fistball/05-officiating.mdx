# Officiating

The referee's difficult call in fistball is one that does not exist in any
other net sport: how many times has the ball touched the ground?

The ball may bounce once before each contact. So a legal sequence can be
bounce, touch, bounce, touch, bounce, touch — six events, alternating, at
speed, across fifty metres, with five players from each side moving through
them. A second bounce before a touch ends the rally, and separating "bounced
then hit" from "hit then bounced" at the margin is the sport's characteristic
judgement.

The second call is the contact itself. One closed fist or one forearm is legal;
an open hand, two hands, or any other part of the body is not. At speed, a
forearm contact and a heel-of-the-hand contact look very similar, and the
distinction decides the rally.

Beyond those: touches of the tape, crossing the centre line, the number of
contacts per side, and whether a ball landed in or out on a large grass field
where the lines are marked rather than painted on a hard surface.

There is no video review, no ball-tracking and no sensor of any kind. A sport
whose central call is the number and order of ground contacts could be
instrumented more usefully than most and has not been, for the reason that
runs through every small federation on this site: nobody can pay for it.
