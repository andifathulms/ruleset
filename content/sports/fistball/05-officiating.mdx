# Who makes the calls

The hardest call in fistball is one no other net game has: how many times has
the ball touched the ground?

The ball may <Term id="bounce">bounce</Term> once before each
<Term id="touch">touch</Term>. So a legal sequence can be bounce, touch,
bounce, touch, bounce, touch: six events in a row, at speed, with players
moving through them. A second bounce before a touch ends the <Term id="rally">rally</Term>. At the
edge, the referee has to tell "bounced, then hit" from "hit, then bounced".

<Photo id="fistball-full-stretch" />

The second call is the contact itself. One closed
<Term id="fist-or-forearm">fist or one forearm</Term> is legal. An open hand, two hands or any other part of the body is not. At speed
a forearm and the heel of the hand look much alike, and the difference decides
the rally.

<Photo id="fistball-forearm-set" />

The referee also watches for touches of the tape, players crossing the centre
line, the number of touches each side takes, and whether a ball landed in or
out on a large grass field.

There is no video review and no ball tracking. Every call is made by eye.
