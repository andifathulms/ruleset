# Where it came from

Fistball is very old, and its written rules are the young part.

Across Europe, people played ball games for centuries in which the ball was
struck with the arm and allowed to <Term id="bounce">bounce</Term>. In Italy
the biggest of them was *pallone col bracciale*. Players wore a spiked wooden
cylinder on the forearm and hit the ball with it. Fano, 1779: the game is
played in a town square, with people watching from the windows. By the
nineteenth century it was a professional sport, with arenas built for it.

<Photos ids="fistball-fano-1779 fistball-sferisterio-cascine" />

Antonio Scaino's treatise of 1555 is usually named as the first written rules
for a game of this family. German *Faustball*, the sport played today, belongs
to the same line.

<Photos ids="fistball-scaino-1555 fistball-scaino-bracciale" />

## The gymnastics clubs

The modern game was written down in the German gymnastics movement in the late
nineteenth century, and its rules were published in the 1890s. Two of them
were old practice put on paper. The ball [may bounce once before each
touch](#fistball-one-bounce), as it always had in games on grass. And it may be
hit [only with the fist or forearm](#fistball-fist-or-forearm), which is where
the name comes from.

The sport kept the marks of a game that grew up outdoors. It is played on
grass, on a long field, with a <Term id="tape">tape</Term> instead of a net,
and with a ball that is allowed to land.

## Two continents, one rulebook

German-speaking emigrants carried fistball to Brazil, Argentina, Chile and
what is now Namibia. There it grew for decades with no contact with the
European game, and the rules drifted apart. In 1960 the International Fistball
Association was formed, and its [first job](#fistball-1960-ifa) was to bring
the European and South American codes into one rulebook: the field, the
height of the tape, the number of touches and the scoring.

<Photo id="fistball-interclubes-2015" />

South America has been a full partner ever since. Brazil, Argentina and Chile
all play for world titles.

<Photo id="fistball-switzerland-argentina-2005" />

Fistball has never been on the Olympic programme. It is on the programme of
the World Games.
