# One body for three games

Cue sports are represented to the Olympic movement by a confederation that
runs none of their biggest events.

The international system recognises one federation for each sport. Carom, pool
and snooker each had a federation, and none was large enough alone to make an
Olympic case. So [the World Confederation of Billiards Sports was formed above
all three in 1992](#billiards-1992-wcbs), to hold recognition and seek a place
at the Games. [Boules](/sports/boules/) had built the same kind of body for
the same reason seven years earlier.

[Recognition by the International Olympic Committee came in
1998](#billiards-1998-ioc-recognition). Since then billiards has been
considered for several Games and chosen for none.

Two things stand in the way.

The first is that the three games are not one sport. A carom player and a
snooker player use different tables, different balls and different cues, and
they share no competition.

The second is that snooker's top level belongs to someone else. Its world
championship, its ranking tour, its money and its television audience sit with
the professional body [formed in 1968](#billiards-1968-wpbsa). Any Olympic
plan would have to bring the two together, and nobody has proposed how.

After nearly thirty years of recognition without a place, people inside the
