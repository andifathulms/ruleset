# Governing body politics

The confederation holding billiards' Olympic recognition does not run its
biggest competition, and the codes it represents cannot play each other.

The **World Confederation of Billiards Sports** was formed in 1992 above the
three codes for an explicitly institutional purpose: the international system
recognises one federation per sport, and none of carom, pool or snooker was
large enough alone to make an Olympic case. Recognition duly arrived in 1998.

Since then, nothing. Billiards has been considered for several editions and
selected for none.

Two structural problems stand in the way, and neither is about the games.

**The codes are not one sport.** A carom player and a snooker player have
different tables, different balls, different cues and no common competition.
Presenting them as a single entry is a legal fiction the confederation
maintains because the alternative is three federations that are each too small.

**The largest code's professional structure is elsewhere.** Snooker's world
championship, its ranking tour, its money and its broadcast presence belong to
a professional body formed in 1968. This site has recorded the same shape in
sumo, where a national professional association governs the best wrestlers, and
in muaythai — but here the professional body governs the sport's global elite
competition outright, not a national tradition.

So the recognised federation represents a sport whose summit it does not
administer, on behalf of three games that are not interchangeable, and has done
so for nearly thirty years.
