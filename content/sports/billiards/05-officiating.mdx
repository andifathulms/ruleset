# The referee at the table

A snooker referee touches the balls more often than most officials touch
anything.

The referee places the balls, cleans them, fetches the cue ball, hands the
player a <Term id="rest">rest</Term> and calls out the score. The work goes on
through the whole match.

<Photo id="billiards-referee" />

Most <Term id="foul">fouls</Term> are plain facts. The player hit the wrong
ball first, potted the cue ball, failed to reach a cushion, or touched a ball
with something other than the tip. The referee sees these and calls them.

## The miss

One call is different. A player who is
<Term id="snookered">snookered</Term> may fail to hit the ball the rules
require. If the referee decides the player did not make a real attempt, the
referee calls a <Term id="miss">miss</Term>, and the shot is played again from
where the balls were.

This is a judgement about what the player was trying to do. The referee makes
it on the spot, from the difficulty of the shot and what the player is likely
to be capable of. It is the most disputed decision in the professional game.
The rule has been rewritten many times, because it decides frames.

## Carom

Carom asks the referee simpler questions. Did the cue ball touch both
<Term id="object-ball">object balls</Term>? Did it reach three cushions first?
The answers are facts, and the hard part is seeing them at speed.
