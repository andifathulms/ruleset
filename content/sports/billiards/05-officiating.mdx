# Officiating

A referee who handles the equipment more than the players do, and one call that
judges what somebody meant.

**The routine work** is unusually hands-on. A snooker referee places balls,
cleans them, retrieves the cue ball, hands a player the rest, and announces the
score — an official participating in the mechanics of the game continuously,
which almost nothing else on this site does.

**Fouls** are largely factual: hitting the wrong ball first, potting the cue
ball, failing to reach a cushion, touching a ball with anything but the tip.
These are observed rather than judged.

**The miss** is the exception and the sport's hardest call. In snooker, a player
who is snookered and fails to hit the required ball may have a miss called, and
the shot replayed — but only if the referee decides no genuine attempt was
made. That is an assessment of intent, made from the referee's reading of the
shot's difficulty and the player's likely capability, and it is the most
disputed decision in the professional game. Very few rules anywhere ask an
official to rule on what a competitor was trying to do.

**Carom** is comparatively simple to officiate: did the cue ball touch both
object balls, and did it reach three cushions first. The difficulty is
observational rather than interpretive, and at speed.

Professional snooker uses video review for touching balls and for close
contacts. Neither carom nor most pool competition is instrumented.
