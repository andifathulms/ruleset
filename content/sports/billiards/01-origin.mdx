# Origin and invention

All of these games are a lawn game that was brought indoors and then diverged
for five hundred years.

Ground billiards was played outdoors in medieval Europe with balls pushed by
maces towards a hoop and a target peg. Moving it onto a table in the fifteenth
century kept the green cloth — the lawn — and the raised edges that became
cushions, and over time the mace gave way to the cue, the hoop and peg
disappeared, and the game split.

**Carom** kept the table without pockets. Its scoring is pure geometry: make
the cue ball touch both object balls in one stroke, and in three-cushion, touch
the cushions three times first. Nothing is ever removed from the table, which
makes it the only cue game where the arrangement of balls only ever becomes
more difficult.

**Pool** and **snooker** put pockets in and made removal the object. Snooker was
devised by British Army officers in India in the 1870s, adding coloured balls
to the existing game of pyramids, and it acquired the twelve-foot table and the
tight pockets that make it what it is.

The institutional history is much later and it fragmented in the other
direction. Carom's international federation dates from 1959. Snooker's
professional players formed their own body in 1968, separate from the amateur
structures, and it has run the sport's world championship ever since.

The World Confederation of Billiards Sports was built above all three in 1992
for a single purpose: to hold recognition and pursue the Olympic programme. It
obtained recognition in 1998 and has never been selected.
