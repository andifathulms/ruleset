# A lawn game brought indoors

Carom, pool and snooker all come from one outdoor game. The green cloth on the
table is what is left of the grass.

<Photo id="billiards-ground-1480" />

Ground billiards was played on lawns in medieval Europe. Players pushed balls
along the ground with a mace, a stick with a flat head, toward a hoop and a
target peg. In the fifteenth century the game moved onto a table. The table
kept the green of the lawn, and its raised edges became
<Term id="cushion">cushions</Term>.

<Photo id="billiards-mace-1730" />

Over time the <Term id="cue">cue</Term> took the place of the mace, the hoop
and the peg disappeared, and the game split in two directions.

## Without pockets, and with them

Carom kept the table without <Term id="pocket">pockets</Term>. A point is
scored when the <Term id="cue-ball">cue ball</Term> touches both of the other
balls in one stroke. In <Term id="three-cushion">three-cushion</Term> it must
touch the cushions three times as well. No ball ever leaves the table.

Pool and snooker put pockets in, and the aim became to remove the balls.
Snooker was devised by British Army officers in India in the 1870s. They added
coloured balls to an existing game called pyramids. Snooker later took on the
twelve-foot table and the tight pockets that it is known for.

## Who took charge

Organisation came much later, one game at a time. [Carom's international
federation dates from 1959](#billiards-1959-umb), when a game played with
national variants across continental Europe, Japan and Latin America was given
one code. [Snooker's professional players formed their own body in
1968](#billiards-1968-wpbsa), apart from the amateur federations. It has run
the world championship ever since.
