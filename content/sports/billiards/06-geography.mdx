# Where it is played

Three codes, three maps, and almost no overlap.

**Carom** belongs to continental Europe, East Asia and Latin America.
**Belgium**, **the Netherlands**, **Spain**, **Turkey**, **Sweden**, **South
Korea**, **Japan**, **Vietnam**, **Colombia** and **Mexico** are the powers, and
three-cushion is a substantial spectator sport in South Korea and Vietnam in
particular — which surprises people who have only met billiards in
English-speaking countries, where carom is essentially unknown.

**Snooker** is British in origin and increasingly Chinese in gravity.
**England** produced the professional structure and most of its history;
**China** now produces a very large share of the sport's growth, its audience
and, increasingly, its players. **Northern Ireland**, **Scotland**, **Wales**,
**Australia**, **Thailand**, **India**, **Iran** and **Belgium** all contribute.

**Pool** is strongest in **the United States**, **the Philippines**,
**Chinese Taipei**, **China**, **Germany**, **Poland** and **the United
Kingdom**, and the Philippines in particular has produced world champions far
out of proportion to its size.

The pattern is that each code took root where its table went. Carom tables
travelled with continental European trade and administration; snooker tables
followed the British Army and the Commonwealth; pool tables followed American
commercial culture into bars everywhere.

That is why the confederation's problem is geographic as well as technical. It
represents three sports whose strongholds barely overlap, and no country is
prominent in all three.
