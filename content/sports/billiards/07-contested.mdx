# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the umbrella should exist.** Three codes with three tables, no
crossover between them, and one confederation formed in 1992 to hold
recognition on their behalf. It has produced recognition and no programme
place in nearly thirty years, and whether the construction is worth
maintaining is a fair question inside the sport.

**Snooker's professional structure.** The sport's elite competition, ranking
system and money belong to a body outside the recognised confederation. Any
Olympic proposition would have to reconcile that, and nobody has proposed how.

**The miss rule.** Snooker's most disputed regulation asks a referee to judge
intent. It has been rewritten repeatedly and continues to decide matches.

**Time.** Long formats are the sport at its best and are difficult to schedule.
Shortened events with shot clocks have grown, and whether they are the future
or a sideshow is argued in both snooker and pool.

**Integrity.** Heavy betting markets on one-against-one matches produce
match-fixing risk, and professional cue sports have had cases and bans.

**Where snooker's centre of gravity should be.** The sport's growth, audience
and increasingly its players are in China while its institutions remain
British. That is a live question about power rather than about rules, and it is
being answered gradually by where the events are held.
