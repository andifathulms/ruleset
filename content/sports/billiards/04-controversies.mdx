# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The miss rule.** Snooker's most argued regulation and one of very few rules
on this site that adjudicates *intent*. A player who fails to hit the required
ball may have a **miss** called against them, and the shot replayed from the
original position, if the referee judges that a genuine attempt was not made.
That is a judgement about what somebody was trying to do, made live, and it has
been rewritten repeatedly because it decides frames.

**Time.** A snooker frame can run an hour and a world final over two days,
which is a scheduling problem no broadcaster loves. The sport's answer has been
shortened formats with shot clocks rather than changing the main event, which
is a gentler version of the rewrites this site records in badminton, volleyball,
table tennis and fistball.

**Ivory.** Balls were made from it until composites arrived in the nineteenth
century — one of the earliest material substitutions on this site driven by
supply rather than performance.

**Integrity.** Cue sports have had match-fixing cases, and professional
snooker in particular has suspended and banned players. A one-against-one sport
with heavy betting markets and long matches has the conditions for it, and the
professional body's disciplinary apparatus exists largely because of it.

**Whether the umbrella is real.** Not a rule change and the confederation's
permanent question: three codes, three tables, no crossover, and one entity
representing them for the purpose of an Olympic application that has not
succeeded in nearly thirty years.
