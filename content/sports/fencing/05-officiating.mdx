# What the machine cannot decide

A fencing bout has one referee, and the referee no longer decides whether a
hit landed. The machine does that. The four side judges who used to do it no
longer exist. [Tennis](/sports/tennis/) lost its line judges in the same way,
long afterwards.

Fencing still needs a human judgement, because of what the machine left
behind.

## Whose hit was it

In foil and sabre both fencers often hit. The point goes to the one who had
right of way: the fencer whose attack was correctly formed and started first.
The machine can say that both blades landed. It cannot say whose attack it
was, because that is a judgement about the shape of a whole action and not
about one instant. The handball law in [football](/sports/football/) asks a
question of the same kind, and cameras have not settled that either.

So the modern referee has one job, and it is the hardest one in the sport. The
decision often decides a medal.

## Video

A fencer may appeal to video a limited number of times. The replay shows the
referee's own call again, to the referee and a video consultant. It is a
second opinion on a judgement. It measures nothing.

## The setting

The machine also makes one decision that looks like a referee's. Its timing
decides whether two hits close together count as one hit or as a
<Term id="double-hit">double hit</Term>. The federation sets that timing. It
is a rule with no words, applied by equipment.
