# The circuit that sees the hit

A fencer today carries a blade, a mask, a
<Term id="conductive-jacket">conductive jacket</Term> and a
<Term id="body-wire">body wire</Term>, and is plugged into a
<Term id="scoring-machine">scoring machine</Term>. The machine decides whether
a <Term id="hit">hit</Term> landed. For most of the sport's history, people
decided that.

<Photo id="fencing-ayat-fonst-1900" />

## Five people watching

A bout used to be run by a president and four judges. A hit existed when they
agreed that they had seen one. A light touch at full speed is very hard to
see, and the panels were drawn from the nations that were competing. The
complaint that judges favoured their own countries never went away.

## One weapon at a time

The federation's answer was to take the question away from the judges. It took
more than fifty years, because each weapon was a harder engineering problem
than the last.

[Épée came first, in 1936](#fie-1936-electric-epee). The whole body is the
target and there is no right of way, so the apparatus only has to register a
point landing anywhere. Épée also needed it most: two hits a few hundredths of
a second apart could not be separated by eye.

[Foil followed in 1957](#fie-1957-electric-foil). Its target is only the
torso, so the machine had to tell the torso from an arm. That needed a
conductive jacket as well as a machine.

[Sabre was last. The change was adopted in 1988 and applied from
1989](#fie-1988-electric-sabre). A sabre scores with its edge, so the
apparatus had to register contact anywhere along the blade, on a jacket
covering everything above the waist.

For twenty-one years fencing had one weapon scored by a circuit and two scored
by eye. Each time a weapon was wired, the fencing in that weapon changed.

## A number inside the box

The machine has a setting that no spectator sees. It is the length of time
after one fencer's hit during which the machine will still register the
other's. The federation chooses that number.
[In 2005 it changed the number for sabre](#fie-2005-sabre-timing), and the
weapon's leading style stopped working within a season. No rule about what a
fencer may do was touched.
