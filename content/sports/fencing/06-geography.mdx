# Where it is played

Fencing's map is Europe plus a small number of deliberate outliers, and the
outliers are the interesting part.

Italy, France, Hungary and Russia have dominated for a century, and the reason
is institutional continuity: fencing academies, military and civilian, that
have run without interruption for generations. Germany, Poland, Ukraine and
Romania add depth. This is one of the oldest and most stable competitive maps
on this site.

The additions are South Korea, China, Japan and the United States, and each
arrived by a decision rather than by tradition. South Korea in particular
identified fencing as a sport where investment could produce medals against a
concentrated European field, built a programme around sabre, and became a
leading nation within about two decades. That is a strategy this site has seen
before — Britain's track cycling programme is the closest parallel.

Egypt and several other African and Middle Eastern nations have produced world
medallists in recent years from very small bases.

The barrier keeping the map narrow is equipment and coaching rather than
facility. A piste and a scoring machine are affordable; a coaching tradition
that knows how to teach priority is not, and it cannot be bought quickly. That
is why fencing's geography has changed by deliberate national programmes rather
than by diffusion, and why it has changed so little otherwise.
