# What is still argued about

**Right of way.** It is the last judgement in the sport that no machine makes.
One referee decides it, it often decides medals, and it is disputed in almost
the same words as before the machines came. There are proposals to define it
more tightly, to widen video review, or to add a second referee. None of them
changes the fact that it is a judgement.

**The timing setting.** The federation can reshape a weapon by changing a
number inside the scoring machine, as it did with sabre in 2005. Some argue
that a decision with that much effect should be examined as closely as a
change to the written rules. At present it is not.

**One sport or three.** Épée, foil and sabre have different rules, different
followings and different strong nations, and fencers choose one weapon early.
Every change to one weapon is argued over by the other two. The question comes
back whenever the Olympic programme is reviewed.

**Watching it.** A spectator cannot tell who scored in foil or sabre without
knowing the right-of-way rule. The sport has tried transparent masks, better
graphics and new formats, and has not solved it. The 2005 change to sabre was
partly another attempt, through shorter and clearer exchanges.
