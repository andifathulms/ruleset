# What an arbiter does

An <Term id="arbiter">arbiter</Term> decides nothing about the game. The
position on the board is a fact. Whether a king is in
<Term id="checkmate">checkmate</Term>, whether a group of stones is captured,
whether a move is legal: none of these needs a judgement.

<Photo id="board-arbiters-2012" />

The work is in the conditions around the game.

**The clock.** A player who runs out of time loses. In fast games the arbiter
must see whether a move was completed before the time ran out, by watching
two hands and a display.

**Illegal moves.** The penalty grows: first extra time for the opponent, then
loss of the game. The arbiter often has to work out what happened after the
position has already moved on.

**Cheating.** The arbiter screens players at the door, controls the playing
area, notes absences from the board and sends suspicious games for
statistical analysis. At any important event this is now a large part of the
job.

**Tie-breaks.** A tournament that ends level is settled by a
<Term id="tie-break">tie-break</Term>. The arbiter's arithmetic can decide a
title.

[Boules](/sports/boules/) and [orienteering](/sports/orienteering/) are
similar. In all three the result is a fact, and the officials are there to
police how it was produced.
