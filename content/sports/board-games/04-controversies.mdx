# The machine across the board

## New York, 1997

In May 1997 the world chess champion, Garry Kasparov, sat down in New York to
play six games against Deep Blue, a computer built by IBM. Deep Blue won the
match. No computer had beaten a reigning world champion under match
conditions before.

<Photo id="board-deep-blue" />

Kasparov disputed the result at the time. He objected to the conditions of
the match and to how open it had been. The consequences came whatever the
truth of that one match. [Since 1997 an engine has been stronger than every
human player](#board-1997-engines-surpass-chess), and the gap has only grown.

Nothing in the laws of chess changed that day. The game around the laws
changed a great deal. Players began to prepare their openings with an
<Term id="engine">engine</Term>. Analysis after a game became a matter of
fact, where before it had been a matter of argument.

## Seoul, 2016

Go was meant to be safe. Its board has 361 points, and the methods that had
worked for chess could not search so many possibilities. It held out for
nineteen years. In March 2016, in Seoul, [the program AlphaGo beat the
professional Lee Sedol by four games to
one](#board-2016-engines-surpass-go). Within a few years engine analysis had
reshaped the openings of go. Moves that generations of professionals had
called mistakes were looked at again.

## A new kind of cheating

In a physical sport, illegal help makes a competitor better. Here it replaces
the competitor. A player who is being sent an engine's moves cannot be
beaten. Once an engine could run on a device small enough to hide, several
cases at open tournaments followed.

[FIDE adopted a formal anti-cheating regime in
2014](#board-anti-cheating-regime). Devices are banned, players are screened,
broadcasts are delayed and players may not leave the board freely.
Statisticians also compare a player's moves with the engine's choices. This
method detects cheating by measuring how good the moves were. Beyond a
certain level of agreement with an engine, talent is no longer the
explanation.

## The end of adjournment

An unfinished game was once sealed and resumed the next day, and analysing
the position overnight was part of the craft. This was
<Term id="adjournment">adjournment</Term>. Engines made the overnight
analysis decisive, and the practice was abolished.

## Two older arguments

Two players can agree a result that suits them both and harms the
tournament. Arranging a result in advance is an offence, and some events ban
any offer of a <Term id="draw">draw</Term> before a set move number.

Chess has also had two world champions at once. During a long split in FIDE
in the 1990s and 2000s, two rival lines of champions ran side by side, and
for more than a decade nobody could say for certain who held the title.
