# Board, pieces and clock

A board, pieces or stones, and a clock: the equipment list is very short, and
most of it has not changed for more than a century.

Chess settled on the Staunton pattern in the nineteenth century. The point
was that a knight, a bishop and a king should be unmistakable in any country.
It was the last change the board and pieces needed.

## The clock

Before the clock, a player could think for as long as they liked. Games were
abandoned, or decided by exhaustion. With a clock, time became something to
spend. Each side of a mechanical clock has a small flag that falls when that
player's time runs out.

Different amounts of time then became different disciplines.

<Term id="rapid-blitz">rapid and blitz</Term> have separate ratings and
separate world championships, because the same player has a different
strength at each speed. An <Term id="increment">increment</Term> adds seconds
for every move.

## What may not be brought in

The longer part of the equipment rules is now a list of banned objects. No
electronic device may be carried into the playing area. Players are screened
at the door. Broadcasts of the moves are delayed, so that nobody outside can
analyse the position and send an answer back in time. Almost everything on
that list was invented in the last thirty years.
