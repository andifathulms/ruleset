# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Cheating.** The sport's permanent problem now that a phone plays better than
any human. Detection is statistical, intrusive and imperfect, accusations are
extremely damaging whether or not they are upheld, and the highest-profile
disputes have ended in litigation rather than in a sporting finding.

**Whether the online game is the game.** Most rated play now happens on
commercial platforms that run their own events, ratings and anti-cheating
systems. The federations govern a shrinking share of the activity and have no
mechanism for changing that.

**Rating inflation.** The sport's own measuring instrument drifts between eras
and the adjustments are argued about. It is the closest thing chess has to a
comparability break and the sport has not settled it.

**Whether these are sports.** Chess and bridge both hold IOC recognition and
neither has a programme place, and the question of whether an activity with no
physical component qualifies is answered differently by different institutions
— including, for bridge, by a court.

**Classical against fast.** Rapid and blitz are more watchable, more online and
more commercially viable; classical is where the depth is. Which represents the
sport is a live argument, and the answer the audience gives is not the one
tradition prefers.

**Go's professional systems.** The best go players are produced by national
professional structures in three countries, and international federation events
are secondary to those titles. Whether that is sustainable as the game spreads
is not the federations' decision to make.
