# What is still argued about

**Cheating.** A phone now plays better than any human. Detection is
statistical, intrusive and imperfect. An accusation does great damage whether
or not it is upheld, and the best-known disputes have ended in court without
a sporting verdict.

**The online game.** Most rated play now happens on commercial platforms.
They run their own events, their own ratings and their own anti-cheating
systems, and they have more rated players than every federation combined.
The federations govern a shrinking share of the game.

**Ratings across eras.** A <Term id="rating">rating</Term> is measured
against other players, and the pool of players keeps changing. Whether 2700
in 1980 means the same strength as 2700 today is a real argument. The formula
has been adjusted more than once.

**Whether these are sports.** Chess and [bridge](/sports/bridge/) are both
recognised by the IOC, and neither has a place on the Olympic programme.
Institutions answer differently when asked if an activity with no physical
part is a sport. For bridge, a court has been one of them.

**Classical or fast.** Rapid and blitz are easier to watch, suit online play
and earn more. Classical chess is where the depth is. Which one stands for
the sport is disputed.

**Go's professional systems.** The best go players come from national
systems in three countries, and the international federation's events rank
below those national titles. The go and xiangqi federations are smaller and
quieter than FIDE.
