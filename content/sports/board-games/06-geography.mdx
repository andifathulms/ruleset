# Where it is played

Three games, three maps, and one of them is genuinely global.

**Chess** is the most widely played competitive game in the world with any
claim to being a sport. Its historic strength is Russian and post-Soviet —
Russia, Armenia, Ukraine, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Uzbekistan far above their
size — with deep structures in the United States, China, India, Iran and across
Europe. **India** is the fastest-rising, on a scale that has changed the sport's
demographics, and **China** has been a leading nation in both the men's and
women's game for two decades.

**Go** is East Asian, professionally speaking, and almost exclusively so.
**China**, **Japan** and **South Korea** have full professional systems that
take children full-time from a young age, and the best players in the world are
produced by them. Everywhere else the game is amateur.

**Xiangqi** is Chinese and follows the Chinese diaspora — Vietnam in
particular, where it is deeply established, along with Chinese communities
across Southeast Asia and beyond.

The distinctive geographic fact about chess in the last twenty years is that it
stopped depending on geography at all. Online platforms mean a player anywhere
with a connection can play rated games against opponents at their exact
strength, at any hour, and much of the world's competitive chess is now played
by people who have never entered a tournament hall.

That is not true of go or xiangqi to nearly the same extent, and it is not true
of any other sport covered on this site.
