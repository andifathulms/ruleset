# Origin and invention

Duathlon is a workaround that became a sport.

Triathlon boomed through the late 1970s and 1980s and ran into two constraints
almost immediately. The first was athletes: a great many people who could run
and ride could not swim well enough to race in a mass open-water start, and
they were not going to learn as adults. The second was venues: organising a
safe open-water swim needs suitable water, lifeguards and a risk appetite that
many organisers did not have.

Run-bike-run events were the answer to both, and they spread through the 1980s
under a variety of names — biathlon among them, until the winter sport's claim
on that word made it untenable.

The International Triathlon Union was founded in 1989, largely to pursue
Olympic inclusion for triathlon, and inherited the multisport formats that had
grown up alongside: duathlon, aquathlon, aquabike, cross and winter variants.
World duathlon championships have been held since 1990.

What the format turned out to be is not simply a smaller triathlon. Removing
the swim removed the barrier, and it also changed which discipline decides the
race — because the final run now begins on legs that have already run hard,
with a bike leg in between that does not let them recover. Elite duathletes
treat the second run as a different event from the first.

Duathlon has a World Games place and world championships and no Olympic
prospect, for a reason that has nothing to do with the sport: its own federation
already has an Olympic event, and it is the one with the swim in it.
