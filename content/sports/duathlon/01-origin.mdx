# Where it came from

Duathlon began as a way around a problem, and became a sport of its own.

[Triathlon](/sports/triathlon/) grew quickly through the late 1970s and the
1980s and soon met two limits. Many people who could run and ride could not
swim well enough to race in a crowded start in open water. And many
organisers did not have suitable water, lifeguards, or the wish to take the
risk.

Races of a run, a ride and a run answered both. They spread through the 1980s
under several names. One was biathlon, until the winter sport's older claim
on that word ruled it out.

## A federation named after another sport

[The International Triathlon Union was founded in
1989](#duathlon-1989-federation), mainly to get triathlon into the Olympic
Games. It took charge of the other formats that had grown up beside
triathlon: duathlon, aquathlon, aquabike, cross and winter races. Duathlon is
the largest of them. [World championships in duathlon have been held since
1990](#duathlon-run-bike-run). The federation is now called World Triathlon.

<Photo id="duathlon-ottawa-2013" />

Triathlon reached the Games at Sydney in 2000. Duathlon has world
championships and a place at the World Games, and no path to the Olympics.
The case for adding it would have to be made by a federation whose main sport
is already there.

Its rules follow triathlon's closely. That lets athletes move between the two
and lets the same officials work at both.

## What the missing swim changed

Taking out the swim removed the barrier that keeps most people out. It also
changed which <Term id="leg">leg</Term> decides the race. The last run starts
on legs that have already run hard, and the ride in between does not let them
recover. Elite duathletes treat the second run as a different event from the
first.
