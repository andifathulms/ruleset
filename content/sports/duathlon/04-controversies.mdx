# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Drafting.** The sport's defining split. Elite short-course racing became
draft-legal in the mid-1990s, following triathlon, because a non-drafting bike
leg spread the field out and produced a race that could not be followed.
Long-course and age-group racing stayed non-drafting. The result is two sports
with different bicycles, different tactics and incomparable results, held under
one name — and the argument about whether elite duathlon needed to follow
triathlon into it has never quite gone away.

**Policing drafting where it is banned.** In non-drafting racing, officials on
motorcycles judge whether a rider is inside a defined distance and whether an
overtake was completed in the allowed time. It is a judgement about position
and elapsed time made at speed on open roads, and it is the most disputed call
in the sport.

**Transition rules.** Mounting before the line, dismounting after it, helmet
fastened before touching the bike, bikes racked in an assigned position — a
detailed body of regulation about the act of changing equipment, tightened over
time because the transitions are timed and therefore raced.

**Penalties rather than disqualification.** Time penalties served in a box
during the race keep a competitor in the event and make the sanction visible.
It is a gentler enforcement model than most measured sports use and it was
adopted deliberately.

**What to call it.** The format was widely called biathlon until the winter
sport's prior claim made that impossible — a naming problem that delayed the
sport's coherent identity by years.
