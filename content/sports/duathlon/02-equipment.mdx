# Shoes, a bicycle and a helmet

A duathlete needs running shoes, a bicycle and a helmet. Which bicycle
depends on the kind of race.

In a <Term id="draft-legal">draft-legal</Term> race the riders are in a
bunch, and they must use road bicycles.
<Term id="time-trial-bars">Time-trial bars</Term> are restricted, because
they put the rider's weight forward and the brakes out of reach. That is
dangerous in a close group. A <Term id="non-drafting">non-drafting</Term>
race allows the full time-trial bicycle: deep wheels, the bars and a low
position.

<Photo id="duathlon-time-trial-bars" />

An athlete who races both kinds owns two bicycles that are hardly the same
machine.

## The helmet

The rule about the helmet is a rule about order. It must be fastened before
the athlete touches the bicycle. It may be unfastened only after the bicycle
is back on the <Term id="rack">rack</Term>.

## The change

The <Term id="transition">transition</Term> is a skill that athletes train.
They leave their shoes open and ready. They rack the bicycle in its assigned
place. Some hold their cycling shoes level on the pedals with elastic bands.
Every second spent here counts in the race time.

<Photo id="duathlon-transition-2012" />

Duathlon needs none of the equipment of open-water swimming: no wetsuit and
no goggles. Taking out the swim took away a large part of the cost of
[triathlon](/sports/triathlon/).
