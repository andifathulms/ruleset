# Equipment evolution

Two disciplines' worth of equipment, and a transition governed in more detail
than either of them.

**Running shoes** and **a bicycle**, and the bicycle is where the sport's split
personality shows. Draft-legal racing requires road bikes with restrictions on
aerodynamic extensions, because a bunch riding in close proximity on time-trial
bars — with the rider's weight forward and the brakes out of reach — is
genuinely dangerous. Non-drafting racing permits the full time-trial setup:
deep wheels, aero bars, aggressive position.

So a duathlete competing across both formats owns two bicycles that are barely
the same machine, which is the plainest evidence that the formats are separate
sports.

**The helmet** is the sport's most tightly regulated item, and the rule is about
sequence rather than specification: it must be fastened before the bike is
touched and unfastened only after it is racked. That is a rule about the order
of actions, which very few sports on this site have.

**The transition** itself is equipment-adjacent and is the sport's hidden
technical discipline. Shoes left open, bikes racked in an assigned position,
elastic bands holding cycling shoes level — these are trained and practised,
and the seconds involved are inside the race time.

What duathlon does not need is the thing triathlon spends most on: a wetsuit,
goggles and the whole apparatus of open-water swimming. Removing the swim
removed a substantial share of the sport's equipment cost along with its entry
barrier.
