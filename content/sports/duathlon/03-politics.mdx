# Governing body politics

Duathlon's institutional position is settled and nobody involved pretends
otherwise: it is the second sport in a federation that already has an Olympic
one.

World Triathlon — founded in 1989 as the International Triathlon Union — was
built to get triathlon onto the Olympic programme, which it achieved for Sydney
2000. It governs duathlon, aquathlon, aquabike, cross and winter formats
alongside, and duathlon is the largest of them.

That produces a specific kind of stasis. Duathlon has world championships, a
World Games place, a real age-group participation base and a modest elite
circuit. It has no realistic route to a Games, because the argument for adding
it would have to be made by a federation whose flagship is already there, in a
category the IOC would reasonably say is covered.

The federation's decisions reflect the priority sensibly rather than
neglectfully. Duathlon's rules track triathlon's — the draft-legal short course
format, the penalty box, the transition regulations — because keeping them
aligned lets athletes move between the formats and lets one officiating corps
work both.

Where duathlon has independent life is at age-group level, and it is
substantial: a large population of amateur athletes for whom the absence of a
swim is the difference between competing and not. That constituency is bigger
than the elite one by orders of magnitude, and it is the sport's actual reason
for existing.

There is one recurring internal argument: whether elite duathlon should be
draft-legal at all, given that the format was adopted to make triathlon
televisable and duathlon is not televised much either way.
