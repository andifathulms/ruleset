# Where it is played

Europe, decisively, and a much wider amateur base than the elite results
suggest.

**Belgium**, **the Netherlands**, **Spain**, **France**, **Great Britain**,
**Germany**, **Italy** and **Switzerland** carry most of duathlon's elite
competition, and Belgium and the Netherlands in particular have a depth in the
sport out of proportion to their size — a product of dense cycling culture,
flat road racing terrain and strong club structures.

**Powerman** long-course racing, centred on Switzerland, is the discipline's
best-established professional circuit and has been for decades.

Beyond Europe, **the United States**, **Australia**, **Canada**, **Japan**,
**South Africa** and increasingly **Latin America** compete, and duathlon's
age-group world championships draw genuinely international fields.

The interesting pattern is where duathlon is strong *relative to* triathlon.
It does disproportionately well in countries with serious road cycling cultures
and less open-water swimming tradition, and in inland regions with no
accessible safe water. That is exactly what the format was invented for, and
the map still shows it.

At amateur level the sport's footprint is far larger than its elite profile,
because it is the multisport event that people who cannot swim can enter. A
great deal of duathlon happens in club races that no federation counts.
