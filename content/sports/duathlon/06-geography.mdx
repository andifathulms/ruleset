# Where it is raced

Duathlon is strongest in Europe, and strongest of all where people ride
bicycles and have nowhere to swim.

Belgium, the Netherlands, Spain, France, Great Britain, Germany, Italy and
Switzerland provide most of the elite field. Belgium and the Netherlands have
more good duathletes than their size would suggest. Both have a strong
[cycling](/sports/cycling/) culture, flat roads for racing and strong clubs.

The best-established professional circuit is Powerman, a
<Term id="long-course">long-course</Term> series centred on Switzerland. Its
best-known race, at Zofingen, hosts the long-distance world championships.

<Photo id="duathlon-zofingen-2017" />

Outside Europe, the United States, Australia, Canada, Japan and South Africa
compete, and Latin America increasingly does. The world championships for
amateurs draw athletes from many countries.

Compared with [triathlon](/sports/triathlon/), duathlon does best in
countries with serious road cycling and little tradition of open-water
swimming, and in inland regions with no safe water nearby. The format was
invented for exactly those places.

The amateur sport is much larger than the elite results suggest. Duathlon is
the multisport race that people who cannot swim can enter, and much of it
happens in club races that no federation counts.
