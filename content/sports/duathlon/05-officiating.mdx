# Officiating

Most of duathlon needs no officiating at all, and the part that does is
extremely difficult.

**The clock** decides the race and is not in dispute. Chip timing records the
start, the transitions and the finish, and the result is a fact.

**Drafting** is where the officials are, and only in the formats that prohibit
it. Motorcycle-mounted officials judge whether a rider is within a defined
distance of the competitor in front and, if so, whether they are completing an
overtake inside the allowed time. That is a judgement about distance and
elapsed seconds, made from a moving motorcycle, on open roads, in a field of
riders who are all trying to sit as close to legal as possible.

It is the single most contested call in the sport, and no technology has
replaced it — proposals for automated proximity detection recur and none is in
general use.

**The transition area** is the other officiated space. Helmet fastened before
the bike is touched and unfastened after it is racked; the bike racked in its
assigned position; no riding inside the area; mounting and dismounting on the
correct side of the line. These are bright-line rules, easily seen, and
frequently broken by athletes in a hurry.

Penalties are time penalties served in a box during the race rather than
disqualifications, which is unusual: the competitor stays in the event, the
sanction is visible to spectators, and the race carries on.
