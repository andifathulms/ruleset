# Who makes the calls

Most of a duathlon needs no judging. The clock decides it. A timing chip
records each athlete's start, both transitions and the finish.

## The gap between two riders

The hard call is <Term id="drafting">drafting</Term>, in the races that
forbid it. Officials ride motorcycles beside the field. They judge whether a
rider is inside the set distance behind the rider in front. If so, they judge
whether the rider is completing a pass within the allowed time. They do this
from a moving motorcycle, on open roads, while every rider tries to sit as
close as the rule permits.

It is the most disputed call in the sport. Automatic systems to measure the
gap have been proposed many times, and none is in general use.

## The transition area

The other place officials watch is the
<Term id="transition">transition</Term>. The rules here are clear lines. The
helmet is fastened before the bicycle is touched and unfastened after it is
racked. The bicycle goes in its assigned place. Nobody rides inside the area.
Athletes mount and dismount on the correct side of the
<Term id="mount-line">mount line</Term>. These rules have been tightened over
time, because the change is timed and so athletes race it. They are easy to
see, and athletes in a hurry break them often.

## The penalty box

An athlete who breaks a rule is usually not disqualified. The athlete serves
a time penalty in a <Term id="penalty-box">penalty box</Term> during the
race, and then goes on. Spectators can see the punishment, and the athlete
stays in the event.
