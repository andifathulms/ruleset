# Where it is played

Badminton is the clearest case on this site of a sport whose competitive centre
and administrative centre were in different places for most of its history. It
was codified in England, and the International Badminton Federation was run from
England for seventy years. It is played, watched and won overwhelmingly in Asia.

China, Indonesia, Malaysia, South Korea, Japan, Denmark and Chinese Taipei
account for very nearly all of the medals. Denmark is the exception that makes
the pattern visible: one European nation with a deep club system has stayed
competitive for decades while the rest of Europe has not, which suggests the
concentration is about participation infrastructure rather than about anything
innate.

Two consequences show up directly in the rules.

**The hardest tournament is often a national trial.** Where a country's depth
exceeds its Olympic quota, players who would medal for another nation do not
qualify at all. That is an eligibility rule shaping the sport's competitive
geography as much as any law of play does.

**The 2012 tanking episode was a geography story too.** All four disqualified
pairs came from China, South Korea and Indonesia, because those were the nations
with two strong pairs in the same tournament and therefore the only ones with
anything to gain from manipulating the draw.

The federation moved its headquarters to Kuala Lumpur in 2005 and renamed itself
the Badminton World Federation in 2006 — the same year it changed how the sport
scores. The sources do not connect those decisions, and this site does not
either. It only notes that they happened within eighteen months of each other.
