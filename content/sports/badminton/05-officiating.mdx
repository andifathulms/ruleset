# Who makes the calls

An international match has an umpire in a raised chair, a service judge
sitting low at the side, and line judges at the boundaries. The umpire keeps
the score and calls <Term id="fault">faults</Term> and
<Term id="let">lets</Term>. The line judges call the shuttle in or out. The
service judge watches one thing only: whether each serve is legal.

<Photo id="badminton-officials" />

A player who disagrees with a line call can challenge it. A ball-tracking
system shows where the shuttle landed, and the call stands or is overturned
within seconds. Each side has a limited number of unsuccessful challenges per
game, so a player has to choose which calls are worth one.

Review settled line calls, because where a shuttle landed is a fact a camera
can see. It could not settle the serve while the legal height depended on the
server's body. That needed a new rule, and since 2018 the limit is a fixed
1.15 metres, which a fixed measuring device shows the same way for every
player.
