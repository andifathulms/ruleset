# Officiating and technology

An international match has an umpire in a raised chair, a service judge sitting
at the side watching one thing only, and line judges at the boundaries. The
service judge exists because badminton decided that one action needed a
dedicated official, which is a good indication of how much of this sport's
rulebook is about the serve.

**Instant review** arrived early here compared with most sports. Players
challenge a line call, a ball-tracking system renders the shuttle's landing
point, and the call stands or is overturned within seconds. Each side gets a
limited number of unsuccessful challenges per game.

The interesting part is what review did *not* fix. A line call is a question of
fact, and technology answers it well. A service fault was a question of
judgement — whether the shuttle was below the server's waist or lowest rib —
and no camera can settle that, because the standard itself moved with the
player. The sport solved that one by changing the rule rather than by adding
technology: since 2018 the test is a fixed 1.15 metres from the floor, which a
sensor or a fixed camera can measure identically for everyone.

That is the pattern worth noticing across this site. Technology can adjudicate a
fact. Where officials disagree, it is usually because the rule was asking them
for an opinion, and the fix is legislative rather than technical.
