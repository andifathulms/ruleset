# Three moments that changed the rules

## The night losing was the right move

London, 2012, the Olympic women's doubles. In the group matches, pairs began
serving into the net again and again, in front of a crowd that booed them.
Four pairs, from China,
South Korea and Indonesia, were disqualified from the tournament for not using
their best efforts.

They were not throwing matches for money. The tournament had a group stage
followed by a fixed knockout bracket, so a pair that finished second in its
group met a weaker opponent than a pair that finished first. Losing was the
sensible choice, and the format had made it so. All four pairs came from the
three nations with two strong pairs in the same draw, the only ones with
something to gain.

The federation changed the format as well as punishing the players. [From 2014
the knockout bracket is drawn after the group results are
known](#bwf-2014-olympic-draw-procedure), so there is no bracket to aim for.

## Scoring rewritten for the clock

Under <Term id="side-out">side-out scoring</Term> a rally could be won without
a point being scored, so nobody could say how long a match would last. That is
a problem for anyone scheduling a broadcast. The federation first tried
shorter games, [best of five to seven points, in
2002](#bwf-2002-seven-point-trial), and dropped the idea within about two
years. In 2006 it changed who could score instead.
[Rally scoring to 21](#bwf-2006-rally-scoring) is the game played now.

The federation has been unusually open about commercial reasons. In 2011 it
announced that women would have to wear a skirt or dress at top events, and
said the aim was to present the women's game better to sponsors and
broadcasters. Players and national associations objected, and [the rule was
dropped before it was ever enforced](#bwf-2011-womens-dress-code).

## The serve nobody could judge

For decades a legal serve was defined against the server's own body: the
shuttle had to be struck below the waist, or the lowest rib. A tall player and
a short player were held to different heights, and a serve passed at one
tournament could be a <Term id="fault">fault</Term> at the next. [Since 2018
the limit is 1.15 metres from the floor](#bwf-2018-fixed-service-height), the
same for everyone.
