# Governing body politics

The International Badminton Federation spent much of the late twentieth century
governing a sport whose competitive centre of gravity had moved to Asia while
its administrative centre had not. The federation's headquarters moved from
England to Kuala Lumpur in 2005, and the rename to Badminton World Federation
followed in 2006. Those two moves and the scoring change happened inside about
eighteen months of each other, which is worth noticing even though the sources
do not connect them causally.

The most consequential political fact about the BWF for this site is that it is
a rare governing body willing to state a commercial motive in plain terms. The
2011 dress regulation for women was defended publicly on presentation and
sponsorship grounds rather than dressed up as a sporting matter, and it was
withdrawn just as publicly. Most federations in this collection give a sporting
reason for a commercial change, which is exactly why the `disputed` cause exists
in this vocabulary. Badminton mostly does not need it.

Olympic status has shaped the federation's decisions more than any other single
factor. Badminton entered the programme in 1992, and the two rule changes with
the clearest triggers — rally scoring in 2006 and the Olympic draw procedure
after 2012 — are both, at root, about how the sport performs in the four hours a
Games gives it.
