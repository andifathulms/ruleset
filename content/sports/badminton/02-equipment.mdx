# Equipment evolution

Badminton is unusual in this collection: its equipment changed enormously and
almost none of it was forced by a rule.

Rackets went from wood to steel to aluminium to graphite composite over the
second half of the twentieth century, and the laws did not chase them. The Laws
of Badminton constrain frame and stringing dimensions, but the sport never had
its javelin moment — no implement was redesigned to bring the game back inside
the stadium, because the shuttle itself sets the ceiling. A feathered shuttle
decelerates so sharply that racket power has a limited effect on how far it
travels, and the equipment arms race therefore expressed itself in speed and
recovery rather than in distance.

The shuttle is the exception, and the interesting thing about it is what did not
happen. Synthetic shuttles have existed for decades and are cheaper, more
durable and more consistent than feathers. Top-tier competition still uses
sixteen goose or duck feathers set in a cork base, because the two behave
differently in flight and the sport declined to change what the game feels like.
That is a rule choice too, and it is a rule choice in the opposite direction
from most of the entries on this timeline.

The one equipment-adjacent rule that did change is the service height, and it
changed because of the human body rather than the kit: a test defined against
the server's own rib cage could not be applied identically to a player of 1.60 m
and a player of 1.95 m. Fixing it at 1.15 metres in 2018 made the measurement
independent of the person being measured.
