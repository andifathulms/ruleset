# Where it came from

Badminton began as a game nobody could lose. Battledore and shuttlecock was
played in Europe and across Asia for centuries: two people with small bats
kept a <Term id="shuttle">shuttle</Term> in the air for as long as they could,
together. There was no net, no court and no score.

<Photos ids="badminton-chardin badminton-hagoita" />

The competitive game is usually traced to British India in the 1860s and
1870s. A net went up between the players, and the aim turned around: make the
shuttle land on the other side. The name comes from Badminton House in
Gloucestershire, the Duke of Beaufort's home, where the game was played by his
household.

<Photo id="badminton-badminton-house" />

A game with no scoring had to borrow some. Early badminton took the convention
already used in other racket sports, in which only the serving side can score.
That system, <Term id="side-out">side-out scoring</Term>, lasted in principle
for about 130 years, until the sport [replaced it in
2006](#bwf-2006-rally-scoring).

The Badminton Association of England published the first widely adopted laws
in 1893. The court they describe is the court still in use. The International
Badminton Federation was founded in 1934 with nine member associations. It
moved its headquarters from England to Kuala Lumpur in 2005 and renamed itself
the Badminton World Federation in 2006.
