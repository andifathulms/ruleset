# Origin and invention

Badminton descends from battledore and shuttlecock, a cooperative game with no
net, no court and no way to lose, played in Europe and across Asia for centuries
before anyone thought to score it. The competitive form is usually traced to
British India in the 1860s and 1870s, and the name is taken from Badminton
House in Gloucestershire, where the game was played by the Duke of Beaufort's
household.

The descent matters for the same reason it matters in football. The parent game
had no scoring system at all, so the first codified badminton had to borrow one,
and it borrowed the side-out convention already in use in racket sports: only
the serving side can score. That borrowed convention survived, essentially
unaltered in principle, for roughly 130 years. When it was finally replaced in
2006 it was replaced for reasons that had nothing to do with the game and
everything to do with the clock.

The Badminton Association of England published the first widely adopted laws in
1893. The International Badminton Federation was founded in 1934 with nine
member associations, and renamed itself the Badminton World Federation in 2006 —
the same year it changed how the sport scores.
