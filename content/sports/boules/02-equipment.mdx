# Boules, jack and tape

A <Term id="boule">boule</Term> was first wood, then wood covered with nails,
then steel.

Traditional boules were turned from boxwood root. Through the nineteenth
century makers hammered nails into the surface in patterns. These *boules
cloutées* were heavier and lasted longer, and the patterns were decorative.
Making them was a craft trade.

<Photo id="boules-studded-boule" />

Steel boules arrived in the 1920s. A competition boule today is hollow steel.
It weighs 650 to 800 grams and is 70.5 to 80 millimetres across. The rules set
those limits and leave the choice inside them to the player. A heavier boule
stays where it lands, which suits <Term id="pointing">pointing</Term>. A
lighter one travels, which suits <Term id="shooting">shooting</Term>. Hardness
changes how much a boule bounces back when it is hit.

<Photo id="boules-steel-boules" />

The jack is a small wooden ball. It is the target, and it moves. A boule that
strikes it changes the whole shape of the <Term id="end">end</Term>.

There is very little else. The rules ask for no special shoes and no
protective equipment. Competition adds one tool, a tape or a pair of
callipers. The players use it themselves, in the middle of the game, to settle
which boule is nearer.
