# Governing body politics

Boules is governed by a confederation of federations that do not play each
other, and that structure exists for a reason this site has seen before.

Pétanque has the FIPJP, formed in 1958. Boule lyonnaise, raffa and volo have
their own international bodies. The codes are related — all involve throwing a
ball towards a smaller target ball — and they are not interchangeable: the
throwing action, the surface, the distances and the tactics all differ, and a
champion in one does not compete in another.

The Confédération Mondiale des Sports de Boules was formed in 1985 above them.
Its purpose is institutional: several small federations, each too small to hold
recognition alone, present themselves as one sport in order to be recognised
and to pursue multi-sport programmes. It is a rational response to how the
international sports system works, and it means the entity holding the
recognition governs no competition itself.

The Olympic story is the sharpest thing in the sport's politics. Pétanque
sought inclusion at Paris 2024 — a Games held in the country where it is a
national pastime, played by millions — and the organising committee chose
breaking, skateboarding, sport climbing and surfing instead.

The host-city mechanism is supposed to let organisers add sports that resonate
locally. In this cycle its stated criteria were youth and urban appeal, and a
French committee applied them to exclude the game most identified with France.
Nothing on this site illustrates the mechanism's actual priorities more
plainly.
