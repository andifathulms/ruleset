# Origin and invention

Pétanque was invented in 1907 so that one man could keep playing, and this site
knows of no other sport with that origin.

Throwing games are ancient and were played across the Mediterranean for
centuries. By the early twentieth century the dominant French form was *jeu
provençal*, in which a player took a three-step run-up before releasing and
threw to a jack fifteen or twenty metres away. It was a game for people who
could run.

The accepted account, told by the sport about itself, is that at La Ciotat near
Marseille in 1907 the rules were adapted for Jules Lenoir, whose rheumatism had
made the run-up impossible. Both feet would stay together on the ground inside
a small circle, and the jack would be thrown much closer. The Provençal for
feet planted — *pieds tanqués* — gave the game its name.

It is folk history rather than a documented committee decision, and it is what
the sport says about itself.

What is not in doubt is the consequence. Removing the run-up removed the
physical barrier, and pétanque became a mass participation game across France
in a way its parent code never did — played in town squares, by anyone, at any
age, on whatever ground was there. Jeu provençal survives; pétanque is what
France plays.

The international federation followed in 1958, and an umbrella confederation
above the several boules codes in 1985. Pétanque sought a place at the Paris
2024 Games and was not selected.
