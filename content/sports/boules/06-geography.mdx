# Where it is played

France, overwhelmingly, and then a map drawn almost exactly by French colonial
history.

**France** has participation numbers in the millions and a licensed
competitive base larger than most sports on this site have players. Pétanque is
played in town squares, on club terrains and at every level from the entirely
social to the professional, and it is bound up with the culture of southern
France in particular.

The rest of the competitive map follows the French language. **Madagascar** is
one of the strongest pétanque nations in the world and has been for decades —
a genuine power, in a country with very little sporting infrastructure of any
kind, because the game arrived under colonial rule and stayed. **Thailand** is
the major exception to the pattern and now a leading nation, having invested
seriously in the sport since the 1980s. **Cambodia**, **Laos**, **Vietnam**,
**Tunisia**, **Morocco**, **Senegal** and **Benin** all play it widely.

In Europe, **Spain**, **Italy**, **Belgium** and **Switzerland** compete, and
the other boules codes have their own geographies: boule lyonnaise in France
and Italy, raffa and volo in Italy, Croatia and Slovenia.

The pattern this site keeps finding holds here too. Fistball follows German
emigration, sepak takraw follows childhood exposure, finswimming follows diving
federations — and boules follows French administration, with Thailand as the
one country that acquired it on purpose.
