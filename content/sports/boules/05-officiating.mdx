# The players measure

Most of the time pétanque has no official. The main question in every
<Term id="end">end</Term> is which boule is nearer the jack, and that is a
fact two people can check.

The players check it themselves, with a tape or callipers, in the middle of
the game, and agree the answer. A friendly game has no umpire at all. In
competition the umpire is called only when the players cannot agree, or when
so much depends on the answer that neither side wants to give it. The gap
between six points and none can be a millimetre. Who measures, and when an
official is called, follow settled customs.

What an umpire does judge is how the players behave.

## The circle

Both feet must stay on the ground inside the
<Term id="circle">circle</Term> until the boule lands. A heel lifted at the
moment of release gives a real advantage and is an easy habit to fall into. It
is the most common technical offence, and it is watched closely.

## The ground

The <Term id="terrain">terrain</Term> is left as it is. Stones, ruts, slope
and hardness are part of the game, and reading them is a skill. That makes it
tempting to improve the ground. Smoothing a rut, filling a hole or removing a
stone near the jack is forbidden.

## A boule that was moved

Sometimes a boule or the jack is moved by something other than play: a
spectator, an animal, a player's foot. The rules say how the position is put
back or the end cancelled, and an official has to rule on it.

Beyond the tape and the callipers there are no instruments.
