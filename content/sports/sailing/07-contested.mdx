# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The medal race.** Seventeen years old and still argued about every cycle.
The series format exists to average out the luck in any single race; the medal
race puts the medals in a single race at double points. Sailors have called it
a lottery since 2008. The federation's position is that a regatta whose result
is known before the last race cannot be broadcast, and it has not moved.

**Class reselection.** Every cycle removes events and ends careers, and there
is a real argument that a sport cannot ask athletes to specialise for a decade
in equipment it reviews every four years. The counter-argument is that a frozen
programme drifts away from how the sport is actually sailed, which is what
happened before foiling was admitted.

**Cost and access.** The federation's stated intent to widen participation runs
directly against equipment decisions that are frequently expensive. Kite and
windsurfing classes are the concession; the foiling classes are the tension.

**Whether right of way can be umpired consistently.** Extending on-water
umpiring resolved the timing problem — results are final when racing ends — but
not the judgement problem. Whether an overlap was established before the zone
is as contestable as fencing's priority, and tracking data has informed it
without settling it.

**The venue.** Olympic sailing needs sheltered coastal water and is frequently
staged far from the host city, which keeps its place on the programme under
periodic review.
