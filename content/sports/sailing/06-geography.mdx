# Where it is played

Sailing has the strongest correlation with national wealth of any sport on this
site apart from equestrian, and the reasons are the same: expensive equipment,
a facility requirement that cannot be built cheaply, and a coaching tradition
that takes generations.

Great Britain, Australia, New Zealand, the Netherlands, France, Spain, Italy,
Denmark and the United States dominate. All are coastal, all are wealthy, and
most have a club structure inherited from the nineteenth century.

The exceptions are instructive. Brazil, Argentina and China have produced
Olympic champions through targeted programmes, and windsurfing and kiteboarding
have a broader map than the keelboat and dinghy classes because the equipment
is cheaper and more portable. That is the strongest argument for the newer
classes: they go places the old ones do not.

The equipment reselection cuts both ways here. Foiling classes are expensive
and technical, which narrows access; kiteboarding is comparatively cheap, which
widens it. The federation has added both.

The venue requirement is the hard limit. Olympic sailing needs sheltered
coastal water with reliable wind, which constrains where Games can host it and
means the sport is frequently staged some distance from the host city — an
awkwardness that has contributed to periodic questions about its place on the
programme.
