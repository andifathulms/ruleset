# Coasts, clubs and money

Sailing is strongest in rich countries with a coast. Great Britain, Australia,
New Zealand, the Netherlands, France, Spain, Italy, Denmark and the United
States win most often. Most of them have a system of clubs that goes back to
the nineteenth century.

<Photo id="sailing-royal-yacht-squadron" />

The reasons are plain. A boat is expensive and has to be kept on or near the
water. A beginner needs a club, sheltered water, and a knowledge of the
right-of-way rules before racing safely. Club dinghies make the start easier,
because the boats are shared, and a newcomer can be racing within a season.
Without suitable water there is no sailing at all.

## The exceptions

Brazil, Argentina and China have produced Olympic champions through targeted
programmes.

<Photos ids="sailing-grael-kunze-49erfx sailing-grael-kunze-2016" />

Windsurfing and kiteboarding are sailed in more countries than dinghies and
keelboats are, because the equipment is cheaper and easier to carry.
Kiteboarding joined the Olympic programme in 2024. The foiling boats that
arrived at the same Games are expensive and technical, so they narrow access
while the kite widens it.

## Far from the host city

Olympic sailing needs sheltered coastal water with reliable wind, and many
host cities have none. The racing is often held a long way off. At the Paris
Games of 2024 it was held at Marseille, on the Mediterranean, at the other end
of France.
