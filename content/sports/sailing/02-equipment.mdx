# Equipment evolution

Sailing has the strangest equipment situation on this site: the boats are
standardised within an event and replaced between Olympic cycles.

**One-design** is the principle. Every boat in an Olympic class is built to the
same specification, so the racing is between sailors rather than budgets. That
is a deliberate answer to the sport's history, in which the fastest yacht
belonged to the richest owner and the America's Cup was substantially a design
competition.

**Reselection** is the consequence nobody outside the sport expects. World
Sailing chooses which classes the Games will be sailed in, and it changes them.
Foiling equipment arrived for 2024; the Finn, sailed at every Games from 1952 to
2020, was removed; windsurfing's class was replaced.

No other sport on this site does this. Athletics does not change which throwing
implements exist every four years. Cycling regulates what a bicycle may be but
does not periodically replace the bicycle. Sailing's Olympic events are defined
by their equipment, and the equipment is on a rolling review.

The effect on athletes is severe and this site should say so plainly. A sailor
specialises in a class for a decade — the boat, its handling, the tactics
specific to it — and the event can be removed. Judo removed techniques and
weightlifting redrew categories, and both were treated as shocks. Sailing does
the equivalent on a schedule.

The counter-argument is that a sport whose equipment is frozen becomes a
museum, and foiling had transformed sailing outside the Games while the Olympic
programme stayed still.
