# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The procession.** Sailing's structural problem for broadcast is that its
scoring system works. Aggregate positions across ten races, discard the worst,
and the best sailor usually leads by enough that the final race is a formality.
Olympic gold medals were routinely settled with a race still to sail.

The 2008 medal race is the answer: top ten only, short course close to shore,
double points, no discard. It guarantees the medals are decided at a known time
in front of a camera.

It is also the clearest case on this site of a federation contradicting its own
epistemics. The series exists because a single sailing race contains too much
luck to trust. The medal race takes the most important decision in the regatta
and puts it in a single race, at double weight. Sailors have objected
continuously since it was introduced, and the federation has kept it.

**The protest room.** Sailing incidents were historically resolved by
competitors protesting each other and a jury hearing evidence hours after
racing, which left results provisional and was impossible to follow. Extending
on-water umpiring has moved the sport towards immediate penalties, and towards
a competitor taking a penalty turn voluntarily rather than arguing about it
later.

**Class reselection.** Not a single controversy but a recurring one. Every
Olympic cycle removes classes and adds others, and every removal ends careers.
The Finn's removal after 2020, having been sailed at every Games since 1952,
was the most contested recent example, partly because it was the class for
larger sailors and its loss narrowed who could compete.
