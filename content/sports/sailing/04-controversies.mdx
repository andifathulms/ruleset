# The race worth double

## A final that was already decided

The scoring system worked too well for television. Add up the places over ten
races, drop the worst, and the best sailor usually leads by a wide margin.
Olympic gold medals were often settled with a race still to sail. The last
day was a procession.

In 2008 the federation
[added a medal race](#sailing-2008-medal-race). Only the top ten boats sail
it. The course is short and close to the shore. The points count double, and
the result cannot be used as a <Term id="discard">discard</Term>. The medals
are now decided at a known time, in front of the cameras and the crowd.

Sailors have objected ever since. The series exists because one race holds
too much luck to trust. The <Term id="medal-race">medal race</Term> puts the
most important decision of the regatta into one race and doubles its weight.
The federation has kept it.

## The end of the Finn

Each time the Olympic classes are chosen, some sailors lose their event. The
loss argued over most in recent years was the Finn. It had been sailed at
every Games since 1952 and was removed after 2020. The Finn was the dinghy
for heavier men, so its removal also narrowed the range of sailors who could
compete.

<Photo id="sailing-ainslie-2012" />

Ben Ainslie won the Finn at the London Games of 2012, off Weymouth. It was his
fourth Olympic gold medal in a row.
