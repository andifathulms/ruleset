# Equipment evolution

The board is the sport's main piece of equipment, and it is the diver's to
tune. A springboard must be at least 4.8 metres long, and its fulcrum must
be movable and easy to adjust, so each diver sets the spring before each
dive — further forward for a stiffer board, further back for more lift.

Wooden boards gave way to aluminium in the second half of the twentieth
century. The rules now ask only that the board be of an approved type with
a slip-resistant surface.

The pool matters too. The water under a ten-metre platform must be at least
4.5 metres deep, and the rules require mechanical agitation of the surface
under the boards — a sprinkler or a gentle bubble machine — because a
diver turning at speed cannot see a perfectly still surface.

Very little else has changed. There is no ball, no racket, no measuring
device. What the sport has instead
is a published table.
