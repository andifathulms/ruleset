# The board, the water and the table

The board is the main piece of equipment in diving, and each diver tunes it.
A <Term id="springboard">springboard</Term> must be at least 4.8 metres long.
Its <Term id="fulcrum">fulcrum</Term> must move easily, so a diver can set the
spring before every dive. Moving it forward makes the board stiffer. Moving it
back gives more lift.

<Photos ids="diving-barcelona-reverse diving-platform-1975" />

Wooden boards gave way to aluminium in the second half of the twentieth
century. The rules now ask only that a board be of an approved type, with a
surface that does not slip. The <Term id="platform">platform</Term> at ten
metres is fixed and gives no spring at all.

The pool matters as much as the board. Under a ten-metre platform the water
must be at least 4.5 metres deep. The rules also require the surface under the
boards to be moved by a machine, such as a sprinkler or gentle bubbles. A
diver turning at speed cannot see a perfectly still surface.

There is no ball, no racket and no measuring device. In their place the sport
has a published table. Each dive has a <Term id="dive-number">number</Term>
and a letter. 107B is a forward three and a half somersaults in
<Term id="pike">pike</Term>, and from three metres it is worth 3.1.
