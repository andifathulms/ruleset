# Origin and invention

Diving began as a way for gymnasts to land. In Germany and Sweden in the
nineteenth century, gymnasts took their somersaults outdoors over water, and
what they did in the air came to matter more than how they reached it.

The Olympic Games took it up in pieces. St Louis in 1904 held a platform
event and a plunge for distance, in which a diver pushed off and floated
face-down as far as possible without moving; the plunge was never held again.

London in 1908 added the springboard, and Stockholm in 1912 held the first
women's event, from the platform.

<Photos ids="diving-london-1908 diving-johansson-1912" />

From 1912 to 1924 the programme also held plain high diving, limited to
simple headers from the platform. It was dropped after 1924, and from 1928
the Games settled on four events — springboard and platform, for men and for
women — and kept them unchanged until 2000.

What defines the sport now is arithmetic. Every dive's difficulty is fixed
by a formula of five published components, added together, so a dive's
value is known before anyone leaves the board.
