# Referee and judges

A referee runs the competition and the judges mark each dive. The referee
decides matters of fact: whether the dive was the one announced, whether a
<Term id="restart">restart</Term> is due, whether the feet entered first. The
referee can declare a <Term id="failed-dive">failed dive</Term>, or cap the
marks before the judges show them.

The judges award from 0 to 10 in half points. They mark their overall
impression of the approach, the take-off, the flight and the
<Term id="entry">entry</Term>. At the Games there are seven. The two highest
and two lowest marks are cancelled. The remaining three are added, and the sum
is multiplied by the degree of difficulty.

<Photos ids="diving-wu-minxia-2012 diving-springboard-final-2012" />

Cancelling four marks is the sport's answer to a biased judge. It does not
settle the question. A 2009 statistical study of the scores from Sydney 2000
is one of several to look for national bias in the marks that remain.

## Two divers, one score

<Term id="synchronised">Synchronised diving</Term> joined the Games at Sydney
in 2000 and [doubled the programme](#ioc-2000-synchronised-diving) from four
events to eight. It had been performed as an exhibition since at least 1939.
Olympedia records that the events were added "purportedly … because
television asked for more diving events". That account is reported, not
official.

<Photos ids="diving-sync-springboard-germany diving-sync-platform-2016" />

A synchronised dive has eleven judges. Three mark each diver's execution and
five mark how well the two match. Each group loses its highest and lowest
mark. The rest are averaged onto the same three-judge scale, so a synchronised
score sits on the same footing as an individual one.

<Photo id="diving-sync-springboard-china" />

What the judges never decide is how hard a dive was. That number is printed
before the competition starts.
