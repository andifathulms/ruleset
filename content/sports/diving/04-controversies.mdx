# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Fu Mingxia, 1991.** Fu Mingxia of China won the world platform title in
Perth in 1991 at the age of twelve. The response was a minimum age of
fourteen for senior competition. Fu still dived at Barcelona the following
year — she turned fourteen twenty days after the final — and won gold.

**Seoul, 1988.** Greg Louganis struck his head on the springboard during the
preliminary round, was stitched, and returned to win the event, and then
the platform. He said in 1995 that he had known at the time that he was
HIV-positive. The episode changed no rule.

**Rio, 2016.** The diving pool at the Maria Lenk Aquatics Centre turned green
midway through the Games, and the water polo pool beside it followed.
Officials blamed the water chemistry. It changed nothing in the diving
rules; it is here because it is the image of the sport most people saw that
summer.

The judging is the sport's standing controversy, and the rules answer it
the way most judged sports eventually do: more judges and fewer of their
marks. Of seven marks only the middle three count, and a 2009 statistical
study of the Sydney 2000 scores is one of several to look for national bias
in what is left.
