# Where the air is good

Air sports are found where the air is good, the money is available and the
state allows the flying.

Gliding is strongest in Germany, Poland, the Czech Republic, France, Britain,
Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and the United States. Germany is the
historic centre. Soaring developed there in the 1920s and 1930s, partly
because the Treaty of Versailles restricted powered aviation. It still has
the deepest club structure and the largest manufacturers.

## Records follow the mountains

The great distance records need
<Term id="mountain-wave">mountain wave</Term>, and it is reliable in only a
few places. The Andes come first, then the Southern Alps of New Zealand and a
few similar ranges. The record book is partly a map of mountains. The
federation certifies the records all the same.

<Photo id="air-wave-pyrenees" />

Paragliding and hang gliding gather around suitable terrain: the Alps, the
Pyrenees, Turkey, Nepal, Colombia and Brazil. They are the most widely spread
of the air sports, because the equipment travels in a car.

<Photo id="air-oludeniz" />

Drone racing grew online. Its strongest pilots are young, and a country can
be good at it without owning an airfield.

Most air sports need airspace, and states hand out airspace for reasons that
have nothing to do with sport. The safety rules are aviation law, enforced by
national authorities. A pilot who strays into closed airspace answers to a
regulator. A country with excellent weather and restrictive airspace has no
gliding.
