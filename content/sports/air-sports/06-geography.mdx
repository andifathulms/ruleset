# Where it is played

Air sports go where the air is good and the money is available, which is a
narrower intersection than it sounds.

**Gliding** is concentrated in Germany, Poland, the Czech Republic, France,
Britain, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and the United States. Germany is
the historic centre — soaring flight developed there in the 1920s and 1930s,
partly because the Treaty of Versailles restricted powered aviation — and it
still has the deepest club structure and the largest manufacturers.

**Records** follow the weather rather than the clubs. The great distance flights
are made where mountain wave conditions are reliable: the Andes above all,
along with New Zealand's Southern Alps and a few comparable ranges. A record
book is therefore partly a map of orography.

**Paragliding and hang gliding** concentrate around suitable terrain — the Alps,
the Pyrenees, Turkey, Nepal, Colombia, Brazil — and are the most globally
distributed of the air disciplines because the equipment travels in a car.

**Drone racing** has no geography at all in the traditional sense. It grew
online, its strongest pilots are young, and national representation reflects
where communities formed rather than where facilities exist. It is the only
discipline here that a country can be good at without owning an airfield.

The unifying constraint is that most air sports need airspace, and airspace is
allocated by states for reasons that have nothing to do with sport. A country
with excellent weather and restrictive airspace has no gliding.
