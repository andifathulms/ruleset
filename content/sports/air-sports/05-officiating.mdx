# Proving what happened

Most air sport results are checked after the flight, because no official can
be there to watch.

A gliding task is flown out of sight. The proof is a sealed flight logger
that records a GPS trace. Officials check the trace against the task
afterwards. Did the pilot round each
<Term id="turning-point">turning point</Term>? Did they stay above the
minimum height? Did they start when they said? [Orienteering](/sports/orienteering/)
works the same way: an electronic record stands in for an official who cannot
be present.

Before loggers, the evidence was a camera and a barograph, an instrument that
draws height against time.

## Measured and judged

In an <Term id="accuracy">accuracy landing</Term> an electronic pad measures
the distance from the centre of the target to the first point of contact, to
the centimetre. Canopy piloting is timed and measured through a course. These
results are the hardest to dispute.

<Photo id="air-accuracy-landing" />

Aerobatics is judged. A panel marks the flight against the declared
<Term id="sequence">sequence</Term>, takes marks off when a figure departs
from its shape, and penalises a pilot who leaves the
<Term id="display-box">display box</Term>. The pilot is the one person who
cannot see the shape being drawn.

## Records

Ratifying records is the federation's oldest task. A record claim is a file
of instrument traces, witness statements and calibration certificates. People
who were not present assess it, which is what the body was created to do.
