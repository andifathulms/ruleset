# Officiating

Almost everything here is verified after the fact, because there is nobody
present to see it.

**Evidence, not observation.** A gliding task is flown out of sight over
hundreds of kilometres. The proof is a secure recording flight logger carrying
a GPS trace, checked afterwards against the declared task — did the pilot round
each turning point, did they stay above the minimum height, did they start when
they said. This is the same structure orienteering uses: an electronic record
replacing an official who cannot be there.

Before loggers, the evidence was a camera and a barograph, and the sport's
officiating history is largely the history of what counted as proof.

**Measured disciplines.** Parachuting accuracy uses an electronic pad measuring
the distance from the target's centre to the first point of contact, to the
centimetre. Canopy piloting is timed and measured through a course. These are
the least disputable results in the federation.

**Judged disciplines.** Aerobatics is marked by a panel against a declared
sequence, with deductions for deviation from each figure's geometry and
penalties for leaving the display box. The pilot is the one person who cannot
see the shape being drawn, which is a difficulty no other judged sport here
imposes.

**Record ratification** is the federation's oldest officiating function and its
most consequential. A record claim is a dossier — instrument traces, witness
statements, calibration certificates — assessed by people who were not present,
which is exactly what the body was created in 1905 to do.
