# A federation founded to check records

Air sports began with an argument about proof. Powered flight was two years
old in 1905, and pilots were claiming performances that nobody had an agreed
way to measure. Aero clubs across Europe needed a body that could say whether
a record had been set. [They founded the Fédération Aéronautique
Internationale that year](#fai-1905-founding).

The FAI was not founded to run competitions. Its job was to decide what
counted as a flight, what evidence a claim needed, and who could ratify it.

<Photo id="air-fai-medal" />

That first purpose has never gone away. The FAI still ratifies absolute
records for aircraft, in altitude, speed and distance, whether or not the
flight had anything to do with sport. Its definitions reach above the
atmosphere too. The widely accepted boundary of space, 100 kilometres up, is
the federation's line. It was drawn to decide which records were aeronautical
and which were astronautic.

## A contest the weather controls

Competition came later. Soaring flight developed quickly in Germany in the
1920s and 1930s, and national contests were held there, but there was no
international competition.

<Photo id="air-rhon-1922" />

[The first world gliding championships were held in
1937](#fai-1937-world-championships). Gliding brought a problem with it. A
glider's flight depends on the day's weather, so a fixed standard would
measure the sky and not the pilot. The answer was to set a
<Term id="task">task</Term> each morning and score every pilot against what
the others achieved in the same air. A gliding score means nothing outside
the day that produced it.

The federation has since added ballooning, parachuting, hang gliding,
paragliding, aerobatics, aeromodelling and, [from 2018, drone
racing](#fai-2018-drone-racing). They share the air and very little else.
