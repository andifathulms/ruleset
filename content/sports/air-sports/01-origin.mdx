# Origin and invention

The Fédération Aéronautique Internationale was founded in 1905, which is two
years after the first powered flight and earlier than the governing body of
almost every sport on this site.

It was not founded to run competitions. It was founded to certify records — to
decide what counted as a flight, what evidence a claim required, and who had
the standing to ratify it. Aero clubs across Europe were watching performance
claims multiply with no agreed way to check them, and the federation was the
answer.

That founding purpose has never gone away, and it explains almost everything
odd about air sports as a governed activity. The FAI ratifies absolute
aeronautical records — altitude, speed, distance — whether or not the flight
was sporting in any sense. Its definitions were used to settle the early
history of aviation, and they extend upward: the widely accepted boundary of
space at one hundred kilometres is a sporting federation's definition, adopted
so that it could decide which records were aeronautical and which were
astronautic.

Competition came later. The first world gliding championships were held in
1937, and gliding brought a problem with it that shaped how the federation
scores things: a soaring flight depends entirely on the day's weather, so a
fixed standard would measure the sky rather than the pilot. The answer was to
set a task each morning and score competitors relative to what the rest of the
field achieved in the same air.

The federation has since accumulated disciplines — ballooning, parachuting,
hang gliding, paragliding, aerobatics, aeromodelling and, from 2018, drone
racing — that share a medium and very little else.
