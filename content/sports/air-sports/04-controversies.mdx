# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Scoring a sport the weather controls.** Gliding's founding problem. A soaring
flight depends on conditions nobody can produce on demand, so a fixed standard
would measure the sky. The answer — set a task each morning and score
competitors relative to what the field achieved in the same air — is one of the
more elegant solutions on this site, and it means a gliding score is
meaningless outside the day that produced it.

**Equipment inequality.** Glider performance varies enormously with money, and
the federation contains it with classes limited by span or by standard design.
It works, and it concedes that without it the sport would be decided by
purchasing.

**Where records can be set.** The great distance records require mountain wave
conditions that occur reliably in a handful of places, so the record book is
partly a map of geography. The federation certifies them anyway, because
certifying performance is what it was founded to do.

**Safety as external regulation.** Air sports' safety rules are aviation law,
enforced by national authorities, not by the federation. A competitor who
violates airspace answers to a regulator. That makes air sports the only
activity on this site whose most serious rules are written by people with no
interest in the sport.

**Drone racing's arrival.** A discipline that grew outside the federation, in
commercial leagues, and was brought inside in 2018. Whether an aviation body
founded in 1905 is the natural governor of a stadium esport-adjacent activity
is argued, mostly by people in drone racing.
