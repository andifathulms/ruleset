# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the sport can be watched.** The federation's largest disciplines
happen out of sight over open country, and its competitive future depends on
the handful that can be staged in a venue. That is a hard constraint, not a
presentation problem.

**Drone racing's place.** It arrived from outside, it is the federation's only
natively watchable discipline, and its constituency has little in common with
the gliding and ballooning communities that built the FAI. How much weight it
should carry is genuinely disputed.

**Equipment classes.** Gliding's classes keep competition between pilots rather
than budgets, and every class boundary is an argument about which aircraft
belong together.

**Records and geography.** The best distance records need weather systems
available in a few places. Whether a record book that partly maps mountain
ranges is measuring the sport is a question the federation has answered by
certifying anyway.

**Airspace.** The binding constraint on air sports everywhere, decided by
national regulators balancing commercial aviation, security and defence.
Recreational flying loses those arguments steadily, and no sporting body has
much leverage in them.

**Being the arbiter of things that are not sport.** The FAI ratifies absolute
aviation and astronautic records and fixed the accepted boundary of space at
100 kilometres. Whether a sporting federation should hold that role, in an era
of commercial spaceflight with a commercial interest in the answer, is a
question being asked from outside the sport rather than within it.
