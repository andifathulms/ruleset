# Equipment evolution

In every other sport on this site the equipment serves the athlete. Here the
equipment is an aircraft, and the athlete serves it.

**Gliders** went from wood and fabric to composite construction, and the
performance metric that matters — glide ratio, the distance travelled per unit
of height lost — improved by a factor that would be scandalous in any sport
with records. A modern open-class sailplane glides more than sixty to one.

The federation's answer to that is the one sailing uses: **classes**. Standard,
15-metre, 18-metre, open and club classes limit span or specify a standard
design, so a championship is a contest between pilots rather than between
budgets. Without classes, gliding would be an equipment competition and
everybody involved knows it.

**Paragliders and hang gliders** made the opposite journey — towards equipment
that packs into a rucksack and launches on foot — and they are the reason
anybody can get into the air without an airfield.

**Parachutes** became steerable ram-air canopies, which turned a descent into a
flight and created the disciplines built on flying a canopy rather than
surviving one.

**Racing drones** are the newest and the most disruptive. They cost very little,
they are assembled and repaired by the pilot, and they are expected to crash.
No other aircraft in this federation is disposable, and that single property is
why drone racing's entry barrier is a small fraction of every other
discipline's.

Airworthiness certification runs through all of it — a category of regulation
almost nothing else here needs, because in other sports equipment failure costs
you the contest rather than more than that.
