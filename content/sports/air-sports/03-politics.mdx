# The sport nobody can watch

A championship gliding <Term id="task">task</Term> covers hundreds of
kilometres of open country over five hours. A spectator sees an empty
airfield and, much later, some aircraft coming back. Ballooning and
cross-country hang gliding have the same problem. Skydivers build their
formations thousands of metres up.

It was not always so. At the early German gliding contests the gliders were
launched off a hill in front of the crowd. Once pilots learned to soar away
across country, the contest left its audience behind.

<Photo id="air-rhon-1924-crowd" />

The federation's recent choices follow from this. The disciplines it takes to
the World Games are paragliding accuracy, canopy piloting, glider aerobatics
and drone racing. Each can be held in a venue, with an audience and a finish
line.

## Drone racing arrives from outside

Drone racing did not grow up inside the federation. Commercial leagues and
online communities organised it first: pilots in goggles, flying by
<Term id="fpv">first-person view</Term> through each lit
<Term id="gate">gate</Term> of an indoor course, in races of about a minute.
The audience sees the same camera picture as the pilot.

<Photo id="air-drl-racers" />

[The FAI held its first world drone racing championship in
2018](#fai-2018-drone-racing) and brought the discipline inside. Some people
in drone racing question whether an aviation body founded in 1905 is the
natural home for a stadium race.

The federation's other business is not sport at all. As the body that
ratifies absolute records, it deals with manufacturers, air forces and space
programmes. Air sports are not on the Olympic programme, and the federation
shows little sign of wanting them to be.
