# Governing body politics

Tug of war's politics are defined by an absence: for the first sixty years of
its organised existence, it had no international federation at all.

It entered the Olympic programme in 1900 as an athletics event and was governed
by athletics officials. When the IOC reduced the size of the programme in the
1920s, tug of war had nobody whose job it was to defend it. Sports with
federations argued their case in that period; this one had no address to send
the argument from.

TWIF was founded in 1960, forty years after the removal, and did the work in
the wrong order — building a rulebook, world championships and a member
structure for a sport that had already lost its platform. It received IOC
recognition in 1999, which confers standing without conferring a place.

The federation's position now is unusual and, by this site's reading, healthier
than most. Tug of war has been on every World Games programme since the first
in 1981, which makes it one of the anchor sports of an event explicitly created
for sports that are not Olympic. It is not a supplicant there; it is a founding
fixture.

The Olympic argument still gets made, and it has a specific shape: the sport is
inexpensive, needs no facility, is played on every continent, and has an
unusually complete gender structure with men's, women's and mixed classes at
world level. What it does not have is a broadcast case, and that is currently
the argument that decides these things.
