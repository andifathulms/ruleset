# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether to keep pursuing the Olympic programme.** TWIF holds IOC recognition
and no place. The sport's advantages — no facility, no expensive equipment,
global participation, a complete men's, women's and mixed structure — are real
and are not the criteria that currently decide additions. Whether the campaign
is worth its cost is argued inside the federation.

**Indoor and outdoor.** Two disciplines with separate championships, testing
different things: outdoors, grip and ground; indoors, pure technique. Whether a
federation of this size should maintain both, and which one represents the
sport, is unresolved.

**Judging by eye.** Every call in the sport concerns a body position held
under maximal load, and every one is made by a human watching. Other sports
with less to gain have instrumented far more. Nothing here has been.

**Making weight as a team.** Combined-weight classification means a squad's
eligibility depends on eight people's weights on one morning, and the pressures
that creates are shared and poorly studied. It is the safety question the sport
actually has, and it gets far less attention than footwear.

**Its own reputation.** Tug of war's ubiquity as a novelty event is its largest
participation asset and its largest credibility problem. A sport that everyone
has played badly is difficult to present as one that rewards a decade of
training, and this site's learning layer exists partly because of cases like it.
