# What is still argued about

None of this has been settled.

**The Olympic Games.** The federation has IOC recognition and no place. Its
case is real: the sport is cheap, needs no special venue, is played on every
continent, and has men's, women's and mixed classes at world level. What it
lacks is a case for television, and that is what decides new sports now.
Whether the campaign is worth its cost is argued inside the federation.

**Indoor and outdoor.** The two disciplines test different things. Outdoors,
grip and ground matter. <Term id="indoor">Indoors</Term>, only technique does.
Whether a federation this size should keep both, and which one stands for the
sport, is not settled.

**Judging by eye.** Every call is about a body position held at full effort,
and every call is made by a person watching. Other sports with less at stake
use far more technology. Tug of war uses none.

**Making weight as a team.** A team's place in its class depends on eight
people's weights on one morning. The pressure this creates is shared and little
studied. It is the real safety question in the sport, and it gets much less
attention than footwear.

**Its own reputation.** Almost everyone has played tug of war as a novelty
game. That is the sport's biggest source of players and its
biggest problem. A game everyone has played badly is hard to present as one
that rewards ten years of training.
