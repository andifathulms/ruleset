# Who may pull

## Clubs, not countries

At the early Olympic Games, tug of war teams were clubs, police forces and
regiments. Two teams from the same country could meet for a medal. At St.
Louis in 1904 the Milwaukee Athletic Club won. In 1912 the British team at
Stockholm came from two London police forces, the City of London Police and
the Metropolitan Police.

<Photos ids="tug-of-war-milwaukee-1904 tug-of-war-britain-1912" />

This was normal in 1900. By 1920 it looked out of place, as the Games moved
towards national teams. Accounts of why the sport was dropped after 1920 point
to it, alongside the general cut in the programme.

## Weighing the team

Tug of war puts teams in a <Term id="weight-class">weight class</Term> by the
combined weight of all eight pullers. No single puller has a limit. A 640 kg
team can be eight people of 80 kg, or a mix of heavier and lighter ones. Picking
a team becomes a sum: how to spread the weight across eight bodies.

<Photo id="tug-of-war-canada-1916" />

The rule brings its own problem. The weigh-in becomes a team event. One
person's weight on the morning can put the whole team out of its class. The
pressure to make weight is shared by eight people, where in other weight-class
sports one person carries it.
