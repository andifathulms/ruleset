# Where it is played

Tug of war is played nearly everywhere as a recreation and competitively in a
narrow band of it.

The competitive strength sits in Northern Europe and East Asia. The
Netherlands, Switzerland, Ireland, England, Scotland, Sweden, Spain — the
Basque Country in particular — and Italy have deep club structures, and
Chinese Taipei, Japan and China have been consistently strong, with Chinese
Taipei especially so in the indoor game. Ireland and the Netherlands have won
a great deal of what there is to win.

That distribution has an identifiable cause: it follows the places where the
sport was organised into clubs rather than left as an occasion. Where a rural
sports tradition, a police and fire service tradition or a school competition
structure exists, tug of war has teams; where it does not, the sport survives
as something people do at a festival.

Basque tug of war, *sokatira*, is worth naming separately as a case where the
sport is part of a living regional culture of strength contests rather than an
import — the same tradition that produced stone-lifting and wood-chopping
competitions.

The gender structure is unusually complete for a sport this size. Men's,
women's and mixed classes are all contested at world championships, and the
mixed classes are full events rather than exhibitions — which puts tug of war
alongside korfball and ultimate among the sports on this site that treat mixed
competition as ordinary.
