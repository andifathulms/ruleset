# Where it is played

Tug of war is played almost everywhere for fun. Serious teams are found in far
fewer places.

The strongest clubs are in Northern Europe and East Asia. The Netherlands,
Switzerland, Ireland, England, Scotland, Sweden, Spain and Italy have deep club
systems. Within Spain the Basque Country stands out. Chinese Taipei, Japan and
China have been strong for a long time, and Chinese Taipei especially in the
indoor game. Ireland and the Netherlands have won a great deal of what there is
to win.

Teams exist where the sport was organised into clubs. Country shows, police and
fire services, and school competitions all keep teams going. Where none of
these exist, tug of war lives on as something people do at a festival. At
Tubbrid Lower in Ireland, a frame with a pulley and a drum of rope still stands
beside the Valley Inn, where teams once trained for competition.

In the Basque Country the game is called *sokatira*. It belongs to a living
local culture of strength contests, the same one that has stone-lifting and
wood-chopping competitions. The team from Sunbilla were Navarre champions in
the 680 kg class in 1991.

<Photo id="tug-of-war-sunbilla-1991" />

Women's competition is not new. In October 1951 women's teams pulled at
Hardegarijp in Friesland, and one competitor carried the heavy match rope
across the field on her shoulder. Today men's, women's and mixed classes are all held at world
championships. The mixed classes are full events, not shows. In this tug of war
sits with [korfball](/sports/korfball/) and ultimate
[flying disc](/sports/flying-disc/), where mixed teams are ordinary.
