# From ritual to rulebook

Tug of war has no inventor. Contests of pulling a rope appear across the
ancient world, in Egypt, China, Greece and India. Most began as ceremony or
ritual before they were a competition. Two groups and a rope is a simple idea,
and people seem to have had it in many places on their own.

<Photos ids="tug-of-war-namba-shrine-1790s tug-of-war-naha-2009" />

The rules came from nineteenth-century Britain. Athletics clubs, police forces,
fire brigades and army regiments put out teams. The sport got its written rules
from athletics bodies, and it had no governing body of its own. By 1877 the
contest was familiar enough in Britain for a cartoon in Punch to use it for any
trial of strength.

## Five Olympic Games

Tug of war [joined the Olympic programme](#tow-1900-olympic-entry) at Paris in
1900, as an athletics event run by athletics officials. Clubs entered, not
nations. At Paris a combined Scandinavian team beat a French one. At St. Louis
in 1904 two American clubs, from New York and Milwaukee, pulled against each
other for a medal.

<Photos ids="tug-of-war-paris-1900 tug-of-war-st-louis-1904" />

The sport was on the programme at five Games in a row: 1900, 1904, 1908, 1912
and 1920. Great Britain, Sweden and the United States won most of the medals,
often with police and club teams entered under their own names. At Antwerp in
1920 Great Britain pulled against the Netherlands, and the London police team
won, as it had in 1908.

<Photos ids="tug-of-war-antwerp-1920 tug-of-war-city-police-1920" />

That was the last time. In the 1920s the IOC cut the size of its programme, and
[tug of war was dropped](#tow-1920-programme-removal). It has never returned.
The stated reason was the general cut. Accounts also point to the entries:
clubs, police forces and regiments, where the Games were moving towards
national teams. Both reasons are given, and the sources do not settle which
mattered more.

## Sixty years without a federation

When the cut came, nobody's job was to defend tug of war. Other sports had
federations to argue their case. This one had no body at all.

The [Tug of War International Federation was founded in
1960](#tow-1960-twif-founded), forty years after the sport left the Games.
Countries had been pulling under their own rules, which differed on team size,
rope length and how a pull was won. The new rulebook fixed eight pullers a
side, a pull of four metres, the best of three <Term id="end">ends</Term>, and
a <Term id="weight-class">weight class</Term> set by the whole team's weight.
World championships followed.

<Photo id="tug-of-war-doetinchem-1975" />

In 1981 tug of war was on the programme of [the first World
Games](#tow-1981-world-games), the event made for sports outside the Olympics.
It has been at every World Games since. The federation received IOC
recognition in 1999. That gives it standing, but no place at the Olympics.
