# Equipment evolution

A short racquet, a fast ball, and two pieces of safety equipment that exist
because of the court.

**The racquet** is capped at twenty-two inches including the handle — much
shorter than a squash or tennis racquet — and moved from wood to aluminium to
composites, with the usual head-size and stringing developments. The cap on
length is a genuine performance regulation: in an enclosed court, a longer
racquet would extend reach into space the opponent needs to occupy.

**The tether** is compulsory. The racquet must be attached to the wrist, because
two players share one unsegmented box and a released racquet has nowhere to go
but into somebody.

**Eye protection** is compulsory too, and this is the equipment rule that most
distinguishes racquetball from its relatives. A hard hollow rubber ball
travelling at high speed inside a sealed room, struck by a player who may be
behind you, is a documented eye-injury mechanism. Racquetball made lensed
eyewear mandatory rather than recommended — a step squash has not taken for
adult play in most jurisdictions.

**The ball** is hollow rubber, about two and a quarter inches across, and much
livelier than a squash ball. It does not need warming up, which sounds trivial
and is a real accessibility feature: a cold squash ball barely bounces, so two
beginners cannot sustain a rally until the ball is hot, and two beginners are
exactly the people who cannot get it hot.

Between them, the ball and the absent boundaries account for why this sport is
so much easier to start than the one it is always compared to.
