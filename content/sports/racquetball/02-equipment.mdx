# What a closed room demands

Racquetball needs a racquet, a ball and two pieces of safety equipment. The
safety equipment exists because of the court: two people, one sealed room, and
a fast ball coming off every wall.

## Racquet and tether

The racquet is short. It may be no more than twenty-two inches long, handle
included, which is much shorter than a squash or tennis racquet. In a closed
court a longer racquet would reach into the space the opponent needs. Racquets
have gone from wood to aluminium to composites, and the limit on length has
held.

The racquet must be tied to the wrist with a <Term id="tether">tether</Term>.
A racquet that slips out of a hand in a shared closed room will hit somebody.

## Eyes

A hard rubber ball travels at high speed inside the room, and it may be struck
by a player standing behind you. Eye injuries from this are documented. The
sport's answer was to make <Term id="eyewear">eye protection</Term> with
lenses compulsory, and not just recommended. No single famous injury forced
the rule. [Squash](/sports/squash/) has not taken the same step for adult play
in most places.

## The ball

The ball is hollow rubber, about two and a quarter inches across, and much
livelier than a squash ball. It does not need warming up. That matters to a
beginner. A cold squash ball barely bounces, and two beginners cannot rally
long enough to warm one.

The lively ball and the missing boundaries are the two reasons racquetball is
so much easier to start than squash.
