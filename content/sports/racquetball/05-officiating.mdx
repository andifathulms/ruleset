# Officiating

One referee, two line judges who can be appealed to, and a single recurring
judgement that consumes almost all of the attention.

**Hinders.** Because the court has no boundaries and no division, both players
want the same middle. Interference is constant and structural rather than
occasional. The referee must decide, live, whether contact or obstruction was:

- an ordinary **hinder**, in which the interference was accidental and
  unavoidable, and the rally is replayed; or
- an **avoidable hinder**, in which the player could have cleared and did not,
  and the rally is lost.

That distinction is a judgement about intent and about what a player could
reasonably have done, made instantly, and it decides a large share of
consequential points. It is the closest thing on this site to korfball's
defended-shot call: a four-part spatial judgement that the sport cannot
instrument and has not tried to.

**The serve** produces the sport's other regular calls. A serve must return
past the short line before touching the floor and may touch at most one side
wall; a serve that reaches the back wall on the full, or the ceiling, or two
side walls, is a fault. These are positional facts and are rarely disputed.

**Two bounces.** Whether a ball bounced twice before the racquet reached it is
the sport's equivalent of a squash "not up", judged by eye at speed and close
to the floor.

There is no video review, no ball-tracking, and no instrumentation of any kind
in a sport played inside a sealed box that would be unusually easy to
instrument.
