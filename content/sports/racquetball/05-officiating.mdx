# One call above all others

A refereed match has one referee and two line judges, and a player can appeal
a call to the line judges. No video review or ball tracking is used. Most of
the referee's attention goes to one question.

## Was it avoidable?

The court has no line between the players, and both of them want the same
place in the middle. They get in each other's way all the time. Each time, the
referee must decide at once which of two things happened.

- A <Term id="hinder">hinder</Term>: the interference was an accident and
  could not be helped. The rally is replayed.
- An <Term id="avoidable-hinder">avoidable hinder</Term>: the player could
  have moved clear and did not. That player loses the rally.

The call is a judgement about what a player could reasonably have done, and it
decides a large share of the points that matter. Every revision of the
rulebook has touched the hinder rules, and none has settled them.

## The simpler calls

The serve produces the other regular calls. Did the ball come back past the
<Term id="short-line">short line</Term> before it touched the floor? Did it
touch more than one side wall, or the ceiling, or reach the back wall without
bouncing? These are facts about where the ball went, and they are rarely
disputed.

The last is the double bounce: whether the ball bounced twice before the
racquet reached it. The referee judges it by eye, at speed, close to the
floor.
