# The boom, and what followed

Racquetball grew very fast in the United States through the 1970s. The growth
was built on commercial fitness clubs, which had the courts.

In the 1980s and 1990s the fitness market moved towards cardio machines and
weights. The clubs turned their courts into gym floor. The sport lost its
buildings faster than it lost its players, and the decline had nothing to do
with its rules or its athletes.

The international federation was formed in 1979, at the top of the curve.
American participation peaked around then and fell sharply. The federation has
spent its life governing a sport that grew abroad while it shrank at home.

## The road squash took

The sport is always compared with [squash](/sports/squash/). Both are racket
sports played in a closed court, and they are of similar size and spread.
Squash campaigned for decades for an Olympic place, rewrote its scoring and
invested in glass courts that show well on screen. It joins the Olympic
programme in 2028. Racquetball did not make those moves, and its place is at
the World Games.

Whether racquetball could have followed is open to argument. Its court is
harder to show. A ceiling in play makes overhead cameras difficult, and a
sealed box is harder to open up for spectators than a squash court.
