# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Eye injuries.** The sport's genuine safety problem. A fast rubber ball, a
sealed court, and an opponent who may be behind you produced a documented
pattern of eye injuries, and racquetball's answer was to make lensed
protection compulsory rather than advisory. It is one of the more decisive
safety responses recorded on this site, and it came without a single famous
incident forcing it.

**The racquet as a projectile.** Mandatory wrist tethers exist because a
released racquet in an enclosed shared court will hit somebody. The
requirement is unremarkable and it is a rule that only a sport with no
separation between players needs.

**Hinders.** The permanent argument. Two players occupy one court with no
dividing line, so interference is constant and the referee must continually
separate the accidental hinder, which is replayed, from the avoidable hinder,
which loses the rally. Every rulebook revision touches it and none has settled
it.

**Scoring.** The change racquetball did not make. Squash, badminton,
volleyball, table tennis and fistball all moved to rally scoring between 1999
and 2006, in each case for schedulability, and in each case severing their own
match statistics. Racquetball kept side-out scoring. The debate recurs, the
argument for changing is broadcast, and the sport has so far declined.

**Racquet length.** Capped, because in an enclosed court reach is not merely a
performance advantage but an intrusion into the space the opponent needs. It is
the sport's quiet equipment regulation and it has held.
