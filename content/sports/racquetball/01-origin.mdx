# One man, one YMCA, 1950

Racquetball was invented in 1950 by Joe Sobek at the YMCA in Greenwich,
Connecticut. Sobek was a professional tennis and handball player who worked
there. He wanted a game that was easier on the hands than handball and quicker
to learn than [squash](/sports/squash/).

He designed a short strung racquet, chose a lively rubber ball and [wrote a
set of rules](#racquetball-1950-invention). He called the game paddle rackets.
The name racquetball came later.

<Photo id="racquetball-greenwich-ymca" />

## He kept the court

Sobek changed the implement and left the court alone. Handball was played in a
sealed box with four walls, and YMCAs across the United States already had
those courts. The new game needed nothing built for it. It could spread as
fast as people wanted to play it, and through the 1960s and 1970s it became
one of the fastest-growing participation sports in American history.

## He removed the lines

Squash has a strip at the foot of the front wall that the ball must clear, a
line above which it is out, and lines along the side walls. Sobek's rules
dropped nearly all of that. [Every surface is in
play](#racquetball-all-surfaces-live), the ceiling included, and the whole
<Term id="front-wall">front wall</Term> is good. A
<Term id="rally">rally</Term> ends when the ball bounces twice or fails to
reach the front wall, and almost nothing else ends it.

A beginner's mishit is usually still a legal shot. Two people can keep a rally
going within minutes of picking up a racquet, which is what the rules were
written to allow.

The [International Racquetball Federation was formed in
1979](#racquetball-1979-irf), when national associations in several countries
had no body to hold a world championship between them.
