# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether to change the scoring.** Side-out scoring gives matches unpredictable
length, which is the precise problem five other sports on this site solved
between 1999 and 2006. The argument for changing is broadcast and
schedulability; the argument against is that the sport's rhythm and its
statistical continuity would both go. It recurs and has not been resolved.

**Courts.** The sport's binding constraint is physical. Racquetball needs a
sealed four-wall court, the stock of them is shrinking in the country that
built it, and almost none are being constructed. No governing body can
legislate its way out of that.

**The squash comparison.** Squash rewrote its scoring, invested in glass-court
presentation and campaigned for decades, and joins the Olympic programme in
2028. Racquetball did not, and is at the World Games. How much of that
divergence was strategy and how much was the ceiling being in play — which
makes the sport genuinely hard to film — is argued inside the sport.

**Hinders.** The referee's central judgement is about what a player could
reasonably have done, made instantly, in a sport with no dividing line. Every
rulebook revision touches it.

**Where the sport's centre should be.** Its institutional home is the Americas,
its Pan American Games place is its most secure asset, and its growth countries
are Latin American. Whether to consolidate there or keep pursuing a global
footprint is a strategic question a federation with limited money has to answer
honestly.
