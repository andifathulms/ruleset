# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The unpredictable match.** Under side-out scoring only the serving team could
score, which meant a rally won by the receiving side produced nothing but the
serve. A long exchange of side-outs could add twenty minutes to a set and
nothing to the scoreboard, and a match could run to two and a half hours or
finish in fifty minutes with no way to tell in advance. Broadcasters could not
schedule it.

The FIVB trialled formats through the 1990s and adopted rally scoring in 1999.
It is the first of the three scoring rewrites this site records, and the
cleanest: the federation was explicit that the problem was match length and the
audience.

**The jump serve.** Not a controversy so much as an arms race the sport
eventually legislated against, twice. As serves got faster, rallies got
shorter, and a well-struck serve began simply ending points. The libero in 1998
was one answer. Relaxing the double-contact interpretation on the first ball in
2015 was another — a rule change that exists purely to let a receiver mishandle
a ball that is travelling too fast to handle cleanly.

Both are cases of a federation intervening to slow its own sport down, which is
the opposite of the direction most rule changes here run.

**Height.** Elite indoor volleyball selects for height as hard as basketball,
and by the 1990s it had become a barrier to entry for an entire body type. The
libero was created in part to answer that, and this site records it as the only
deliberate widening of a sport's physical door anywhere in the collection.

**The net cord.** A small one. Serves that clipped the net were replayed until
2000, which produced dead time in a sport that had just made every rally
count.
