# Three problems the rules had to solve

## The match nobody could schedule

Until 1999 only the serving team could score. If the receiving team won a
rally, it won the serve and nothing else. This was
<Term id="side-out">side-out scoring</Term>. Two well-matched teams could
trade the serve for twenty minutes without the score moving. A match could
last two and a half hours or be over in fifty minutes, and nobody could say
which in advance. Broadcasters could not schedule it.

<Photo id="volleyball-tokyo-1964" />

Through the 1990s the federation tried several fixes: rally scoring in some
sets only, a format of fixed length, different targets. In 1999 it [gave a
point for every rally and raised the set from 15 points to
25](#fivb-1999-rally-scoring). It said plainly that the problem was match
length and the audience.

[Table tennis](/sports/table-tennis/) rewrote its scoring in 2001 and
[badminton](/sports/badminton/) in 2006, both for the same stated reason.
Volleyball went first. The cost was its own record: a set to 15 under the old
scoring and a set to 25 under the new one cannot be compared, so no set or
match statistic crosses 1999.

## The serve that ended the rally

The <Term id="jump-serve">jump serve</Term> kept getting faster. Rallies got
shorter, and a well-struck serve often ended the point by itself.

<Photo id="volleyball-jump-serve" />

The sport answered more than once, and each time it sided with the receiver.
[The libero arrived in 1998](#fivb-1998-libero), a defensive specialist who
can replace any back-row player. In 2015 [referees were told to judge the
first touch more leniently](#fivb-2015-double-contact-serve-receive), because
a ball travelling that fast often cannot be handled cleanly, and a strict
<Term id="double-contact">double contact</Term> call was ending rallies on
the serve.

A smaller change belongs here. A serve that clipped the net used to be
replayed. [Since 2000 it stays in play](#fivb-2000-let-serve), which removed a
stoppage from a game that had just made every rally count.

## A game for tall players

By the 1990s the top indoor game selected for height as hard as
[basketball](/sports/basketball/) does. The libero was created partly to
answer that. The position cannot attack above the net or block, so height
matters much less for it, and it gave shorter players a place in the elite
game.
