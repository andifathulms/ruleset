# Invented in a gym

Volleyball was invented in 1895 by one man, in a YMCA gym in Holyoke,
Massachusetts. William G. Morgan was an instructor there, and he wanted a game
his older members could play.

[Basketball](/sports/basketball/) had been invented four years earlier in
Springfield, ten miles away, by a man Morgan knew. For Morgan's members it was
too strenuous, and there was too much contact. So he raised a tennis net
across the gym and took the bladder out of a basketball to use as the ball.
The result was a game in which the ball never touches the ground and the two
teams never touch each other.

<Photos ids="volleyball-morgan-1897 volleyball-washington-1923" />

He called it mintonette. The name volleyball came from an observer, who
remarked that the players were volleying the ball back and forth.

Two of Morgan's decisions still govern the sport.

The first is that the ball may not come to rest. A player cannot catch it or
hold it, so every <Term id="touch">touch</Term> is a rebound. That is what
produces the sequence seen in every <Term id="rally">rally</Term> today: a
<Term id="pass">pass</Term>, a <Term id="set-pass">set</Term>, an
<Term id="attack">attack</Term>.

The second is the net between the teams. A volleyball player cannot interfere
with an opponent, only with the ball. There is no tackling and no marking, as
there is in [football](/sports/football/) or basketball.

<Photo id="volleyball-block-hands" />

This is why the rulebook spends most of its length on one question: what
counts as a legal touch of the ball. It has very little to say about what
players may do to each other, because they cannot reach each other.
