# Origin and invention

Volleyball is the other invented sport on this site, and it was invented four
years after basketball, ten miles away, for the opposite reason.

William G. Morgan was a YMCA instructor in Holyoke, Massachusetts, and in 1895
he was looking for something his older members could play. Basketball, invented
in nearby Springfield in 1891 by a man Morgan knew, was too strenuous and
involved too much contact. Morgan raised a tennis net, took the bladder out of
a basketball, and produced a game in which the ball never touches the ground
and the two teams never touch each other.

He called it mintonette. The name volleyball came from an observer's remark
that the players were volleying the ball back and forth.

The two design decisions that still govern the sport were both there at the
start. **The ball may not come to rest**, which forces every contact to be a
rebound and makes the sport's characteristic sequence possible. **The teams are
separated by a net**, which means volleyball has no defence in the sense
football or basketball has one — you cannot interfere with an opponent, only
with the ball.

That second property makes volleyball unusually clean to officiate and
unusually pure as a contest, and it is why its rulebook spends almost all its
length on what a legal contact is rather than on what players may do to each
other.
