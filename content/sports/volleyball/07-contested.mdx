# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the serve has won.** The jump serve has been fast enough to end
rallies outright for twenty-five years, and the sport's two answers — the
libero and the relaxed first-contact interpretation — have slowed the problem
rather than solved it. Proposals recur to restrict the serve directly, by
limiting the jump or requiring the server to remain behind a deeper line, and
the counter-argument is that the serve is the one moment a single player
controls entirely.

**Set length.** Rally scoring solved unpredictability without making matches
short, and there is a periodic push for shorter sets or fewer of them,
particularly from broadcasters. Volleyball has been here before and knows what
it costs: it severed its own statistics in 1999 and would sever them again.

**The libero's restrictions.** The position was defined narrowly in 1998 and
has been loosened since. Whether a libero should be allowed to serve, or to set
from the front zone, or to captain a team, are live rule questions — and each
one nudges an invented position closer to being an ordinary one.

**Two sports, one federation.** Beach and indoor volleyball share a governing
body, a name and an Olympic sport listing, and increasingly little else. There
is no serious movement to separate them, and no coherent account of why they
are one thing.
