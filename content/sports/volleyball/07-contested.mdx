# What is still argued about

**Has the serve won?** The <Term id="jump-serve">jump serve</Term> has been
fast enough to end rallies outright for twenty-five years. The libero and the
more lenient first touch slowed the problem and did not solve it. Proposals
keep returning to restrict the serve itself, by limiting the jump or by moving
the server further back. The argument against is that the serve is the one
moment a single player controls completely.

**Shorter sets.** <Term id="rally-scoring">Rally scoring</Term> made matches
predictable. It did not make them short. Broadcasters in particular push from
time to time for shorter sets, or fewer of them. The sport knows the price: it
cut itself off from its own statistics in 1999, and a new format would do so
again.

**What a libero may do.** The position was defined narrowly in 1998 and has
been loosened since. Whether a <Term id="libero">libero</Term> should be
allowed to serve, or to set from the front zone, is still argued. Each
loosening moves an invented position closer to an ordinary one.

**Two games, one federation.** Indoor volleyball is six a side on a hard
court. Beach volleyball is two a side on sand, with a smaller court, sets to
21 and no substitutions. It has its own rulebook, and it joined the Olympic
programme in 1996 as a commercial proposition. Indoor volleyball has the
older institutions and more players. The two share a governing body, a name
and one line in the list of Olympic sports. Nobody is seriously trying to
separate them.
