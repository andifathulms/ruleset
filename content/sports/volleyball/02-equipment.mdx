# Equipment evolution

Volleyball has almost no equipment history, and the little it has is a useful
counter-example.

The ball has been specified by circumference, weight and pressure for decades,
and the specification has not needed to move. What did change was construction:
manufacturers went from eighteen smooth panels to fewer, dimpled ones in the
2000s, which measurably altered how a float serve behaves in the air. Nothing
in the rules had to change, and no series was severed.

Set that against athletics, where a tightly written javelin specification had to
be amended twice and cut the record book both times. Volleyball wrote its ball
rule loosely and never paid the price. The pattern across this site is
consistent: the tighter the specification, the more often the sport has to
break its own numbers.

The one equipment rule that matters is a shirt. The libero must wear a
contrasting one, because a position defined by restrictions — cannot attack
above the net, cannot block, cannot serve — can only be officiated if the
official can identify the player instantly. It is the only place on this site
where a uniform requirement exists to make a rule enforceable rather than to
distinguish teams.

Knee pads and the sprung floor are the safety story, and like most such
histories they happened without a rule.
