# Governing body politics

The FIVB governs two sports that share almost nothing and calls them one, and
the tension between them is most of its modern politics. Beach volleyball has a
different court, a different team size, a different scoring target and a
different audience, and it arrived on the Olympic programme in 1996 as a
commercial proposition. Indoor volleyball has the older institutions and the
larger participation base.

The federation's other defining project is the one this site is built to
notice. Through the 1990s the FIVB experimented repeatedly with scoring — rally
scoring in some sets, a fixed-length format, a variety of targets — before
adopting rally scoring outright in 1999. It was the first of the three racket
and net federations to do it, and badminton and table tennis followed within
seven years.

That sequence is worth stating plainly, because the homepage of this site rests
on it. Three separate federations, governing three different sports on three
continents, changed the arithmetic of their competitions inside eight years and
each gave the same reason: a match had to be able to fit in a slot.

The libero is the FIVB's most interesting single act of governance. Faced with
a sport its own rules had made too fast and too tall, it did not restrict the
serve or lower the net. It invented a position, wrote restrictions around it,
and created an elite career path for players the sport had been excluding. No
other federation on this site has responded to a structural problem by adding a
new kind of player.
