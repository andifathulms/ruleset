# Where it is played

Volleyball has the broadest competitive geography of any team sport on this
site. Brazil, Italy, Poland, the United States, Russia, Serbia, France, Japan,
China, Turkey and Iran have all been serious international contenders within
living memory, and the medal table has no single dominant nation of the kind
badminton, table tennis or archery has.

The reason is partly the facility. A court and a net will fit in any sports
hall, the sport is standard in school physical education across Europe, Latin
America and East Asia, and it needs no expensive surface or equipment. Beach
volleyball needs even less.

Poland and Brazil are the two cases worth recording, because both built
sustained success on domestic professional leagues rather than on centralised
state programmes — a route to elite depth that most sports here do not have,
because most sports here do not have viable professional club structures
outside a handful of countries.

The women's game has a notably different map from the men's, with China, the
United States, Serbia and Italy strong, and it has a longer history of
professional structures than most women's sports on this site.

The rule consequence is mainly negative and worth stating: because no single
nation dominates, volleyball's rule changes are almost never read as dominance
suppression. Compare table tennis, where every change the ITTF makes is
interpreted through China. A federation with an even competitive map has more
freedom to legislate.
