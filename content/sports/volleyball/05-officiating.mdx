# Who makes the calls

The two teams never touch each other, and that makes volleyball easier to
referee than most team sports. Almost every decision is about where the ball
was, or whether a touch was legal. Neither needs a judgement about what a
player intended.

Two referees work each match, with line judges and a scorer. The first
referee stands on a platform at the net, with eyes level with the top of it. The hardest calls happen there: whether a player touched the net,
whether a back-row attacker jumped from in front of the
<Term id="attack-line">attack line</Term>, whether a
<Term id="block">block</Term> touched the ball on its way out. The second
referee stands on the floor by the post.

<Photos ids="volleyball-referee-stand volleyball-second-referee" />

A coach who disagrees with a call can [ask for a video
review](#fivb-2009-video-challenge). The questions that can be reviewed are
questions of fact: in or out, touched or not touched, foot on the line, net
touched. Each is decided in a fraction of a second at floor level, and a
camera settles each one. A coach has a limited number of unsuccessful
challenges per set. Enough challenges succeed that the system causes little
argument.

<Photo id="volleyball-serve" />

One judgement stays with the referee: whether a touch was legal. Was a set
struck or thrown? Did the first touch hit the receiver twice? The second
question has been made easier by relaxing the rule. [Since
2015](#fivb-2015-double-contact-serve-receive) the first touch on a
hard-driven ball is judged much more leniently, so that a receiver is not
punished for the server's speed.
