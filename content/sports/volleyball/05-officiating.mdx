# Officiating and technology

Volleyball is one of the easier sports on this site to officiate, for a
structural reason: the two teams never touch each other. Almost every decision
is about where the ball was or whether a contact was legal, and neither
requires a judgement about intent.

Two referees work each match — one on a stand at the net, one on the floor —
with line judges and a scorer. The referee on the stand is level with the top
of the net, which is where the sport's hardest calls happen: whether a player
touched the net, whether a back-row attacker took off in front of the attack
line, whether a block touched the ball on its way out.

**Video challenge**, introduced in 2009, fits volleyball better than almost any
other sport here. The reviewable questions are: in or out, touched or not
touched, foot on the line, net touched. All of them are facts with correct
answers, all of them are decided in a fraction of a second at floor level, and
all of them are settled instantly by a camera. Coaches challenge, a limited
number of times, and the success rate is high enough that the system is
uncontroversial.

The judgement that remains is the legal contact — whether a set was thrown
rather than struck, whether a first contact was a double. That is the volleyball
equivalent of football's handball law, and unlike football's it has been
resolved partly by relaxing the rule: since 2015 the first contact on a
hard-driven ball is judged much more leniently, because the alternative was
punishing a receiver for the server's speed.
