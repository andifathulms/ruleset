# What is still argued about

**The plastic ball.** Players still say that the plastic ball of 2014 takes
less <Term id="spin">spin</Term> and shortens rallies. That is the opposite
of what the larger ball of 2000 was meant to do. The federation has not
accepted that view. Manufacturing has improved and the balls from different
makers have become more alike. The open question is whether the way the game
plays should depend on what a factory is able to make.

**The service.** The law against hiding the ball has not produced a service
that officials judge the same way every time. People keep proposing a test
that can be measured, such as a minimum throw checked by camera or a fixed
zone where the ball must be hit. The federation has not adopted one.

**Too many changes.** In about twenty-five years the sport has changed its
ball, its scoring, its glue and its service law. Some inside the game argue
that the federation legislates too readily, and that records and statistics
no longer mean anything across time.

**One nation on top.** Should a governing body act against one country's
dominance at all? And what would a fair way of doing it look like? The
question stays alive in table tennis because the dominance is so complete.
