# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The plastic ball.** Ten years on, players still argue that the 2014 material
change reduced spin and shortened rallies — the opposite of what the 2000 size
change was for. Manufacturing tolerances have improved and the balls have
converged, but whether the sport's physics should be set by what is
commercially manufacturable is an open question that the federation did not
choose to face and cannot avoid.

**The service, again.** Two laws have been written to stop concealment and
neither has produced a service that officials judge consistently. Proposals
recur to replace the visibility test with something measurable — a fixed
minimum throw height enforced by camera, or a required contact zone — which is
the move badminton made in 2018. Table tennis has not made it.

**Frequency of change.** The sport has altered its ball, its scoring, its
glue and its service law inside a quarter of a century. There is a real
argument within it that this is a federation too willing to legislate, and that
the accumulated effect is a sport whose records and statistics mean nothing
across time. This site is sympathetic to that reading and notes that it is a
reading, not a finding.

**Dominance.** Whether a governing body should act against one nation's
supremacy at all — and what a legitimate instrument would even look like —
remains live in table tennis in a way it is not in most sports, precisely
because the supremacy is so complete.
