# Governing body politics

The ITTF's defining political fact is that one country has been overwhelmingly
dominant for four decades, and that the federation's rule changes are routinely
read as responses to it. China's supremacy in table tennis is more complete
than any nation's in any other sport on this site, and the 40 mm ball, the
eleven-point game and the speed glue ban have all been described by somebody as
attempts to blunt it.

This site records that reading and does not endorse it. In every case the
federation gave a different reason — broadcast, health, manufacturing — and in
every case that reason is independently plausible. Where a stated reason and a
widely believed one diverge, the vocabulary here has a word for it, and the
honest position is that these particular changes have not been shown to be
dominance suppression rather than what they claim to be.

The federation's other long-running project is genuinely admirable and worth
recording: table tennis is the most globally distributed racket sport by
participation, and the ITTF has pursued that deliberately through cheap
equipment standards and a development programme. The sport is played
competitively in more national associations than football is.

Table tennis diplomacy is the one piece of this history everyone knows. The
American team's visit to China in 1971, arranged around a world championship,
preceded the resumption of relations between the two countries. It is the only
occasion on this site where a sport's governing structure was used as an
instrument of foreign policy and said so.
