# Who makes the calls

Table tennis is judged almost entirely by eye, at very high speed. The umpire
sits at the side of the table, level with the net, and calls the score. An
assistant umpire sits on the other side.

<Photos ids="table-tennis-umpire table-tennis-yokohama-2009" />

The assistant's main job is to watch the
<Term id="service">service</Term>. To judge one, an official must decide
three things. Did the ball rise 16 centimetres? Was the palm flat and still?
Could the receiver see the ball the whole time? The movement lasts less than
a second, and the officials see it from the side.

<Photo id="table-tennis-boll-serve" />

Video review came late and is still limited. Some top events use it to check
whether the ball touched the table or its edge. Those are questions of fact,
and a camera answers them well.

No camera has settled the service. "Visible to the receiver" is a statement
about what another person could see, so it needs a judgement.
[Badminton](/sports/badminton/) had a similar problem with its serve and
solved it in 2018 with a fixed height. Table tennis has found no number that
could do the same job.

Officials also check the bat. The <Term id="rubber">rubbers</Term> must come
from the approved list, be the right colours and stay within the thickness
limit. Bats are inspected before matches.
