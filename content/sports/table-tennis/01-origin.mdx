# Where it came from

Table tennis began as [lawn tennis](/sports/tennis/) brought indoors for the
winter. It appeared in England late in the reign of Queen Victoria as a game
for after dinner. A row of books made the net. The lid of a cigar box made
the bat. The ball was a champagne cork or a piece of rubber.

<Photos ids="table-tennis-hassall-1902 table-tennis-cartoon-1902" />

The name most people still use came from a manufacturer. Ping-Pong was a
trademark, registered around 1901 for one company's set of equipment. Within
a year or two the word was known well enough to carry a political joke in a
newspaper cartoon and to sell sheet music in Ohio. The sport's governing
bodies have always said "table tennis", to keep clear of the brand.

## What made it a sport

Two materials turned the parlour game into a sport. The first was the
celluloid ball, adopted around 1900. It bounced evenly enough for a real
<Term id="rally">rally</Term>. The second was
<Term id="pimples">pimpled</Term> rubber on the bat, which let a player put
<Term id="spin">spin</Term> on the ball. Spin is what separates table tennis
from the game played with cigar box lids, and most of its laws follow from
it.

The International Table Tennis Federation was founded in 1926 and held its
first world championships in the same year. Table tennis joined the Olympic
programme in 1988.
