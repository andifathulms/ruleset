# Bat, glue and ball

Table tennis keeps a close watch on its equipment, because small changes to
the bat or the ball change how much <Term id="spin">spin</Term> there is in
the game.

## The rubber

Pimpled rubber gave the sport spin. In the 1950s a layer of
<Term id="sponge">sponge</Term> under the rubber multiplied it. At the world
championships in Bombay in 1952, Hiroji Satoh of Japan won the men's singles
with a thick sponge bat. His opponents were using plain pimpled rubber. The
federation has set a limit on the thickness of the covering ever since.

Later rubbers did the opposite job. Long <Term id="pimples">pimples</Term>
and anti-spin surfaces reversed or killed the spin on the incoming ball. That
led to a problem of its own, and to [the law that the two sides of a bat must
be different colours](#ittf-1986-two-colour-racket).

## Speed glue

From the 1970s, players found that fresh glue made with strong solvents
swelled the sponge. For a few hours the bat gave more speed and more spin.
The effect faded, so the <Term id="rubber">rubber</Term> had to be taken off
and glued again before every match.

<Photo id="table-tennis-speed-glue" />

Everyone who took the game seriously did it. That meant players, many of them
children, were breathing solvent fumes every day, often in rooms with no
fresh air. [These glues were banned from 2008](#ittf-2008-speed-glue-ban).
The federation gave health as its reason. Some accounts add a second one:
the ban removed a large, legal gain that favoured players with the time and
the help to re-glue before every match.

## The ball, twice

[In 2000 the ball grew from 38 millimetres to
40](#ittf-2000-forty-millimetre-ball). This was done on purpose. A larger
ball is slower through the air and takes less spin, so rallies last longer
and the ball is easier to follow.

<Photos ids="table-tennis-ball-38-40 table-tennis-celluloid-balls" />

[In 2014 the ball changed from celluloid to
plastic](#ittf-2014-plastic-ball). This change was forced from outside.
Celluloid burns easily. It was becoming hard to make, to store and to ship,
and fewer suppliers were willing to produce it. The plastic ball is very
slightly larger and harder, and it takes less spin again.
