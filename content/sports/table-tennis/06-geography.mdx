# Where it is played

Table tennis is played almost everywhere and won almost always by one
country.

## A room is enough

A table, a net and two bats will equip a club, and the game needs a room, not
a sports ground. The international federation has worked to spread the sport
on purpose, through cheap equipment standards and a development programme. It
has more national associations than football's world body.

## China

Since the 1980s Chinese players have won the large majority of the world and
Olympic titles open to them. China finds its players in childhood and trains
them in a national system. Its home league is stronger than most countries'
international teams. The dominance is real. Its causes are disputed, as they
are for [badminton](/sports/badminton/) in Asia.

<Photos ids="table-tennis-ma-long-2014 table-tennis-fan-zhendong-2017" />

A country may enter only two players in each Olympic singles event. So the
hardest tournament in the world can be China's own trial.

Because one country wins so much, almost every rule change is read by
somebody as an attempt to stop it. That has been said of the 40 millimetre
ball, of the game to 11 and of the ban on speed glue. Each time the
federation gave a different reason: television, health, manufacturing. Each
of those reasons stands up without the other reading, and neither has been
proved against the other.

## Ping-pong diplomacy

In 1971 the American team visited China. The trip was arranged around a world
championship, and it came before the United States and China took up
relations again. In February 1972 President Nixon went to Peking, and on the
23rd he watched an athletic exhibition with table tennis tables set out on
the arena floor. A Chinese team travelled to the United States in the same
year.
