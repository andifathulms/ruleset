# Where it is played

Table tennis has the widest national spread of any racket sport and the
narrowest elite concentration of any sport on this site. Both are true at once,
and the combination is unusual enough to be the point of this section.

The spread comes from the equipment. A table, a net and two bats will outfit a
club, the space required is a room rather than a facility, and the ITTF has
pursued global distribution deliberately. The sport is organised in more
national associations than football.

The concentration is China. Since the 1980s Chinese players have won the large
majority of world and Olympic titles available to them, through a training
system that identifies players in childhood and a domestic league whose
internal standard exceeds most countries' international one. As with badminton
in Asia and distance running in the Rift Valley, the honest thing to say is
that the concentration is real and its causes are contested.

The rule consequence is the one recorded in this sport's politics: because the
dominance is so complete, essentially every rule change the ITTF makes is read
by somebody as an attempt to end it. That reading attaches to the 40 mm ball,
the eleven-point game and the speed glue ban alike, and in each case the
federation gave a different and independently plausible reason. This site
records both and settles neither.

Olympic quota rules bite here as they do in badminton: a country may enter only
two players per singles event, so the deepest field in the world is a national
trial.
