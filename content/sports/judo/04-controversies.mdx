# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The white-on-white problem.** Not an incident but a broadcast fact: two
contestants in identical white judogi gripping each other on a pale mat could
not be told apart on television, which made the sport unwatchable and made
refereeing decisions impossible for a viewer to follow. The blue judogi arrived
in 1998 over sustained objection from within the sport.

**Wrestling in a judogi.** Through the 2000s, techniques that attacked the legs
directly with the hands — morote-gari and its relatives — became increasingly
effective at the highest level, particularly among competitors from wrestling
cultures. They were legal, they worked, and to the federation they looked
wrong. Restricted in 2010, banned outright from 2013.

This is the clearest case on the site of a governing body legislating on
aesthetics and saying so. There is a competing reading, recorded in the entry,
that the ban advantaged the classical throwing styles of the sport's
traditional powers over approaches that had beaten them. The sources do not
settle it, and the federation's own reason — identity — is unusually honest
about not being a sporting one.

**Winning by not losing.** By the mid-2010s a contest could be won by taking a
minimal score or drawing a penalty and then defending for four minutes. Three
separate changes closed that off: yuko abolished in 2017, unlimited golden
score in the same year, and in 2018 the removal of penalties from the score
altogether. A judoka can now only win by doing something.

**Necks and shoulders.** The one uncontested area. Throws taken onto the head,
or finished with the opponent's arm trapped, produced a documented pattern of
serious cervical and shoulder injuries, and the 2022 restrictions attracted
none of the argument that surrounds everything else here.
