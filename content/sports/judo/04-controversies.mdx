# Three arguments that changed the rules

## Wrestling in a jacket

Through the 2000s a group of throws became more and more successful at the top
of the sport. The thrower dropped low and attacked the opponent's legs
directly with the hands. Morote-gari and kata-guruma were two of them, and
they were winning medals. They worked especially well for competitors from
countries with a strong [wrestling](/sports/wrestling/) tradition.

The throws were legal. To the federation they looked wrong. It restricted them
in 2010 and [banned them outright from 2013](#ijf-2013-leg-grab-ban). Since
then a <Term id="leg-grab">leg grab</Term>, a hand on the opponent's leg or
trousers, brings an immediate penalty.

The federation did not say the throws were dangerous or unfair. It said they
made judo look like freestyle wrestling, another sport on the same Olympic
programme, and that judo had to stay recognisable as judo. Others read the ban
differently. In their account it helped the classical throwing styles of the
sport's traditional powers against newer approaches that had started to beat
them. The sources do not settle which reason counted for more.

## Winning by not losing

By the middle of the 2010s there was a safe way to win a judo contest. A
contestant took one small score, or drew one penalty from the opponent, and
then defended until the four minutes ran out.

The federation closed that road with three changes. In 2017 it [abolished
yuko](#ijf-2017-scoring-simplified), the smallest score, so that only
substantial throws counted. In the same year it [took the time limit off
golden score](#ijf-2017-golden-score), which also ended the draw and the
referees' decision. In 2018 it [separated penalties from the
score](#ijf-2018-shido-rewrite): a <Term id="shido">shido</Term> stopped
giving the opponent anything, and three of them meant disqualification.

After those changes a contestant could no longer win by provoking the
opponent. The scoring change has a second reading as well. Some see it as a
return to Kano's idea that <Term id="ippon">ippon</Term>, the decisive throw,
is the point of judo, and that anything less is an unfinished version of it.

## Necks and shoulders

One change met almost no argument. Throws that landed a contestant on the
head, or that were finished with the opponent's arm trapped, were causing
serious neck and shoulder injuries. The sport's own medical commission
documented the pattern. [In 2022 the federation restricted those
throws](#ijf-2022-safety-restrictions) and banned diving head-first into the
mat.
