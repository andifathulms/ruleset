# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**The leg grab ban, twelve years on.** It has not settled. The argument for
reinstatement is that the sport removed a legitimate and effective family of
techniques on aesthetic grounds and narrowed itself doing so; the argument
against is that judo is a defined thing and a governing body is entitled to
defend the definition. This site records that the federation gave identity as
its reason, which is rare enough to be the interesting fact regardless of who
is right.

**Rule churn.** Judo has changed what wins a contest three times in a decade.
There is a real argument inside the sport that this is too much: that athletes
build careers on techniques and tactics that the rulebook then removes, and
that the accumulated effect is a sport whose eras cannot be compared. Judo has
no comparability break in the sense this site usually means, because it keeps
no quantitative series — but it may have the most severe continuity problem
here, and no chart can show it.

**Weight cutting.** The sport is contested at a made weight and the practice of
extreme dehydration before weigh-in is a documented health risk that the
federation regulates lightly. Same-day weigh-ins and tighter tolerances are
proposed periodically.

**Passivity.** Every combat sport with a clock has to decide what to do about a
contestant who does not engage, and judo's answer — shido for passivity — asks
a referee to judge intent over time. It is the most disputed call left in the
sport, and unlike a score it cannot be settled by looking again.
