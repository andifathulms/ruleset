# What is still argued about

**The leg grab ban.** It has not settled. One side says the sport removed a
legitimate and effective family of techniques because of how they looked, and
made itself narrower. The other side says judo is a defined thing, and a
governing body may defend the definition.

**Too many rule changes.** In about ten years judo changed what wins a contest
three times, and the smallest score, <Term id="yuko">yuko</Term>, was
abolished in 2017 and restored in 2025. A judoka builds a career on certain
techniques and tactics, and the rulebook can then remove them. Some inside the
sport say this is too much, and that contests from different years can no
longer be compared.

**Cutting weight.** Judo is fought in weight categories, and contestants make
the weight for the weigh-in. Extreme dehydration before a weigh-in is a
documented health risk, and the federation regulates it lightly. Weigh-ins on
the day of the contest and tighter limits are proposed from time to time.

**Passivity.** A contestant who does not attack receives a shido. To give it,
the referee must judge what the contestant intended over a period of time. It
is the most disputed call left in judo. A score can be checked by looking
again, and this cannot.
