# Origin and invention

Triathlon is the only sport on this site invented by people trying to settle an
argument in a bar.

The modern form is usually traced to San Diego in 1974, and to Hawaii in 1978,
where a dispute among naval officers about whether swimmers, cyclists or
runners were the fittest athletes was resolved by combining three existing
local races into one. The Ironman was born from that, and the sport grew
outwards from long distances rather than towards them.

That origin matters, because triathlon's Olympic form is almost the opposite of
what it started as. The founding events took most of a day; the Olympic
distance takes under two hours and was chosen because that is what a broadcast
slot can hold. The founding events were three individual efforts summed
together; Olympic triathlon since 1995 is a pack race.

The International Triathlon Union was founded in 1989 and the sport entered the
Games in 2000, which makes it one of the youngest here — younger than any
sport except breaking, skateboarding, surfing and sport climbing.

Its rules were therefore written recently, deliberately, and with television in
mind from the start, which is unusual. Most federations on this site inherited
a rulebook and have been amending it under commercial pressure ever since.
Triathlon designed for that pressure at the outset.
