# Where it came from

Triathlon began as the answer to an argument about who is fittest: the
swimmer, the cyclist or the runner.

The modern sport is usually traced to San Diego in 1974 and to Hawaii in
1978. In Hawaii a group of naval officers disagreed about which kind of
athlete was the fittest. They settled it by joining three local races into
one. That race became the Ironman.

<Photos ids="triathlon-kona-swim-2005 triathlon-kona-bike-course" />

So the sport started long and grew shorter. The first events took most of a
day, and each <Term id="leg">leg</Term> was a separate effort made alone.
Long-distance racing is still like that. The ride on the Hawaii course is 180
kilometres, a distance chosen before television had any say.

The Olympic form is nearly the opposite. The International Triathlon Union
was founded in 1989, and [triathlon entered the Games at Sydney in
2000](#itu-2000-olympic-distance) with a 1500 metre swim, a 40 kilometre ride
and a 10 kilometre run. That distance was chosen because it finishes in under
two hours, which is what an Olympic broadcast can hold.

<Photos ids="triathlon-ironman-frankfurt-2013 triathlon-hyde-park-run-2012" />

Most federations inherited an old rulebook and changed it later under
commercial pressure. Triathlon's rules were written recently, with television
in mind from the start. The union renamed itself World Triathlon in 2020.
