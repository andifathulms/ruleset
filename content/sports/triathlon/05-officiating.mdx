# Who makes the calls

Triathlon solved its hardest officiating problem by removing the rule.

Until 1995 the main work of an official was judging
<Term id="drafting">drafting</Term>. Was this rider inside the zone behind
that one? For how long? Was the rider overtaking? Marshals on motorcycles
made these judgements over the whole bicycle course, and their calls caused
most of the sport's disputes. When drafting became legal in elite racing, the
calls were no longer needed. [Hockey](/sports/hockey/) did the same thing
when it abolished offside in 1998.

<Term id="age-group">Age-group</Term> racing kept the rule and kept the
problem. Marshals still judge the gap there, and an athlete who is caught
serves a time penalty in a <Term id="penalty-box">penalty box</Term>.

In elite races the officials now mostly watch the
<Term id="transition">transition</Term>. Is the helmet fastened before the
bicycle leaves the rack? Does the athlete mount and dismount on the correct
side of the <Term id="mount-line">mount line</Term>? Is all the equipment
inside the marked area? Each question has a clear answer. The rules are
applied strictly, and they have decided races.

Timing is done with transponders, and the sport publishes a
<Term id="split">split</Term> for every leg.
