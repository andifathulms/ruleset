# What is still argued about

**Whether drafting should be legal.** Thirty years on, critics still say that
an Olympic triathlon is a swim, a ride and a run in which only the run
matters. Proposals return to bring back racing without drafting at elite
level, or to add an Olympic event without it. The answer is the one that won
in 1995: nobody could officiate the old rule, and the racing was worse to
watch.

**The two rulebooks.** Elite and <Term id="age-group">age-group</Term> racing
have had different central rules and different bicycles for thirty years.
Nobody proposes joining them. The safety argument for the amateur rule is
strong, and so is the broadcast argument for the elite rule.

**Water quality.** Swims are shortened or cancelled fairly often, and the
sport has only its limits and its fallback plan. A race that became a
<Term id="duathlon">duathlon</Term> sits in the same records as a full
triathlon. The federation has not said what that does to its numbers.

**What the times mean.** Every triathlon has a different course, so a time at
one event cannot be compared with a time at another. A bicycle
<Term id="split">split</Term> from before 1995 measures a rider alone, and
one from after it measures a pack. [Rowing](/sports/rowing/) decided that its
times were not records and said so. Triathlon publishes its splits and has
made no statement about them.
