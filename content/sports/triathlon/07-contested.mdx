# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether drafting should have been legalised.** Thirty years on, the argument
that an Olympic triathlon is a swim, a bike ride and a running race — in which
only the running race matters — has not gone away. Proposals to restore
non-drafting racing at elite level, or to add a non-drafting Olympic event,
recur. The counter-argument is the one that won in 1995: nobody could officiate
it, and the racing was worse to watch.

**The two rulebooks.** The elite sport and the age-group sport have had
different central rules and different bicycles for thirty years. Nobody
proposes unifying them, because the safety case for the age-group rule is
strong and the broadcast case for the elite rule is strong. It is simply an
unresolved division that the sport lives with.

**Water quality.** Swims are cancelled or shortened with some regularity, and
the sport has no mechanism beyond thresholds and contingency. A result that is
sometimes a duathlon sits in the same records as one that is a triathlon, and
the federation has never addressed what that does to its own numbers.

**Course standardisation.** Every triathlon is a different course, so times
mean nothing across events. Rowing decided its times were not records and said
so. Triathlon has not made any statement at all, and continues to publish
splits as though they were comparable.
