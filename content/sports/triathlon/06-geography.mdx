# Where it is played

Triathlon has one of the wealthiest participation profiles of any sport on this
site and a competitive map that follows it closely.

Great Britain, France, Australia, New Zealand, the United States, Germany,
Spain, Switzerland and Norway dominate. What they share is a swimming culture,
cycling infrastructure, and a large age-group population with the disposable
income for three sports' equipment and the time to train them.

The swim is the binding constraint on where the sport can grow, exactly as it
is for swimming itself. A country without accessible pools and a culture of
teaching children to swim cannot produce triathletes, and no amount of running
or cycling talent substitutes.

The age-group base is unusual and worth recording. Triathlon has more
recreational competitors relative to its elite than almost anything here, and
that base funds the sport — entry fees, equipment, events. It is also the part
racing under the older, non-drafting rules, so the sport's commercial engine
and its Olympic showcase are different disciplines.

Long-course racing is more commercially organised than federation-organised,
which gives triathlon a governance shape closer to golf's or tennis's than to
most Olympic sports: the events most of the public knows are not run by the
governing body.

Recent growth in Latin America and East Asia has followed pool infrastructure
rather than anything specific to the sport.
