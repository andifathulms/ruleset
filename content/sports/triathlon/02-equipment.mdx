# Equipment evolution

Triathlon's equipment story is unusual because the sport uses two different
sets of it, in two versions of itself, at the same time.

**Elite racing is drafting-legal**, which means riders sit in packs. Tri-bars —
the aerodynamic extensions that let a rider hold a low position — are
prohibited, because a rider on tri-bars in a pack cannot brake or steer
adequately. Elite triathletes ride road bicycles in a bunch, like a cycling
peloton.

**Age-group racing is not**, so riders are alone, tri-bars are permitted, and
the equipment arms race is entirely about aerodynamics. Deep-section wheels,
aero helmets, tri-bars and time-trial frames all matter in a way they simply do
not at the top of the sport.

The result is that the equipment most triathletes own is equipment the
professionals are forbidden to use.

**Wetsuits** are the other regulated item and they are governed by temperature
rather than by preference: mandatory below a threshold, prohibited above one,
because a wetsuit provides both warmth and buoyancy and the second is a
performance gain.

**The helmet rule** is the sport's most distinctive small provision. It must be
fastened before the bicycle is removed from the rack and may not be unfastened
until it is replaced, which makes a safety requirement into a timed procedural
one — and it is enforced strictly enough to have decided races.
