# Governing body politics

World Triathlon's central decision was made in 1995 and it has defined the
sport since: legalise drafting at elite level, keep it banned everywhere else.

The stated reasons were sound. Policing a moving gap between bicycles across a
40 km course requires motorcycle marshals making a judgement call repeatedly,
and it was expensive, inconsistent and disputed. A drafting-legal race also
finishes as a pack, which is dramatically better television than a procession
of solo riders arriving separately.

The consequence was that the bike leg stopped deciding anything. Competitors
and coaches argued at the time that a triathlon in which one of the three
disciplines is neutralised is not a test of three disciplines, and the argument
has never gone away.

What is genuinely unusual is the split. The federation kept the old rule for
age-group racing, on grounds of safety in mass fields — which is defensible —
and so has run two versions of its own sport for thirty years. Baseball's
designated-hitter split lasted forty-nine years across two leagues and is
treated on this site as remarkable. Triathlon's split is between the
professionals and everybody else, and almost nobody remarks on it.

The other governance issue is water. Swim legs are held in water the federation
does not control, and events have been shortened or converted to duathlons
after rainfall. The sport has thresholds and contingencies, and no ability to
fix the underlying problem.
