# Equipment evolution

There is almost no equipment, and the rules keep it that way. A swimmer
wears a swimsuit and a nose clip. Goggles are banned from routines unless
approved for medical reasons, and so is anything that might aid speed,
buoyancy or endurance.

The nose clip is the sport's one piece of gear and its signature: it lets a
swimmer stay upside down without water forcing its way in, and so makes the
long inverted sequences possible.

The pool is the rest of it. Routines at the Games are swum in water at
least three metres deep, deep enough that no one can stand, and the
organisers must provide underwater speakers so the music can be heard below
the surface, where most of the counting is done.

The costume rules reach as far as the face. Natural make-up is allowed and
theatrical make-up is not, and swimwear must be "dignified and appropriate
for athletic competition" — the rules' own phrase, and a fair summary of the
argument the sport has been having with itself since the Aquacades.
