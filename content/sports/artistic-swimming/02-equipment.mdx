# A swimsuit and a nose clip

An artistic swimmer takes almost nothing into the water, and the rules keep
it that way. A swimmer wears a swimsuit and a <Term id="nose-clip">nose clip</Term>.
Goggles are banned from a <Term id="routine">routine</Term> unless a doctor's
reason is approved. So is anything that might help speed, buoyancy or
endurance.

The nose clip is the one piece of gear. It lets a swimmer stay upside down
without water forcing its way into the nose, and that makes the long inverted
sequences possible.

The pool does the rest. At the Games the water is at least three metres deep,
so nobody can stand. The organisers must put speakers under the water, because
the swimmers spend much of a routine below the surface and count the music
there.

The rules reach as far as the face. Natural make-up is allowed and theatrical
make-up is not. Swimwear must be "dignified and appropriate for athletic
competition", in the rules' own words. The sport has argued about show and
athletics since the Aquacades, and that phrase takes a side.
