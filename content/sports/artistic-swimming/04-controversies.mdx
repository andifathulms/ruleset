# Three decisions the sport argued over

## A new name, voted in Budapest

Budapest, July 2017. The general congress of FINA, the federation that then
governed all the aquatic sports, voted to rename synchronised swimming. From
that day it was [artistic swimming](#fina-2017-renamed-artistic-swimming).
The rules, the events and the scoring did not change.

The people who voted were mostly not from the sport. Each country sent two
delegates, from any aquatic sport, and a country had its votes whether or not
it had a single synchronised swimmer.

FINA's vice-president, Sam Ramsamy, gave the reason. The name "brings it into
line with artistic gymnastics and will be better understood by the public and
the media", he said. The model was [gymnastics](/sports/gymnastics/). FINA's
executive director said the question had come from the media and also from the
International Olympic Committee. Another account says the idea was the IOC's,
and that the sport's own technical committee resisted before it gave way. The
two accounts have not been settled.

Swimmers protested. Critics inside the sport said the word artistic reduced
their event to a show. A petition gathered nearly 11,000 signatures. FINA said
it would not go back, and the name has stayed.

## Men at the Games

Men could swim at the world championships from 2015, when the mixed duet was
introduced. Bill May won the first mixed duet world title that year. At the
Olympic Games there was still no place for a man.

On 22 December 2022 the IOC [approved men in the Olympic
team](#ioc-2022-men-in-olympic-team), up to two in a team of eight. World
Aquatics announced it as the result of a campaign led by athletes such as May.
The change came in its narrowest form. The Olympic duet stayed a women's
event, and there is no Olympic men's or mixed event. No man was selected for
Paris in 2024.

## Difficulty that can be checked

Until 2023, difficulty was one of the judges' marks. A mark given on
impression is hard to check.

[The scoring system of 2023](#fina-2023-new-scoring-system) was built to
change that. A team now declares every <Term id="element">element</Term> and
its <Term id="difficulty">difficulty</Term> on a
<Term id="coach-card">coach card</Term> before it swims. The difficulty of
each <Term id="hybrid">hybrid</Term> and each acrobatic element comes from a
formula. Controllers verify what was done, with video review. The federation
said its aim was "objective and detailed measurement". It is the step
[diving](/sports/diving/) took with its difficulty tables.
