# From water show to sport

Artistic swimming began as a show. In the early twentieth century the
Australian swimmer Annette Kellerman performed in glass tanks on the
vaudeville stage and in films. She made swimming to music into something
people paid to watch.

<Photos ids="artistic-swimming-kellerman-1907 artistic-swimming-kellerman-champion" />

Clubs took it up as figure swimming. In Berlin in November 1932 the women's
club Nixe was photographed from above in the wave pool at Lunapark. The
swimmers lie on dark water in the shape of a wheel. Teams still build
patterns like it today.

<Photo id="artistic-swimming-berlin-1932" />

The name came from Chicago. Katherine Curtis coached women's water shows
there in the 1930s. A show she staged at the Century of Progress fair of
1933–34 is credited with the term synchronised swimming.

Then the shows grew. Billy Rose's Aquacades played at the New York
World's Fair in 1939 and in San Francisco in 1940. Esther Williams's films of
the 1940s and 1950s made swimming to music famous around the world. They also
fixed a picture of it in the public mind as a spectacle, and the sport has
argued with that picture ever since.

<Photos ids="artistic-swimming-aquacade-1939 artistic-swimming-aquacade-audition-1940" />

<Photos ids="artistic-swimming-aqua-follies-1952 artistic-swimming-esther-williams-1955" />

It became a competition. Synchronised swimming joined the first world
aquatics championships in 1973 and the Olympic Games in 1984, with a solo and
a duet. The Olympic events changed three times before they settled. In 1996
there was a team event alone. From 2000 there was a duet and a team. From
2024 [the team may include men](#ioc-2022-men-in-olympic-team).
