# Origin and invention

Artistic swimming began as a show. In the early twentieth century the
Australian swimmer Annette Kellerman performed in glass tanks on the
vaudeville stage and in films, and made swimming to music into something
people paid to watch.

<Photos ids="artistic-swimming-kellerman-1907 artistic-swimming-kellerman-champion" />

Clubs took it up as figure swimming. In Berlin in 1932 a women's club was
photographed from above forming a wheel in a wave pool, the kind of pattern
the sport still builds today.

<Photo id="artistic-swimming-berlin-1932" />

The name came from Chicago. Katherine Curtis coached women's water shows
there in the 1930s, and a show she staged at the Century of Progress fair
of 1933–34 is credited with the term synchronised swimming. Billy Rose's
Aquacades of the late 1930s and Esther Williams's films of the 1940s and
1950s made it famous, and fixed in the public mind an image
the sport has argued with ever since.

<Photos ids="artistic-swimming-aquacade-1939 artistic-swimming-aquacade-audition-1940" />

It became a competition. Synchronised swimming joined the first world
aquatics championships in 1973 and the Olympic Games in 1984, with a solo
and a duet. The Olympic events changed three times before they settled: a
team event alone in 1996, then duet and team from 2000, and from 2024 a team
that may include men.
