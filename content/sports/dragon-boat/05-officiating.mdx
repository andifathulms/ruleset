# Checked before the start

Most of an official's work in dragon boat racing is done before the boats
leave the dock.

**Who is in the boat.** Each <Term id="division">division</Term> has rules
about its crew. A <Term id="mixed">mixed crew</Term> must have a set number of
men and of women, and officials count them. Breast cancer survivor divisions
have their own rules on who may race. In divisions with a weight limit, the
crew is weighed.

**The boat.** Hulls are inspected and weighed. The dragon head and tail must
be fitted, and buoyancy aids are checked. When the organisers supply the
boats, they move them between crews from heat to heat so that nobody keeps a
better one.

**During the race** there is less to do. Officials watch that each crew stays
in its <Term id="lane">lane</Term> and does not get in another crew's way.
Paddlers must stay seated, the steerer must stay standing, and nobody may be
swapped. In the 2000-metre race they also watch each
<Term id="turn">turn</Term> around the marks.

Nobody judges the drummer. A drummer may beat well or badly, fast or slow. The
rules require the position and set no standard for it. The only measure is
whether the boat goes faster.
