# Officiating

Lane racing is easy to officiate and dragon boat's officials spend most of
their attention before the race rather than during it.

**During the race** the calls are the ordinary ones for a lane sport: staying
in your water, not interfering with another crew, and — in the 2000 metre event
— taking the turns correctly around the marks, which is the only part of the
sport where steering is genuinely contested.

**Before the race** is where the work is:

**Crew composition.** Divisions specify who may be in the boat. Mixed events
set the number of each sex, and the count is verified. Breast cancer survivor
divisions have their own eligibility. A boat that does not match its division
is not racing in it.

**Boat and equipment.** Hulls are inspected and weighed, the dragon head and
tail must be fitted, buoyancy aids checked. Because boats are commonly supplied
by organisers, allocation and rotation between heats are administered so that
no crew keeps a better hull.

**Seating and positions.** Paddlers remain seated, the steerer stands, and
nobody swaps during a race.

The drummer is the one position on this site whose performance nobody judges. A
drummer can beat well or badly, fast or slow, and no official assesses it — the
consequence is simply whether the crew is faster. It is a required role with no
governing standard, which is unusual enough to be worth stating.
