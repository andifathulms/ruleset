# A festival first, a sport second

Dragon boats were raced at a Chinese festival for more than two thousand years
before anyone timed them.

The story told at the Duanwu Festival is about Qu Yuan, a poet and minister of
the state of Chu. In the usual account he drowned himself in the Miluo River
in the third century BC. His neighbours raced out in boats to find him. They
beat drums and threw rice into the water to keep the fish away from his body.
It is debated whether the races began this way, or whether the story was
attached later to older rites for dragons. Either way, racing at the festival
has been recorded for a very long time.

It still goes on. Most dragon boat racing in the world today is
<Term id="festival-racing">festival racing</Term>, on local water, with crowds
on the banks. The sport with federations and championships is a thin layer on
top of it.

## Hong Kong, 1976

The modern sport began as a tourism event. In 1976 an international race was
organised in Hong Kong, and crews came from abroad. Through the 1980s the
racing spread around the world, with boats, crew sizes and distances that
differed from country to country. In 1991 the
[International Dragon Boat Federation was formed](#dragonboat-1991-idbf) to
fix them: a standard boat of twenty paddlers, a small boat of ten, and races
of 200, 500 and 2000 metres.

<Photo id="dragon-boat-stanley-2011" />

The new rules could have dropped the parts of the festival that do not make a
boat faster. They kept them.
[Every crew must carry a drummer](#dragonboat-crew-composition), and every
boat must wear its <Term id="head-and-tail">dragon head and tail</Term>.

In 2010 the sport was
[raced at the Asian Games in Guangzhou](#dragonboat-2010-asian-games), in the
region where the festival tradition is strongest. It has no place at the
Olympic Games.
