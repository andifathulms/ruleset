# Origin and invention

Dragon boat is the oldest thing on this site that is still, simultaneously, a
religious festival and a governed sport.

The tradition is associated with Qu Yuan, a poet and minister of the state of
Chu who, in the standard account, drowned himself in the Miluo River in the
third century BC, and whose neighbours raced boats out to recover him and beat
drums and threw rice into the water to keep the fish away. Whether that is the
origin or a story attached later to older dragon-propitiation rites is
debated; either way, racing at the Duanwu Festival is attested for a very long
time and continues now.

That is not background. Most dragon boat racing in the world today is still
festival racing, in enormous numbers, and the governed sport is a small
formalised layer on top of a living tradition. Very few sports on this site are
in that position — karate's traditional schools and sepak takraw's circle games
are the closest comparisons, and neither is on this scale.

Competitive codification began in Hong Kong in 1976, when an international race
was organised as a tourism event and crews came from abroad. From there the
sport spread through the 1980s, and the International Dragon Boat Federation
was formed in 1991 to fix boat specifications, crew sizes and distances.

What the codification kept is the striking part. It could have removed the
dragon head and tail as drag, and it could have deleted the drummer as
ceremonial. It required both.
