# Equipment evolution

A racing boat that is required to carry decoration, which is not a sentence
that applies to anything else on this site.

**The dragon head and tail** are fittings mounted at bow and stern, and
competition rules require them. Every other racing craft covered here has been
stripped of everything that does not make it faster: a rowing eight, a canoe, a
kayak and an Olympic dinghy carry nothing ornamental. Dragon boat kept the
ornament and made it a rule, because removing it would have made the boat
something else.

**The hull** has moved from heavy wooden festival craft to composite racing
boats, and this is where the sport's fairness problem sits. Boats are not
one-design in the way a sailing class is, and championship organisers usually
supply identical boats to remove the variable — which is an admission that
otherwise it would decide races.

**The paddle** is single-bladed and short, used seated, facing forward, from a
fixed bench. That combination is the sport's technical signature: no oarlock,
so no lever against the hull; no sliding seat, so no leg drive; no double
blade, so no alternating stroke. Power comes from trunk rotation, which is why
elite dragon boat paddlers look nothing like rowers.

**The drum** is equipment carried for a competitor who supplies no propulsion,
and **the steering oar** is a long sweep worked standing at the stern.

Buoyancy aids are required, boats are weighed, and in some divisions crews are
too.
