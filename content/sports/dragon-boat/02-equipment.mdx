# The boat, the paddle and the drum

A racing dragon boat must carry decoration. The carved head at the front and
the tail at the back do nothing for speed, and the rules require both. A
[rowing](/sports/rowing/) eight or a racing kayak carries nothing it does not
need. Dragon boat kept the ornament because without it the boat would be
something else.

## Paddling from a fixed seat

The <Term id="paddle">paddle</Term> is short, with a single blade. Paddlers sit
two to a <Term id="bench">bench</Term>, facing forwards. The bench does not
slide, so the legs cannot drive the stroke as they do in rowing. The paddle is
not fixed to the boat, so there is nothing to lever against. Power comes from
turning the hips and the trunk. The nearest thing to it is the stroke in sprint
[canoeing](/sports/canoeing/), done sitting where a canoeist kneels.

<Photo id="dragon-boat-alkmaar-2022" />

Two pieces of equipment belong to people who do not paddle. The
<Term id="drummer">drummer</Term> has a drum. The
<Term id="steerer">steerer</Term> has a long
<Term id="steering-oar">steering oar</Term> and works it standing up.

## Whose boat is it?

Hulls have changed from heavy wooden festival boats to light composite racing
boats, and they are not all built to one design. A crew in a better boat is
faster. Championship organisers usually deal with this by supplying identical
boats to every crew. Boats are weighed and inspected before racing.
