# The medical advice that was wrong

One division in dragon boat racing began as a medical study.

In 1996 the standard advice to women who had had surgery for breast cancer was
to avoid hard, repeated exercise with the upper body. Doctors feared it would
cause lymphoedema, a swelling of the arm, or make it worse. Dragon boat
paddling is exactly that kind of exercise.

A sports medicine researcher at the University of British Columbia, in
Vancouver, set out to test whether the advice was justified. The researcher
recruited breast cancer survivors and put them in a dragon boat as a crew.

The findings went against the advice.

The study ended and the crew kept paddling. Other crews formed around the
world, some in countries with no other dragon boat tradition at all. They hold international festivals of their own. The federation later
[added breast cancer survivor divisions to its world championships](#dragonboat-bcs-crews),
where <Term id="bcs">BCS</Term> crews now race for titles.
