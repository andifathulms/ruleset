# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Whether the drummer is a competitor.** A crew member who contributes no
propulsion, in a sport decided by boat speed, is an obvious target for anyone
optimising. The rules require the position, so the argument is settled in the
rulebook and not in the sport: whether the beat genuinely helps a trained crew,
or whether the drum survives because removing it would make dragon boat a
generic paddling event, is discussed by crews constantly.

**The boats.** Because hulls are not one-design, a crew in a better boat is
faster. Championship organisers supply identical boats for exactly this reason,
which is a fairness solution that concedes the problem.

**The medical advice that was wrong.** In 1996 a Vancouver study put breast
cancer survivors into a dragon boat crew to test the standing advice against
strenuous repetitive upper-body exercise after surgery. The advice did not
survive the test. The crews did — and BCS divisions are now contested at world
championships. This site records it as a rule change caused by clinical
evidence, which is a category it has otherwise only met in lifesaving.

**Two federations.** Dragon boat is governed in parallel by the IDBF and by the
international canoe federation. Athletes, crews and national bodies have to
choose, and no mechanism reconciles the results.

**Festival and sport.** The overwhelming majority of dragon boat racing is
community and ceremonial. How much a governing body should regulate it, and
whether championship rules should apply to a festival, is a live question in
several countries.
