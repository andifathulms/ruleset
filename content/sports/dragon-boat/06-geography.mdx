# Where it is played

A Chinese festival that became a global club sport, with the two layers still
visible everywhere.

**China** is the origin and remains central, with festival racing across the
south in particular and a strong competitive programme. **Hong Kong** codified
the modern competition and its international races from 1976 are where the
sport's global spread began. **Chinese Taipei**, **Singapore**, **Malaysia**,
**Indonesia**, **Thailand**, **the Philippines**, **Myanmar** and **Vietnam**
all have deep traditions, and **Japan** and **South Korea** compete strongly.

Outside Asia the sport travelled first through Chinese diaspora communities and
then, unusually, into the mainstream. **Canada** is a genuine power with a very
large club and festival scene, and so are **Germany**, **Hungary**, **Italy**,
**the Czech Republic**, **Poland**, **Great Britain**, **the United States**,
**Australia** and **New Zealand**.

That spread is more successful than most sports on this site manage, and the
reason is the entry structure. A dragon boat crew needs twenty-two people and
supplies all the equipment, so corporate teams, community groups, universities
and charity crews can form and race within weeks. The sport recruits sideways
into populations that were not looking for a sport.

The breast cancer survivor movement is the clearest case of that. It began in
Vancouver in 1996 and is now a worldwide network of crews, with international
festivals of its own, in countries with no other dragon boat tradition at all.
