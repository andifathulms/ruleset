# From southern China to everywhere

Dragon boat racing started in China and is still strongest there, above all in
the south, where the festival races are held.

Hong Kong gave the sport its modern form, and its international races were
where the spread began. Chinese Taipei, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia,
Thailand, the Philippines, Myanmar and Vietnam all have deep traditions. Japan
and South Korea are strong competitors.

Outside Asia the sport travelled first with Chinese communities abroad. Then
it reached everyone else. Canada is one of the leading nations and has a very
large club and festival scene. Germany, Hungary, Italy, the Czech Republic,
Poland, Great Britain, the United States, Australia and New Zealand are strong
too.

## Why it spread so easily

A newcomer needs nothing except to be able to swim. The club owns the boats
and the paddles. The boat is wide and steady and holds twenty-two people, so a
beginner cannot tip it over and is never alone with a mistake. In a rowing or
canoeing club, a beginner in a single boat spends the first sessions falling
into the water.

So companies, community groups, universities and charities form crews and are
racing within weeks. The sport finds people who were not looking for a sport.

A top crew is another matter. Twenty paddlers must put the blade in, pull and
take it out together, for the whole race. A paddler who is out of time does
worse than fail to help. That paddler slows the boat.
