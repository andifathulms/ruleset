# Governing body politics

Two international federations claim dragon boat, and both run championships.

The **International Dragon Boat Federation**, formed in 1991, is the body that
codified the sport out of the festival tradition and holds its own world
championships across standard and small boats, several distances and open,
women's, mixed and breast cancer survivor divisions.

The **international canoe federation** also runs dragon boat as one of its
disciplines, with its own competitions. Its claim rests on dragon boat being a
paddling sport within its remit; the IDBF's rests on having built the
international sport. Neither ratifies the other's results.

This site records the same structure in powerlifting, where federations split
over drug testing and never reunified, and the consequence is identical: not a
shortage of competition, but the absence of anybody with the standing to say
what a world championship is.

For dragon boat the stakes are concrete. The canoe federation is on the Olympic
programme. Any route dragon boat has to a Games plausibly runs through it
rather than through the body that built the sport, which is a difficult
proposition for the IDBF and an obvious one for everybody else.

The Asian Games place, from Guangzhou in 2010, is the sport's only multi-sport
platform, and it sits in the region where the festival tradition is strongest.

There is one further political fact worth recording plainly: the sport's
largest single act of institutional imagination was not the federation's at all.
The breast cancer survivor divisions came out of a clinical study, and the
federation adopted them.
