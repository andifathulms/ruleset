# What is still argued about

## Two federations, one sport

Two international bodies run dragon boat racing, and each holds its own world
championships. The International Dragon Boat Federation built the
international sport. The international [canoeing](/sports/canoeing/)
federation counts dragon boat as one of its paddling disciplines. Neither
accepts the other's results, so athletes and national bodies have to choose.
[Powerlifting](/sports/powerlifting/) is split in a similar way. There is
plenty of competition, and nobody with the standing to say which world
championship is the real one.

The canoe federation is on the Olympic programme. If dragon boat ever reaches
the Olympic Games, the road probably runs through that body and not through
the one that built the sport.

## Smaller arguments

**The drummer.** The <Term id="drummer">drummer</Term> adds no power, and the
rules require one. Crews argue about whether the beat really helps a trained
crew, or whether the drum stays because a boat without it would be an ordinary
paddling race. The rule prevents the test that would settle it.

**The boats.** Organisers supply the boats at championships. That is fair, and
it means a national team cannot develop its own equipment, which most water
sports treat as part of the contest.

**Festival and championship.** Most dragon boat racing is older than every
federation involved. Several countries are arguing over how far a governing
body should regulate a festival, and whether championship rules belong there.

**The survivors' crews.** People in the movement discuss whether their racing
should be drawn further into the championships, kept as its own festival
circuit, or opened to people with other conditions.
