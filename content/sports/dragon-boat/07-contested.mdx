# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Which federation governs.** The IDBF built the international sport; the canoe
federation runs dragon boat as one of its disciplines and is on the Olympic
programme. Both hold competitions, neither recognises the other's results, and
any Olympic route plausibly runs through the body that did not build the sport.

**The drummer.** Required by rule, contributing no propulsion, and assessed by
nobody. Whether the position is a competitive asset or a preserved ceremony is
argued by crews, and the rulebook prevents the experiment that would settle it.

**Boats.** Not one-design, so championship organisers supply them. That works
and it means a national programme cannot develop equipment, which most water
sports treat as part of the discipline.

**Festival and championship.** Most dragon boat racing in the world is
ceremonial and community racing that predates every federation involved. How
much governance it should attract, and whether championship rules belong there,
is contested in several countries and is not really a sporting question.

**The BCS divisions.** A competitive category that came out of a clinical
study and now spans the world. Whether it should be integrated further into
championship programmes, kept as its own festival circuit, or extended to other
conditions, is discussed within the movement rather than by the federation.

**Olympic prospects.** An Asian Games place, an enormous participation base, no
Olympic place, and a governance dispute standing directly in the path.
