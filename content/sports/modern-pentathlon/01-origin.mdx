# Where it came from

Modern pentathlon was invented by one man for one event, and it came with a
story. Pierre de Coubertin, the founder of the modern Olympic Games, devised
it for the Games of 1912.

The story was about a cavalry officer of the nineteenth century, caught
behind enemy lines. To get away he must ride a horse he does not know, fight
with a sword, shoot a pistol, swim a river and run. That gave five
<Term id="discipline">disciplines</Term> for one competitor in one
competition. The first edition, at Stockholm, was contested largely by army
officers. One of them was George S. Patton of the United States.

The name comes from the pentathlon of the ancient Games, which was discus,
javelin, long jump, running and wrestling. The two share the number five and
the aim of finding a complete athlete, and nothing else.

<Photo id="modern-pentathlon-tokyo-stamp" />

The story explains the oddest rule in the sport. The officer's horse was a
stranger to him. So the pentathlete rides a
<Term id="drawn-horse">drawn horse</Term>, given by lot and met twenty
minutes before the round.

The governing body, the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne, was
founded in 1948. The sport has been on the programme of every summer Games
since 1912.
