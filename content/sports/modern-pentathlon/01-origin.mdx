# Origin and invention

Modern pentathlon is the only sport on this site invented by the founder of the
modern Olympics, for the modern Olympics, and it comes with a stated purpose
that is unusually specific.

Pierre de Coubertin devised it for the 1912 Games as a test of the skills a
nineteenth-century cavalry officer would need if caught behind enemy lines. He
would ride an unfamiliar horse, fight with a sword, shoot a pistol, swim a
river and run. Five disciplines, one competitor, one day, and a narrative
justification for the combination that no other multi-sport event has.

The ancient pentathlon it is named after — discus, javelin, long jump, running,
wrestling — has nothing in common with it beyond the number five and the
ambition to find a complete athlete.

That founding story matters here more than most origins do, because it is the
thing the sport has spent fifteen years dismantling. The narrative required a
horse the competitor had never met, which is why the riding discipline works
the way it does. Remove the horse and the story stops holding: the remaining
four disciplines are a swim, a fence, a run with shooting, and an obstacle
course, which is a fine set of tests and is no longer an account of anything.

De Coubertin regarded it as his favourite event. The UIPM was founded in 1948,
and the sport has been on every summer programme since 1912.
