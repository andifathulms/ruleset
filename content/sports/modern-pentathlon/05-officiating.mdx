# Officiating and technology

Modern pentathlon does not really have officiating of its own. It borrows four
sports' worth, which is unique here and produces a specific kind of problem.

Fencing is judged by fencing rules, with electric apparatus registering hits.
Swimming is timed by touchpad. Show jumping is judged by show jumping's faults.
The laser run is timed and its shooting is registered electronically. Each
component arrives with its own apparatus, its own officials and its own
tolerance for error, and the pentathlon adds only the conversion arithmetic on
top.

Which means the sport's real officiating instrument is the **points table** —
the conversion from a swim time or a fencing record into a common currency. No
official applies it in the moment; it is applied by the federation in advance,
when it decides what a second is worth against a hit. That is a rule doing the
work an official does elsewhere, and it is the least visible consequential
decision in this collection.

Technology has been kind to the sport in one specific way. Laser shooting
registers electronically and instantly, so the discipline that once needed a
range, a target-scoring process and a safety officer now needs a sensor. That
is the same move taekwondo made with electronic body protectors, and the same
move archery made with optical scoring: a judgement or a manual process
replaced by an instrument.

Riding was the exception and the one that could not be instrumented. Whether a
horse refuses, and whose fault it is, is not a question a sensor can answer —
and the sport has resolved it by removing the question.
