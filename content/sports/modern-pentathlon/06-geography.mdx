# Where it is played

Modern pentathlon has the narrowest participation base of any sport on this
site, and its geography is a direct map of who can afford four facilities.

Hungary, Russia, Italy, Great Britain, Germany, France, Poland, South Korea,
China, Egypt and Mexico account for most international results. What those have
in common is not a pentathlon culture but the infrastructure: a national
programme that can put an athlete in a pool, a fencing hall, a shooting range
and an equestrian centre, and coach them in all four.

Hungary is the outlier worth recording. It has treated modern pentathlon as a
national sport for most of a century and has produced more Olympic champions in
it than any other country, on the back of a club system that does not exist
anywhere else.

Riding was the binding constraint, and everybody in the sport knew it. Access
to horses is expensive, unevenly distributed and socially narrow almost
everywhere, and a discipline requiring an equestrian facility is a discipline
requiring a certain kind of country. That is one of the federation's stated
reasons for the 2028 change, and it is the one this site finds most credible on
its own terms — obstacle racing needs a frame in a field.

Whether replacing riding actually widens the sport's map is a genuine open
question. Three facility-dependent disciplines remain, and no sport has ever
broadened its base by removing a quarter of itself.
