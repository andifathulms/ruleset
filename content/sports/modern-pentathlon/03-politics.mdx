# Governing body politics

Every rule change on this page is a move in a single long negotiation: modern
pentathlon's place on the Olympic programme.

The sport is expensive to stage, hard to broadcast, thinly participated
worldwide, and has no professional structure or independent audience. Under the
IOC's programme review criteria — cost, universality, audience, youth appeal —
it has been near the bottom for two decades and has been repeatedly named as a
candidate for removal.

Read the rule changes in that light and they stop looking like separate
decisions. Merging shooting and running in 2009 gave the sport a finish line
somebody could point a camera at. Laser pistols in 2011 cut the venue
requirement. Compressing the Olympic final to ninety minutes in 2024 made it
fit a broadcast window. Removing riding from 2028 removed the most expensive
component and the one attracting welfare objections.

That is a governing body reshaping its sport, component by component, to
survive an assessment.

The 2022 riding decision is where this became a governance crisis rather than a
strategy. A large part of the sport's own athlete body opposed it, publicly,
and argued that the process was not legitimate and that the stated reasons were
not the real ones. Pentathlon United was formed in opposition. The federation
proceeded anyway.

This site records both the federation's reasons and the athletes' objection,
and does not adjudicate. What is not in dispute is that a sport removed a
founding discipline against the stated wishes of a substantial share of the
people who compete in it.
