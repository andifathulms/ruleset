# Controversies that forced rule changes

**Tokyo, 6 August 2021.** In the women's riding round, the horse drawn by
Annika Schleu refused repeatedly. She was in the lead. Her coach, Kim Raisner,
struck the horse; Schleu used her whip and was in visible distress; the round
ended without a jump completed. It was broadcast live and the footage spread
far outside the sport within hours.

Raisner was removed from the Games. Welfare organisations, much of the public
and a good deal of the sport's own membership concluded that asking a
competitor to jump an unfamiliar animal after twenty minutes was indefensible.

**Nine months later**, the UIPM Congress voted to remove riding from modern
pentathlon after 2024.

This is the most drastic trigger-to-rule sequence on this site and it is worth
being precise about the causal claim, because the entry records it as disputed.
The cost and welfare arguments against riding predate Tokyo by decades. The
sport's Olympic position was already under review. What Tokyo did was make an
existing argument unanswerable in public — which is a different thing from
causing it.

**The athletes' objection.** A substantial part of the competing body opposed
the decision, formed an organisation to fight it, and argued that the
consultation was inadequate and the reasons given were not the operative ones.
That objection is unusual: on most pages of this site, the people affected by a
rule change were not organised enough to say so on the record.

**The scoring tables.** A quieter and recurring one. Because each discipline
converts to points on tables the federation writes, changing a table changes who
wins, and every revision has been argued about by the athletes whose strengths
it moved.
