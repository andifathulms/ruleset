# One round in Tokyo

## 6 August 2021

It was the riding round of the women's competition at the Olympic Games in
Tokyo. Annika Schleu was in the lead. The horse she had drawn refused its
fences, again and again. Her coach, Kim Raisner, struck the horse. Schleu
used her whip, and she was plainly in distress. All of it was broadcast live,
and within hours the pictures had travelled far outside the sport.

Raisner was removed from the Games. Welfare organisations, much of the public
and many people inside the sport reached the same view: an athlete should not
be asked to jump an animal she met twenty minutes earlier.

## The vote

Nine months later the congress of the federation [voted to remove riding from
modern pentathlon](#uipm-2022-riding-removed) after the 2024 Games.

What caused the decision is disputed. The federation's stated reasons were
animal welfare, cost, and the difficulty of providing equestrian facilities.
All three arguments are decades older than Tokyo, and the sport's Olympic
place was already under review. On another reading, Tokyo made an existing
argument about cost impossible to answer in public, and welfare was the
reason that could be given. Many athletes said the reason given was not the
real one. The sources do not settle it.

## The points tables

A quieter dispute returns every few years. Each result is turned into points
on the federation's <Term id="points-table">points table</Term>. When the
table changes, the value of a second in the pool changes against the value of
a <Term id="hit">hit</Term> in fencing, and a different athlete may win.
Every revision has been argued over by the athletes whose strengths it moved.
