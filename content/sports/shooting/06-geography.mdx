# Where it is played

Shooting's map is drawn by national firearms law more than by wealth, climate
or tradition, which makes it unlike anything else on this site.

China, South Korea, India, the United States, Italy, Germany, Russia, Hungary,
Serbia and Norway are the sustained powers. What they have in common is not
obvious until you look at regulation: each has a legal framework under which
sport shooting is organised, licensed and club-based, and a facility network
that follows from it.

Countries with restrictive firearms law can still be strong — the air events at
ten metres are the route, and they require low-powered equipment that many
jurisdictions treat differently from firearms proper. India's rise is largely
in air rifle and pistol for exactly this reason, and China's programme is
concentrated there too.

The shotgun disciplines have a different and more traditional map, following
hunting and clay shooting cultures in Italy, Britain, the United States and the
Nordic countries.

The sport is one of very few here where a competitor's ability to practise
depends on where they live in a legal rather than a physical sense. A swimmer
without a pool is unlucky; a shooter in a country with restrictive licensing
may be unable to train at all regardless of resources.

That also makes shooting's public position unusually fragile. Its Olympic
standing has been questioned after mass shootings in several countries, and the
sport's drift towards low-powered indoor air events at ten metres is partly a
response to a debate it cannot win on its own terms.
