# Equipment evolution

Shooting regulates its equipment more tightly than almost any sport here, and
the most interesting item is not a firearm.

**The clothing.** Rifle shooters wear jackets and trousers stiff enough to
support the body in position, and their thickness and rigidity are measured
with gauges at competition. A stiffer jacket is a steadier hold, so the
specification is a performance rule in the shape of a uniform rule — the same
structure as equestrian's noseband gauge and judo's judogi tolerances.

**The firearms** are specified by trigger weight, stock dimensions, sight type
and total weight, with optical sights prohibited in ISSF events. The
specifications are detailed and stable, and shooting has produced no
equipment-driven comparability break of the javelin or supersuit kind.

**The targets** are where the real change happened. Electronic targets replaced
paper and manual gauging at international level through the 1990s and 2000s,
registering a shot's position acoustically or optically. That did two things:
it removed the sport's routine dispute — whether a hole touched a line, judged
by an official with a plug gauge — and it made decimal scoring possible at all.

The second consequence is the one that matters. Once the instrument could
resolve tenths of a ring, the sport discovered that whole-ring scores had
stopped separating its best shooters, and moved qualification onto decimals in
2013. A better instrument revealed that the old scale had run out, which is the
opposite of the pattern everywhere else on this site, where better instruments
revealed that the old measurements were wrong.
