# Guns, jackets and targets

Shooting sets tight limits on equipment, and one of the strictest is on
clothing.

## The jacket

Rifle shooters wear a <Term id="shooting-jacket">shooting jacket</Term> and
trousers stiff enough to help hold the body in position. A stiffer jacket
gives a steadier hold. So the thickness and stiffness of the clothing are
measured with gauges at each competition.
[Equestrian](/sports/equestrian/) sport measures its nosebands and
[judo](/sports/judo/) its jackets in the same spirit.

## The gun

Each event sets the <Term id="trigger-weight">trigger weight</Term>, the size
of the stock, the kind of sight and the total weight. Telescopic sights are
banned in ISSF events, and shooters aim through a small round opening. These
limits are detailed and have been stable. No change of equipment has made old
shooting scores impossible to compare with new ones.

## The target

The big change came in the target. For a long time a shot was scored on
paper. An official pushed a plug gauge into the hole and judged whether it
touched a line. Through the 1990s and 2000s, [electronic targets replaced
paper at international level](#issf-2005-electronic-targets). An
<Term id="electronic-target">electronic target</Term> registers the position
of a shot by sound or by light.

<Photos ids="shooting-paper-target shooting-electronic-target-monitor" />

That ended the routine argument over whether a hole touched a line. It also
made <Term id="decimal-scoring">decimal scoring</Term> possible. A monitor can
show a shot as 10.3, a value no official with a gauge could give.

Once tenths of a <Term id="ring">ring</Term> could be measured, a problem
showed. The best shooters were posting nearly the same whole-ring totals, and
the old scale could no longer separate them. [In 2013 qualification in air
rifle moved to decimals](#issf-2013-decimal-qualification).
