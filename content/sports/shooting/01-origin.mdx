# Origin and invention

Shooting is on this site's programme because of a specific historical fact: it
was a military skill that civilian societies organised into competition, and
the Baron de Coubertin was himself a pistol shooter. It has been on the Olympic
programme since 1896.

The founding institutions were national rifle associations, formed in the
nineteenth century largely for military preparedness — Britain's dates from
1859, the American one from 1871 — and the sport carries that origin in its
structure. Its events are derived from military and hunting practice: prone
rifle, rapid fire pistol, running target, clay shooting as a substitute for live
birds.

The transition from those origins to a precision sport happened gradually and is
essentially complete. Modern Olympic shooting is contested with air rifles and
pistols at ten metres indoors, in events with no military application at all,
and the sport's technical problem is stillness rather than marksmanship in any
practical sense.

What has not changed is the sport's relationship to law. Shooting is the only
sport on this site whose participation depends directly on national firearms
regulation, which means its accessibility varies more by country than anything
else here — and it means the sport's public standing is tied to arguments that
have nothing to do with sport.

The ISSF in its present form dates from 1998, having reorganised from the older
International Shooting Union.
