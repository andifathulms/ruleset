# From the army range to the indoor hall

Sport shooting began as practice for war. National rifle associations were
formed in the nineteenth century, largely so that civilians would be ready to
fight. Britain's dates from 1859 and the American one from 1871.

In July 1860 the British association held its first meeting on Wimbledon
Common, and Queen Victoria fired the first shot. A year later riflemen lay
down at the same firing point to shoot for the Queen's Prize.

Shooting was at the first modern Olympic Games in 1896. Baron de Coubertin,
who founded the Games, was a pistol shooter himself. The United States rifle
team at Stockholm in 1912 was made up mostly of soldiers and sailors.

<Photo id="shooting-us-rifle-team-1912" />

## Events taken from war and hunting

The early events came from military and hunting practice: rifle shot lying
down, rapid fire pistol, a running target, and clay shooting in place of live
birds. At Stockholm in 1912 the target in one event was a running deer, and
Sweden's team won it. In Paris in 1900, live pigeons were shot during the
Exposition held alongside the Games. Clay targets later replaced the birds.

## A sport of stillness

The move from those beginnings to a precision sport was slow, and it is almost
complete. Much of modern Olympic shooting is done with air rifles and air
pistols, indoors, at ten metres. These <Term id="air-events">air events</Term>
have no military use. The difficulty is to stand still.

<Photo id="shooting-air-rifle-2024" />

One thing has stayed the same. Who may shoot depends on each country's
firearms law, so access to the sport differs greatly from one country to the
next.

The International Shooting Union was founded in 1907. It was reorganised as
the International Shooting Sport Federation, the ISSF, in 1998.
