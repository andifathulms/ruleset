# Officiating and technology

Shooting has almost eliminated officiating from its scoring, and it did so
earlier and more completely than any sport on this site except tennis.

For a century, a shot was scored by an official examining a paper target with a
plug gauge and deciding whether the hole touched a line. It was slow, it was
disputable, and disputes were routine. **Electronic targets** removed the
question entirely: the shot's position is registered and displayed, and there
is nothing for anyone to interpret.

What that left officials with is procedure — timing, commands, equipment
inspection and safety — and safety is absolute in a way it is nowhere else
here. A firearm handled outside procedure ends a competitor's participation
regardless of anything else.

**Equipment inspection** is the sport's most substantial officiating activity,
and it is genuinely consequential: jacket stiffness and trigger weight are
measured with gauges, and a shooter whose kit fails does not compete.

The interesting thing about shooting's technology is what it revealed rather
than what it corrected. Elsewhere on this site, better instruments showed that
officials had been getting things wrong — tennis line calls, football offsides,
swimming's breaststroke kick. In shooting the officials had been broadly right;
what the instrument showed was that the *scale* had run out. Elite shooters
were producing identical whole-ring totals, and the sport had to add decimal
places to see a difference that had always been there.

That is a measurement problem rather than a judgement problem, and shooting is
the only sport here that has had one.
