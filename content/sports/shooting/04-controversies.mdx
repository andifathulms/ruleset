# Controversies that forced rule changes

**The invisible sport.** Shooting's structural problem is not an incident. For
most of its history competitors fired their series over hours and a result
appeared on a board, which is an excellent way to determine the best shooter
and no way at all to hold an audience.

Everything on this page follows from attempts to fix it. Finals in 1988, so
there is an ending. Decimal scoring in those finals, so the ending can separate
people. Progressive elimination and a reset to zero in 2013, so the ending is a
head-to-head with medals at stake.

**2013, and the discarded score.** The change that shooters objected to most.
Before it, a qualification lead carried into the final, which frequently made
the final a formality. After it, qualification decides only who is in the
final. A competitor can outshoot the field for sixty shots and finish eighth.

The objection is precise and this site finds it strong: the sport's claim is
precision, and it has arranged its competition so that its most precise
measurement has the least bearing on the medal. The federation's answer is that
a final nobody watches serves nobody, which is the same answer archery gave in
2011 and sailing gave in 2008.

**Running out of decimals.** A quieter one. By the 2010s elite air rifle and
pistol shooters were producing near-identical whole-ring totals, so the sport's
scale had stopped discriminating. Extending decimal scoring to qualification in
2013 restored resolution — and changed the unit every qualification record was
expressed in.
