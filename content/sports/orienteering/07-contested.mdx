# What is contested now

Recorded as unsettled.

**Whether the sport can be watched.** Sprint orienteering, mass-start relays
and live GPS tracking are all answers to the same problem, and the problem is
not solved. The core disciplines still happen out of sight, and the federation's
Olympic ambitions run into that before they run into anything else.

**Sprint versus forest.** Sprint is the discipline that broadcasts, and a
substantial part of the sport regards it as a different event — urban, fast,
and testing route choice at a street level rather than navigation in complex
terrain. Which discipline represents orienteering is a genuine internal
argument, with the World Games contesting sprint and relay.

**Terrain rotation.** Where a world championship is held decides a lot about
who wins it. That the rotation is a fairness mechanism is agreed; how it should
be balanced is not.

**GPS.** Required for spectators, banned for competitors, and the gap between
those two facts narrows every year as devices get smaller. The sport has to
keep drawing a line that technology keeps moving.

**Mapping labour.** The sport depends on volunteer surveyors producing maps to
an exacting specification. That workforce is ageing in several countries, and
no federation has a plan for replacing it. It is a more immediate threat to
orienteering than anything about programmes or broadcast.

**Whether the absence of records matters.** Orienteering gave up comparable
times in exchange for fairness across terrain. Nobody inside the sport proposes
reversing it, and it does mean the sport can never tell a story about getting
faster — which is the story most measured sports use to explain themselves.
