# What is still argued about

**Can the sport be watched?** The sprint, the mass-start relay and live GPS
tracking are all answers to one problem, and the problem is not solved. The
main disciplines still happen out of sight, and that stands in the way of the
federation's Olympic hopes before anything else does.

**Sprint or forest.** The sprint is the discipline that works on a screen.
Many in the sport see it as a different event: urban, fast, and a test of
<Term id="route-choice">route choice</Term> at street level more than of
navigation in complex terrain. The World Games contest the sprint, the middle
distance and the relay. Which discipline stands for orienteering is a real
argument inside it.

**Terrain rotation.** Everyone agrees the rotation of championship terrain is
there for fairness. How to balance it is not agreed.

**GPS.** Spectators must have it and runners must not. The tracker a runner
carries for the audience must not be readable by the runner, and that has to
be policed. Devices get smaller every year, and the line gets harder to hold.

**Who will draw the maps.** The sport depends on volunteer surveyors working
to an exacting specification. In several countries they are growing old, and
no federation has a plan to replace them.

**Does it matter that there are no records?** Orienteering gave up comparable
times in exchange for fairness across terrain, and nobody inside it proposes
going back. [Athletics](/sports/athletics/) is built so that a time in one
stadium can be set against a time in another fifty years later. Orienteering
can never tell a story about getting faster.
